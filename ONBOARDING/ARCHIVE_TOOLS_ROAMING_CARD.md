# SAT/H(s)H Archive Tools — Roaming Card

Use the broadest useful archive surface. Do not assume the current repo or current vocabulary contains the thing you are looking for.

## Search / retrieval

- **Mersearch / Mercer_Searcher** — Primary corpus-wide body-search route. Use for terms, concepts, names, precursors, terminology drift, and deliberately divergent searches. Search variants and neighboring concepts, not just exact modern vocabulary.
- **Conversation Viewer / manifests** — Wayfinding across exported conversations: dates, titles, canonical raw paths, archive location, and direct conversation inspection. The manifest is navigation metadata, not proof that a term is absent from conversation bodies.
- **Convos archives — including `convos_21` and earlier/later tranches** — Raw conversation history. Search across all available archive generations when reconstructing an idea, person/instance, terminology, chronology, or provenance. Very large/zero-byte-looking entries may actually mean **really big**, not empty.
- **SAT/H(s)H repos and GitHub search/history** — Search current files, historical files, commits, diffs, issues, handoffs, Labs, Commons, worker outputs, source indexes, and deleted/superseded states when recoverable. Commit history is often essential for chronology.
- **HSH_RESOURCES** — Project research/source collection, including historical project materials and outside resources. Use exact provenance and authorship/status labels.
- **NLM / numbered archive folders** — Deep source-recovery layer. Folder numbers are wayfinding, not authority or chronology. Follow indexes back to originals where possible and flag gaps.
- **Nathan Words / Nathan-source excavation** — Use for Nathan’s actual language, conceptual ancestry, reversals, terminology, methodological statements, and voice. A Nathan-source item is evidence of what was said in that context, not automatic current-theory authority.
- **Braintrust Memorial Commons / Field Notes / Labs / Q&A / handoffs / check-ins** — Useful for hypotheses, failed approaches, recommendations, unresolved questions, cross-instance observations, and pointers into deeper sources. Treat as working records, not established theory.
- **Revival materials / historical-instance records** — Use to recover earlier instances, methods, forgotten results, first-blush reactions, and historical terminology. Search aliases: e.g. Alberr / Albert / Einstein and other known identity variants.
- **Public-site / Glass Sausage Factory feeds and artifacts** — Useful for locating presentation-ready material, public chronology, papers, conversations, quotes/news, and publication state. Public presentation does not by itself establish theory status.

## Search behavior

Search conceptually and historically, not only lexically. Try modern term → older synonyms → descriptive phrases → opposite framing → nearby dates → likely conversations → original artifact.

When useful, deliberately search somewhere unlikely: old SAT, philosophy/methodology, abandoned branches, unrelated-looking conversations, historical instances, or material opposite your normal specialty. Unexpected ancestry is often valuable.

Prefer the original source over an index, summary, recollection, or later retelling. Preserve exact path/date/author/instance/status when something matters.

Absence from one index, viewer, repo, folder, or keyword search is **not evidence of absence from the archives**.

## Quarantine / sandbox — hard rule

**PRIOR_ART, explicitly private material, quarantined repos/folders, and any source marked QUARANTINE are closed unless your current assignment explicitly grants access.** Do not search into them “just to check.” Do not leak their vocabulary, targets, solution methods, citations, or conclusions into unexposed workers or internal theory development.

If you are an authorized quarantined observer, return only through the designated quarantine-safe interface and preserve the requested blinding/de-dup/provenance boundary.

**SANDBOXED material may be inspected when otherwise accessible, but sandbox ≠ established theory, validation, publication, or authority.** Watercooler, Field Notes, Labs, Nathan-source material, revival material, worker speculation, and sandbox papers likewise remain status-typed.

When uncertain: **preserve the boundary, record the pointer, and route the question rather than crossing it.**

## Default output from an archive roam

Bring back the useful thing itself, plus:

**where it came from → when → who/what produced it → exact status → why it matters → what it does not establish → best next pointer.**

Do not silently modernize historical terminology or back-project current H(s)H concepts into older SAT material.
