# Public-facing editorial layer

**Status:** CURRENT Sites copy rule — 2026-09-30

The public site explains the research, archive, history and current activity. It does not narrate the internal production machinery unless a visitor deliberately opens a provenance/process view.

## Translate internal state into reader-facing state

Prefer:
- “The geometry team is testing recursive ᚼ constructions and comparing their invariants.”
- “Current work is checking finite-core readouts against the earlier worldline formulation.”
- “The archive team is connecting historical SAT calculations to their original notes, figures and conversations.”
- “New papers are being prepared with source tracing and independent review.”
- “The visual atlas is being expanded with newly recovered diagrams and notebook material.”

Avoid as primary public copy:
- worker-instance names;
- round-robin/conga language;
- packet IDs;
- queue/handoff/scheduler details;
- branch/commit plumbing;
- “Nathan asked X to tell Y…” narration;
- backend filenames as headlines.

Those details can remain available in provenance, changelog, source and institutional-history views.

## Page hierarchy

Every technical page should answer, in this order:

1. **What is this?** Short plain-English orientation.
2. **Why is it here?** What question/result/history it helps a reader understand.
3. **What does it show?** The important geometry, calculation, evidence or historical relationship.
4. **How certain/current is it?** Historical / current hypothesis / mathematical result / method / sandboxed / reviewed, as applicable.
5. **Go deeper.** PDF, related figures, calculations, provenance and source records.

Internal workflow is never the hook.

## Visual entry point

The main-menu visual entry should be titled **Visual Atlas** or **Start with pictures** and open a thumbnail-first gallery landing page—not a generic reader/browse route.

Its first screen should expose:
- Geometry & visual models
- Debating A.I. / podcast
- Historical SAT
- Sketchbooks & provenance
- Solver & result figures
- Project history

Every thumbnail opens either a lightbox/detail card or its relevant presented document/episode/history page. It must never strand the reader on a repository file path.
