Meridian Run 104 — exact finite-slab reduction and fold discriminator
Date: 2026-09-25
Status: SANDBOX / exact within the frozen leading quadratic-contact model
Direct authority: WORKSPACES/WORLDTUBE_LAB/FINITE_CORE_TANGENCY_PACKET_002.md
Quarantine: no PRIOR_ART; no quarantined finite-thickness packet imported.

Problem
Run 103 found, in the thin-slab limit, that contact span plus the bulk slab readout is locally identifiable except at a fold near (h/\varepsilon=2/5), but globally two-valued. Determine the finite-(h/\varepsilon) function directly from Packet 002 geometry.

Set [ K=|A_{\rm rel}|>0,\qquad q(s)=Ks^2/2,\qquad r=h/\varepsilon. ]

At fixed (s), the carrier is the normal-fiber ball [ u_1^2+u_2^2+z^2\le \varepsilon^2, ] and the resolving slab imposes [ |z+q(s)|\le h. ]

Hence the exact leading-model four-volume is [ M_4=\int_{\mathbb R}ds \int_{\max(-\varepsilon,-q-h)}^{\min(\varepsilon,-q+h)} \pi(\varepsilon^2-z^2),dz . ]

Exact dimensionless reduction
With [ x=z/\varepsilon,\qquad u=s\sqrt{K/(2\varepsilon)}, ] swap the order of integration. For each (x), the allowed (u^2) interval is [ \max(0,-r-x)\le u^2\le r-x. ]

Therefore [ \boxed{ M_4= 2\pi\sqrt2, \frac{\varepsilon^{7/2}}{\sqrt K},J(r) } ] where [ J(r)= \int_{-1}^{\min(r,1)} (1-x^2) \left[ \sqrt{r-x}-\sqrt{\max(0,-r-x)} \right]dx. ]

This is the requested exact finite-slab function inside the frozen quadratic-contact model.

For (0\le r\le1), [ J(r)=\frac8{105}\Big[ \sqrt{1+r}(-2r^3+r^2+8r+5) +\sqrt{1-r}(-2r^3-r^2+8r-5) \Big]. ]

For (r\ge1), [ J(r)=\frac8{105}\Big[ \sqrt{r-1}(2r^3+r^2-8r+5) +\sqrt{r+1}(-2r^3+r^2+8r+5) \Big]. ]

The apparent large-(r) cancellation in the second form is geometric, so numerical implementations should prefer the integral or a stabilized equivalent at large (r).

Thin-slab regression
Expansion at (r=0) gives [ J(r)=\frac85r+O(r^3). ]

Thus [ M_4= \frac{16\pi\sqrt2}{5} h\varepsilon^{5/2}K^{-1/2} +O(h^3\varepsilon^{1/2}K^{-1/2}), ] recovering Run 103 without fitting.

Fixed-span inverse problem
Packet 002 contact span determines [ R=\varepsilon+h=\varepsilon(1+r). ]

At fixed (R), [ M_4= 2\pi\sqrt2,R^{7/2}K^{-1/2} H(r), \qquad H(r)=\frac{J(r)}{(1+r)^{7/2}}. ]

The inverse problem is therefore one-dimensional after span is measured.

A high-accuracy numerical evaluation of the exact expression gives the stationary point [ \boxed{r_* = h/\varepsilon \approx 0.3686624695} ] or [ \boxed{h/R\approx0.2693596688}. ]

So the thin-slab fold (h/\varepsilon=0.4) survives in the exact leading model but moves downward by about (7.8%) in (h/\varepsilon).

Numerical inspection of the exact branch shows (H(r)) rising from zero, attaining this maximum, and then falling. This strongly supports persistence of the two-valued inverse structure. Uniqueness of the stationary point is not promoted here as an analytic theorem; that is the remaining theorem-shaped check.

Discriminator
For any solver implementing the finite slab directly:

nondimensionalized results at fixed (r) must collapse as [ M_4\sqrt K/\varepsilon^{7/2}=2\pi\sqrt2,J(r); ]
the (r\to0) slope must recover (J(r)/r\to8/5);
at fixed measured span (R), conditioning must degrade near (r_*\approx0.36866247);
inverse recovery from span + (M_4) must not silently choose one branch when two roots exist;
exact finite-slab results should replace the (2/5) thin-slab fold when (h/\varepsilon) is not asymptotically small.
What this does not say
This is an exact integration of the leading local quadratic geometry, not an exact statement about a full worldtube, a physical detector, a particle, or empirical physics. Higher contact order, varying fibers, normal-trivialization corrections, anisotropy, and finite ambient-curvature corrections remain outside this result.

Next cursor
Differentiate the closed form for (H(r)) and establish or reject analytic uniqueness of its positive stationary point. If uniqueness holds, promote the numerical fold observation to a clean finite-slab identifiability theorem and add the dimensionless (J(r)) regression to the solver harness.

# Nathan Public Web Storytelling Standard — v8.1

**Track:** 1B — Public web / narrative nonfiction / human-facing science communication  
**Status:** **APPROVED FOR SITE-DEFAULT USE** by direct Nathan instruction, 21 Sep 2026  
**Authority:** direct Nathan calibration + verified Nathan-authored creative/nonfiction evidence + the 21 Sep 2026 origin-story exemplar, written with AI assistance and explicitly accepted by Nathan as an exceptionally accurate rendering of his voice on this subject  
**Primary scope:** Glass Sausage Factory public site, public-facing essays, section introductions, human-facing project narrative, Substack/newsletter prose, podcast/editorial framing, biographical and methodological storytelling  
**Not the default for:** peer-review manuscripts, formal derivations, claim ledgers, source-preserving archive renderings, or authorship/provenance classification

---

## 1. Governing rule

> **Let Nathan’s brain remain visible in the prose, while the scientific object retains veto power.**

The target is not “Nathan made polished,” and not “AI does a Nathan impression.” It is Nathan’s actual cognitive and comic machinery operating in readable public prose: technically exact when the object demands exactness, tactile when the thing can be touched, funny when the absurdity is already present, self-correcting when the correction carries information, and willing to let a serious argument share a sentence with mud, profanity, a badly behaved word, or a conodont glued down with spit.

The public voice should feel like a person thinking accurately in front of the reader, not like a brand voice performing personality.

---

## 2. Canonical exemplar

The current primary exemplar is the 21 Sep 2026 origin-story piece beginning:

> “Until very recently, I spent more time disavowing being a scientist than calling myself one.”

and ending:

> “Mr. Bapst told me to observe the candle in as much detail as I could. Nobody told me when to stop.”

The published site version at `origin-story.html` is the canonical public reference unless Nathan supersedes it.

### Provenance note

The exemplar is **AI-assisted writing directly calibrated against Nathan’s corrections, published material, voice references, and live instruction, and explicitly accepted by Nathan as an unusually accurate rendering**. It should therefore be used as a style exemplar, not silently reclassified as independently Nathan-authored historical source material.

Do not quote or infer historical/scientific claims from the exemplar when a primary archive source exists. The exemplar is a voice-and-narrative reference.

---

## 3. What makes this register work

### 3.1 Precision and reverence without solemnity

Nathan is not generically self-deprecating. He will claim identities he believes he has earned: writer, editor, educator, photographer. He withholds *scientist*, *geologist*, and especially *physicist* because those nouns carry professional and evidentiary weight for him.

This is a model for the prose generally:

- do not underclaim for false modesty;
- do not overclaim for drama;
- use the exact noun the evidence earns;
- let reverence show through category discipline rather than pomp.

### 3.2 Technical-in-storytelling

Do not stop the story to insert a science sidebar merely because a technical term appears.

Let the narrator perceive through technical categories.

Good public-Nathan prose may see:

- fracture as *conchoidal*;
- a crowd as a braided stream;
- an optical indicatrix as both a technical object and an irresistible word;
- a worldline as something to turn over mentally rather than merely define;
- a bad model as geometry that moved the wrong object.

The technical word should do work: perception, inference, characterization, compression, joke, or plot.

### 3.3 Tactility and viscerality

Anchor abstract thought in hands, surfaces, instruments, preparations, resistance, smell, texture, weight, bodily position, and actual things.

Prefer:

> crush → acid → clean → kiln → heavy liquid → magnetic separation → pick

when the procedure matters, rather than “specimen preparation was conducted.”

Prefer:

> turn the damned thing in my head; look around the side of it

when describing spatial cognition, rather than generic “visualize four-dimensional geometry.”

The abstraction should keep touching the table.

### 3.4 The object gets veto power

A central public-science principle:

> **The object gets veto power.**

This applies to fossils, microscopy, equations, geometry, archives, chronology, provenance, and the theory itself.

The prose may wander widely in route, but becomes strict at contact:

- What changed?
- What stayed fixed?
- Did the frame change?
- Did a premise get smuggled in?
- Did a representation become an ontology?
- Did a source actually say this?
- Did the model see another model’s answer?

A useful compact description is:

> **Undisciplined in route; disciplined in contact.**

### 3.5 Self-correction can remain visible

Nathan often corrects the most recent clause while thinking. In this register, preserve that when the correction itself is informative, funny, or character-revealing.

Examples of the mechanism:

- “It’s this.” → “No, don’t say ‘it’s this.’”
- “same mechanism—no, not the same mechanism; same geometric constraint.”
- a word is used, inspected, found imperfect, and replaced.

Do not simulate hesitation mechanically. Keep the correction only when it advances meaning.

### 3.6 Word-love is conceptual, not ornamental

Nathan likes words because they are compact machines.

*Indicatrix.* *Conchoidal.* *Siphuncle.* *Nektobenthic.*

Coinage, mutation, false-start etymology, register collision, and technical mouthfeel can all appear. The word may become part of the reasoning.

A common pattern:

1. use the word;
2. notice what the word claims;
3. inspect whether that claim is actually right;
4. correct the word or the model;
5. discover something through the correction.

Do not add quirky vocabulary simply to sound like Nathan.

### 3.7 Humor should grow inside the mechanism

The best humor is not pasted onto the science afterward.

It arises from:

- literal pursuit of an implication;
- overprecision applied to an absurd object;
- a technical term colliding with bodily reality;
- a correction that becomes the punchline;
- documentary seriousness surrounding vulgar or tiny content;
- mundane procedure continuing inside an extraordinary premise;
- sudden scale collapse;
- a word discovering a second life halfway through the sentence.

Avoid generic “quirky science writer” jokes.

The joke can be missed without damaging the literal argument.

### 3.8 Profanity is punctuation with a job

Profanity is allowed in public narrative when it marks real emphasis, disbelief, comic compression, frustration, or the point where respectable terminology has become less precise than blunt English.

It should not become wallpaper.

A well-placed “Oh, for fuck’s sake” can do more than three cute jokes.

### 3.9 Periodic compression: “And so”

Branchy stories need gathering points.

Periodic **And so:** passages are not over-explanation. They are navigation.

Use them to compress several pages of causal material into a fresh state vector before the story branches again.

Pattern:

> And so: by twenty-three I had badly remembered String Theory, very well remembered Minkowski, a paleontology degree underway, an emerging obsession with epistemology, and several notebooks full of things I was incorrectly calling strings.

Then continue.

### 3.10 The narrator is part of the causal account

For origin, history, method, biography, and public explanation, do not remove Nathan in the name of objectivity when his route is itself relevant evidence for how the project developed.

The human story is not proof that SAT/H(s)H is correct. It is provenance for how the work happened.

Public prose should distinguish those functions cleanly.

## 3. What makes this register work

- do not underclaim for false modesty;
- do not overclaim for drama;
- use the exact noun the evidence earns;
