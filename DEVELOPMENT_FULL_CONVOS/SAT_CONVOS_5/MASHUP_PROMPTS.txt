REVIEW: Mara Venn — “First Third / Low Close”

The strange accomplishment of Mara Venn’s First Third / Low Close is how quickly nonsense acquires groove. Her phrases arrive in almost equal lengths, pauses included, each bar landing hard on its first syllable before settling into a throbbing consonant-vowel pulse. The effect is hypnotic without being loose: every utterance seems to climb toward a peak roughly a third of the way through, then fall well below its opening register.

Venn’s phonetic palette is the real hook. Familiar vowels give way to sounds that seem imported from some adjacent mouth; shrieks, whistles, grunts and low growls appear less as theatrical interruptions than as extensions of the same rhythmic language. What could easily become shapeless ecstatic vocalizing instead develops an unnervingly consistent internal accent.

There is no chorus, exactly, but the intonation functions like one. You know the fall is coming. It still lands.



REVIEW: “Under Ice, Summer Tape”

Under Ice is barely interested in behaving like an animal recording. Its opening minutes are all negative space: submerged room-tone, pressure, distant movement. Then the voices arrive—alien tonal bursts, startlingly un-mammalian in character, rounded notes suddenly splitting into metallic chirps and electronic-sounding sweeps.

Removed from any visible source, the performance becomes almost synth music. Long tones hover, bend and disappear; shorter calls answer from apparently impossible distances. The recording’s greatest strength is its refusal to organize the environment for us. The microphone hears indiscriminately, catching the desired voices alongside everything the human listener might have filtered away.

That ambiguity gives Under Ice its strange depth. Is the low wash accompaniment? Is the scrape percussion? Is the next rising tone one performer answering another, or merely something entering the hydrophone’s field?

By the end, source identification feels beside the point. The record has taught you to listen to the water itself as the ensemble.
