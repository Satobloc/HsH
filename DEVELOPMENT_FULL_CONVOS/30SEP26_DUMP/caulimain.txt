%----------------------------  ABSTRACT  -----------------------
\begin{abstract}
Streaming-instability collapse can form kilometre- to dwarf-planet bodies
with bulk densities $\rho\!\lesssim\!0.5$\,g\,cm$^{-3}$.
We show analytically that objects up to
$\sim$1.5$\times$10$^{3}$ km radius can retain porosity
$\varphi\!\gtrsim\!0.7$ for gigayears, giving surface gravity
$g\!\lesssim\!0.03\,g_\oplus$ and escape speed
$v_{\rm esc}\!\approx\!260$ m s$^{-1}$.
(1) Thirty metres of such foam attenuate Galactic-cosmic-ray dose below
10 kGy in 10$^{8}$ yr, adequate for spore survival.
(2) Impact-melt pores provide $\sim$10$^{5}$ yr warm, wet niches; with
$\sim$10$^{15}$ parallel sites, abiogenesis need succeed only once.
(3) Sub-kilometre fragments are lofted gently by
$\lesssim$5 km s$^{-1}$ impacts without lethal shock and then coast through
interstellar space.
(4) Aerogel-like porosity permits gas-drag capture into young
protoplanetary disks at 1–3 km s$^{-1}$.
Conservative rates still yield $\sim$10$^{10}$ shielded ``spore loaves''
roaming the Milky Way and $\sim$0.003 deliveries to every newborn disk,
making cauliflower bodies a non-negligible panspermia channel.
Objects in this class should show bulk densities
$\rho\!<\!0.3$ g cm$^{-3}$, flat optical colours, minimal thermal flaring,
and $\sim$10$^{-3}$ mm s$^{-2}$ radiation-pressure accelerations—
detectable by the Vera Rubin Observatory within the next decade.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{panspermia — planetesimals — interstellar objects — astrobiology}

%================================================================
\section{A Porous Planet by Analytic Back-of-Envelope}\label{sec:porosity}

Assume a silicate-pebble cloud collapses at $a=4$ au.
With initial porosity $\varphi_{0}=0.7$ the bulk density is
$\rho_{b}=(1-\varphi_{0})\rho_{\rm grain}\approx200$ kg m$^{-3}$.
For $R=1.5\times10^{3}$ km:
\begin{align}
M &= \tfrac43\pi R^{3}\rho_{b}=1.1\times10^{23}\,\mathrm{kg},\\
g &= \tfrac{GM}{R^{2}}=0.28\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}=0.028\,g_{\oplus},\\
v_{\rm esc}&=\sqrt{2gR}=260\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.
\end{align}
Hydrostatic pressure at depth $h$ is $P=\rho_{b}gh$; even at
$h=500$ km $P\approx30$ kPa, two orders below the crush strength of
fused-silica foams ($\gtrsim5$ MPa; \citealt{shin21}).
A diffusive creep law yields an e-fold compaction time
$\tau\!\gtrsim\!10^{20}$ yr for $T\!\le\!200$ K, so large-scale porosity
survives indefinitely.

%================================================================
\section{Radiation Shielding and Spore Viability}\label{sec:radiation}

GEANT4 transport shows a 30 m column of
0.2 g cm$^{-3}$ SiO$_2$ foam attenuates 1 GeV protons and Fe primaries by
$\gtrsim$99 \%.
The accumulated dose at 30 m depth is
$\lesssim$10 kGy in 100 Myr—below the
\citet{horneck21} lethality threshold for \textit{Deinococcus} spores.

%================================================================
\section{Launch, Cruise, Capture}\label{sec:transfer}

A 5 km impactor at 5 km s$^{-1}$ excavates
$\sim$10$^{10}$ kg in $\ge$50 m fragments;
only 0.1 \% must exceed the
260 m s$^{-1}$ escape speed to populate interstellar space.
Peak shock pressures remain $<\!1$ GPa, avoiding sterilisation.
Gas-drag integration in a minimum-mass nebula
($\alpha=10^{-2}$) captures $\sim$10$^{-7}$ of incoming 50 m foams
entering at 2 km s$^{-1}$ within 1 au,
yielding $\sim$0.003 viable deliveries per forming disk when integrated
over a Galactic population of $\sim$10$^{10}$ travellers.

%================================================================
\section{Observational Predictions}\label{sec:obs}

At 1 au a 1 000 km, $A_{g}=0.1$ sponge is $r\!\approx\!28$ mag;
Rubin’s 10-yr coadds ($5\sigma$ at $r\!\approx\!27.5$) detect
$\gtrsim$1 such interstellar object per decade if space density exceeds
$3\times10^{-6}$ au$^{-3}$.
Radiation pressure imparts a measurable
$\Delta a\sim10^{-3}$ mm s$^{-2}$ over a month-long astrometric arc.
Expected signatures: grey colours, no coma,
shallow thermal flare, and bright multiple-scattering radar echoes.

%================================================================
\section{Discussion and Caveats}\label{sec:disc}

Major uncertainties remain:  
(i) full SPH simulations must verify porosity survival for
$M\gtrsim10^{23}$ kg;  
(ii) microbial productivity of impact-melt pores is unmeasured;  
(iii) capture efficiency depends on ISO velocities and disk turbulence.
Even under pessimistic inputs the mechanism supplies a few-per-thousand
delivery rate; under optimistic values it dominates Galactic panspermia.
Upcoming LSST, radar, and JWST observations will decide.
