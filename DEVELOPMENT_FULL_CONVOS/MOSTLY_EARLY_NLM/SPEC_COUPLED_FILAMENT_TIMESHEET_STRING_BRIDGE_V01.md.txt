# 1. Minimal geometry

Work in a four-coordinate premetric representation

\[
X^A=(x^1,x^2,x^3,q),
\]

where \(q\) is the SAT propagation coordinate which becomes the Minkowski time-normal direction in the flat readout.

Let the resolving timesheet be the graph

\[
\Sigma_\tau:\qquad
F(X,\tau)=q-c_\Sigma \tau-h(\mathbf x,\tau)=0,
\]

with \(\mathbf x=(x^1,x^2,x^3)\).

The flat Minkowski limit is

\[
h=0,\qquad q=c_\Sigma\tau.
\]

A filament/tube center carrier is

\[
\Gamma(s,\tau)=\bigl(\mathbf u(s,\tau),\,q(s,\tau)\bigr),
\]

with long axis approximately parallel to \(q\).  
A finite-core carrier thickens this by a normal fiber \(K_s\subset N_s\Gamma\).

The observed/readout intersection is the operator

\[
\mathcal I_{\Sigma_\tau}[\Gamma]
=
\Gamma(s_\star,\tau),
\qquad
F(\Gamma(s_\star,\tau),\tau)=0.
\]

This is deliberately separate from the ontology of \(\Gamma\): the trace can be helical even when the material carrier is not.

# 2. Hypersuperhelix operator and its relation to a string-mode expansion

A minimal explicit version of one ᚼ nesting step is

\[
\mathcal H_{r,k,\phi,\Pi}[\Gamma](s)
=
\Gamma(s)
+
r\Big[
\cos(ks+\phi)\,N_1(s)
+
\sin(ks+\phi)\,N_2(s)
\Big],
\]

where \(N_1,N_2\) span a selected transported normal plane \(\Pi(s)\).

Recursive hypersuperhelices are

\[
\Gamma_{j+1}=\mathcal H_j[\Gamma_j].
\]

This separates:

- **carrier geometry**: the actual recursively nested path;
- **normal-frame choice**: how each new coil is attached to the previous one;
- **topology/closure**: integer/rational relations among phases and boundary conditions;
- **metric data**: radii, pitches, curvature, torsion.

For small nested amplitudes,

\[
N_a^{(j)}(s)=e_a+O(r_{<j}k_{<j}),
\]

so

\[
\Gamma_N
=
\Gamma_0
+
\sum_j
r_j
\big[
\cos\phi_j\,e_{a_j}
+
\sin\phi_j\,e_{b_j}
\big]
+
O(r_i r_j k_i k_j).
\]

Therefore:

> **Free string-mode superposition is naturally the linearized limit of recursive hypersuperhelix geometry.**

The first nonlinear distinction appears in the moving normal frame. This is the cleanest place to put genuinely H(s)H-specific mode coupling rather than pretending a free Fourier sum and a recursively nested superhelix are exactly the same object.

# 3. Exact "guitar-string → helix trace" theorem

Take an almost straight material filament with circularly polarized transverse vibration

\[
u_x(s,\tau)=A\cos(ks-\omega\tau),
\]

\[
u_y(s,\tau)=A\sin(ks-\omega\tau),
\]

\[
q=s.
\]

A flat moving timesheet \(q=v_\Sigma\tau\) samples

\[
s_\star=v_\Sigma\tau.
\]

Hence its intersection trace is

\[
\gamma(\tau)=
\left(
A\cos\Omega\tau,\,
A\sin\Omega\tau,\,
v_\Sigma\tau
\right),
\]

with

\[
\boxed{\Omega=k v_\Sigma-\omega}.
\]

This is exactly a circular helix when \(\Omega\neq0\).

Its pitch per turn is

\[
\boxed{
P_{\rm trace}
=
\frac{2\pi v_\Sigma}{|\Omega|}
=
\frac{2\pi v_\Sigma}{|kv_\Sigma-\omega|}
}.
\]

So a helical particle worldline/readout does **not** logically require a materially helical filament. It can be the moving intersection of a straight vibrating filament with the timesheet.

Generalization:

\[
u_x+i u_y
=
\sum_j A_j
e^{i(k_j s-\omega_j\tau+\phi_j)}
\]

gives

\[
u_{\rm trace}(\tau)
=
\sum_j A_j
e^{i[(k_j v_\Sigma-\omega_j)\tau+\phi_j]}.
\]

Thus the trace frequencies are

\[
\boxed{\Omega_j=k_jv_\Sigma-\omega_j}.
\]

Commensurate \(\Omega_j\) give closed/repeating trace geometry; irrational ratios give quasiperiodic/nonclosing traces.

# 4. Does slicing commute with ᚼ?

In general,

\[
\mathcal I_\Sigma\circ\mathcal H
\neq
\mathcal H\circ\mathcal I_\Sigma.
\]

But there is an exact minimal commuting case:

1. flat sheet \(q=v_\Sigma\tau\);
2. ᚼ adds only transverse displacement;
3. ᚼ leaves the longitudinal coordinate \(q=s\) unchanged.

Then \(s_\star=v_\Sigma\tau\) is unchanged by the transform, so slicing and transverse ᚼ mode addition commute.

If ᚼ has a longitudinal component \(\delta q\), or the sheet tilts/flexes, the intersection parameter shifts. Linearizing

\[
F(\Gamma+\delta\Gamma,\tau)=0
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
\delta s_\star
=
-
\frac{n\cdot\delta\Gamma}
{n\cdot\partial_s\Gamma}
}
\]

for sheet normal \(n\).

This denominator is exactly the incidence factor. It becomes ill-conditioned near tangency and is replaced by the finite-core quadratic-contact law.

This provides the formal bridge between:
- ᚼ nesting,
- timesheet slicing,
- and the existing finite-core tangency program.

# 5. Minimal angular coupling

Let \(T\) be the local unit filament tangent and \(n\) the unit timesheet normal.

Define

\[
\mu=n\cdot T=\cos\theta,
\]

where \(\theta=0\) is normal incidence / filament tangent parallel to the sheet normal.

The smallest even, sign-independent resistance function with a minimum at normal incidence is

\[
\boxed{f(\theta)=\sin^2\theta=1-(n\cdot T)^2}.
\]

A local angular interaction energy can therefore begin as

\[
U_\theta
=
\frac{K_\theta}{2}\sin^2\theta.
\]

Its orientational restoring torque has magnitude

\[
\boxed{
|\tau_\theta|
=
K_\theta|\sin\theta\cos\theta|
=
\frac{K_\theta}{2}|\sin2\theta|
}.
\]

Important consequence:

> An angle-only interaction on an exactly flat, homogeneous sheet creates alignment torque, but by itself it does not create a long-range translational force.

A gravitational/Newtonian branch therefore needs an actual sheet deformation field or another nonuniform mediator.

# 6. Helix orientation relative to the sheet

For a helix whose local tangent makes internal pitch angle \(\beta\) with its long axis, and whose long axis is tilted by \(\psi\) relative to the sheet normal,

\[
T(\phi)
=
\cos\beta\,a
+
\sin\beta\,e_\perp(\phi).
\]

Averaging around one turn gives

\[
\left\langle(n\cdot T)^2\right\rangle
=
\cos^2\beta\cos^2\psi
+
\frac12\sin^2\beta\sin^2\psi,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{
\bar f(\beta,\psi)
=
1
-
\cos^2\beta\cos^2\psi
-
\frac12\sin^2\beta\sin^2\psi
}.
\]

Special cases:

\[
\bar f(\beta,0)=\sin^2\beta,
\]

\[
\bar f(\beta,\pi/2)
=
1-\frac12\sin^2\beta.
\]

So both **internal coil pitch** and **axis-to-sheet orientation** influence the mean load.

If a Newtonian/gravitational limit is intended, this orientation dependence cannot remain arbitrary: in the low-velocity weak-field limit it must either be absorbed into the particle's fixed inertial/rest state or average away sufficiently to preserve universality of free fall.

# 7. Coil-squash parameter

Use the simple helix

\[
\Gamma(\phi)
=
(R\cos\phi,\,
R\sin\phi,\,
p\phi).
\]

Arc length per radian is

\[
\ell=\sqrt{R^2+p^2}.
\]

If the filament material is approximately inextensible on the squash time scale,

\[
R^2+p^2=\ell^2,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
R\,\delta R+p\,\delta p=0
}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\delta R
=
-\frac{p}{R}\delta p
}.
\]

Axial compression \(\delta p<0\) widens the coil \(\delta R>0\).

Let \(K_{\rm sq}\) be the effective axial pitch stiffness and \(P_c\) the local compressive generalized load from the timesheet. Define

\[
\Pi_{\rm sq}
=
\frac{P_c}{K_{\rm sq}p_0}.
\]

In the small-deformation regime,

\[
\boxed{
S_{\rm coil}
:=
-\frac{\delta p}{p_0}
\approx
\Pi_{\rm sq}\,f(\theta)
}
\]

for the simplest angular loading choice.

This is a **parameterization**, not yet a prediction, because \(K_{\rm sq}\) and the absolute coupling scale have not been derived.

The linear regime requires

\[
\Pi_{\rm sq} f(\theta)\ll1.
\]

Outside it, one must solve the coupled elastic geometry rather than extrapolate the linear squash law.

# 8. Sheet flex and the Newtonian limit

Let the timesheet have scalar normal displacement \(h(\mathbf x,t)\).  
Use the smallest isotropic quadratic energy with tension and bending:

\[
E_\Sigma
=
\frac12
\int d^3x
\left[
\rho_\Sigma \dot h^2
+
T_\Sigma|\nabla h|^2
+
B_\Sigma(\nabla^2h)^2
\right].
\]

Add optional damping \(\eta_\Sigma\dot h\) only as a separate constitutive term.

Let one coil apply integrated normal load \(q_i\) at \(\mathbf x_i\). Then

\[
\rho_\Sigma\ddot h
+
\eta_\Sigma\dot h
-
T_\Sigma\nabla^2 h
+
B_\Sigma\nabla^4h
=
\sum_i q_i\delta^3(\mathbf x-\mathbf x_i).
\]

At long wavelength and in the static limit,

\[
-T_\Sigma\nabla^2h
=
q_i\delta^3(\mathbf x-\mathbf x_i),
\]

so in three spatial dimensions

\[
\boxed{
h_i(r)
=
\frac{q_i}{4\pi T_\Sigma r}
}.
\]

If another coil couples by \(U_j=-q_j h\), then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf F_{ij}
=
-
\frac{q_iq_j}{4\pi T_\Sigma}
\frac{\hat{\mathbf r}}{r^2}
}.
\]

This inverse-square law is not inserted manually; it is the Green function of a tension-dominated scalar displacement field living on a **3-dimensional** sheet.

To reproduce Newtonian gravity in the weak-field limit one needs

\[
q_i=\alpha m_i
\]

for the same proportionality \(\alpha\) for all low-velocity test bodies. Then

\[
\boxed{
G
=
\frac{\alpha^2}{4\pi T_\Sigma}
}.
\]

This is a hard consistency condition, not merely a calibration convenience.

It also explains what must be true for the "heavy shock absorber in syrup" intuition to become gravity-like:

- the heavy coil must source a proportionally larger sheet deformation;
- the test coil must also couple proportionally to its inertial mass;
- then \(F\propto m_{\rm source}m_{\rm test}\), so acceleration is independent of the test mass.

A drag force with a fixed friction coefficient does **not** by itself reproduce this.

# 9. Short-scale regularization / finite-core limit

With bending stiffness,

\[
\ell_b=\sqrt{\frac{B_\Sigma}{T_\Sigma}}
\]

is the sheet bending crossover scale.

The static Green function becomes

\[
\boxed{
h(r)
=
\frac{q}{4\pi T_\Sigma r}
\left(1-e^{-r/\ell_b}\right)
}.
\]

The force magnitude is

\[
\boxed{
F(r)
=
\frac{|q_1q_2|}{4\pi T_\Sigma r^2}
\left[
1-
\left(1+\frac{r}{\ell_b}\right)e^{-r/\ell_b}
\right]
}.
\]

Thus:

- \(r\gg\ell_b\): ordinary \(1/r^2\);
- \(r\sim\ell_b\): crossover;
- \(r\ll\ell_b\): the point-source divergence is softened;
- a finite \(B^3/B^2/S^2\) carrier smears the source further.

The dynamic long-wave sheet speed is

\[
\boxed{
c_\Sigma=\sqrt{\frac{T_\Sigma}{\rho_\Sigma}}
}.
\]

If this field is intended to represent the gravitational/time-sheet disturbance, the minimal calibration is

\[
c_\Sigma=c,
\]

while \(B_\Sigma\) must be small enough that its \(k^4\) dispersion is below observational limits over tested gravitational-wave scales.

A literal viscosity term \(\eta_\Sigma\) would attenuate waves and define a preferred dissipative medium. That is much harder to reconcile with GR/Lorentz constraints. The syrup analogy is therefore safer as an intuition for **response/damping**, while the Newtonian branch comes from **elastic/tension backreaction**.

# 10. Tilted sheet: exact single-trace / multi-trace threshold

Take a static helix

\[
\Gamma(\phi)
=
(R\cos\phi,\,
R\sin\phi,\,
p\phi)
\]

and a tilted moving plane

\[
q-a x=v_\Sigma t,
\qquad
a=\tan\psi.
\]

Intersection requires

\[
p\phi-aR\cos\phi=v_\Sigma t.
\]

The left side is globally monotone if

\[
p>|a|R.
\]

Therefore the moving sheet has exactly one centerline intersection at each time if

\[
\boxed{
|\tan\psi|\frac{R}{p}<1
}.
\]

Since \(R/p=\tan\beta\),

\[
\boxed{
|\tan\psi\,\tan\beta|<1
}
\]

is the exact single-trace condition for this simple helix/plane model.

At

\[
|\tan\psi\,\tan\beta|=1
\]

a fold/tangency appears.

Beyond it, the same sheet can intersect the helix in multiple places.

This is not automatically particle creation; it is a **readout multiplicity transition**.

Near the fold,

\[
\frac{d\phi}{dt}
=
\frac{v_\Sigma}
{p+aR\sin\phi}
\]

becomes large because the intersection point is a kinematic locus rather than a material signal.

For a finite tube/slab, the current finite-core result replaces this singularity by the quadratic contact equation

\[
n\cdot y+\alpha s+\frac12A_{\rm rel}s^2=0
\]

and crossover parameter

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda
=
\frac{|\alpha|}
{\sqrt{|A_{\rm rel}|\rho_{\rm eff}}}
}.
\]

So the exact centerline tilt threshold and the existing finite-core tangency machinery are consistent pieces of the same picture.

# 11. Closed-string conjecture: four distinct assignments

## Assignment A — free-string-like vibration field on an almost straight filament

This is the cleanest version of the "guitar string" model.

Require

\[
\partial_\tau^2 u^i
-
c_f^2\partial_s^2u^i
=
0.
\]

For periodic \(s\sim s+L\),

\[
u^i(s,\tau)
=
u_L^i(s-c_f\tau)
+
u_R^i(s+c_f\tau),
\]

which has the same classical left/right decomposition as a free closed-string transverse field after coordinate rescaling.

**What is corresponding:** mode kinematics / wave equation / periodicity.

**What is not yet corresponding:** Polyakov/Nambu-Goto constraints, target-space dimension, quantization, mass-shell relation, gauge charges.

This is a defensible **structural correspondence**, not yet string theory identity.

---

## Assignment B — timesheet intersection trace is "the string"

A moving sheet samples

\[
\gamma(\tau)
=
u(s_\star(\tau),\tau).
\]

If \(\gamma\) closes, one can reparameterize its period as a circle.

But a one-parameter trace cannot generally reconstruct the two-parameter worldsheet \(u(s,\tau)\).

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{closed trace}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{closed string state}
}
\]

without an additional injectivity/reconstruction assumption.

This is the mathematically weakest literal ST identification.

It can still be useful as an observational projection.

---

## Assignment C — finite tube/support is primary; its sheet section is \(S^1\)

This is the strongest version of "pants, but the spiral on the leg may just be a marking."

For a closed string, a constant-worldsheet-time section is topologically \(S^1\).

A full H(s)H \(B^3\) core intersected by a 3D timesheet generically yields a 3D bulk section (or \(S^2\) boundary section), **not \(S^1\)**.

Therefore an \(S^1\) string-like section requires extra selected structure, for example:

- a rank-two \(B^2\) material support with boundary \(S^1\);
- a distinguished 2D cylindrical sub-surface \(S^1\times I\);
- or an effective boundary mode living on such a sub-support.

This is a powerful discriminator:

> If the closed-string conjecture is literally correct at the intersection level, the H(s)H carrier cannot be only an undifferentiated isotropic \(B^3\) core. It needs a selected string-supporting substructure.

When an \(S^1\) evolves into two \(S^1\) components, the corresponding 2D history has pair-of-pants topology—the standard topological picture of closed-string splitting/joining.

That is a genuine topology match. Dynamics still need to be checked.

---

## Assignment D — material hypersuperhelix itself is the string

Here \(\Gamma_N\) is taken literally as the string embedding.

This requires the strongest conditions:

1. a 2D worldsheet \(X^\mu(\sigma,\tau)\), not only a 1D historical curve;
2. periodic \(\sigma\) for closed strings;
3. Polyakov/Nambu-Goto equations (or a declared effective-string replacement);
4. Virasoro constraints in conformal gauge,
   \[
   (\partial_\tau X\pm\partial_\sigma X)^2=0;
   \]
5. closed-string level matching after quantization;
6. a physically fixed string tension
   \[
   T_s=\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'};
   \]
7. a target metric/signature map from SAT's premetric or induced geometry to the Lorentzian metric used by the string action.

Without these, "looks like a string vibration" is not the same claim as "is a string-theory state."

# 13. Moving timesheet as a chirality-selective sampler

For a free-string-like mode with \(\omega=c_fk\),

co-propagating wave:
\[
u_L\sim e^{i(ks-\omega\tau)}
\]

gives sampled frequency

\[
\Omega_L=k v_\Sigma-\omega,
\]

while the counter-propagating wave

\[
u_R\sim e^{i(ks+\omega\tau)}
\]

gives

\[
\Omega_R=k v_\Sigma+\omega.
\]

If

\[
v_\Sigma=c_f,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\Omega_L=0,\qquad
\Omega_R=2\omega
}.
\]

So a sheet moving at the filament wave speed freezes the co-propagating phase and doubles the sampled frequency of the counter-propagating phase.

This is an exact kinematic property of the sampling map.

It is **not yet** an explanation of physical chirality/helicity, but it is important enough to test because a moving SAT timesheet is not a neutral observer of left/right string modes.

# 15. Highest-value next questions

1. **Mass/load map:** Is the SAT inertial quantity derived from coil geometry proportional to the integrated sheet load \(q\)? If yes, Newtonian universality may follow with one coupling constant.
2. **Carrier dimension:** Is the physically privileged finite carrier \(B^3\), \(S^2\), \(B^2\), or layered? Literal closed-string sections strongly prefer an \(S^1\)-supporting substructure.
3. **Constitutive law:** What fixes \(K_{\rm sq}\), \(T_\Sigma\), \(\rho_\Sigma\), and \(B_\Sigma\) from existing SAT/H(s)H geometry rather than by fit?

5. **Covariance:** Can the sheet equations be written geometrically enough that the preferred resolving foliation is representation/readout rather than an observable Lorentz-violating medium?
6. **String constraints:** Does any current ᚼ-generated two-parameter surface satisfy the Polyakov/Nambu-Goto equations and Virasoro constraints?
7. **Interaction topology:** Can a Three-Spheres / braid / finite-core event generate an \(S^1\to S^1\sqcup S^1\) pair-of-pants history without inserting topology change by hand?
8. **Kerr/ER branch:** Only after the tube/string support is fixed should Kerr/ER be tested as a possible realization; it should not be used to define the geometry retroactively.
