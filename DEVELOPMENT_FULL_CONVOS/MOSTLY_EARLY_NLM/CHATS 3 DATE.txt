To ensure your boneless half pork loin is moist and tender, follow these cooking methods:

### **1. Roasting (Best for Juicy, Tender Meat)**
**Ingredients:**  
- 1 boneless pork loin  
- 1 tbsp olive oil  
- 1 tsp salt  
- 1 tsp black pepper  
- 1 tsp garlic powder  
- 1 tsp dried thyme or rosemary  

**Instructions:**  
1. **Preheat oven** to 375°F (190°C).  
2. **Season the pork loin** with olive oil, salt, pepper, garlic powder, and herbs.  
3. **Sear the meat**: Heat a skillet over medium-high heat and brown the pork loin on all sides for about 2-3 minutes per side.  
4. **Roast**: Place the pork loin on a rack in a roasting pan and bake for **20 minutes per pound** (about 80 minutes for a 4 lb loin).  
5. **Check temperature**: Use a meat thermometer to ensure it reaches **140-145°F (60-63°C)** for a perfectly tender and slightly pink center.  
6. **Rest the meat**: Let it sit for **10-15 minutes** before slicing to retain juices.

---

### **2. Slow Cooking (For Extra Tender Meat)**
**Ingredients:**  
- 1 boneless pork loin  
- 1 cup chicken broth  
- 1 tbsp soy sauce or Worcestershire sauce  
- 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar  
- 1 tsp garlic powder  
- 1 tsp onion powder  

**Instructions:**  
1. **Season the pork loin** with salt, pepper, garlic, and onion powder.  
2. **Sear the meat** in a pan for a few minutes on each side (optional but recommended).  
3. **Slow cook**: Place in a slow cooker with broth, soy sauce, and apple cider vinegar. Cook on **LOW for 6-8 hours** or HIGH for 3-4 hours.  
4. **Shred or slice** and serve with the juices.

---

### **3. Sous Vide (For Precision Cooking)**
- **Set sous vide to 140°F (60°C)** and cook for **3-4 hours**.  
- **Sear in a hot pan** for a crispy crust before serving.

Can you define “non-monotonic reasoning”

Non-monotonic reasoning is a type of logical reasoning where adding new information can invalidate previous conclusions. This contrasts with *monotonic reasoning*, where once something is proven true, it remains true even if more information is added.  

In non-monotonic reasoning, conclusions are often based on incomplete or defeasible knowledge, meaning they may need to be revised as new evidence emerges. This is common in real-world decision-making, artificial intelligence, and expert systems, where assumptions might change based on new data.  

Examples of non-monotonic reasoning include:  
- **Default reasoning**: Assuming a bird can fly unless given information that it is a penguin.  
- **Abductive reasoning**: Inferring the most likely explanation for an event but revising it if contradictory evidence arises.  
- **Belief revision**: Updating knowledge when encountering new facts that contradict previous beliefs.

Movie ratings began in the United States with the **Motion Picture Production Code (Hays Code)** in 1930, which set moral guidelines for films but did not use a rating system.  

The modern **movie rating system** was introduced by the **Motion Picture Association of America (MPAA)** on **November 1, 1968**. The original ratings were:  
- **G** (General Audiences)  
- **M** (Mature Audiences, later replaced by PG in 1972)  
- **R** (Restricted)  
- **X** (Adults Only, later replaced by NC-17 in 1990)  

This system evolved into the current **MPA (formerly MPAA) ratings**, which include **G, PG, PG-13, R, and NC-17**.
