9. Lorentz Consistency

SAT/Blockwave introduces a dynamical, unit-timelike vector field \( u^\mu \) — the local “flow” of the medium — which might appear to define a preferred frame. This raises a natural question: does the theory violate local Lorentz invariance?

The answer is no. SAT can be implemented in ways that fully respect Lorentz symmetry in physical observables. Because \( u^\mu \) selects a direction in spacetime, it could seem to pick out a preferred frame. But SAT is designed so that:
- Lorentz invariance is restored in vacuum,
- Matter fields couple only to \( g_{\mu\nu} \), not to \( u^\mu \),
- Any explicit Lorentz-violating (LV) effects are either gauge artifacts, environmentally screened, or suppressed by a high scale \( M_* \).

The flow vector \( u^\mu \) does not break Lorentz symmetry in observables — it defines a dynamical internal frame that can be interpreted in multiple consistent ways.

9.2 Route 1: Gauge–Khronon (Preferred Frame as Gauge)

One safe realization treats \( u^\mu \) as the normalized gradient of a scalar field T(x):
\[

**Outcome:**
- In vacuum, all timelike directions are physically equivalent.
- Local Lorentz symmetry is preserved in all observables.
- Gravitational wave speed \( c_T = 1 \) by construction.
- PPN parameters \( \alpha_1 = \alpha_2 = 0 \).

This is the most conservative route: no preferred frame exists at all — only a gauge redundancy.

9.3 Route 2: Screened Alignment (Spontaneous but Safe)

An alternative is to keep \( u^\mu \) as a physical field but align it dynamically with the rest frame of θ. This can be achieved with an alignment term:
\[

**Outcome:**

- Apparent Lorentz violation is environmental and screened.
- All matter fields still couple only to \( g_{\mu\nu} \).

This route maintains physical flow without violating Lorentz constraints.

A third option is to treat \( u^\mu \) as an effective field arising from coarse-graining microscopic degrees of freedom:
\[
u^\mu \propto \langle T^{\mu\nu} \rangle\, \xi_\nu

Here, \( u^\mu \) is defined only in a hydrodynamic sense — the local direction of energy transport, averaged over microscopic dynamics.

**Outcome:**
- At short scales, there is no flow — Lorentz invariance is exact.
- The effective flow emerges only in strained or thermally excited regions.
- Apparent LV is entirely a macroscopic phenomenon, like viscosity.

This approach links naturally to SAT’s lattice UV completion and supports Lorentz symmetry microscopically.

Regardless of how \( u^\mu \) is implemented, all matter fields in SAT couple minimally to the metric:

SAT can satisfy all known Lorentz-related bounds by choosing safe values for elastic coefficients and mixings:
