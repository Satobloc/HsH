So You Want to Fall In? Black Holes Unleashed

Imagine a black hole: a cosmic whirlpool whose size is fixed by its mass.  For a non-spinning (Schwarzschild) black hole, the radius of the event horizon is  R_s=2GM/c^2.  Plugging in numbers shows how tiny or huge these objects are: e.g. a one-solar-mass black hole has R_s\approx3 km, while an Earth-mass black hole is only ~9 mm across ￼.  In other words, squeezing ordinary matter below its Schwarzschild radius instantly forms a black hole ￼.  Once formed, its horizon area A=4\pi R_s^2 obeys a striking rule: Hawking’s 1971 theorem says the total horizon area can never decrease ￼.  In fact, recent LIGO/Virgo detections of merging black holes confirm this beautifully: the final black hole’s area is larger than the sum of the originals ￼.  This is the second law of black hole mechanics – an echo of thermodynamics – telling us classical general relativity treats black holes like entropy-filled objects.

These thermodynamic hints go further.  In the 1970s Bekenstein and Hawking showed a black hole’s entropy is S_{\rm BH}=k_B A/(4\hbar G), i.e. proportional to its horizon area ￼.  Hawking famously discovered that black holes radiate like blackbodies with temperature T_H=\hbar c^3/(8\pi G M k_B) ￼.  A black hole thus slowly loses mass by emitting thermal radiation – evaporating over enormous timescales.  In this view, a black hole is anything but static: it behaves like a hot object (a “black hole thermodynamic object” ￼), with heat and entropy.  In fact, the LIGO results underscore this paradigm shift: GR alone already “knows something about the quantum nature of these objects,” and the information content of a black hole is tied to its area ￼.

On the quantum side, early work by Bekenstein and Mukhanov suggested the black hole horizon area might be quantized in Planck-sized chunks.  In such a picture, A= n \cdot 4\ln(2)\,\ell_P^2 (for integer n), giving discrete “energy levels” for the hole’s area ￼.  Intriguingly, gravitational-wave data might one day probe this quantization via tiny spectral echoes ￼.  Another surprise from modern theory is that black holes can carry elusive “hair.”  Classically, John Wheeler quipped a black hole “remembers” only its total mass and spin – “no hair” ￼ – so everything thrown in (even a television set or a book) is forgotten except for those conserved quantities ￼.  But recent work by Hawking, Perry & Strominger shows that asymptotic symmetries give black holes an infinite number of zero-energy “soft hair” modes ￼.  These soft gravitons/photons live on the horizon and can, in principle, store quantum information.  In fact, one finds an effective number of horizon degrees of freedom ∝ area in Planck units ￼, suggesting a concrete mechanism for how information might be encoded by the black hole surface.

Experiment and theory together point to a rich picture: black holes are unique thermodynamic machines with quantized characteristics.  LIGO’s ringdown “songs” confirmed not only area increase but also the Kerr solution for spinning holes ￼: two black holes with the same mass and spin are indistinguishable, a stunning mathematical uniqueness ￼.  And while classical GR predicts a singularity inside, quantum insights (soft hair, holography) hint at microstructure.

From the SAT/Blockwave perspective, we can re-imagine black holes through the lens of our master action.  Instead of a naked singularity, the black hole interior may be a knot of the SAT fields: the two-form filament current J^{\mu\nu} could form a web of loops threaded through the horizon, giving a concrete realization of “hair.”  The compact phase \theta might oscillate around the hole, encoding microscopic states, while the elastic time-flow field u^\mu captures how time itself stretches and compresses near the hole.  In effect, the black hole becomes a resonant loop or “block” in spacetime’s fabric – a place where gravity, quantum loops and topology all meet.  (This resonates with ideas like Wheeler’s “geons” or stringy fuzzballs, but emerges here from SAT’s basic fields.)  Thus, instead of a featureless hole, SAT suggests a black hole is a dynamical structure built from loops and springs: its entropy and information lie in the ways these internal components (loops, holonomies, clocks) can arrange themselves at the event horizon.  These speculations hint at truly novel connections: perhaps Planck-scale elasticity or loop networks will one day explain the black hole interior.  For now we can say that black holes are no longer the ultimate unknown – they’re just the next great playground for our fundamental intuitions.

Key points:
	•	A black hole of mass M has Schwarzschild radius R_s=2GM/c^2 (e.g. Earth-mass → 9 mm, Sun-mass → 3 km) ￼.  Compressing matter below R_s yields a horizon ￼.
	•	Hawking’s area theorem (second law) says horizon area never decreases ￼; LIGO’s BH mergers confirm this increase.  Black holes obey laws analogous to thermodynamics ￼.
	•	Bekenstein–Hawking entropy S\propto A ￼ and Hawking radiation (T_H=\hbar c^3/8\pi GMk_B) ￼ mean black holes are hot, radiating objects with entropy.
	•	Quantum ideas propose horizon quantization: area comes in Planck-sized units ￼, possibly visible in gravitational-wave echoes.
	•	The “no-hair” theorem (Wheeler) implies BHs classically forget all but mass/spin ￼, but soft hair allows a huge amount of quantum information to be stored on the horizon ￼ ￼.
	•	LIGO ringdown data also confirm Kerr’s 1963 solution for rotating black holes (same mass+spin = same hole) ￼.
	•	In SAT/Blockwave terms, a black hole is a tangle of our fundamental fields: loops (J), phase (\theta), and elastic flows (u^\mu) weaving the event horizon.  This gives a concrete picture of black hole microstates in the language of SAT’s intuitions – hinting at new physics beyond the vanilla GR picture.

Each of these points is grounded in modern research ￼ ￼ ￼ ￼ ￼, yet we see how SAT’s vision could bring fresh insight into these exotic objects.

\section*{I. Standard GR Thermodynamics (facts we stand on)}

We work in units $G=\hbar=c=k_B=1$ unless noted. For a non-rotating, uncharged (Schwarzschild) black hole:
\begin{align}
r_h &= 2M,\\
A &= 4\pi r_h^2 = 16\pi M^2,\\
\kappa &= \frac{1}{4M}, \qquad T_H = \frac{\kappa}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{8\pi M},\\
S_{\rm BH} &= \frac{A}{4} = 4\pi M^2.
\end{align}
The first law reads
\begin{equation}
dM = \frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\, dA \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad dS_{\rm BH} = \frac{dA}{4}.
\label{eq:firstlaw}
\end{equation}

\section*{II. SAT/Blockwave Flux Sector: Structure and Quantization}

\subsection*{II.1 Fields and topological coupling}
In the SAT/Blockwave kernel, the conserved \emph{3-form} flux current $J_{\mu\nu\rho}$ (a closed form, $\nabla_{[\mu}J_{\nu\rho\sigma]}=0$) couples to a compact scalar $\theta$ via the topological term (Lorentzian signature)
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm top} \;=\; \frac{1}{2\pi}\,\theta\, \nabla_\mu \tilde J^\mu,
\qquad
\tilde J^\mu \equiv \frac{1}{3!}\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}J_{\nu\rho\sigma}.
\label{eq:Ltop}
\end{equation}
Gauge invariance under $\theta \to \theta + 2\pi$ implies the \emph{quantization} of the conserved charge on any spacelike 3-surface $\Sigma_3$:
\begin{equation}
Q(\Sigma_3)\;=\;\int_{\Sigma_3}\!\! d\Sigma_\mu\, \tilde J^\mu
\;=\;\frac{1}{3!}\int_{\Sigma_3}\!\! \varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} J_{\nu\rho\sigma}\, d\Sigma_\mu
\;=\; n \in \mathbb{Z}.
\label{eq:Qn}
\end{equation}
We refer to $n$ as the \emph{flux integer}. On a stationary black hole spacetime, a natural choice for $\Sigma_3$ is a spatial slice whose boundary is the 2-sphere cross-section of the horizon.

\subsection*{II.2 Energy content of flux}
The dynamical part of the $J$-sector we take as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{J} \;=\; \frac{g_h}{12}\,F(\theta)\, J_{\mu\nu\rho}J^{\mu\nu\rho},
\label{eq:LJ}
\end{equation}
with $F(\theta)>0$ near the background $\bar\theta$ (so the quadratic form is positive). For a \emph{homogeneous} background flux threading the region inside the horizon worldtube, write
\begin{equation}
\overline{J}_{\mu\nu\rho} \;=\; \mathcal{J}(r)\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho t}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\overline{J}^2 \equiv \overline{J}_{\mu\nu\rho}\overline{J}^{\mu\nu\rho} = -6\,\mathcal{J}(r)^2,
\end{equation}
so that the $J$-sector contributes an \emph{effective} energy density
\begin{equation}
\rho_J \;=\; -\mathcal{L}_{J}\big|_{\bar\theta,\overline{J}}
\;=\; -\frac{g_h}{12}\,F(\bar\theta)\,\overline{J}^2
\;=\; \frac{g_h}{2}\,F(\bar\theta)\,\mathcal{J}(r)^2 \;\;\ge 0.
\end{equation}
(Here we used the Lorentzian sign; the sign choice makes $\rho_J\!\ge 0$.)

\subsection*{II.3 Relating the integer $n$ to a surface density on the horizon}
For stationary configurations, Gauss’ law applied to \eqref{eq:Qn} localizes $n$ onto the horizon 2-sphere $\mathcal{H}$ as a \emph{surface density} $\sigma_J$:
\begin{equation}
n \;=\; \int_{\Sigma_3}\!\nabla_\mu \tilde K^\mu \, d^3x
\;=\; \oint_{\partial\Sigma_3=\mathcal{H}} \tilde K^\mu\, d\Sigma_\mu,
\qquad
\tilde K^\mu \sim \tilde J^\mu \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; n \;=\; \int_{\mathcal{H}}\!\sigma_J\, dA,
\label{eq:surfden}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_J$ is the \emph{horizon density} (per unit area) associated with the pullback of $\tilde J^\mu$ to $\mathcal{H}$. In words: the integer $n$ counts horizon ``pixels'' weighted by $\sigma_J$.

\section*{III. From One More Flux Quantum to One More Area Pixel}

\subsection*{III.1 First law as the bridge}
Add \emph{one} flux unit at fixed angular momentum and charge (Schwarzschild sector): $n\to n+1$. The black-hole first law \eqref{eq:firstlaw} enforces
\begin{equation}
\Delta M \;=\; \frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\,\Delta A.
\label{eq:bridge}
\end{equation}
So if we compute $\Delta M$ for adding a flux quantum, the corresponding \emph{area increment} is fixed:
\begin{equation}
\Delta A \;=\; \frac{8\pi}{\kappa}\,\Delta M.
\label{eq:DeltaAfromDeltaM}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{III.2 Energy (mass) of one flux quantum}
Let $\mathcal{E}_1$ denote the energy cost of adding a single, minimally localized horizon flux unit. There are two equivalent ways to parametrize it:

\paragraph{(A) Local energy density picture.}
Treat the new unit as a horizon \emph{patch} of proper area $a_\star$ with surface energy density $\varepsilon_\star$ arising from \eqref{eq:LJ}. Then
\begin{equation}
\Delta M \;=\; \mathcal{E}_1 \;\equiv\; \varepsilon_\star\, a_\star,
\qquad
\text{with}\quad \varepsilon_\star \sim \frac{g_h}{2}\,F(\bar\theta)\,\mathcal{J}_\star^2.
\label{eq:localE}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{(B) Canonical quantum (ladder) picture.}
From Chs.\ 8–10, small fluctuations in the $(\theta,J)$ sector diagonalize into normal modes with frequencies $\omega_\pm(n)$. A \emph{minimally excited} horizon-bound mode carrying one unit of the conserved flux contributes
\begin{equation}
\Delta M \;=\; \mathcal{E}_1 \;\equiv\; \omega_\star(n),
\label{eq:modeE}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_\star$ is the lowest \emph{horizon-tied} flux mode (its precise value depends on $g_hF(\bar\theta)$ and geometry but not on the bookkeeping convention).

Both (A) and (B) define the same physical $\Delta M$ (they are two descriptions of the same excitation).

\subsection*{III.3 Area rung from a single quantum}
Insert either \eqref{eq:localE} or \eqref{eq:modeE} into \eqref{eq:DeltaAfromDeltaM}:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\Delta A \;=\; \frac{8\pi}{\kappa}\,\mathcal{E}_1\;}.
\label{eq:masterRung}
\end{equation}
For Schwarzschild, $\kappa=1/(4M)$, hence
\begin{equation}
\Delta A \;=\; 32\pi M\,\mathcal{E}_1.
\end{equation}
Two \emph{natural} normalizations are common in the literature; we show both and how SAT/Blockwave hits them by a choice of $\mathcal{E}_1$:

\paragraph{(i) Bekenstein minimal increase.}
Bekenstein’s gedanken experiments suggest a universal minimal area jump
\begin{equation}
\Delta A_{\rm Bek} \;=\; \alpha_{\rm Bek}\,\ell_P^2,\qquad \alpha_{\rm Bek}\sim \mathcal{O}(8\pi).
\end{equation}
Matching \eqref{eq:masterRung} to $\Delta A_{\rm Bek}$ fixes the single-quantum energy to
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}_1 \;=\; \frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\,\Delta A_{\rm Bek}
\;=\; \frac{\alpha_{\rm Bek}}{8\pi}\,\kappa\,\ell_P^2
\;=\; \frac{\alpha_{\rm Bek}}{32\pi M}\,\ell_P^2.
\label{eq:E1bek}
\end{equation}
Thus a \emph{scale-free} choice of the microscopic parameters in \eqref{eq:LJ} (or the horizon-tied normal mode frequency in \eqref{eq:modeE}) reproduces Bekenstein’s rung.

\paragraph{(ii) Mukhanov--Bekenstein (log-degeneracy) rung.}
If each area patch carries a fixed state degeneracy $k$ (so $\Delta S=\ln k$ per rung) and $S=A/4$, then
\begin{equation}
\Delta S \;=\; \frac{\Delta A}{4} \;=\; \ln k
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Delta A \;=\; 4\,\ln k\, \ell_P^2.
\end{equation}
Matching to \eqref{eq:masterRung} gives
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}_1 \;=\; \frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\,4\ln k\,\ell_P^2
\;=\; \frac{\ln k}{4\pi}\,\kappa\,\ell_P^2
\;=\; \frac{\ln k}{16\pi M}\,\ell_P^2.
\label{eq:E1MB}
\end{equation}
Again, a choice of SAT/Blockwave horizon mode $\omega_\star$ (or surface density $\varepsilon_\star$) realizes this normalization.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Conclusion of III:} Once the single-quantum energy $\mathcal{E}_1$ is fixed (by microscopic SAT parameters or by a phenomenological choice consistent with Bekenstein/Mukhanov), the first law enforces a \emph{uniform area rung} $\Delta A$. Counting $n$ quanta yields
\begin{equation}
A \;=\; A_0 \;+\; n\,\Delta A,
\qquad
S \;=\; \frac{A}{4} \;=\; \frac{A_0}{4} + n\,\frac{\Delta A}{4}.
\end{equation}
Choosing $A_0$ to match the ground configuration sets the origin of $n$.

\section*{IV. Fingerprints and Cross-Checks}

A single rung change alters the mass by $\Delta M=\mathcal{E}_1$ and the entropy by $\Delta S=\Delta A/4$. Thermodynamic consistency demands

which is exactly \eqref{eq:masterRung}. Thus the rung prescription is \emph{thermodynamically exact}.

Once $(g_h,F,\mathcal{J}_\star,a_\star)$ are fixed by the horizon microphysics (SAT), $\omega_\star$ is determined and so is $\Delta A$.

\section*{Step 0 — The Usual Way (sloppy but not wrong)}

Suppose you want the mass of a black hole. Easy, right? Start with Schwarzschild:
\[
ds^2 = -\Big(1-\tfrac{2M}{r}\Big)dt^2 
+ \Big(1-\tfrac{2M}{r}\Big)^{-1}dr^2 + r^2 d\Omega^2.

From the horizon condition $r_h=2M$, the area is
\[
A = 4\pi r_h^2 = 16\pi M^2,
\]
so $M=\sqrt{A/16\pi}$. You could differentiate, plug into $dM=\tfrac{\kappa}{8\pi}dA$, throw in $T_H=\hbar c^3/(8\pi GMk_B)$, and then \ldots

Throughout the main text we wrote schematically
\[
Q(\Sigma) = \int_\Sigma J.

\[
Q(\Sigma) = \int_\Sigma J_{\mu\nu\rho}\,d\Sigma^{\mu\nu\rho},

with $d\Sigma^{\mu\nu\rho}$ the oriented volume element on the hypersurface $\Sigma$. We use the shorthand $Q(\Sigma)=\int J$ elsewhere, but it should be understood as the full component expression.

\subsection*{2. Prefactor $\alpha$ (Sec. 3)}
In the mass-loss equation
\[
\dot M \sim -\frac{\alpha}{M^2},
\]
the constant $\alpha>0$ absorbs all dimensionful prefactors. Explicitly, $\alpha$ collects:
\begin{itemize}
\item the Stefan–Boltzmann constant,
\item frequency-dependent graybody transmission factors,
\item the number of particle species contributing at the black hole temperature.
\end{itemize}
Numerical values of $\alpha$ differ by order-one factors depending on these inputs (Page 1976).

\section*{Math spine: “So You Want to Watch One Evaporate?”}

\subsection*{1. Hawking temperature from surface gravity}
For Schwarzschild,
\[
ds^2=-\Big(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Big)dt^2+\Big(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Big)^{-1}dr^2+r^2 d\Omega^2,
\quad r_h=2M.
\]
Surface gravity \(\kappa\) is
\[
\kappa=\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_r g_{tt}\Big|_{r=r_h}=\frac{1}{4M}.
\]
Hawking’s relation \(T_H=\kappa/(2\pi)\) gives
\[
T_H=\frac{1}{8\pi M}.
\]
% (Hawking 1974, 1975)

\subsection*{2. Radiated power scaling}
Blackbody estimate (ignoring graybody factors): \(P\sim A\,\sigma T_H^4\) with area \(A=4\pi r_h^2=16\pi M^2\).
Thus
\[
P \sim 16\pi M^2 \times \sigma \Big(\frac{1}{8\pi M}\Big)^4
= \frac{\sigma}{(8\pi)^4}\,\frac{16\pi}{M^2}
\;\propto\; \frac{1}{M^2}.
\]
Restoring the frequency-dependent transmission (graybody) factors just renormalizes the prefactor \(\alpha\) below (Page 1976).

\subsection*{3. Mass-loss ODE and solution}
Energy conservation: \(\dot M=-P\Rightarrow \dot M\sim -\alpha/M^2\) for some constant \(\alpha>0\).
Integrate:
\[
\int_{M_0}^{M(t)} M^2\,dM = -\alpha\!\int_0^t dt' 
\;\Rightarrow\;
\frac{M(t)^3-M_0^3}{3}=-\alpha t,
\quad
M(t)=\big(M_0^3-3\alpha t\big)^{1/3}.
\]

\subsection*{4. Area quantization and rung size}
Assume uniformly spaced horizon area eigenvalues
\[
A=n\,\Delta A,\qquad n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}.
\]
First law \(dM=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\,dA\) implies a discrete step “energy”
\[
\Delta M \equiv \mathcal E_1=\frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\,\Delta A.
\]
For Schwarzschild \(\kappa=1/(4M)\):
\[
\boxed{\;\mathcal E_1(M)=\frac{\Delta A}{32\pi\,M}\;}
\]
Two common choices:
\[
\Delta A_{\text{Bek}}=8\pi \ell_P^2
\;\Rightarrow\;
\mathcal E_1=\frac{\ell_P^2}{4M},
\qquad
\Delta A_{\text{Hod/Muk}}=4\ln 3\,\ell_P^2
\;\Rightarrow\;
\mathcal E_1=\frac{\ln 3}{8\pi}\frac{\ell_P^2}{M}.
\]

\subsection*{5. Flux difference equation (SAT ladder)}
In SAT, area rungs come from flux integers \(n\rightarrow n-1\). Identifying one rung emission with one quantum of energy \(\mathcal E_1\) yields
\[
M_{n}-M_{n-1}=\mathcal E_1(M_{n})=\frac{\Delta A}{32\pi\,M_n}.
\]
\emph{Dimensions check (SI):} with \(\Delta A\) in m\(^2\), \(\kappa=c^4/(4GM)\), 
\(
\mathcal E_1=\kappa\,\Delta A/(8\pi)
= \frac{c^4}{4GM}\frac{\Delta A}{8\pi}
\) has units J, as required.

\subsection*{6. Continuum limit (ladder \(\to\) ODE)}
Write \(A=16\pi M^2=n\Delta A\Rightarrow n=\frac{16\pi M^2}{\Delta A}\).
A step \(n\to n-1\) changes mass by \(\Delta M=\mathcal E_1(M)\).
With step time \(\tau_{\rm step}\), the finite difference
\[
\frac{M_{n}-M_{n-1}}{\tau_{\rm step}}
\;\to\;
\frac{dM}{dt}
\quad\text{as}\quad \Delta A\to 0,
\]
and using \(\mathcal E_1\propto 1/M\) one recovers \(\dot M\propto -1/M^2\) after integrating over the blackbody spectrum (the prefactor matches the Hawking/Page \(\alpha\) once graybody factors are included). Thus Hawking’s smooth fade is the \(\Delta A\!\to\!0\) limit of the SAT staircase.

\subsection*{7. Entropy step and emission probability}
Bekenstein–Hawking entropy \(S=A/4\) gives a per-rung entropy change
\[
\boxed{\;\Delta S=\frac{\Delta A}{4}\;}
\]
so the Boltzmann-like weight for a single step scales as
\[
\Gamma_{n\to n-1}\;\propto\; e^{-\Delta S}=e^{-\Delta A/4}.
\]
For \(\Delta A=4\ln k\,\ell_P^2\), one finds \(\Delta S=\ln k\) and evenly spaced line intensities by a factor \(k^{-1}\) per rung (Bekenstein–Mukhanov 1995).

\subsection*{8. Line (comb) spectrum and thermal limit}
A single rung drop releases energy \(\Delta E=\mathcal E_1\). The fundamental angular frequency spacing is
\[
\Delta\omega=\frac{\Delta E}{\hbar}.
\]
In SI, with \(\Delta A=8\pi\ell_P^2\), one gets
\[
\Delta E=\frac{\hbar c}{4M},
\quad
\Delta f=\frac{\Delta E}{h}=\frac{c}{8\pi M}.
\]
With \(\Delta A=4\ln 3\,\ell_P^2\),
\(
\Delta f=\frac{c\ln 3}{16\pi^2 M}.
\)
As \(M\) grows, \(\Delta f\to 0\) and closely spaced lines merge into a thermal continuum; as \(M\) shrinks late in evaporation, spacing widens and discreteness can imprint a comb-like modulation.

\subsection*{9. Numbers (order-of-magnitude, SI)}
Planck area \(\ell_P^2\simeq 2.612\times 10^{-70}{\rm~m}^2\).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Solar mass} \(M_\odot=1.9885\times10^{30}{\rm~kg}\).
\[
\Delta A_{\text{Bek}}=8\pi\ell_P^2\simeq 6.57\times10^{-69}{\rm~m}^2,
\quad
\Delta f_{\text{Bek}}=\frac{c}{8\pi M_\odot}\approx 6.0\times 10^{-24}{\rm~Hz}.
\]
Hod/Mukhanov spacing:
\(\Delta f \approx \dfrac{c\ln 3}{16\pi^2 M_\odot}\approx 1.05\times10^{-24}{\rm~Hz}\).
(Effectively unobservable: periods \(\gtrsim 10^{16}\) years.)

\item \textbf{Primordial BH} \(M=10^{12}{\rm~kg}\).
\[
\Delta f_{\text{Bek}}\approx \frac{c}{8\pi M}\approx 1.19\times10^{-5}{\rm~Hz}\quad(\text{period }\sim\! 1~\text{day}),
\]
\[
\Delta f_{\text{Hod/Muk}}\approx 2.09\times10^{-6}{\rm~Hz}.
\]
Still tiny, but late-time steps for even smaller \(M\) move into Hz–kHz as \(M\to 10^{7}\!-\!10^{4}{\rm~kg}\):
\[
\Delta f_{\text{Bek}}\approx
\begin{cases}
\;1.19~{\rm Hz} & (M=10^{7}{\rm~kg}),\\
\;1.19~{\rm kHz} & (M=10^{4}{\rm~kg}).
\end{cases}
\]
Such BHs are ultra-short-lived; this scaling just shows the trend.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Appendix F: Technical Notes for Black Hole Chapter}

\subsection*{F.1 Flux Integral Notation}
In the main text we wrote schematically $Q(\Sigma)=\int_\Sigma J$.  
Since $J$ is a conserved 3-form, the explicit component form is
\[
Q(\Sigma) = \int_\Sigma J_{\mu\nu\rho}\,d\Sigma^{\mu\nu\rho},
\]
where $d\Sigma^{\mu\nu\rho}$ is the oriented volume element on the hypersurface $\Sigma$.
This clarifies how flux integers $n$ arise in the area quantization $A=n\Delta A$.

\subsection*{F.2 Mass Loss Prefactor $\alpha$}
The evaporation law $\dot M=-\alpha/M^2$ hides a collection of constants in $\alpha$:
\begin{itemize}
\item Stefan–Boltzmann constant,  
\item transmission probabilities (greybody factors),  
\item number of particle species at temperature $T_H$.  
\end{itemize}
Page (1976) provides explicit values for $\alpha$ by summing over species and multipoles. In natural units, $\alpha\sim 10^{-3}$ for the Standard Model spectrum.

\subsection*{F.3 Units}
Unless noted, we adopt natural units
\[
G=\hbar=c=k_B=1.
\]
In these units $M$ has dimension of length, $A$ has dimension of length$^2$, and entropy is dimensionless. For numerical estimates in SI we use
\[
\ell_P = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^3}} \approx 1.616\times 10^{-35}\,\mathrm{m}.
\]

\subsection*{F.4 Tunneling Derivation of $\Gamma=e^{-\Delta S}$}
The emission rate can be computed by WKB tunneling across the horizon (Parikh–Wilczek 2000). In Painlevé coordinates,
\[
\Gamma \sim e^{-2\,\mathrm{Im}S},\qquad
\mathrm{Im}S = \int_0^\omega \frac{2\pi}{\kappa(M-\omega')}\,d\omega'.
\]
Evaluating for Schwarzschild $\kappa=1/(4M)$ gives
\[
\Gamma = \exp\!\Big[-8\pi\omega\Big(M-\tfrac{\omega}{2}\Big)\Big]
= \exp\!\big(S(M-\omega)-S(M)\big),
\]
with $S(M)=4\pi M^2$. Thus
\[
\Gamma = e^{-\Delta S_{\rm BH}},
\]
showing that the step probability in the discrete SAT ladder is the back-reaction corrected Hawking emission probability.

\subsection*{F.5 Comb Spectrum vs Continuum}
Each rung emits a quantum of energy
\[
\Delta M = \frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\Delta A.
\]
The spectrum is a comb:
\[
\omega_m = \frac{m\,\Delta M}{\hbar},\quad m\in\mathbb Z.
\]
For $M\gg M_P$, $\Delta M$ is tiny and the comb merges into the Hawking continuum. For $M\sim 10^{12}\,\mathrm{kg}$ primordial black holes, the line spacing can reach observable $\mathrm{GHz}$ frequencies.

\subsection*{F.6 Entropy Step and Probability}
With area quantization $A=n\Delta A$, the entropy change per emission is
\[
\Delta S = \frac{\Delta A}{4}.
\]
The probability is thus
\[
\Gamma_{n\to n-1} \propto e^{-\Delta A/4}.
\]
This matches Bekenstein–Mukhanov (1995) and follows rigorously from the tunneling derivation.
