\begin{abstract}
We introduce the SATO/Blockwave SAT framework, a four-dimensional tensorial and discrete twist integration theory expressed within the Lean~4 formal environment. \section{Introduction}

The SATO/Blockwave Self-Adjusting Tensor (SAT) framework proposes a field-theoretic structure that bridges continuous tensor dynamics and discrete topological information. It extends conventional 4D field models by integrating a \emph{twist charge} $\tau$ sector, encoding winding, linking, and writhe numbers. These invariants enter both the mass spectrum and the topological action, resulting in a discretely modulated continuous Lagrangian.

\section{Lagrangian Formulation}

The total Lagrangian density is defined as
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} &=
\underbrace{(\theta_\mu)^2}_{\text{scalar potential}}
+ \underbrace{(u_\mu)^2}_{\text{vector kinetic term}}
+ \underbrace{(J^{\mu}_{\ \mu})^2}_{\text{filament current energy}}
\nonumber\\[4pt]
&\quad
+ \underbrace{\mathcal{S}_{\text{BF}}(u,\theta,\tau)}_{\text{BF topological term}}
+ \underbrace{\mathcal{K}_{\text{fil}}(J,\tau)}_{\text{filament kernel}}.
\label{eq:L_total}
\end{align}

Each component corresponds to a physically distinct sector:

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\theta_\mu(x)$ — scalar field amplitude in 4D spacetime.
  \item $u_\mu(x)$ — vector (strain-like) field encoding elastic deformations.
  \item $J^{\mu\nu}(x)$ — antisymmetric filament current tensor.
  \item $\tau = (L_{\text{wind}}, L_{\text{link}}, W_{\text{writhe}})$ — discrete twist charge describing topological embedding.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Topological Sector and BF Action}

The BF-type coupling introduces the discrete twist charge $\tau$ through
\begin{align}
\mathcal{S}_{\text{BF}}(u,\theta,\tau)
= \int_0^1 \!\! \dd{x}\, 
\Big[ B_\mu(x)\, \nabla^\mu A(x)
+ Q(\tau) \Big],
\end{align}
where $Q(\tau) = L_{\text{wind}} + L_{\text{link}} + W_{\text{writhe}}$ is the total twist number.

\subsection{Filament Kernel Contribution}

A damping kernel suppresses the topological mass via
\begin{align}
\mathcal{K}_{\text{fil}}(J,\tau)
= \int_0^1 \!\! \dd{x}\,
J_{00}(x)\, e^{-Q(\tau)}.
\end{align}

\subsection{Mass Spectrum}

The effective mass of a topologically charged mode is given by
\begin{align}
m_{\text{eff}} &= m_{\text{topo}} + m_{\text{ind}},\\[3pt]
m_{\text{topo}} &= \frac{m_0}{Q(\tau)},\\[3pt]
m_{\text{ind}} &= \frac{g_1^2}{M^2}E_{\text{overlap}},
\end{align}
with $g_1$ a coupling constant, $M$ a mass scale, and $E_{\text{overlap}}$ an energy overlap integral parameterizing interaction strength.

\subsection{Euler--Lagrange Structure}

The dynamical evolution follows from the Euler--Lagrange equations for the primary fields:
\begin{align}
\text{(i)}~&~\nabla_\mu \nabla^\mu \theta + 3\sin(3\theta) = 0,\\
\text{(ii)}~&~\nabla_\mu \nabla^\mu u^\nu + \Lambda u^\nu + \theta = 0,\\
\text{(iii)}~&~\nabla_\mu J^{\mu\nu} = 0,
\end{align}
where $\Lambda$ is a Lagrange-type multiplier coupling scalar and vector components.

\section{Discussion}

The SAT framework encapsulates a rich interplay between continuous fields and discrete topology. The integral structure over $\tau$ permits quantized deformation modes and potential realizations of discrete topological sectors in elastic or quantum condensate systems. Canonical quantization proceeds through symbolic commutators,
\begin{equation}
[\xi(\lambda),\pi(\lambda')] = i\pi\, \delta(\lambda-\lambda'),
\end{equation}
indicating a standard quantization condition over the continuous sector.

\section{Conclusion}

We have formulated the SATO/Blockwave SAT theory as a discrete-continuous hybrid model incorporating a 4D tensorial field space and a topological twist charge. The resulting Lagrangian structure naturally blends topological invariants with dynamic field degrees of freedom, yielding a potentially rich landscape for emergent mass spectra and geometric quantization effects.

\section{Derivation of General Relativity from the SAT Framework}
%=====================================================

The emergence of General Relativity (GR) from the SAT/Blockwave framework follows from the
variational principle applied to the constrained tensorial action. The time–flow vector
$u^\mu$ acts as a dynamically normalized timelike field whose fluctuations encode the
spacetime metric curvature. The Einstein–Hilbert action thus arises as the continuum limit
of the projected, constraint-enforced Lagrangian.

\subsection{Constrained Tensor Action}

From the normalization constraint,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C_1: \quad u^\mu u_\mu + 1 = 0,}
\end{equation}
we introduce a Lagrange multiplier $\lambda(x)$ enforcing the timelike nature of the
flow. The gravitationally extended Lagrangian density becomes
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{grav}} &=
\frac{1}{2\kappa} R
+ \lambda (u^\mu u_\mu + 1)
+ \alpha P_{\mu\rho} P_{\nu\sigma}
(\nabla^\mu u^\nu)(\nabla^\rho u^\sigma),
\label{eq:gravL}
\end{align}
where $R$ is the Ricci scalar curvature and
$P_{\mu\nu} = g_{\mu\nu} + u_\mu u_\nu$ is the spatial projector
onto the hypersurface orthogonal to $u^\mu$.

\subsection{Variation with respect to the Metric}

Variation of the total action
\begin{equation}
S = \int d^4x \, \sqrt{-g}\, \mathcal{L}_{\text{grav}}
\end{equation}
with respect to the metric $g^{\mu\nu}$ yields
\begin{align}
\delta S &= \frac{1}{2\kappa}
\int d^4x \, \sqrt{-g}\,
(G_{\mu\nu} - \kappa T_{\mu\nu})\, \delta g^{\mu\nu},
\end{align}
where the Einstein tensor arises from the variation of $R$,
\begin{equation}
G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R,
\end{equation}
and the effective stress–energy tensor receives contributions
from the SAT fields and constraint terms:
\begin{align}
T_{\mu\nu} &=
-2 \frac{\delta \mathcal{L}_{\text{SAT}}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
+ g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{\text{SAT}}.
\end{align}

Requiring $\delta S = 0$ for arbitrary variations $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$
immediately produces the Einstein field equations,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa\, T_{\mu\nu}.}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Effective Stress–Energy Tensor}

In the SAT sector, the projected kinetic term for $u^\mu$
[see Eq.~\eqref{eq:gravL}] contributes a stress–energy component
\begin{align}
T_{\mu\nu}^{(u)} &=
2\alpha \left[
(\nabla_\mu u_\rho)(\nabla_\nu u^\rho)
- (\nabla_\rho u_\mu)(\nabla^\rho u_\nu)
\right]
\nonumber\\
&\quad
- \alpha g_{\mu\nu}
(\nabla_\rho u_\sigma)(\nabla^\rho u^\sigma)
+ 2\lambda\, u_\mu u_\nu.
\end{align}
This structure parallels the aether-type field models in
Lorentz-violating gravity, but here $\lambda(x)$ dynamically enforces
the SAT time-flow normalization, preserving diffeomorphism invariance.

\subsection{Canonical Form and Hamiltonian Reduction}

The Hamiltonian density associated with the gravitational SAT sector is
obtained by Legendre transforming $\mathcal{L}_{\text{grav}}$ with
respect to $\dot{g}_{ij}$ and $\dot{u}_i$. Using the projector formalism
from the SATO–BLOCK Hamiltonian construction, the canonical Hamiltonian
is expressed as
\begin{align}
\mathcal{H}_{\text{GR+SAT}} &=
N \mathcal{H}_0 + N^i \mathcal{H}_i
+ \lambda(u^\mu u_\mu + 1),
\end{align}
where $N$ and $N^i$ are the ADM lapse and shift variables, and
$\mathcal{H}_0, \mathcal{H}_i$ are the Hamiltonian and momentum
constraints:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{H}_0 &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}
\left( \pi^{ij}\pi_{ij} - \frac{1}{2}\pi^2 \right)
- \sqrt{h}\, R^{(3)}
+ \mathcal{H}_{u},\\
\mathcal{H}_i &= -2\nabla_j \pi^j_{\ i}.
\end{align}
Here $\pi^{ij}$ is the canonical momentum conjugate to $h_{ij}$,
the spatial metric, and $\mathcal{H}_{u}$ represents the contribution
of the projected $u^\mu$ sector from the SAT constraint.

\subsection{Resulting Einstein–Hilbert Limit}

In the limit $\alpha \to 0$ and with $\lambda(x)$ enforcing
$u^\mu u_\mu = -1$ identically, the projector term vanishes and the
action reduces to the standard Einstein–Hilbert form:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{EH}} = \frac{1}{2\kappa}
\int d^4x\, \sqrt{-g}\, R.
\end{equation}
Thus, GR emerges as the low-energy, purely geometric limit of the
SATO–BLOCK SAT theory, while the full framework retains higher-order
corrections that encode the discrete topological and filament
contributions discussed in Sec.~II.
