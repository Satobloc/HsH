
\section*{Promise of the Chapter}
Thus far we have seen elasticity, gravity, and a scalar QFT emerge from one action. In this chapter we activate the \textbf{two-form current $J^{\mu\nu}$}. This sector carries conserved flux lines, and when coupled to the compact scalar $\theta$ it produces quantized holonomies: topological ``memory'' that cannot be erased by local operations. We do it both ways: the long textbook derivation (exterior calculus, Stokes’ theorem) and the short \textbf{Mini-Method}.

\section{Switch On the Two-Form Sector}
The action terms are
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_J[g,u,J] + \frac{g_h}{2}F(\theta)\,J^{\mu\nu}J_{\mu\nu},
\end{equation}
with constraints:
\begin{equation}
\nabla_\mu J^{\mu\nu}=0, \qquad J^{\mu\nu}=-J^{\nu\mu}.
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
\item $J^{\mu\nu}$ is a conserved antisymmetric tensor, interpretable as the flux density of filaments/strings in spacetime.
\item $F(\theta)$ is a function of the compact scalar, coupling topology to local dynamics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Equations of Motion}
Varying with respect to $J^{\mu\nu}$ gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\delta S}{\delta J^{\mu\nu}}=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad g_h\,F(\theta)\,J_{\mu\nu}+ \frac{\delta \mathcal L_J}{\delta J^{\mu\nu}}=0.
\end{equation}
A simple quadratic choice $\mathcal L_J=-\tfrac{1}{4}\,J^{\mu\nu}J_{\mu\nu}$ yields
\begin{equation}
\nabla_\mu J^{\mu\nu}=0, \qquad J^{\mu\nu} \propto F(\theta)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Thus $J$ is divergenceless and its flux is conserved.

\section{Flux Conservation and Quantization}
Interpret $J^{\mu\nu}$ as the dual of a two-form current:
\begin{equation}
j^\mu = \frac{1}{6}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\nabla_\nu B_{\rho\sigma}, \quad \text{with } J^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} j_\sigma.
\end{equation}
Then $\nabla_\mu J^{\mu\nu}=0$ enforces charge conservation. Integrating over a spatial 2-surface $\Sigma$,
\begin{equation}
Q(\Sigma)=\int_\Sigma J^{\mu\nu}\,d\Sigma_{\mu\nu}.
\end{equation}
By Stokes’ theorem, $Q(\Sigma)$ depends only on the homology class of $\Sigma$. For compact $\theta$, the holonomy coupling $F(\theta)$ enforces
\begin{equation}
Q(\Sigma)\in 2\pi \mathbb Z,
\end{equation}
i.e.\ flux quantization.

\section{Holonomy as Memory}
Coupling $F(\theta)J^2$ means that transporting $\theta$ around a closed loop detects the presence of conserved $J$-flux. This is a \textbf{holonomy}: the loop integral of a connection that returns a nontrivial phase. In physics language, the system ``remembers'' the flux, even if local excitations have died away.
