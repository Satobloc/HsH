There are multiple ways to accomplish the result we need, which is: 1) Polar curves capture the entirety of information defined by the input equations; that is, for either curve, its exact input equation can be reproduced directly from the curve. 2) The composite curve captures the totality of the information of both curves simultaneously. 3) The composite curve drawn on the inner surface of the torus should ideally be reverse-engineerable to produce an equation of the composite curve; ideally, a precise one, fully specifying the entirety of the curve (though approximately is acceptable, within some reasonable boundaries). 

So... what I've been thinking, to make the curve more well-behaved, and to force it to reconnect with itself after a finite (or even set) number of rotations, is something like this--
Construct a second crossbar that behaves exactly as the first, except 1) Its contact points with the curves are mirror-flipped... if the first crossbar intersects the northern curve in the eastern hemisphere, then the second intersects it in the western. 2) It swings about its trace of the two curves in the opposite direction, and it rotates in the opposite direction. 3) The elbow of the second crossbar (it's own elbow; neither mechanism is attached to the other anywhere). 4) Instead of tracing a composite curve, the pointer arm is anchored on the inside rim of the donut hole (and by virtue of that, it also intersects the center of the torus and the outer large circumference, 'slicing' the torus bagelwise).
