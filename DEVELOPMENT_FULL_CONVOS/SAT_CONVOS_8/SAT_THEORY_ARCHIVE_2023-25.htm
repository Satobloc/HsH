Introduction to the Archive

This is primarily a personal archive and indexing system, built so I can access and track my own files more easily. That said, the underlying idea is public: anyone is welcome to consider it, criticize it, share it, fork it, test it, or ignore it.

The project started small. It grew because once the original ontological commitments were established, many later structures seemed to fall into place naturally and intuitively. The archive records that development: the intuitions, the translations, the overextensions, the corrections, the audits, the prediction attempts, and the ongoing effort to separate what is solid from what is speculative.

The archive should be read as a theory-building record, not as a finished theory.

SAT = Scalar–Angular Theory / Scalar–Angular–Torsion Theory

Earlier SAT = Stringing-Along Theory.

The original name was informal and partly self-deprecating. The later names reflect the framework’s central geometric vocabulary: scalar structure, angular interaction, and torsion/twist in four-dimensional worldline geometry.

Core Idea

SAT begins from a simple geometric reversal:

The real physical object is not the point-particle as seen at an instant. The real physical object is the extended four-dimensional worldline.

SAT calls these physicalized worldlines filaments.

A particle, as we observe it, is the intersection between a filament and a moving/resolving time surface.

Fundamental Intuitions of SAT

0th —

  • the real world is the world of particle worldlines plotted in four dimensions
  • this world is a hypercomplex tangled Zottenwelt of worldlines
  • SAT calls these worldlines filaments

1st —

  • time moves like a wavefront through these worldlines
  • SAT calls this wavefront the time surface

2nd —

  • a fundamental particle is the intersection of a filament with the time surface

3rd —

  • there must be a real interaction between the time surface and the filaments
  • this interaction involves energy transfer

4th —

  • filament/time-surface interactions govern particle formation and stability

5th —

  • filament/time-surface interactions exert force back on the time surface

6th —

  • back-transmission from the filament network curves the time surface

7th —

  • filaments and the time surface are physical, not merely conceptual
  • forces may propagate along a filament’s four-dimensional extent
  • “backbleed” effects are structurally self-limiting

8th —

  • known deviations from SM, GR, or QM may be explainable through SAT geometry

9th —

  • particle properties may arise from intersection geometry
  • θ₄ = filament/time-surface interaction angle
  • early SAT treated θ₄ as a primary mass contributor

10th —

  • physical constants should emerge from SAT geometry or phenomena arising from it

Pillars and Propositions

Pillar 1 —

  • the Standard Model describes the evolving three-dimensional structure of matter
  • SAT treats SM structures as importable unless evidence or internal logic forces revision
  • SM behavior is translated into filament/time-surface geometry

Proposition A —

  • the Standard Model is treated as essentially accurate unless forced otherwise

Pillar 2 —

  • the back-pull of the filament structure on the time surface recreates curved spacetime
  • gravity emerges from time-surface curvature induced by filament structure

Proposition B —

  • General Relativity is treated as essentially correct unless forced otherwise

Proposition C —

  • backbleed effects are limited by filament and time-surface geometry
  • causality violations are structurally constrained
  • quantum-scale weirdness may reflect limited cross-slice effects

Pillar 3 —

  • Quantum Mechanics is treated as essentially correct unless forced otherwise
  • QM is translated into the geometry of SAT rather than discarded

Prime General Prediction Pathway —

  • cross-temporal tensions are not explicitly accounted for in SM, GR, or QM
  • this blind spot may produce observable anomalies

Pillar 4 —

  • filament behavior matters, not only filament/time-surface angle
  • vibrating-string, coil, twist, and locking analogies are used as geometric scaffolds
  • particle laws may correspond to repeatable intersection traces
  • QCD-like behavior may arise from two-part and three-part geometric locking

Proposition D —

  • force carriers may be structural transitions rather than persistent filaments
  • light is modeled as an excitation/ripple in the filament network

Proposition E —

  • vacuum and matter may be different regimes of the same filament/time-surface system
  • vacuum may correspond to near-aligned filaments with low energy transfer
  • matter may correspond to higher-misalignment filament intersections

Key Insight —

  • vacuum and matter lie on one continuum of filament/time-surface interaction

Proposition F —

  • some forms of String Theory may be partial mappings of filament/time-surface geometry
  • SAT may import useful string-theoretic structures by translation rather than identity
