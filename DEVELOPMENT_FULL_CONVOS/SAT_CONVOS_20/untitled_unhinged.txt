Where it genuinely risks losing the work The first paragraph is nearly 900 words long. How it fits everything we just said This may be the fullest example of your method. You start with something that looks maximally inexplicable. You permit associative, bodily, personal, philosophical, and phonetic material to accumulate. You introduce recurring entities and rules. Then you begin pointing:

Look at him. Look at it. Witness. See the answer sheet now? You get to make a whole. The apparent nonsense becomes retrospectively legible—not because every phrase receives a translation, but because the reader finally understands what sort of thing the whole performance is. My plain assessment: this is not a failed conventional essay buried under deranged language. It is a successful experimental prose work whose language is its model of reality.

It is also exhausting, excessive, sometimes impenetrable, frequently hilarious, intermittently beautiful, and far more emotionally serious than its obscene babble initially suggests.

It does not merely say, “Look how unhinged this is.”

It says:

This is the slop from which selves and worlds are made. Now look what it does. It's.... maybe... idiolalia? “Idiolalia” is surprisingly close. The word exists and is generally treated as equivalent to idioglossia: an idiosyncratic or “private” language used by one person or a very small group. Conventionally, idioglossia is most often applied to children or twins who develop shared speech forms. But yours is not idiolalia in the narrow conventional sense. You have not replaced English with a stable invented language that another initiated speaker could straightforwardly learn. Nor is it quite glossolalia, which linguists generally describe as phonologically language-like utterance without a systematic relationship between its units and communicated concepts. Your piece is loaded with intended relationships, recurring concepts, private referents, arguments, and instructions for rereading it.

I would call it literary idiolalia.

Or, more specifically:

semantically loaded idiolalia It is your own language-state emerging inside English. Ordinary grammar remains underneath, but personal associations, phonetic attractions, typos, invented words, compressed memories, bodily images, names, conceptual shortcuts, and shifting voices become strong enough to bend English into a temporary private dialect. What makes it especially idiolalic is that you are not merely representing a character speaking nonsense. You are writing in a register that appears to have developed between you, Amanda, Chat/Chachi, “Out,” and the immediate act of composition. Its words acquire meaning through that local relationship. Things such as the Lucky One, Out, slop, the answer sheet’s weave, polishing the silverware while being the silverware, and the badminton-birdie universe become understandable through recurrence rather than definition.

But it has one unusual feature that ordinary private language may not: it is constantly trying to make an outsider almost understand it.

That is where your deictic move comes in:

“Stop here. Read.” “See the answer sheet now?” “Look at the weave…” “Take a look at that.” The piece does not fully translate its private language. It periodically points to the pattern and says: you have enough now; look again. So I might sharpen the term further:

Deictic idiolalia A private, highly personal language that includes strategically lucid pointing gestures, allowing an uninitiated reader to recognize the shape of a meaning without receiving a complete decoding key. Or:

Open idiolalia A private language that is not sealed. Outsiders probably cannot recover every reference, but the work gives them enough recurrence, emotional consistency, ordinary anchors, and explicit pointing that they can enter its interpretive field. That seems importantly different from pure glossolalia. Glossolalia presents the form of language without recoverable propositional organization. Your piece presents apparent linguistic disintegration while repeatedly demonstrating that an organization exists underneath and across it. The local sentence may collapse; the larger weave keeps carrying meaning.

So yes: idiolalia may be the family name.

My fullest description would be:

A work of deictic literary idiolalia: private associative language generated in real time, retaining enough English structure, recurring imagery, and lucid self-annotation to let an outside reader witness meaning organizing itself without fully translating it. And “UNTITLED, UNHINGED” may be the extreme case where your ordinary method—wander, accumulate, zero in, point—has swallowed the entire prose surface.
