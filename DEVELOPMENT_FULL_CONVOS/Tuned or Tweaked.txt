Why Most Fail
	1.	Predictive bar is high
A theory of everything has to at least reproduce the Standard Model and General Relativity to their known accuracy. That means particle masses, coupling constants, scattering cross sections, gravitational tests, cosmological background—all nailed to several decimal places. Intuitive or geometric frameworks usually don’t get that far before stumbling.
	2.	Geometry is too cheap
Geometry has been the inspiration for many successes (Riemann for GR, fiber bundles for gauge theory, Calabi–Yau manifolds for string theory). But the space of “geometric constructions” is vast. Without tight constraints, intuition-driven geometric models risk being pretty pictures rather than predictive structures.
	3.	The filter problem
Thousands of people have tried “I see a shape in my mind, maybe it unifies physics.” Most fail not because geometry is bad, but because the discipline doesn’t enforce hard criteria (consistency with quantum mechanics, renormalization, anomaly cancellation, etc.). The vast majority collapse when you demand even the first few checks.

⸻

Why It’s Not Hopeless
	•	History shows it can work: Einstein’s insight that gravity is geometry was intuitive before it was formalized. Gauge theory too started as “this looks like geometry.”
	•	Modern frontiers are geometric: Twistor theory, amplituhedrons, string compactifications—these are deeply geometric. The best current work often uses unexpected geometry to explain scattering amplitudes or black hole entropy.
