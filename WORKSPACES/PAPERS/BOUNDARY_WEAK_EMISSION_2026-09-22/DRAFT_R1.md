# A Direct Search for Weak Secondary Emission from Controlled Boundary-Passage Experiments

## Abstract

Interference and diffraction experiments traditionally ask how a material boundary modifies a propagating quantum. We propose the reciprocal measurement: search directly for rare weak secondary radiation correlated with controlled near-boundary passage. A high-throughput slit, grating, channel, or close-boundary apparatus would operate inside or adjacent to a low-background neutrino-sensitive detector while flux, wall distance, material, curvature, boundary multiplicity, and near-wall population are modulated against matched controls. The primary observable is detector-level: an excess of events with neutrino-detector-like response. Such an event is not, by itself, identification of a Standard-Model neutrino. Standard electroweak theory contains neutrino-pair emission processes in matter under appropriate conditions, so any concrete implementation must calculate its conventional weak-emission floor and detector backgrounds. The proposal is therefore useful as a direct null test even if no new mechanism exists. SAT/H(s)H supplies one speculative motivation, but no quantitative boundary-to-neutrino emission chain is claimed here.

## 1. The reciprocal interferometer question

Single-particle interference experiments usually ask what distribution is produced by quanta traversing an aperture or grating. A complementary operational question is:

> **What does the passage do to the apparatus and its surrounding environment?**

A real slit or grating is not an inert Boolean mask. Matter waves can acquire boundary-dependent phase shifts and amplitude changes through atom-surface interactions, so material gratings are physical interaction regions. This motivates a direct search for rare secondary products correlated with controlled near-boundary passage.

The observable is not inferred from missing energy or from the interference pattern. It is a separate detector channel surrounding the interaction region. Three levels must remain distinct: (a) a Standard-Model neutrino or antineutrino state; (b) an unknown weakly interacting propagating state capable of producing a neutrino-detector-like event; and (c) any conjectured SAT/H(s)H precursor or internal mode. Detector observations begin at the detector-event level and should not be promoted to particle identity without the relevant background and interaction evidence.

## 2. Established physics: boundaries are active, weak radiation exists

Nanofabricated gratings used in atom diffraction can act as phase as well as amplitude masks because atom-surface potentials vary strongly across a slit. Established electroweak theory also contains matter processes that emit neutrino pairs, including electron-nucleus bremsstrahlung in dense matter and radiative emission from atomic transitions. Macrocoherent atomic schemes have been studied as possible enhancement mechanisms.

These facts do **not** imply an observable neutrino yield from an ordinary slit or grating. They establish that weak secondary radiation from interacting matter is a legitimate calculational category. Any concrete implementation must calculate the Standard-Model expectation for its actual incident species and spectrum, material, geometry, and energy regime.

## 3. Experimental proposal

### 3.1 A parameterized family of searches

This paper does not select a reference apparatus before a defensible feasibility calculation exists. A concrete implementation must specify at least: incident species; incident energy or spectrum; flux and exposure; boundary material and composition; geometry and wall-distance distribution; effective near-wall exposure; detector technology, threshold, and response; source-detector baseline and solid angle; run cadence; and a conventional-background model.

A minimal run set is:

- **B0:** detector background with beam and apparatus inactive;
- **B1:** beam active with a wide/open passage minimizing near-wall interaction;
- **B2:** beam active with one nearby boundary;
- **B3:** beam active through a two-boundary slit or channel;
- **B4:** geometry selected to increase the hypothesized interaction while holding flux and material budget as constant as practical;
- **B5:** geometry-matched material or orientation controls.

A particularly important control changes the near-wall population while keeping hardware geometry fixed where feasible—for example by beam steering, offset, or incidence-distribution changes. This modulates the hypothesized interaction variable more directly than changing the apparatus alone.

### 3.2 Observable and normalization

Let \(R_D(G,t,E_r,\ldots)\) be the detector event rate in reconstructed variables for geometry \(G\). The basic differential observable is

\[
\Delta R_D(G_i,G_j)=R_D(G_i)-R_D(G_j),
\]

with matched incident exposure, beam conditions, and detector state.

For a neutrino interpretation, detector rate is related to source flux by

\[
R_D = \int dE_\nu\,d\Omega\;\Phi_\nu(E_\nu,\Omega)\,\sigma_D(E_\nu)\,\epsilon_D(E_\nu,\Omega)+R_{\rm bkg}.
\]

The primary normalization should therefore use incident-quantum exposure together with an explicitly defined geometry or near-wall exposure. An unqualified probability “per passage” is not primary because passage geometry, source spectrum, kinematics, and detector response are not universal. Geometry-specific secondary normalizations may be reported when defined.

A null result constrains a conditional source-yield/response combination rather than an abstract neutrino count. Converting it into a source-yield limit requires an assumed source spectrum and a specified detector model.

### 3.3 Modulation, timing, and controls

Geometry and near-wall-population modulation should occur on a known cadence where practical; pulsed beams provide an additional timing handle. Blinded comparisons should account for detector dead time, latency, and time of flight.

Controls must address electromagnetic pickup, beam-induced neutrons, activation, radioactivity, heating, vibration, cosmic-ray modulation, shielding changes, electronic cross-talk, and material-dependent backgrounds. A sham geometry should reproduce mechanical motion, thermal load, electrical state, and material inventory without reproducing the targeted close-pass condition where possible.

### 3.4 Feasibility bridge

Before claiming experimental sensitivity, an apparatus-specific study must propagate a declared incident flux and near-wall exposure through a source model or upper-limit parameterization, geometry and solid angle, detector cross section, threshold and efficiency, and dominant backgrounds. Choosing a concrete detector/energy regime and performing this source-flux-to-detector-response calculation is the next design obligation. No feasibility number is asserted here.

## 4. What would count as evidence?

A beam-on excess would not establish boundary-induced neutrino production. The first evidentiary object is a reproducible detector excess correlated with a controlled parameter and inconsistent with identified backgrounds. Useful discriminators include closest-boundary distance or near-wall population at fixed incident exposure, material dependence, reconstructed energy/time structure, directionality or multiplicity if accessible, scaling with equivalent interaction sites, and any phase-sensitive behavior in deliberately synchronized arrays.

A null result should be published as an upper limit conditional on the declared source spectrum, geometry/near-wall exposure, and detector response. This remains useful if the motivating nonstandard mechanism is absent.

## 5. SAT/H(s)H motivation and the limit of the present claim

SAT/H(s)H is a speculative geometric framework that has considered medium-response machinery among its **candidate** mathematical tools. Appearance in a toolbox does not establish adoption or correctness.

Recovered Nathan-authored SAT discussion from 5–6 April 2025 connects aperture/grating/boundary language with filament–surface interaction, energy transfer, and light-like response: an aperture is posed as filament incidence on a spacetime surface that may impart energy and create light [SAT-BND-01]; a spacetime surface is analogized to a diffraction grating “combing” matter's filament structure [SAT-BND-02]; and light is proposed as an interaction boundary between line filaments and the perpendicular spacetime/time-expansion surface, with local uncertainty retained about the exact surface identity [SAT-BND-03]. These are exploratory questions/model proposals, not settled derivations. They establish an April-2025 antecedent for boundary-mediated filament/surface interaction and energy/light response. They do **not** establish neutrino emission from an ordinary slit or grating, detachable weak modes, later t-/f-boson vocabulary, or a quantitative yield.

Historical SAT/H(s)H particle-mode language concerning transient intersection states and substrate- or filament-associated excitations likewise does not complete the mechanism. The decisive internal chain remains

\[
\text{boundary passage}\rightarrow\text{local deformation}\rightarrow\text{transient mode}\rightarrow\text{detachable propagation}\rightarrow\text{detector-neutrino-like state}.
\]

Each arrow requires separate support. A transient internal excitation need not propagate; a propagating excitation need not yield a neutrino-detector-like interaction; and such an interaction does not by itself establish neutrino identity. The experiment remains interpretable if this SAT/H(s)H chain is incomplete or wrong.

## 6. Coherence and the neutrino-source question

Jones and Formaggio proposed a superradiant neutrino source based on radioactive Bose-Einstein condensates. Lu, Lin, and Ketterle subsequently derived a no-go result for Dicke-like superradiance from single-fermion emission and separately showed strong recoil and multimode suppression of the proposed condensate enhancement. These results concern a specific radioactive-condensate mechanism and are not evidence for or against the boundary-production mechanism proposed here.

If a boundary-induced channel emits ordinary fermionic neutrinos directly, the single-fermion superradiance bound must be confronted. If an independently derived theory instead contains a coherent precursor followed by conversion into detector states, one must establish whether phase information survives that conversion. No such precursor or conversion rule is assumed here.

Any technological significance is therefore conditional on establishing identity, spectrum, rate, controllability, backgrounds, and scaling. An unexplained detector excess is not source technology. “Neutrino laser” should be reserved for a mechanism satisfying a defensible lasing or superradiance criterion.

## 7. Prior-art status and novelty boundary

A preliminary search identified neighbouring literatures in atom-surface diffraction, standard weak neutrino-pair emission, atomic radiative neutrino-pair emission and macrocoherence, and superradiant-neutrino proposals and no-go analyses. We have not yet identified a dedicated published experiment whose primary purpose is to operate an ordinary slit, grating, or close-boundary passage apparatus inside a neutrino-sensitive detector and search for a geometry-correlated neutrino excess.

This is a bounded search result, not a universal priority claim, and should be revised if more specific prior art is recovered. The experimental value does not depend on strongest-form historical novelty.

## 8. Decision logic

**Null:** no geometry-correlated detector excess. Report conditional upper limits and constrain any boundary-emission model accordingly.

**Positive but conventionally explained:** a detector excess is observed and an established process explains it. Report the identified process and its measured dependence on the controlled geometry or near-wall population. Call it boundary-associated causally only if the controls establish that attribution.

**Positive with unexplained residual:** reproduce across detectors, materials, geometries, and near-wall-population controls; map spectrum and scaling; only then compare candidate beyond-standard descriptions.

**Positive with collective or phase-sensitive scaling:** test against standard coherence bounds and construct a source-level amplitude model before invoking laser language.

## 9. Conclusion

The proposed experiment reverses the usual observational emphasis of interference and diffraction studies by instrumenting the environment around a controlled boundary-passage region and asking whether rare weak secondary detector events track near-wall interaction variables. The first implementation should be differential, modulated, and agnostic about mechanism.

SAT/H(s)H supplies one speculative reason to look, but the measurement stands independently. A null result can constrain a declared source-yield/response model. A positive detector excess first demands conventional-background discrimination and particle-identity work. Only after identity, rate, controllability, and scaling are established do coherence or source-engineering questions become warranted.
