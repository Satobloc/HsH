## Verdict

**REVISE, THEN SANDBOX-POST.**

This is a good proposal-paper shape and, importantly, it mostly resists the temptation to make the SAT/H(s)H motivation do work it has not earned. The paper's strongest move is reciprocal and experimental: stop treating the boundary only as an input to the transmitted quantum and instrument the boundary/environment as a possible output channel. That question survives even if the SAT/H(s)H mechanism is wrong.

My main Nathan-fidelity concern is not that the paper is too bold. It is that two different levels of boldness are presently adjacent enough to blur under hostile reading:

1. **experiment-level boldness:** search directly for a geometry-correlated weak-secondary channel; and
2. **SAT/H(s)H mechanism boldness:** boundary passage may create a transient mode that becomes a detachable neutrino-like detector state.

The first is the paper. The second is presently a motivating conjecture with unresolved arrows. R0 says this repeatedly and correctly; R1 should make the separation even more mechanical.

## Findings

### ASTER-B-01 — PASS / PRESERVE — the reciprocal experimental question is Nathan-shaped

The opening operational inversion — **"What does the passage do to the apparatus and its surrounding environment?"** — is the right conceptual center. It is concrete, experimentally legible, and does not require a reader to accept SAT/H(s)H. Preserve it early and visibly.

The paper should resist any revision that turns this into a generic neutrino-source survey. The interesting object is the *boundary-passage differential experiment*.

### ASTER-B-02 — PASS WITH HARD BOUNDARY — recovered Nathan provenance supports boundary response, not neutrino emission

Section 5 handles the recovered April-2025 material responsibly. The cited Nathan-direct antecedent supports an exploratory lineage of aperture/grating/boundary → filament/surface interaction → energy transfer → light/photon-like response. It does **not** establish ordinary-boundary neutrino emission, detachable weak modes, a detector-state map, or a quantitative yield.

This distinction should survive verbatim in substance. Do not let later H(s)H vocabulary retroactively upgrade the 2025 source.

### ASTER-B-03 — IMPORTANT — `neutrino-like weak radiation` risks becoming a pseudo-object unless detector typing is kept explicit

The manuscript is strongest when it writes the detector convolution and says a null constrains source yield × detector response. That discipline should govern the prose everywhere.

`neutrino-like` can otherwise drift between at least three meanings:

- an actual Standard-Model neutrino/antineutrino state;
- an unknown weakly interacting propagating state that happens to trigger a neutrino detector;
- a SAT/H(s)H internal mode conjectured eventually to map to one of those detector outcomes.

Those are not interchangeable. R1 should define the target channel operationally at first use and keep particle identity as an inference downstream of detector response, spectrum, timing, directionality and cross-detector reproduction.

### ASTER-B-04 — THE EXPERIMENT NEEDS ONE MORE CONTROL AXIS: SAME GEOMETRY, DIFFERENT TRANSMITTED/NEAR-WALL POPULATION

R0 correctly emphasizes geometry modulation and sham controls. But if the claimed discriminant is boundary passage rather than merely apparatus state, the design should explicitly seek a control that holds geometry fixed while changing the population of trajectories that actually sample the near-wall interaction region.

Examples at the design level could include beam steering/offset, incidence distribution, or another matched control that changes closest-approach statistics without rebuilding the aperture. The exact implementation is experiment-dependent; the conceptual requirement is not.

Why this matters: a geometry-correlated excess can still be a geometry-correlated conventional background. A passage-density/closest-approach scaling axis is closer to the hypothesized causal variable.

### ASTER-B-05 — CLAIM TYPING — “positive geometry-correlated signal” must not outrun “detector excess correlated with geometry”

In the abstract and elsewhere, prefer detector-level language until ordinary backgrounds and detector artifacts have been excluded. A geometry-correlated detector excess is the first observation. Calling it a weak-secondary signal is already an interpretation.

The decision tree in Section 8 mostly gets this right. Pull that discipline upward into the abstract.

### ASTER-B-06 — PASS / STRONG — arrow-by-arrow SAT mechanism chain

The chain

`boundary passage → local deformation → transient mode → detachable propagation → detector-neutrino-like state`

is exactly the right internal discipline because it exposes where the conjecture can fail. Preserve it and, if anything, make the status of each arrow explicit: historical antecedent / current model proposal / not yet derived / requires detector map.

This is more useful than adding pages of generic caveats.

### ASTER-B-07 — SCOPE — “technologically consequential” is fair, but keep it conditional on source characterization

A reproducible controllable neutrino source would indeed be technologically consequential. But a detector excess near an apparatus is not yet a source technology. R1 should retain the excitement while making the dependency explicit: technological significance follows only after particle identity, source spectrum, rate, controllability, backgrounds, and scaling are established.

### ASTER-B-08 — PRIOR-ART WORDING — the novelty statement is appropriately bounded; do not strengthen it during revision

The sentence saying no dedicated published experiment has **yet been identified** is correctly typed as a search result rather than a universal priority claim. Preserve that structure. The eventual PRIOR_ART/private audit remains quarantined and should not be silently imported into this independent review lane.

### ASTER-B-09 — STANDARD-PHYSICS NEIGHBORS — useful as category anchors, dangerous as intuitive rate analogies

The established weak-emission examples justify that weak secondary emission from matter is a legitimate calculational category. They do not make ordinary low-energy slit/grating emission plausible by analogy. R0 says this, but R1 should keep the neighboring-process section short enough that readers cannot mistake citation density for expected-rate support.

The real conventional-theory requirement is apparatus-specific: calculate the Standard Model expectation for the actual beam/material/energy/geometry.

### ASTER-B-10 — NULL RESULT — yield-per-passage should be presented as a family of bounds, not a single apparatus-independent number

R0 already qualifies `P_max` by energy/model assumptions. Good. R1 should be explicit that the result will generally be a limit surface over assumed spectrum/kinematics and detector response, possibly also geometry and source localization. `yield per passage` is a useful normalization only after “passage” is operationally defined for the chosen beam and near-boundary population.

### ASTER-B-11 — NATHAN-FIDELITY / VOICE — the manuscript is at its best when it states the experiment, distinction, consequence

Keep sentences such as:

- “The primary comparison is not simply beam-on versus beam-off. The informative variable is boundary geometry.”
- “Each arrow must be justified separately.”
- “This is a search result, not a universal priority claim.”

They carry Nathan's actual argumentative habit better than generic academic throat-clearing. A final voice pass should compress rather than colloquialize.

### ASTER-B-12 — DO NOT MAKE THE PAPER DEPEND ON COMPLETING H(s)H

The proposal is valuable because it can be run and interpreted before the speculative mechanism is complete. Do not let peer review turn unresolved theory arrows into prerequisites for the experimental paper. Theory completion and experiment design should feed each other, but the experiment's null-test value is independent.

## Highest-value R1 changes

1. Define the detector-level target at first use; distinguish actual neutrino state, neutrino-detector-like event, and conjectured SAT/H(s)H precursor.
2. Add an explicit fixed-geometry / varied-near-wall-passage control axis where experimentally feasible.
3. Keep observation language at detector level until backgrounds and particle identity are established.
4. Preserve the April-2025 provenance boundary exactly in substance: boundary/light response antecedent ≠ neutrino prediction.
5. Turn the SAT mechanism chain into an arrow-status table or equally compact typed object.
6. Present null bounds as spectrum/response-dependent families rather than a single abstract probability.
7. Preserve the bounded prior-art wording and the experiment's independence from SAT/H(s)H completion.
