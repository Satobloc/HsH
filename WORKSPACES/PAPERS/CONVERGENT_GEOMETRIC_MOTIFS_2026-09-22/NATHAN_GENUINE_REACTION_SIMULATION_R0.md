# I. NOTES TO SELF — BEFORE TRYING TO SOUND LIKE NATHAN

## 1. First reaction: the paper exists for a reason; do not review the reason out of it

The central danger in the review pile is not scientific sloppiness. The reviewers are mostly identifying real problems. The danger is that a correct sequence of local corrections could turn a genuinely interesting comparative claim into a beautifully sourced paper whose conclusion is basically: geometry occurs in physics.

Nathan is likely to have very little patience with that outcome.

The operative distinction is:

- **strong claim with weak typing** → repair the typing;
- **strong claim whose evidence disappears under typing** → weaken or remove the claim;
- **strong claim that survives typing** → keep it strong;
- **interesting conjecture without a map yet** → label it conjecture and make the missing map the task, rather than deleting the conjecture because it is not already solved.

This is already the best idea in the hot-paper directive: *more ambitious architecturally and more conservative sentence-by-sentence*. That is probably very close to how Nathan himself would formulate the desired tension.

## 2. Nathan will probably like the reviewers more than he likes some of the draft

R0 is clean, intelligent, cautious, structurally sound — and in several places too fucking clean.

The draft occasionally reads as though SAT was conceived by a committee after a literature review and then executed according to a mature methodological charter. Nathan's actual developmental record is messier, iterative, opportunistic, recursive and in places frankly ridiculous-looking until the continuity becomes visible later.

That mess is not embarrassing provenance to hide. It is evidence.

The more historically exact story is stronger:

- there was an old 4D intuition;
- the Minkowski worldline picture mattered very early;
- vocabulary and interpretation were crude;
- later AI conversations allowed the idea to be attacked from hundreds of angles very quickly;
- labels and mechanisms changed repeatedly;
- constraints, geometry and recovered continuity did much of the pruning;
- later formal language often arrived *after* the geometric object had already been played with in less formal terms.

If the paper wants to make an independence/chronology argument, sanitizing that history makes it less believable, not more.

## 10. “Why this is not just geometry everywhere” is mandatory

The potentially interesting object is the *dependency pattern / conjunction* and the chronology of its appearance in this lineage.

So say:

- individual ingredients: unsurprising, often old;
- conjunction: selected comparison object;
- dependency arrows: what must be typed;
- historical chronology: independently auditable;
- reduction conjecture: separate high-risk claim.

Then invite someone to break it.
