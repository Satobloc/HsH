## Load-bearing primary wording

### Hinton

Hinton states:

> “If we pass a spiral through the film the intersection will give a point moving in a circle…”

and then reverses which object moves:

> “Suppose now the spiral to be still and the film to move vertically upwards…”

He continues:

> “the record of traversing the spiral is a point moving in a circle.”

Immediately afterward he generalizes to:

> “structures consisting of filaments”

whose intersections with the moving film become apparent atoms and bodies in “a world of apparent motion,” while the higher structures are stationary.

### Whately Smith

Smith describes:

> “a thin thread passing through the film”

and states that the cut-point remains stationary if the thread is perpendicular but moves if the thread is slanted.

He then proposes:

> “an enormous number of such threads”

that are:

> “twisted and intertwined in the most complicated possible manner”

whose passage across the film generates complex lower-dimensional change.

### Nathan

Nathan independently constructs the same geometry in the recovered March 2024 conversation:

> “One is a straight line, the other is a helical line wrapping around the straight line…”

with a moving intersecting plane.

He corrects the assistant:

> “If the plane is moving perpendicular to the straight line, then point S would appear motionless. Only point H would appear to be moving with time. Assuming a perfect helix, point H would trace out a perfectly *circular* path around point S…”

and later generalizes:

> “If two lines are intertwined like a braided string, the points on the moving plane will appear to orbit one another.”

He then explicitly moves from worked examples to an approximate grammar for motion/interactions.

Later in the same conversation he states that the 4D universe may be treated as static except for propagation of a three-dimensional time surface, with apparent 3D dynamism arising from cord/rope geometry and changing intersections.

## Prior-art verdict

### 1. Generic moving-section novelty: **NOT AVAILABLE**

Hinton and Smith decisively predate SAT on the generic idea that lower-dimensional apparent motion can be generated by a higher-dimensional extended structure intersected by a moving section.

### 2. Spiral/helix → circular moving point: **SUBSTANTIVE PRIOR ART**

Hinton's 1904 wording is effectively the same construction class as Nathan's March 2024 helix/orbit example.

This is stronger than a vague thematic resemblance.

### 3. Thread/filament intersection + twisted/intertwined structures: **SUBSTANTIVE PRIOR ART**

Smith 1920 supplies a particularly close thread/film/intertwining antecedent.

### 4. Influence: **NOT ESTABLISHED**

Nothing in the sources checked here establishes that Nathan knew Hinton or Smith before the March 2024 construction.

Similarity does not prove influence.

Therefore:
- historical priority belongs to Hinton/Smith for the generic construction;
- Nathan's March 2024 record remains relevant as evidence of **independent development / rediscovery** if exposure review continues to find no pre-2024 contact;
- the correct claim is not “Nathan invented moving-section filament geometry,” but something closer to “Nathan independently reconstructed a strikingly old higher-space construction and then developed it into a distinct SAT programme.”

### 5. Potential Nathan-specific elaborations in the checked set

The checked historical passages do **not** by themselves establish:
- the exact rule “two intertwined lines → two points orbit one another”;
- Nathan's later Minkowski/time-surface physical mapping;
- his quark-braid assignment;
- the specific SAT/H(s)H force and particle programme;
- his later block-universe/worldtube extensions.

Those require separate prior-art comparison and should not be declared novel merely because Hinton/Smith do not supply them in these passages.
