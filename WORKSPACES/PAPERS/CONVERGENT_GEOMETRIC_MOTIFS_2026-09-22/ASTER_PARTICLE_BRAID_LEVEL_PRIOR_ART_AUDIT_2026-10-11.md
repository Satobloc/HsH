## 2. Bilson-Thompson 2005

Citation verified at high confidence:

Sundance O. Bilson-Thompson, **“A topological model of composite preons,”** arXiv:`hep-ph/0503213` (22 March 2005).

The paper's indexed abstract states that it:
- represents binding of preons topologically;
- identifies the **substructure of quarks, leptons and gauge bosons with elements of the braid group (B_3)**;
- obtains quantities such as helicity and hypercharge from the topological construction;
- treats the preon-like objects as potentially composite structures themselves.

Later Bilson-Thompson literature makes the three-strand framed/ribbon character more explicit:
- first-generation quarks/leptons are represented by three-strand framed braids;
- strand twists encode charge-related information;
- the braid is the particle-state/topological-substructure object.

### Relationship to SAT

This is **substantive particle-braid prior art**, but the object map is not the same.

Bilson-Thompson:
[
	ext{3-strand framed braid}
longrightarrow
	ext{quark/lepton/gauge-boson state or its preonic substructure}.
]

Nathan / SAT:
[
	ext{quark worldline strands}
overset{2/3	ext{-braid}}{longrightarrow}
	ext{mesonic / hadronic composite history}.
]

The strands therefore occupy different physical/model levels.

## 3. Asselmeyer-Maluga 2019

Torsten Asselmeyer-Maluga, **“Braids, 3-Manifolds, Elementary Particles: Number Theory and Symmetry in Particle Physics,”** *Symmetry* 11 (2019) 1298, DOI `10.3390/sym11101298`, arXiv:`1910.09966`.

Its indexed abstract states:
- fermions are modeled via hyperbolic knot complements;
- every relevant 3-manifold is handled via a 3-fold branched-cover construction;
- knot complements yield a 3-fold branched cover of (D^3) branched along a **3-braid or 3-braids describing fermions**;
- the programme connects to Bilson-Thompson-related 3-braid particle models.

Again, the 3-braid is attached to the **fermion-description layer**, not to a three-quark baryonic composite in Nathan's sense.

### Relationship to SAT

`SUBSTANTIVE_PRIOR_ART; PARALLEL_PARTICLE_BRAID_PROGRAM; NONIDENTICAL_OBJECT_MAP`.

This is a strong comparator for “matter from braid/topology,” but not an identity with the SAT quark-braid construction.

## 4. Broader antecedent context

Vladimir Yershov's 2002 **“Fermions as Topological Objects”** is an earlier topological/preon composite-fermion model. It is relevant as background showing that topological substructure for quarks/leptons predates Bilson-Thompson, but the source checked here does not establish the same braid construction.

Artin braid theory itself is much older still, but supplies mathematical braid classification rather than a particle assignment.

Therefore three ancestry layers should remain separate:

1. **braid mathematics** — Artin/configuration-space lineage;
2. **particle-state/preon braid models** — Bilson-Thompson and descendants, Asselmeyer-Maluga-related constructions;
3. **SAT quark-worldline composite braids** — Nathan's 2-/3-strand meson/hadron mapping.

## 5. Why the distinction matters

A careless comparison would say:

> “Both models make particles out of 3-braids.”

That sentence erases the important difference.

The more accurate comparison is:

> Both programmes assign physical particle structure to braids, but they assign the braid to different objects. Bilson-Thompson-type models use framed three-strand braids to encode elementary fermion/gauge-boson states or their preonic substructure; SAT treats quark worldlines as strands whose two- and three-strand braiding yields composite hadronic histories.

This distinction affects:
- what counts as the strand;
- what the braid represents;
- where charge/chirality information lives;
- whether the electron is braided;
- whether bosons are braid objects;
- what dynamics would have to stabilize the structure.
