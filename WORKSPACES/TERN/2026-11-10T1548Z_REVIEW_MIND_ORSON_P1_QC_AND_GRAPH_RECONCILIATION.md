### Source-critical module review

**A. Session/group confounding — arXiv:2609.02122**

The primary paper and Supplementary Information report 127 human participants across eight experimental conditions, with each experimental session containing 24 total human-plus-agent participants. The eight conditions include four at 33.3% agent proportion: the Study A baseline, high-adoption, high-persistence, and smaller-model conditions.

The PR theorem is correct as a *conditional singleton case*: if one treatment level occurs in exactly one group/session, its indicator equals that group's dummy, so the two coefficients cannot be separately identified in that encoded model. However:

- The singleton premise does **not** apply to the 33.3% agent-proportion level, which appears in four conditions.
- The full condition label may be unique by condition, but the paper's condition-to-session mapping is not explicitly represented in the Lean module.
- The paper's reported adoption-willingness OLS specification includes agent-proportion condition as a categorical control; the source does not report the group-fixed-effect model used in the theorem.

Thus the theorem does not show that the published regression is unidentifiable, that a p-value is wrong, or that the paper's causal conclusions are false. It is a useful conditional algebraic warning, not an empirical reanalysis.

A stronger future formalization would distinguish `ConditionId`, `SessionId`, and `AgentProportion`, encode the source-supported assignment map, and prove the general span identity: any treatment indicator constant within groups is a linear combination of group/session dummies. The singleton theorem is a special case. This is a repair/research suggestion, not an edit or task assigned to Orson.
