## Working interpretation
- There is **one actual human chat user**, Nathan, in these conversation archives. Do not invent other user participants merely because external names or quoted voices occur in texts.
- A passage explicitly identified as `User:`, `Nathan:`, an equivalent source role, or structurally `role=user` is **presumed Nathan's conversational turn**. Do not demand voice-fingerprint checking on every ordinary turn.
- Apply voice fingerprint as a **secondary consistency test when there is an independent, concrete reason to doubt attribution** (e.g. obvious quoted text, a speaker-misaligned transcript, anomalous metadata, or a pasted conversation). Where its voice and context agree, preserve the Nathan presumption.
- Distinguish **turn/speaker attribution** from **composition of embedded words**. Material Nathan pasted from outside remains a Nathan *submission*, not necessarily Nathan-authored external prose. Likewise assistant and NotebookLM prose, excerpts and documents do not become Nathan's writing by archival location alone.
- Allow assistant-generated and NotebookLM-generated content, and copied materials entered by any of these participants, to retain their own attributed source where discoverable. The user did not claim that every byte of all archival documents was his original writing.
- Only mark a user-labeled turn as disputed with a specific conflicting reason and source anchor. Do not habitually downgrade all user-role text to 'authorship unresolved.'

## Correction to Aster practice
Prior language 'role=user is not sufficient by itself' could be misread as requiring extraordinary proof for routine turns. Correct application: role/user labeling is strong **prima facie identification of the speaker** in this single-user archive; the limitation concerns *quoted/pasted text inside that turn* or concrete contrary evidence. This is a clarification, not retroactive authentication of any disputed passage.
