## 5. H957 two-plane mapping: narrowed, not rejected

The assistant-generated **DERIVATIVE INDICATRIX** extraction defines a more general two-frequency path with variable radii:

[
H(\lambda)=
\big(
R_d(\lambda)\cos(\omega_s\lambda+\phi_0),
R_d(\lambda)\sin(\omega_s\lambda+\phi_0),
r_h(\lambda)\cos(\omega_h\lambda+\psi_0),
r_h(\lambda)\sin(\omega_h\lambda+\psi_0)
\big).
]

The extraction identifies (R_d(\lambda)) as the hypersphere radius/expansion factor and (r_h(\lambda)) as the minor superhelix radius. It is an assistant-voiced document requested by Ravel/ChatGPT, not a Nathan Direct equation.

Mercer h957's constant-amplitude special case is mathematically correct. For constants (A,B), with (\rho=\sqrt{A^2+B^2}>0),

[
H_{A,B}(\lambda)=
(A\cos\omega_1\lambda,A\sin\omega_1\lambda,
 B\cos\omega_2\lambda,B\sin\omega_2\lambda),
]

[
x_0=(A/\rho,0,B/\rho,0),qquad
Q(\lambda)=R_2(\omega_1\lambda)\oplus R_2(\omega_2\lambda),
]

[
H_{A,B}(\lambda)=\rho Q(\lambda)x_0.
]

This is a valid coordinate identity and a fixed Clifford-torus orbit. It is not the full variable-radius derivative ansatz and does not exercise the UI's proposed scale/time control because (r=\rho) is constant.

For variable (R_d(\lambda),r_h(\lambda)), define

[
\rho(\lambda)=\sqrt{R_d(\lambda)^2+r_h(\lambda)^2},
\qquad
u(\lambda)=H(\lambda)/\rho(\lambda)
]

where (\rho(\lambda)>0). A general (Q(\lambda)\in SO(4)) can map a fixed seed to (u(\lambda)); a continuous lift can be selected on a suitable interval, but it is nonunique up to the (SO(3)) stabilizer. The fixed block-diagonal (R_2\oplus R_2) subgroup used in h957 does not vary the relative torus radii and therefore covers only the constant-amplitude subfamily.

The h957 “great-circle when (|\omega_1|=|\omega_2|)” statement also assumes both amplitudes are nonzero. If one amplitude vanishes, the remaining occupied plane is a great circle regardless of the unused frequency.

**Correct reading:** h957 established a useful special-case orbit identity. It did not establish that the entire variable-radius derivative ansatz is the direct UI construction, that the source's scale/time law is satisfied, or that the physical carriers are equivalent.

## 6. Historical assistant mathematics and discrepancies

### A. Hypersphere constraint mismatch

The derivative extraction defines

[
L_{UI}=\frac{\lambda_s}{2}
\left(x^2+y^2+z^2+t^2-R_d(\lambda)^2\right)^2.
]

But the same extraction's path definition implies

[
\|H(\lambda)\|^2=R_d(\lambda)^2+r_h(\lambda)^2.
]

The residual against the stated major-radius constraint is therefore (r_h(\lambda)^2). A nonzero minor radius cannot satisfy the exact constraint as written. If the term is intended as a soft penalty, it penalizes the minor radius; if it is intended as a hard constraint, the ansatz and constraint are incompatible unless (r_h=0). The source does not resolve this interpretation.

Existing PR #752's `MercerUIDerivativeConstraint` formalizes the constant-amplitude version: norm squared (A^2+B^2), residual (B^2) against (A^2), and exclusion of nonzero minor radius under the major-radius constraint.

### B. Full-frame versus point-path kinetic term

The March assistant message `2267b5c9-d807-43a8-91a2-64d63633737b` introduced a six-plane (SO(4)) Lagrangian. Its full-frame angular contraction is not automatically the kinetic term induced by a single moving marker. The point-path correction using (\|\Omega u\|^2) rather than the full Frobenius contraction was already recorded in issues #203 and #213; it is not republished here.

### C. Integration domains are not interchangeable

The direct phrase “full four-dimensional volume of the hypersphere” needs a domain convention:

- (S^3) is the three-dimensional boundary.
- (B^4) is the four-dimensional interior.
- (SO(4)) is the six-dimensional frame group.
- A space of histories (r(\cdot),Q(\cdot)) is an infinite-dimensional path space.

A finite-dimensional volume integral over (B^4) or (S^3) is not automatically a functional integral over path histories. The assistant's `integral Dr DR` and “every possible configuration” interpretation requires a defined history space, measure, action, constraints and treatment of frame-stabilizer redundancy.

### D. Holonomy and local-geometry claims

Nathan Direct proposes the hypersphere as a holonomy generator and as a way to model gravity-wave-like distortions, wells and singularities. Those are preserved as direct hypotheses. A sphere alone does not specify a connection, bundle, parallel-transport law or holonomy representation. The local-geometry proposal additionally needs a metric/curvature map, field equations and an observable/readout contract. No physical calculation or validation was performed.

### E. Translator scope

The direct UI goal includes equation-to-curve, curve-to-equation, comparison and shortest derivation. The current mapper is a bounded forward representation for known four-coordinate histories. It does not yet implement the inverse translator or define a shortest-derivation metric. These are separate missing capabilities, not consequences of the point-orbit identity.
