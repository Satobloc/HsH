# Selected Specimen: One Chondrule

## Selection

Use one representative chondrule: a small, rounded silicate object formed as a droplet of melt in the early Solar System's protoplanetary disk, cooled into a solid, and later incorporated into a primitive chondritic planetesimal (a small rocky asteroid parent body).

This is a specimen class for a thought-map. It is not an identified individual chondrule, and we do not claim to know its unique trajectory.

## Why this is a useful scale

A chondrule is a discrete solid object with a compact sequence of mesoscopic changes:
- precursor dust aggregate;
- transient heating and melting;
- cooling and crystallization;
- movement within the protoplanetary disk;
- accretion into a small rocky body.

This is more developed than mapping only a molecular-cloud environment, but bounded enough to avoid a planetary atmosphere, biosphere, or global carbon-cycle history.

A recent review describes chondrules as droplets of crystallized silicate melt formed by rapid heating of dust aggregates to temperatures above approximately 1800 K, followed by cooling over hours to days. It places planetesimal accretion in the roughly 10–100 km size regime as an important stage of early planet formation. The broad sequence is suitable for this exercise; detailed heating mechanisms and exact trajectories remain unresolved.

## Operational boundaries

- **Beginning:** the chondrule-forming heating/melting episode that transforms the precursor dust aggregate into a melt droplet. We include the heating/melt/cooling interval needed for the droplet to become a solid chondrule.
- **End:** incorporation/accretion of that solid chondrule into a primitive, undifferentiated chondritic planetesimal.

We do not yet assign dates, orbital radii, disk coordinates, or a particular parent body. Those would be extra commitments and are not required to establish the first path.

## Scope exclusions

Do not trace:
- every constituent's stellar or molecular-cloud history;
- the complete gas/dust evolution of the protoplanetary disk;
- competing detailed chondrule-heating mechanisms;
- multiple heating cycles unless later required for the selected representative case;
- parent-body differentiation, impact disruption, meteorite delivery, terrestrial weathering, atmosphere, or biosphere.

The tracked procession ends at incorporation into the small rocky body.

## Scientific source

Schönbächler, M., Bouvier, A., Kita, N. T., Kruijer, T. S., et al. (2025). “Initial Conditions of Planet Formation: Time Constraints from Small Bodies and the Lifetime of Reservoirs in the Solar Protoplanetary Disk.” *Space Science Reviews*, 221, 97. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11214-025-01216-z

Relevant statements in the review:
- planetesimals of roughly 10–100 km are a key early accretion scale;
- chondrules are crystallized silicate-melt droplets formed by rapid heating of dust aggregates above ~1800 K and cooling over hours to days;
- chondrules and CAIs preserve records of the early protoplanetary disk;
- chronometers can be disturbed by parent-body thermal or aqueous alteration, so the present exercise stops at initial incorporation rather than silently adding later processing.
