## Current checkpoint

The project has deliberately moved away from the carbon-14 nucleus and its global carbon-cycle complications. The selected mesoscopic specimen is **one representative chondrule**, traced from its formation in the early Solar System's protoplanetary disk to its incorporation into a small, primitive, undifferentiated rocky body (a chondritic planetesimal / asteroid parent body).

This is a representative class-level path, not a claim that we have identified one particular chondrule or reconstructed its unique trajectory. The exact heating mechanism and detailed transport path remain unspecified.

## Selected simple path

1. A precursor dust aggregate exists in the protoplanetary disk.
2. A transient heating event melts or strongly heats the aggregate, producing a silicate melt droplet (the chondrule-forming event).
3. The droplet cools and crystallizes into a solid chondrule.
4. The chondrule moves within the protoplanetary disk.
5. It is incorporated into a small, primitive, undifferentiated planetesimal.

For this first pass, the tracked history ends at incorporation into the rocky body. Do not add the body's later impact, disruption, delivery to Earth, weathering, terrestrial atmosphere, biosphere, or other subsequent histories.

## Scientific footing and uncertainty

A 2025 review describes chondrules as droplets of crystallized silicate melt produced by rapid heating of dust aggregates to temperatures above about 1800 K, followed by cooling over hours to days. It describes planetesimals of roughly 10–100 km as an important scale in early planetary assembly. These are class-level descriptions; they do not identify the unique history of an individual chondrule or settle the detailed heating mechanism.

Source: Schönbächler et al. (2025), “Initial Conditions of Planet Formation: Time Constraints from Small Bodies and the Lifetime of Reservoirs in the Solar Protoplanetary Disk,” *Space Science Reviews* 221, 97. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11214-025-01216-z
