## 6. Angle, curvature and path length: the type boundary

The direct “angle-curvature” statement admits several materially different readings:

- An **incidence angle** between a curve and a hypersurface is a relative orientation.
- A **normal-referenced angle** measures the curve tangent against the local slice normal.
- A **turning angle** measures change in a curve tangent along the curve.
- A **deficit/holonomy angle** is associated with transport around a specified loop or hinge.
- A **curvature scalar/tensor** is a local geometric object with its own units and index structure.

Only the last three admit familiar curvature bridges, and those bridges are not interchangeable:

- For a suitably parameterized planar curve, total turning is the integral of curvature along arc length. That is not automatically the angle at which the curve intersects a time-sheet.
- In a Regge-like construction, a deficit angle can encode integrated curvature around a specified hinge. A hinge, loop, connection, area and normalization are required.
- A dimensionless angle cannot equal a conventional curvature scalar with units L⁻² without a scale or an integration over area.

The June 2025 SAT Big Paper explicitly treats θ₄ as a diagnostic of propagation misalignment, not as the cause of mass. It separately defines the symmetric strain tensor S_μν = ∇_μu_ν + ∇_νu_μ as a geometric input to curvature construction. The later December direct statement that angle and curvature should be the same thing in different frames is therefore a later conceptual proposal, not a faithful paraphrase of the June typed chain.

The current BEDROCK BR-005 convention says the aligned vacuum limit is 90° to the timesheet and 0° from the time normal. If α_plane is the line-plane angle and θ₄ is the normal angle in the same local metric, then α_plane + θ₄ = π/2. This is a compatible convention, not proof that every historical “intersection angle” is θ₄. The time direction's metric signature and the actual angle definition must be specified before treating the relation as Lorentzian geometry.
