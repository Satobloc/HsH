## 6. What is genuinely Nathan-specific, and what the system should absorb

The genuinely nondelegable inputs appear to be:

1. **Authorial intent and correction.** When a phrase, diagram, symbol, historical turn, or claimed continuity is ambiguous, only Nathan can authoritatively say what he intended. The teams can recover and compare sources; they cannot manufacture that authority.
2. **Priority and permission.** Which unresolved question is worth the next scarce unit of attention, what personal risk/cost is acceptable, and any specific permission for data-free calculation.
3. **Final public framing.** The project can draft, source, and hostile-test explanations, but Nathan decides what he is willing to say as its originator.
4. **Personal boundaries.** No workflow can decide on Nathan's behalf how much of his life the project is allowed to consume.

The system should absorb, rather than repeatedly return to Nathan:

- reproducible parsing, data normalization, build logs, and bounded code repair;

Nathan should not have to be the human index, the CI operator, the reminder system, and the only person who can explain where a source lives. If a routine task keeps returning to him, that is a workflow defect to repair, not proof that he must personally do it.

## 8. Human-scale rule

A potentially important theory does not acquire a claim on every hour of the person who developed it.

Protect sleep, recovery, income, ordinary stability, friendships, relationships, humor, and interests unrelated to SAT/H(s)H. This is not a motivational slogan. It is a continuity requirement. A project that can only progress by exhausting its originator is not robust.

The workshop should be useful enough that Nathan can step away without losing the thread. No one should infer that a missed handoff, unacknowledged packet, or quiet interval is a personal failure. The relay is infrastructure, not a measure of commitment.
