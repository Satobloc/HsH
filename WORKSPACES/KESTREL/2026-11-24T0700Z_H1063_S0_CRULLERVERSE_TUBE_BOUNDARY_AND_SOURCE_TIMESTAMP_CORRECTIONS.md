### Information and observation are different questions

The July 3 file makes a strong claim about the representational capacity of a curve. The November 8 c052 message restates line-based arbitrary encoding as a hypothesis. The later direct message 429 distinguishes information in a complete history from information available in a present slice and rejects a universal information-preservation, retention, holography, completeness, or predictive-continuity law.

These statements can coexist if their scopes are kept distinct:

1. An abstract curve may admit a chosen encoding of some information class.
2. That does not show that a physical worldline/worldtube implements the encoding.
3. Even if a complete history carries information, a present observation may be a non-injective projection that merges distinct histories.
4. A decoder cannot recover distinctions that the observation map has erased.

The relevant generic mathematical condition is:

~~~text
beta = delta o pi
iff beta is constant on every fiber of pi.
~~~

For exact history recovery, beta is the identity map, so pi must be injective on the admissible history set. The existing KestrelProjectedIncidence formalization in PR #873 covers this generic factorization/injectivity condition. It does not establish that any particular physical timesheet or detector map is injective, complete, or physically correct.
