## 3. Primary-source comparison: finite-core vortex measurements

### Nature Physics: linear Kelvin-wave propagation

Barckicke, Falcon & Gissinger, “Kelvin wave propagation along vortex cores,” Nature Physics 22, 409–414 (2026), version of record 2026-02-10, DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03175-w. The paper declares a Figshare dataset, DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.30903347.

The source defines the two antipodal contour samples and derived channels:
\[
r_0=r_c(z,t,0),\qquad r_\pi=r_c(z,t,\pi),\qquad
\eta=\frac{r_0-r_\pi}{2},\qquad a=\frac{r_0+r_\pi}{2}.
\]
The inverse on these two measured coordinates is r0=a+η, rπ=a−η. The operator is invertible on the pair; it is not injective on the full azimuthal mode index or full cross-sectional shape.

The paper prints the Doppler-shifted frequency as
\[
\widetilde\omega=\omega+m\Omega_0+k v_z.
\]
Its z coordinate is measured from the free surface downward, with downward axial flow treated as positive. The paper's fit uses an averaged core radius and fits (Γ,vz) to dispersion data before checking those parameters a posteriori against PIV/PTV measurements. That is a fit-conditioned standard comparison, not a blind holdout prediction.

The Methods report typical core-radius/position uncertainty around 0.1 mm, PIV circulation uncertainty around 0.005 m² s⁻¹, and PTV vertical-velocity uncertainty around 0.05 m s⁻¹. The figure caption's statement that the uncertainty on the averaged radius is negligible is retained separately; it is not silently substituted for the typical per-measurement uncertainty. Exact Figshare version, file inventory, raw contour arrays, and checksums remain unretrieved.
