### 4.1 2024 moving-plane toy

Let the straight axis be the z-axis and a perfect helix have radius `r` and pitch `p`:

\[
A(z)=(0,0,z),\quad
H(z)=\left(r\cos(2\pi z/p+\phi_0),r\sin(2\pi z/p+\phi_0),z\right).
\]

For the plane family `Pi_z={(x,y,z)}`, each fixed plane has one point from each curve. The helix point's **locus across the plane family** is a circle of radius `r`. The circle is not the intersection set of one fixed plane.

Pitch controls phase advance, not radius. If `z=vt`, the conditional angular rate is `omega=2 pi v/p`. This is a mathematical reconstruction of the source description, not a physical law.

For a tilted line `ell(s)=(0,a s,b s)`, its intersection with `z=z0` has `y=(a/b)z0`; with `z0=vt`, `ydot=(a/b)v`. The historical assistant's claim that the point moves at the plane speed `T` is only true under a special slope normalization.

The two-parabola proposal lacks exact parametric curves and an unambiguous plane-motion direction. The assistant chose negative y although the inherited plane family moved along z. Do not silently supply missing coordinates.
