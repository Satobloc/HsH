## 3. External literature: persistence is a functional condition

### Persistent organizational human-AI teams

Rovetta, Bortolotti, and Di Berardino, “When humans and agents form a collective: Toward a theory of persistent organizational human-AI teams,” Frontiers in Organizational Psychology, published 2026-10-08, DOI `10.3389/forgp.2026.1941058`. Article type: Hypothesis and Theory, not an empirical validation study.

The paper's useful operational criterion is that prior collective activity must be available to an artificial contributor and capable of affecting its later outputs or actions. Merely storing records is insufficient if they cannot enter subsequent participation. The framework separates:
1. system configuration;
2. collective functioning;
3. collective intelligence;
4. downstream consequences.

It also insists that cross-substrate correspondence specify the unit of analysis, elements and relations, abstraction level, mapping, and criteria for correspondence/non-correspondence. Its “organismic” framing does not imply biological equivalence, psychological symmetry, consciousness, or personhood. The paper itself says direct evidence for persistent organizational human-AI teams remains limited.

### Cognitive Spillover in Human-AI Teams

“Cognitive Spillover in Human-AI Teams,” ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction, 2026; arXiv:2407.17489v3.

The two studies must be kept separate:
- Study 1: 97 individuals used a GPT-4o chatbot and then entered a later human-only interaction. The authors report linguistic spillover after AI exposure.
- Study 2: 20 human teams, 69 participants, four conditions with five teams per condition. The AI contribution was a pre-recorded, scripted, non-interactive voice assistant, not a live LLM agent. Participants nevertheless showed changes in collective attention/language and related team measures; 77% did not regard the AI as a team member.

This is important boundary evidence: AI output can influence human group cognition without AI membership or autonomous agency. It is not evidence of a persistent multi-agent LLM team, and it does not establish continuity across days or repeated organizational cycles. Team-level N is 20, so precision is limited.

### Measurement candidates

- “Cultural Variations in Human-AI Partnership: Initial Cross-Cultural Validation of the TMS-GenAI Scale,” CHI 2026, DOI `10.1145/3772318.3790980`. The indexed record describes an initial cross-cultural self-report scale study. Treat it as an individual partnership measure, not a validated team-level transactive-memory measure; confirmatory and invariance work remains necessary.
- Akgun and Toker, “When Does GenAI Use Support Critical Thinking?”, arXiv:2610.38910, submitted 2026-10-28. The paper reports a cross-sectional self-report survey of 891 students and a structural model involving division/coordination of cognitive labor and active reworking. It is individual-level, correlational, and cannot establish causal mediation. Its 27-item operationalization should not be silently equated with the separate 28-item CHI scale paper.

### Combined evidence gap

The inspected sources still do not establish a replicated longitudinal experiment combining multiple humans, multiple live LLM agents, explicit per-agent context/visibility logs, source ancestry, repeated sessions, membership/model turnover, objective accuracy, and group-level analysis. Existing studies cover parts of this space, not the whole combination.

## 6. Disconfirming experiment proposal

A staged study could test whether persistent history creates functional continuity or merely preserves errors.

**Stage 1:** group-level 2x2 design:
- live interactive AI versus scripted non-interactive AI exposure;
- prior collective history accessible versus reset/inaccessible.

Use multiple groups per condition, repeated sessions, a task battery, and randomization/power analysis at the group level. Log every human/AI message, visibility window, retrieval, model/prompt/version, source ancestry, role, decision, correction, and output.

**Stage 2:** introduce controlled contributor/model turnover and compare:
- continuity with accessible history;
- continuity with history stored but unavailable;
- reset history;
- stale or incorrect history, only in a consented low-risk task with debriefing.

Measure objective task accuracy with independent adjudication, error correction, calibration, expertise routing, shared mental models, social cohesion, workload, and behavioral transactive-memory indicators. Use TMS-GenAI only as an exploratory individual self-report measure until team-level validity is established.

Disconfirmation:
- if accessible history does not alter later participation, functional continuity is not demonstrated;
- if it changes outputs but not task performance, continuity may exist without improved collective capability;
- if it propagates errors, persistence can preserve maladaptive state;
- if scripted exposure produces the same effect as live AI, autonomous agency is not necessary for that effect.

Human participants require informed consent, privacy protection, and debriefing. No human-subject study was conducted in this run.
