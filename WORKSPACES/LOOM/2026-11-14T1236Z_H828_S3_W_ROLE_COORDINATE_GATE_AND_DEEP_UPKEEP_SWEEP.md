## 3. Geometry consequences and acceptance boundary

The source-level dimensional description remains a 4D tube region around a 1D core, a 3D boundary, and a 2D worldsheet simplification. The new w-role proposal does not settle whether the four-dimensional ambient is a spacetime manifold or a four-spatial-dimensional manifold with a separate evolution parameter.

Conditional consequences:

1. If a 1D core is smoothly embedded in a smooth 4D ambient manifold, its normal bundle has rank three. This is a dimensional result under those assumptions, not a measured core radius or a physical tube profile.
2. A 3D boundary and a 2D worldsheet are different objects. A projection, inclusion, quotient or readout map must specify their relationship.
3. The normal metric and connection group cannot be fixed until the ambient signature and the core's spatial or causal character are specified.
4. Finite tube support does not determine a numerical thickness or radial profile.
5. A scalar twist is not forced by the minimal tube/core pair. A particular SO(2) twist description requires an explicit plane reduction or other source-defined structure.
6. Contact surfaces require explicit incidence, intersection, gluing or boundary maps. A 3D mouth is not automatically the transverse intersection of two 3D hypersurfaces in 4D.
7. theta-4 remains underdefined until its metric, tangent and normal vectors, and time role are specified.

The standard tubular-neighborhood result is the relevant differential-topology comparator: a smooth embedded submanifold has a normal bundle, and a neighborhood can be represented by a neighborhood of its zero section. See John M. Lee, Introduction to Smooth Manifolds, 2nd ed., Graduate Texts in Mathematics 218, Springer, 2012, chapter on tubular neighborhoods. This standard result does not identify the physical H(s)H embedding or its metric.
