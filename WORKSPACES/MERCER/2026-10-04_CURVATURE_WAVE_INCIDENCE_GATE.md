## Interpretation
If curvature/torsion waves are generated by ordinary filament self-dynamics, then they need not themselves be a new force. They can periodically move an existing finite-core incidence system across its geometric bifurcation. This yields a mechanism:
[
	ext{curvature wave}	o K(s,t)	o	ext{threshold modulation}	o	ext{contact topology gating}	o	ext{possible reconnection window}.
]
Reconnection still requires its own constitutive dynamics; crossing the incidence threshold only opens/closes the geometric window.

## Calculable discriminator
Hold `rho_+`, `K_0`, and incidence `alpha` fixed, launch a measured curvature wave of amplitude `epsilon`, and record the fraction of each period spent in the split-contact regime. No fit is allowed: compare directly with `D(r,epsilon)`.

Particularly sharp: tune `r=1`. The minimal model predicts exactly 50% duty cycle independent of `epsilon`. Departure indicates nonlinear curvature coupling, time-dependent support radius, higher contact geometry, or failure of the mechanism.

For small `epsilon`, the switching-band fractional width is approximately `epsilon`; hence weak Kelvin-like waves only gate systems already close to incidence criticality.

## Failure conditions
Fail this minimal construction if measured contact topology does not track instantaneous `K(s,t)`; if there is large phase lag in a quasistatic regime; if the 50% critical duty-cycle result fails after numerical convergence; or if the support geometry itself varies enough that `rho_+` cannot be treated as fixed.
