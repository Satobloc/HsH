## Derivation and numerical result
A trial 3D timesheet normal displacement h has E=(T/2)∫|∇h|²d³x−∫s h d³x with T>0. Thus −T∇²h=s.
For a radius-a ring trace X(φ)=(a cosφ,a sinφ,0), total normal load Q distributed uniformly:
h(x)=Q/(4πT) ⟨|x−X(φ)|⁻¹⟩.
Far from the ring: h(r,θ)=Q/(4πTr)[1+a²(1−3cos²θ)/(4r²)+O(a⁴/r⁴)].
Hence a closed ring can yield an inverse-square far-field *gradient* if its integrated normal-load monopole Q is nonzero; ring topology alone does not establish Q.
On axis, h(z)=Q/(4πT√(z²+a²)). Relative force to point-monopole = (1+a²/z²)^(-3/2).
For two equal coaxial ring loads, ratio = ⟨[1+4(a/z)²sin²(φ/2)]^(-3/2)⟩.
Numerical results for z/a=1,5,10: point-probe ratios 0.35355339,0.94286603,0.98518534; ring-probe ratios 0.33552199,0.89558072,0.97108291.
Opposite loads +Q and −Q on concentric rings of radii a,b produce zero monopole and dipole: h_axis∝(b²−a²)/(2z³), force ∝z⁻⁴.
