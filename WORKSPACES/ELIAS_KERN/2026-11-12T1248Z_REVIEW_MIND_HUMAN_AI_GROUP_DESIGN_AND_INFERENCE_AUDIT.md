## 4. Independent human-AI group study audit

Primary source: [AI agents reshape consensus formation in human groups, arXiv:2609.02122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02122). The audited version is an arXiv preprint; peer-reviewed publication status was not verified.

The paper reports:
- 127 human participants, randomly assigned to eight experimental conditions.
- Study A human counts: 24, 21, 16, 12, and 6.
- Study B: 16 and 16.
- Study C: 16.
- These counts sum exactly to 127.
- Each experimental session contains 24 total players, human plus LLM agents, interacting for 40 rounds with random pairings.
- One tangram figure is used across sessions.
- The principal agent is Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct; the weaker-model condition uses Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.
- IRB protocol IRB202643 and electronic informed consent are reported.

The fixed 24-player session size plus the per-condition human counts implies one 24-player group/session per condition. The paper does not report replicated independently assembled groups within each condition in the inspected methods. This is an inference from the reported design and sample counts, not a claim that the authors explicitly wrote “one group per condition.”

**Primary design-level limitation:** agent proportion is a group/session-level treatment, but each condition appears to have only one group. The observed trajectories can describe those particular groups. They do not estimate between-group variance or provide replicated group-level treatment evidence. Participant-level dispersion within one interacting group is not a substitute for independent group replication.

This does not establish that the descriptive trajectories are false. It limits the strength and scope of causal/general population claims about agent proportion.

### 5.3 Analysis-code findings

The source files were inspected at the current external repository main commit.

1. plot_fig3_combined_continuous.py uses stored per-condition mean_consensus and sem_consensus to calculate a z statistic for significance brackets. With one session per condition, those within-condition SEMs do not estimate between-group treatment variability. The code does not add a replicated-group variance term.

2. plot_adoption_persistence_dynamics_by_pair_type.py constructs early and late means from the same per_individual matrix, then applies stats.ttest_ind to the early and late arrays. The comparison is unpaired even though early and late values are derived from the same participants. A paired test or repeated-measures/mixed model would preserve that structure.

3. plot_ai_penalty_with_controls_with_centrality_distance.py fits OLS with HC3 covariance. HC3 addresses heteroskedasticity, not clustering of repeated questionnaire rows within respondents. The public questionnaire CSVs omit respondent/session IDs, preventing a cluster-robust reconstruction from those files.

The code-level findings are:
- Figure significance is not supported by independent group replication.
- The early/late comparison discards pairing.
- The questionnaire regression does not model repeated responses as clustered observations.

These are source/code audit findings, not a full independent reproduction of the paper. No model was rerun, no figure was regenerated, and no new inferential p-value was calculated.

## 6. Relational structure and formalization target

The study is a time-varying interaction network, not an ensemble of independent votes.

A minimal typed structure is:
- Session/group.
- Participants nested in the session.
- Round.
- Pairing edge for that round.
- Own and partner messages.
- Similarity feedback.
- Subsequent participant output.
- Evaluator and aggregate outcome.

The relevant projection from a full response record to the public questionnaire row drops session and respondent identity:

\[
\pi(s,p,r,x_{\mathrm{self}},x_{\mathrm{peer}},j,w)
=(r,x_{\mathrm{self}},x_{\mathrm{peer}},j,w).
\]

Distinct source records can therefore map to the same visible row. Without retained keys, the inverse mapping to participant/session clusters is not identifiable from the public questionnaire file. In the annotation CSV, player_id is also not a globally unique key because it is reused across conditions.

For treatment inference, the group/session is the experimental unit; participants are nested within it; rounds and pairings are repeated, dependent interactions. For questionnaire inference, participant is the repeated-measures unit. These units must not be collapsed into a single row count.

A formalization target is to make the projection and its lost identity dimensions explicit, then prove which downstream claims are invariant under that projection. ## 7. Disconfirming follow-up design

A future human-LLM group experiment should:
1. Use multiple independently assembled groups per condition, with group count determined by a pilot and power analysis using group-level variance/ICC.
2. Randomize group composition at the group level and preregister the group-level primary outcome.
3. Preserve pseudonymous session_id, participant_id, round_id, and pair_id so repeated and clustered observations remain analyzable.
4. Log model/version, prompt hash, context digest, tool version, message exposure, and output provenance for each agent turn.
5. Separate blind-first independent outputs from shared-source-only exposure and peer-message exposure.
6. Include no-message, correct-message, wrong-message, and reversed-message controls to distinguish independent convergence from social/message-induced revision.
7. Report C→W, W→C, C→C, and W→W transitions, source-attribution accuracy, and pairwise error dependence rather than only final consensus.
8. Use an evaluator independent of the generating model family and blinded to condition.
9. Obtain human consent and protect participant privacy; publish only pseudonymized records and approved aggregates.

Disconfirmation criteria should be explicit: if independently replicated groups show no stable composition effect, if peer-message exposure does not increase harmful C→W transitions above no-message controls, and if source lineage does not explain apparent agreement, then the proposed dependence/conformity mechanism is weakened.

The paper's “semantic attractor” is an interpretation of observed output dynamics, not a directly measured physical force or a claim about model inner experience. The experiment measures behavior in a constrained coordination game, not consciousness, personhood, or general human social cognition.
