## 4. Mathematical triage

### 4.1 O1: a 1D centerline does not define a 4D finite-core volume

O1 gives γ:ℝ→ℝ^(3,1) but defines the worldtube as a 4D volume. The image of the one-parameter curve is not, by itself, a 4D finite-core region. A thickening map or equivalent finite-core data are missing.

A minimal representation needs a labelled support stratum and its core/framing data. Locally, one possible mathematical realization is a rank-3 normal/core fiber over the 1D centerline, with an embedding of the resulting 4D disk bundle into the ambient 4-manifold. This is a representation completion, not a new physical mechanism and not yet a canonical BEDROCK choice.

### 4.2 J2 and J3 are naturally different support strata

For a four-dimensional ambient manifold, the degree-level support map is:

| Support stratum | k | Poincare-dual current degree 4-k | Transverse intersection with a 3D timesheet |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1D centerline/filament | 1 | 3-form J3 | 0D point |
| 2D swept worldsheet | 2 | 2-form J2 | 1D curve |
| 3D finite-core boundary shell | 3 | 1-form | 2D rim |
| 4D finite-core region | 4 | 0-form density/indicator | 3D mouth/section |

This supplies a coherent candidate hierarchy: J3 can encode a 1D filament stratum; J2 can encode a 2D swept worldsheet stratum; the 4D finite-core region is a different object. This is not a recovered historical conversion rule.

A possible chain-level relation is d PD(S²)=±PD(∂S²), when the J2 support is an oriented 2-chain and the J3 support is its 1D boundary. The sign depends on current/orientation conventions. This is a boundary/cobordism relation, not Hodge duality.

Important: the Blockwave equation ∇μJμν=0 is a divergence/codifferential condition for a 2-form (equivalently d(*J2)=0 under standard conventions). It is not automatically the same as dJ2=J3. The source must specify whether J2 is a smooth field strength, a distributional Poincare-dual current, or a chain current, and how the boundary is represented.

## 5. Minimal typed carrier candidate

A compact representation with maximal cross-frame coverage is:

C = (M⁴, g, τ, X, U?, ∇⊥, Γ, O, Π).

- M⁴,g: ambient four-dimensional geometry and declared metric/frame convention.
- τ: timesheet parameter; Σt is the corresponding 3D hypersurface when the required regularity/transversality conditions hold.
- X: a labelled, typed support complex carrying dimension k, embedding, orientation, multiplicity and boundary data. It may contain a 1D centerline, 2D swept worldsheet, 3D core boundary and 4D finite-core region without identifying them.
- ∇⊥: declared normal/frame transport; holonomy is path-dependent and gauge-covariant, while conjugacy-class data are gauge-invariant.
- U?: separate responsive-medium state, included only when the existing medium hypothesis is being represented; it is not silently promoted to a new fundamental field ontology.
- Γ: labelled interaction/composition graph.
- O: observation/readout map, separating physical support from what a particular timesheet/instrument detects.
- Π: provenance and representation map, preventing assistant equations, user editorial choices and source documents from being conflated.

Geometry-to-equation direction: typed supports determine the degree of a Poincare-dual current; timesheet intersections determine readout strata; a specified connection and path determine transport/holonomy. Equation-to-geometry direction is non-unique: a degree, smooth field, conserved current or holonomy value does not by itself recover a unique embedding, core radius, framing, gauge potential or support history. Those data must be explicit.

Composition candidates: ∂²=0 for the support chain; boundary-current composition when a worldsheet has a filament boundary; transport composition under path concatenation; gauge transformations act on representatives while preserving appropriate invariant classes. These are candidate structural interfaces, not promoted SAT dynamics.
