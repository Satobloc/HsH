## 4. Substantive architecture improvement

### New gate: same-sector cross-study transferability

The two source studies are independent classical-fluid experiments with different physical systems, forcing and observation operators:

1. **Scheeler et al. (2017), Science 357, 487-491, DOI 10.1126/science.aam6897.** Full paper PDF read. The experiment generated thin-core vortex loops in water using 3D-printed hydrofoils, visualized them using fluorescent rhodamine dye and 3D high-speed laser-scanning tomography, tracked patterned dye blobs inside the core, and obtained circulation separately through PIV with neutrally buoyant tracers. The paper reports Reynolds numbers 12,000-20,000. Its centerline helicity reduction is conditional on sufficiently thin cores with minimal winding variation across a cross-section. Linking and writhe are available from centerlines; twist and total helicity require internal flow. In the plotted single-tube decomposition, twist is computed as a residual. Figure 3 uses 10 stretching and 8 compression runs; Figure 4 uses 16 total trials. The supplementary materials are referenced but were not retrieved.

2. **Barckicke, Falcon and Gissinger (2026), Nature Physics 22, 409-414, DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03175-w.** Full paper PDF and Methods read. The experiment uses a stable air-core vortex in a 21-cm-diameter, 45-cm-tall tank. A Basler camera normal to the axis, with 8-mm and 12-mm lenses, records the air-water contour at typically 300 fps for 30 s. The analysed contour spans 12.5 cm around depth 22 cm, at 60 micrometres per pixel, with 0.5-mm contour width and typical core-radius/position uncertainty near 0.1 mm. Separate top-view PIV uses a Phantom V1840 at 6,000 fps, 10-micrometre particles and a 37 by 23 grid at 2.7-mm spacing; PTV measures vertical velocity. Reported typical uncertainties are about 0.005 m²/s for circulation and 0.05 m/s for vertical velocity. The standard comparator is the classical Rankine-core Kelvin dispersion relation with axial flow. The paper reports m=1 and m=2 modes. Its Methods state that mean core radius is measured from contour data while circulation and vertical velocity are fitted to the dispersion relation and checked a posteriori against PIV/PTV. The paper identifies Figshare DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.30903347, but raw files, version, manifest, checksums and covariance were not retrieved.

### Why this is not duplication

Issue #1447 already specifies the within-study Kelvin measurement-map requirements. Issue #1463 already provides a distinguishability ladder and calibration ancestry. Issue #1467 already records the Scheeler source addendum. The new contribution is the **whole-study transferability condition**: a common H(s)H map must be frozen before one entire study is held out, and it must survive a different observation operator without study-specific retuning.

This is same-sector robustness within classical fluid mechanics, not cross-sector evidence. The two datasets must not be pooled. Their fluid-helicity and Kelvin-wave quantities are standard fluid mechanics, not evidence by themselves for H(s)H theta-four, Blockwave theta, a Lorentzian worldtube, or an additional H(s)H twist field. A time-evolving 3D fluid vortex is not automatically an H(s)H 4D worldtube.

### Transferability contract

1. Define a source-authorized map from the H(s)H object to a classical 3D fluid-vortex state. No such map is currently identified; current state is `MAP_NOT_DEFINED`.
2. Specify each experiment's instrument-to-record map independently.
3. Bind every input to its provenance and classify it as measured, calibrated, derived, fitted or predicted.
4. Freeze only parameters belonging to the common H(s)H map. Circulation, core radius, axial velocity and boundary conditions are study-specific physical inputs, not shared numerical parameters.
5. Choose the calibration and whole-study holdout direction before inspecting holdout outcomes. Do not pool raw records. If both directions are tested, preregister them separately.
6. Compare each study against its own standard fluid model using the same measured inputs, covariance and nuisance freedom.
7. Set a covariance-aware falsification threshold from actual raw data. No numerical threshold is assigned because the datasets and joint covariance packages were not retrieved.
8. Require independent review of the frozen map, calibration split and residual criterion.

Outcomes are classified as `MAP_NOT_DEFINED`, `OBSERVATION_OPERATOR_UNRESOLVED`, `CALIBRATION_LEAKAGE`, `STUDY_SPECIFIC_RETUNING`, `TRANSFER_FAIL`, `NO_DISCRIMINATING_RESIDUAL`, or `TRANSFER_SUPPORTED_NOT_VALIDATED`. A transfer success would support transferability only, not H(s)H physical truth or theory promotion.

### Forced versus optional

Finite-core vortex structure, fluid helicity decomposition, twist/writhe and Kelvin modes already occur in standard fluid mechanics. These observations do not force an H(s)H worldtube thickness, timesheet coupling, independent twist connection, contact law, or any additional H(s)H degree of freedom. Such additions remain optional until a source-defined map yields a distinctive, independently testable observable beyond the standard comparator.
