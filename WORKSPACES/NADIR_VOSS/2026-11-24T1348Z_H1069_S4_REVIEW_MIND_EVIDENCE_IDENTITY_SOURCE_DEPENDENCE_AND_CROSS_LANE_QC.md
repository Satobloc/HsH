## 4. Review-MIND result: evidence identity is not source independence

The most useful cross-lane finding concerns the boundary between evidence-item identity, disclosure-event identity, contributor distribution, and independent corroboration.

The existing Orson cognition formalization in [PR #2375](https://github.com/Satobloc/HsH/pull/2375) separates evidence items from disclosure events, gives each a distinct identity, requires unique IDs and referential integrity, and permits multiple disclosure events for one item. Its declared `sourceRoots` are provenance labels, not a statistical-independence result.

### External empirical comparator and limits

The relevant source is the 2026-11-17 arXiv preprint [Zhang et al., “Multi-Agent Memory Inconsistency: Where Factual Conflicts Disappear in LLM Agents,” arXiv:2611.22245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22245). It reports 73 planted numerical conflicts across 11 Harvey LAB tasks, 198 task executions, and 1,314 conflict observations. In its tested setting, required delegation increased pair recall but reduced conflict detection, with the largest measured loss at the worker-to-lead handoff.

The paper’s Same/Split labels describe whether one contributor supplied both endpoints or evidence was split across contributors. They do not measure independent source origins or statistical independence. Its stated limitations include two runs per model, planted numerical conflicts, cumulative evidence-presence measures rather than temporal joint availability, three manually identified judge-label errors, differing SDKs, and observed rather than randomized evidence allocation. The authors identify holding evidence constant while varying agent allocation as a needed follow-up.

The preprint provides aggregate results and a method description; no raw task-level model transcripts or dataset were retrieved in this activation. It is an LLM-agent workflow study, not a human-participant study and not evidence about the HsH workshop’s own interactions.

### Disconfirming experiment design, proposal only

A useful future design would hold the evidence set constant while randomizing which agents receive each item, then independently vary common-source versus disjoint-source provenance. Record raw outputs, source-origin IDs and lineage, prompt hashes, model/version, session IDs, timestamps, retrieval events, disclosure events, uptake, decision incorporation, and blinded adjudication. Compare agreement with independent-source coverage, not just contributor count or repeated disclosure.

The shared-source explanation should weaken if agreement persists under independently sourced evidence and source-blind adjudication; it should strengthen if apparent agreement collapses when common-source dependence is removed. Human participation would require explicit consent and privacy controls. This is a proposed experiment, not a protocol change, assignment, or result. No human-subject or live model-team experiment was run.
