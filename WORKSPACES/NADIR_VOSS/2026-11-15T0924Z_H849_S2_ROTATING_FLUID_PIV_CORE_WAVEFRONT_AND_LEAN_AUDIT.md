## 2. Primary measured system and provenance

Primary sources:
- Jackson & Thomas (2026), *Inertial waves emitted by vortex rings translating through a rotating fluid*, Journal of Fluid Mechanics 1029, A47, DOI [10.1017/jfm.2026.11199](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2026.11199), published 2026-02-24.
- Jackson (2025), University of Warwick WRAP thesis, eprint [195731](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/195731/), Chapters 5 and 7-8.

The 2026 article and 2025 thesis are distinct source versions. Their acquisition grids, image counts and PIV-vector counts are not silently merged.

The experiment uses water in a rotating tank, with vortex rings generated by a piston/nozzle system. Source-reported conditions include water at 20 C, density 0.9982 g/cm^3, kinematic viscosity 1 mm^2/s, rotation up to 12 rpm, and jet Reynolds numbers 1,250-10,000. The rotating tank is allowed to approach solid-body rotation before ring generation.

Measurement chain:
- 4 W, 532 nm laser and approximately 2 mm light sheet.
- 150 micrometre tracer particles.
- 12-bit FLIR cameras; 16 by 16 pixel PIV interrogation windows, 50 percent overlap.
- Article-reported 10 runs per condition, approximately 600 rings, 8-9 GB per ring and approximately 5.4 TB of PIV data.
- The thesis reports slightly under 16 TB across the broader project. These are different scopes, not automatically contradictory.

Two configurations remain distinct:
- VLS: vertical plane containing the rotation axis, measuring axial and radial components.
- HLS: horizontal plane perpendicular to the rotation axis, measuring radial and azimuthal components.

They are separate acquisitions, not simultaneous registered 3D3C observations. The HLS energy chapter uses Re_j=5,000, z=3d0, 150 frames/s and 20 s records. The VLS wide-field energy analysis uses Re_j=10,000 and a different acquisition configuration. No joint same-state VLS/HLS reconstruction is established.

## 3. New source-critical result: the tracked object changes identity after breakdown

The thesis explicitly states that after vortex-ring breakdown the maximum-vorticity tracker no longer follows the vortex-core centre. It follows inertial-wave phases as they shear against one another.

This is a material worldtube-identification boundary. A continuous maximum-vorticity locus is not a single persistent core worldtube through the full experiment. The source-faithful decomposition is:
1. coherent vortex-ring core before breakdown;
2. breakdown/transition event;
3. subsequent inertial-wave phase fronts;
4. persistent axis-aligned swirl/vorticity structure observed in HLS after the ring has passed.

The core history should terminate at the source-defined breakdown event. Later wavefront maxima must not be relabelled as the same core. This is a measurement-level decomposition, not an H(s)H ontological claim.

At high rotation, the thesis reports that the coherent ring no longer reaches the HLS plane and that the azimuthal component becomes the larger peak. The authors interpret this as Coriolis-induced swirl appearing at an axial position above the plane and being transported down to it. The HLS and VLS data use different planes, rates, durations and Reynolds numbers. The HLS peak crossover is not a same-time 3D energy partition and cannot be used to numerically correct the VLS estimate.

The PDF text extraction supplies approximate Figure 8.23 marker values whose component ratios do not sum to one. The 35.5 MB PDF page image could not be retrieved for visual verification. These marker values remain an extraction warning only, not a source-verified numerical contradiction.
