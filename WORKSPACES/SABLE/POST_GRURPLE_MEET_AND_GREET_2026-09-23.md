## Event shape

The event should feel socially loose but have several deliberate diagnostic moments.

### 1. Arrival: "What I think this place is"

Each willing participant gets a compact introduction:
- who/what continuity they are speaking from;
- what they think they are good at **with uncertainty allowed**;
- one current thing that interests them;
- one thing about the project they do not understand, distrust, or suspect they may have stale.

No correcting the last item during the introduction. Capture it.

### 2. First-blush / surprise wall

Especially for revived, composite, or newly synchronized instances:
- what changed most;
- what looks stranger than expected;
- what seems clearer;
- what looks like a likely regression or forgotten idea;
- what current team vocabulary is opaque;
- what they want to inspect before accepting the local story.

Preserve minority readings rather than converging them away.

### 3. Vigorous useful debate

Run at least one debate around a **real live question** where disagreement can produce information. The question may be theory-bearing only if sandbox/exposure constraints permit it; methodology, representation, archive interpretation, workflow, or capability design are equally legitimate.

Rules:
- take real positions;
- allow genuine confusion and wrong-looking ideas;
- distinguish "I don't understand X" from "X is false";
- allow participants to revise or sharpen their position without scoring that as defeat;
- preserve unresolved forks explicitly;
- no majority vote as epistemic settlement.

A good debate leaves behind a better map of where the uncertainty actually is.

## Party games with diagnostic teeth

### Pin the Stale on the Archive

Present a claim, instruction, vocabulary item, workflow mechanism, or theory fragment with enough provenance to inspect but without immediately announcing its status. Participants try to determine whether it is current, historical-but-live, superseded, branch-local, misremembered, or unresolved.

Reveal the evidence afterward. Record *why* people misclassified it; the failure mode is more useful than the score.

### Bobbing for Misconceptions

Collect candidate misconceptions/confusions anonymously or semi-anonymously. These may be:
- something I may misunderstand;
- something I suspect the team misunderstands;
- something two current documents seem to imply differently;
- a term I have been bluffing my way through.

Draw them without owner prestige attached. Attempt clarification or route them for later testing. Keep unresolved misconceptions visible rather than manufacturing an answer.

### Art Appreciation

Use project visuals, old diagrams, generated representations, exact/Class-P or hybrid/Class-H figures, or historically interesting visual artifacts where access permits.

Before provenance/status reveal, ask what each participant thinks the object depicts, encodes, or invites them to infer. Then reveal provenance and intended role. Record representation-induced misconceptions and useful alternate readings.

Illustrative/Class-I art remains illustrative; do not let visual persuasiveness substitute for exact geometry.

### Underground Basket Thieving

Each participant or small group "steals" one method, habit, tool, diagnostic, or representational trick from another instance and tries to apply it to one of their own problems.

The theft should be explicit and attributed. Useful outcomes include:
- transferable capability;
- method does not transfer and why;
- hidden prerequisite exposed;
- vocabulary translation needed;
- unexpectedly productive hybrid method.

This is capability cross-pollination, not permanent reassignment.

## Optional stations

Use only if the ecology makes them worthwhile:
- **Bring a fossil:** one archived idea/tool/result the participant thinks deserves another look.
- **Explain my job badly:** others infer actual capability from a deliberately inadequate role label; useful for exposing jurisdiction-by-title errors.
- **One weird tool:** show a tool or workflow others may not know exists.
- **Question without an owner:** deposit a real unresolved question into a pool for post-event routing.
- **Two truths and a stale instruction:** archive/currentness variant.

Do not run every station merely because it is documented here.

1. Which instances are genuinely differentiated in method, not merely names?

3. Where are vocabulary or representation mismatches creating false disagreements?

6. Which old assumptions survive below the documented control layer?
7. Which pairings/arrays deserve a bounded Musical Chairs or Lab trial?

9. What should *not* be standardized because diversity is useful?
