The various H(s)H toolbox/toolkit documents are a **central source of candidate mathematical and representational formalisms for the SAT/H(s)H rebuild**. They are not peripheral background reading and should not be treated merely as an external-reference shelf.

## Operating rule

Use toolbox/toolkit material aggressively inside the **sandboxed theory-building lane** to ask:

- what mathematics can represent the live H(s)H geometry cleanly;
- what structures can formalize worldtubes, recursive helices/superhelices, braids, winding, chirality, holonomy, intersections/projections, causal structure, spinorial behavior, solitons, scale recursion, and related live constructions;
- which existing mathematical languages make current SAT/H(s)H claims sharper, testable, computable, or provable;
- whether multiple candidate formalisms encode the same geometric content in different ways;
- where a formalism exposes a contradiction, missing degree of freedom, unnecessary primitive, or hidden constraint;
- whether candidate structures can support current proof-quality targets such as the GR–QM correspondence/isomorphism question, He-3 holotype scaling, soliton recurrence across scale, and dimensional repair of cosmological formulations.

## Epistemic boundary

A formalism's presence in the toolkit means it is **available candidate machinery**, not automatically SAT ontology, a current SAT assertion, or a required ingredient of H(s)H.

Do not reason:

`toolkit contains X` → `H(s)H fundamentally consists of X`.

Instead test:

`live H(s)H geometric requirement` → `does X faithfully and economically formalize it?`

Keep the direction of explanation from the H(s)H geometry/constraints to the mathematics, unless a specific historical source establishes otherwise.

## Rebuild workflow

For each candidate formalism, prefer a bounded packet:

1. **H(s)H requirement:** state the live geometric/problem requirement first.
2. **Candidate tool:** identify the mathematical formalism and the exact feature that may help.
3. **Mapping:** give an explicit proposed correspondence between H(s)H objects and formal objects.
4. **Payoff:** state what becomes calculable/provable/clear if the mapping works.
5. **Failure test:** identify what would show the formalism is a bad fit or is smuggling in unwanted ontology/assumptions.
6. **Status:** candidate / useful local representation / strong fit / rejected / superseded / unresolved.
7. **Provenance:** cite the toolkit source and any H(s)H source motivating the requirement.

Do not demand one universal formalism prematurely. Different mathematical languages may be useful at different scales or for different questions while encoding the same underlying geometry. Compare and cross-map them before collapsing alternatives.
