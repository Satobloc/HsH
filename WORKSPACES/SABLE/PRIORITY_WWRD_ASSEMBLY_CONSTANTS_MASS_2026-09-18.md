## A. WWRD justification for rarity of stable tetra+ quarks, and relation to assembly theory

Clarify the SAT/H(s)H / WWRD account of why stable tetraquark-and-higher multiquark configurations may be **structurally stable once correctly woven or assembled**, while still being **extremely unlikely to arise by chance in the proper configuration**.

The central distinction to preserve is:

> **stability conditional on successful assembly** is not the same thing as **high spontaneous formation probability**.

The intended reconstruction target is therefore not merely `tetra+ quarks are unstable`. Instead, recover and formalize the hypothesis that the correct woven configuration may have a deep or persistent stability basin, but that the measure/probability of dynamically landing in that basin through ordinary stochastic/high-energy formation pathways may be very small.

### Required source recovery

Search GLASS, the 2023–25 archive, HsH, NotebookLM extractions, podcast/source packets, and solver/theory notes for:

- `WWRD` and expansions/definitions of that acronym or named mechanism;
- `woven`, `weave`, `braid`, `multiquark`, `tetraquark`, `pentaquark`, `hexaquark`, `exotic hadron`, `hadron assembly`, `quark braid`, `stable braid`, `metastable`, `formation probability`, `configuration space`, `topological basin`, `reconnection`, and adjacent terminology;
- any explicit argument distinguishing energetic stability from assembly likelihood;
- diagrams or combinatorics showing the required ordering, handedness, crossing pattern, phase, or reconnection sequence;
- any earlier attempt to explain why ordinary 3-quark baryon structures are easy/common relative to tetra+ woven structures.

Preserve exact provenance and distinguish Nathan-direct formulations, assistant-originated GLASS hypotheses, current interpretations, and superseded variants.

### Formalization target

Where possible, turn the verbal hypothesis into a quantitative or at least mathematically explicit assembly problem. Candidate approaches include:

- configuration-space volume / measure of admissible stable braid states;
- number/fraction of random assembly trajectories that reach an allowed woven topology;
- kinetic barriers and reconnection sequence constraints;
- topological accessibility versus energetic depth;
- phase/handedness/orientation matching requirements;
- combinatorial suppression as constituent count rises;
- basin-of-attraction size under a specified dynamical rule;
- lifetime after formation versus formation cross-section/probability.

The key deliverable should make it possible to say separately:

1. whether a correctly assembled tetra+/multiquark configuration is stable or metastable under the candidate H(s)H dynamics;
2. how hard it is to assemble that configuration from ordinary initial conditions;
3. whether the predicted rarity is compatible with the observed pattern of exotic-hadron production and decay, without smuggling those observations into the mechanism.

### Intersection with assembly theory

Determine carefully what, if anything, should be borrowed from **assembly theory** as a mathematical/organizational framework.

Do not assume identity merely because both use the language of assembly. Ask instead:

- Can assembly-theory measures such as path length, construction depth, combinatorial accessibility, or related complexity notions help quantify the rarity of reaching a stable woven state?
- Does assembly theory provide a useful description of **historical/path-dependent construction constraints** that ordinary energy-minimization arguments miss?
- Can WWRD supply a physically/geometrically specific version of an assembly process, while assembly theory supplies a formal complexity/rarity language?
- Which parts of assembly theory require ontological or dynamical assumptions not licensed by H(s)H?
- Is the useful intersection exact, analogical, or merely methodological?

Treat this as a toolbox-formalism test. Do not import assembly theory wholesale or make it SAT ontology unless the source and mathematics warrant it.

### Preferred outcome

A strong result would be a two-axis model:

- **post-assembly stability** of a topology;
- **pre-assembly accessibility / formation probability** of that topology.

That would directly clarify why a deliberately/optimally woven tetra+ state might be robust even if naturally occurring correctly woven states are rare.

## B. Projection constant, proton–electron mass ratio, mass spectrum, and constant/scaling dependency graph

Treat reconstruction and verification of the SAT/H(s)H quantitative constant-and-mass machinery as a separate high-priority formal program.

Nathan's current instruction is that these calculations have been worked out in substantial detail in parts of the corpus, but the detailed derivations have been obscured or swamped by later oversimplifications. Therefore **do not begin from simplified podcast descriptions or summary equations and call the job done**. Recover the most canonical and technically complete derivations available.

### 1. Projection constant

Prioritize:

- identifying the canonical definition and symbol history of the projection constant;
- locating the earliest and strongest derivations;
- determining whether variants such as `B`, `3/(4π)`, projection angle/ratio, packing-related descriptions, or other constants are actually the same object or historically conflated objects;
- checking dimensions, normalization, domain, geometry, and assumptions;
- separating exact derivation from mnemonic/geometric analogy;
- distinguishing a true invariant from a fitted/tuned or context-dependent quantity.

Do **not** assume that every occurrence of `3/(4π)`, `~0.2387`, `B`, `v_crit/c`, or related numerical value denotes the same mathematical object. Build a collision-safe constant registry and prove identities where they are claimed.

### 2. Proton–electron mass-ratio calculation

Recover the canonical SAT/H(s)H derivation of the proton–electron mass ratio, including:

- exact equations and intermediate steps;
- which candidate Lagrangian or action is being used;
- definitions of electron and proton geometry in that derivation;
- normalization/scaling assumptions;
- role of the projection constant or other constants;
- whether the result is exact, approximate, asymptotic, or fitted;
- which inputs are empirical and which are derived;
- units and dimensional checks;
- chronology of corrections or alternate derivations.

Explicitly compare the technically complete derivation against simplified public retellings and identify where summaries have dropped essential assumptions or steps.

### 3. Mass spectrum calculations

Recover and verify all serious SAT/H(s)H attempts to derive or predict a particle mass spectrum.

For each calculation, record:

- target particles/states;
- geometric state assignment;
- equations/Lagrangian used;
- scale order and recursion rule;
- constants and coefficients used;
- whether those constants are independently derived or inherited from another calculation;
- fit inputs, if any;
- predicted values and residuals;
- stated precision and actual numerical precision;
- later corrections/supersession.

Do not merge distinct mass-spectrum programs merely because they share some constants or scaling language.

### 4. Candidate Lagrangians and scaling relationships

Treat the various SAT/H(s)H candidate Lagrangians as key reconstruction nodes for the constants/mass program.

The goal is to determine which quantitative relationships are consequences of:

- curvature/torsion terms;
- worldline versus worldtube formulations;
- recursive/superhelical scale relations;
- material/kinematic parity or related constraints;
- projection/intersection geometry;
- braid/topological complexity;
- timesheet interaction/back-reaction;
- normalization conventions;
- scale factors such as Ξ / 1.84 or other historical scaling pivots;
- any other candidate terms actually present in source.

Build a dependency graph rather than a flat glossary:

`assumption / geometry → Lagrangian term → scaling relation → constant → mass or observable`.

This should make circular derivations, duplicated constants, hidden fitted inputs, and genuine derived invariants visible.

### 5. Overall constants relationship map

Create a collision-safe crosswalk for all major constants/coefficients/angles/scales that have appeared in SAT/H(s)H.

For each object record:

- symbol;
- name;
- units/dimension;
- numerical value/range;
- source/date;
- definition;
- derivation;
- dependencies;
- equations where used;
- whether exact, derived, fitted, approximate, mnemonic, or shorthand;
- historical renamings;
- confirmed identities versus merely similar numbers;
- current status.

Give special attention to quantities numerically near one another or repeatedly collapsed in summaries, including projection constants, holonomy/glitch angles, velocity ratios, scale factors, and any reused symbols across different source traditions.

### 6. Verification standard

The preferred output is not a polished narrative first. It is a reproducible derivation package:

1. canonical source location(s);
2. definitions and assumptions;
3. complete symbolic derivation;
4. dimensional audit;
5. numerical reproduction;
6. independent recalculation where possible;
7. sensitivity to normalization/scaling choices;
8. comparison with alternate derivations;
9. explicit statement of what is genuinely derived versus assumed/fitted;
10. current epistemic/theory status.

Where a derivation fails, preserve the failure precisely rather than treating it as evidence that all historical attempts failed. Where multiple derivations succeed, keep them distinct until equivalence is demonstrated.
