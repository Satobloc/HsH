## 2. Minimal Kerr-ring trace without borrowing Kerr metric as a premise

**STD geometry in explicitly selected flat Minkowski coordinates** \((ct,x,y,z)\) with signature \((-+++)\); local symbols \`a_K\` = ring/rotation radius (L); \`eps_c\` = cross-sectional core radius (L), **not automatically \`a_K\`**; \(\lambda=ct\) (L); angles \(\phi,\chi\) dimensionless.

A candidate time-extended *ring locus* has a 2D history
\[
X_0^\mu(\lambda,\phi)=(\lambda,a_K\cos\phi,a_K\sin\phi,0),\quad 0\le\phi<2\pi .
\tag{1}
\]
The induced worldsheet metric is \(h=\mathrm{diag}(-1,a_K^2)\); topology is \(S^1\times\mathbb R\). Two independent spacelike normals exist at each point: \(n_r=(0,\cos\phi,\sin\phi,0)\), \(n_z=(0,0,0,1)\). A literal finite tubular neighborhood around the ring is
\[
X^\mu(\lambda,\phi,\rho,\chi)=X_0^\mu+
\rho[\cos\chi\, n_r^\mu+\sin\chi\,n_z^\mu],\quad
0\le\rho\le\epsilon_c<a_K .
\tag{2}
\]
Each fixed \(\lambda\) intersection is a **solid torus**, with 3-volume
\[
V_3=2\pi^2a_K\epsilon_c^2.
\tag{3}
\]
This construction shows what the conjectured "intersectional trace" *could* mean in a local flat control. **It does not produce an Einstein–Rosen bridge, an Einstein metric, a singularity, a spinor, charge, or a string-theory action.** An ER bridge requires a separately declared spacetime metric/topology and boundary/gluing rules; ordinary Minkowski metric and a nontrivial classical Kerr curvature cannot be asserted to be the same physical metric without an explicit map. An ordinary positive-tension free string-like circular loop has inward curvature \(1/a_K\), so without additional constitutive support it is not statically stabilized simply by being a ring.

For Kerr parameter \`a_K=J/(mc)\`, the electron-like standard Kerr control is over-extreme, \`GM/(c²a_K)~3.5e-45\`: an ordinary Kerr horizon/ergosphere cannot be imported literally. The ring rotation scale, actual material core thickness, and observed charge form-factor radius are distinct until derived.

**Fermionic exclusion gate:** a ring trace or hard contact prohibits overlap only if a mechanical repulsion is additionally specified. Pauli exclusion is quantum exchange antisymmetry. For two identical particles in three spatial dimensions, exchange has two 1D representations \(\pm1\); geometry alone does not choose the fermionic \(-1\). It changes geometry on *both sides* of \(\lambda=0\), but as a block boundary-value solution this is **not itself a backward-in-time signal**. This is an independent toy of interbraid/transtemporal constraints, not electromagnetic field propagation.

\]
