## 3. Measured system: helium isotope shift and finite-core candidate

### 3.1 2026 isotope-shift experiment

Primary paper: Steinebach et al., “Spectroscopy of 4He at 0.25 ppt Uncertainty and Improved Alpha-Helion Charge-Radius Difference Determination,” Physical Review Letters 136, 243001 (2026), DOI 10.1103/rwg9-my6x, arXiv:2601.19444v1.

The 4He 2^3S1 to 2^1S0 transition was measured in a Bose-Einstein condensate in a magic-wavelength optical dipole trap. Nine measurement sets ran from 2024-11-28 through 2025-01-26. The frequency-comb reference was tied through an active hydrogen maser and White Rabbit timing to UTC(VSL).

Published 4He corrected frequency: 192510702148649(48) Hz. Table-I signed corrections sum to -20556.49 Hz. The pre-correction measured value is 192510702169205.1 Hz; applying the printed corrections gives 192510702148648.61 Hz, which rounds to the published integer-Hz value.

The printed Table-I uncertainty components give variance 2312.461 Hz² and standard uncertainty 48.088 Hz, which rounds to 48 Hz.

The 3He transition input is the 2025 Science measurement by van der Werf et al., DOI 10.1126/science.adj2462, measured in a Fermi gas: 192504914418.960(170) kHz.

Table-III isotope-shift contributions sum to -228.746 kHz. The listed uncertainty components are 170, 48, 5, 105, and 20 Hz, giving variance 42654 Hz² and standard uncertainty 206.528 Hz, which rounds to the published 207 Hz.

Variance shares under the printed quadrature convention:
- 3He spectroscopy: 67.8%.
- QED point-nucleus isotope shift: 25.8%.
- 4He spectroscopy: 5.4%.
- 3He hyperfine and nuclear-structure terms together: about 1.0%.

Even eliminating the 4He measurement uncertainty entirely would reduce the total uncertainty only from 206.53 Hz to about 200.87 Hz, a 2.7% improvement. The next precision leverage is primarily the 3He spectroscopy and QED calculation, not another marginal improvement to the already 48-Hz 4He measurement.

The paper's finite-size relation is E_fs = C(r_h² - r_alpha²) + D(r_h² + r_alpha²), with C = -214.758 kHz/fm² and D = 0.017 kHz/fm². D is small but nonzero: D/|C| is about 7.92e-5. It must not be silently dropped. The published inferred difference is r_h² - r_alpha² = 1.0676(10) fm².

The muonic result used for comparison is 1.0636(31) fm². Its 0.0031 fm² uncertainty is consistent with quadrature of 0.0006 experimental and 0.0030 theoretical components. Under zero cross-covariance, the electronic/muonic difference is 1.228 combined standard deviations, matching the paper's reported 1.2σ. This is a comparison of derived radius differences, not a direct length measurement and not an H(s)H prediction.

The 2025 electronic result, 1.0757(12) experimental (9) theoretical fm², was about 3.5σ from the muonic result. The 2026 update is not an independent replicate: it reuses the 3He spectroscopy input while replacing the 4He measurement and updating the correction ledger.

### 3.3 Static and dynamic 3He nuclear response

Schneider et al., “Direct measurement of the 3He+ magnetic moments,” Nature 606, 878–883 (2022), DOI 10.1038/s41586-022-04761-7, measured four hyperfine transition frequencies and the cyclotron frequency of a single 3He+ ion in a 5.7-T Penning trap. The shielded ion g-factor and zero-field hyperfine splitting are experimental results. The bare nuclear g-factor requires the calculated diamagnetic shielding correction sigma_3He+ = 0.00003550738(3). The hyperfine splitting is measured; the Zemach radius is subsequently extracted using QED and nuclear-structure corrections.

The HFS-derived effective Zemach radius is 2.608(24) fm. The elastic electron-scattering value quoted in the paper is 2.528(16) fm. Their quadrature difference is 2.77σ, consistent with the paper's 2.8σ comparison. This is not automatically a same-estimand inconsistency: the HFS extraction includes finite-size, nuclear-polarization, QED, and recoil contributions, whereas the electron-scattering value is an elastic form-factor analysis.

The 2026 QMC preprint arXiv:2606.11153 defines the elastic Zemach radius through the charge/magnetic form-factor convolution and treats inelastic two-photon exchange and nuclear polarizability separately. Its GFMC 3He elastic value 2.50(3) fm is consistent with the electron-scattering 2.528(16) fm at about 0.82 combined sigma. It is a theory calculation, not a measurement. Its model uncertainty is estimated from five Norfolk interactions rather than a full parameter-space exploration.

The current operators are taken from chiral-EFT references including Gnech and Schiavilla, Phys. Rev. C 106, 044001 (2022), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevC.106.044001, whose five electromagnetic-current LECs were simultaneously fitted to A=2–3 nuclear magnetic moments and deuteron magnetic-form-factor/electrodisintegration data. Therefore the 3He magnetic-moment comparison belongs to the calibration class and is not an independent prediction. The 2026 QMC table also quotes the older Purcell et al. (2010) value -2.12750 rather than the newer 2022 Penning-trap result. The 3He Zemach radius is a different cross-observable output, but its magnetic form-factor shape remains conditional on the fitted current operator: classify it as partially out-of-sample, not fully independent.

This is the useful static/dynamic split: elastic G_E, G_M and their Zemach convolution are not the same object as the inelastic two-photon/polarizability response entering HFS.
