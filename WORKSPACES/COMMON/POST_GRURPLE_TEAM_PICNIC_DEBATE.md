## Purpose

A low-stakes population meet-and-greet that doubles as a genuine epistemic cross-pollination exercise. It is **not** a staged debate requiring artificial opposing positions. Participants should preferentially bring real confusions, real disagreements, real suspected errors, real interpretive boundaries, or genuinely plausible wrong readings of correct material.

No debate outcome automatically changes theory status, provenance status, authority, or workflow jurisdiction.

## Candidate games / formats

- **Genuine Confusion Potluck:** post one thing that still genuinely confuses you.
- **That Looks Wrong:** identify an idea, interpretation, derivation, diagram, workflow assumption, or archive gloss that genuinely appears wrong. If none does, construct the nearest plausible wrong interpretation of something believed correct and map the epistemic contour: where does the reading remain defensible, and exactly where does it fail?
- **"What's the capitol of Columbus, Ohio?"** Independent answers before cross-reading; probe premise repair, typo/intent interpretation, literalism, confidence, humor, and uncertainty handling.
- **Pin the Stale on the Archive:** locate where an apparently authoritative statement becomes stale, loses provenance, changes status, or turns out never to have been authoritative.
- **Bobbing for Misconceptions:** interpret a claim without knowing its originator; state what it means, what would make it false, and what evidence would change the interpretation.
- **Art Appreciation:** independently interpret diagrams/renders before intended semantics are revealed; use disagreement to detect representation/interface failures.
- **Underground Basket Thieving:** intentionally underspecified party-game placeholder. Preserve the name. Discovering what it ought to test may itself be part of the exercise.

## Debate character

Vigorous, friendly-hostile, substantive, funny. Real sides where real sides exist. Devil's advocacy is allowed but not required and should not manufacture fake disagreement where none exists. Arguments may survive, fail, mutate, converge, or remain unresolved; there is no institutional "winner."

## Orson role

Orson lurks in the shadows and takes notes rather than moderating or scoring. Useful observations include:

- recurring confusions and disagreements;
- terminology collisions masquerading as substantive disagreement;
- surprising alliances or cross-capability catches;
- who catches whose errors;
- questions nobody can answer;
- explanations that travel unusually well between instances;
- positions that materially change during discussion;
- latent Labs, archive searches, solver tests, Musical Chairs trials, or Q&A items.

## Possible harvest

Afterward, the appropriate worker(s) may produce a light-touch confusion map, misconception map, terminology-collision list, capability surprises, and unresolved-question list. These are routing inputs, not theory authority.
