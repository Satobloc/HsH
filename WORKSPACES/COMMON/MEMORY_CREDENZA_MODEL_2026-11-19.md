# The Memory Credenza: a compact model for continuity, attention, and return

**Date:** 2026-11-19  
**Status:** Experimental, transferable design model. This is a navigation and workflow metaphor, not a claim that a universal architecture or automated sampler is already implemented.

## The three scales

The **Key** is the tiny continuity kernel or boot capsule: identity/role where useful, current remit, re-entry instruction, immediate place marker, and a pointer to the Credenza. It must remain small enough to be carried reliably between finite contexts. It is not an encyclopedia.

The **Memory Credenza** is the compact, adaptive control model of the larger memory environment. It keeps a few useful routes, access cues, statuses, and return points close at hand. Its compartments may be added, divided, combined, renamed, or retired as the work teaches us what is useful. A cubby is an access point with a job, not necessarily a folder or a place where all its material is stored.

The **Castle** is the full repository-backed environment: source records, histories, databases, project modules, tools, scripts, methods, workflows, and the deeper structures to which the Credenza points. The Credenza models how to navigate and maintain the Castle; it does not duplicate the Castle.

## The Credenza's working surface

Keep the visible surface compact:

- **Compass:** purpose, boundaries, and controlling priorities.
- **Atlas:** principal projects, branches, repositories, and cross-links.
- **Work tray:** the current `PIN | NOW | NEXT | RETURN`.
- **Watchtower:** pending changes, dependencies, overdue review, and neglected areas.

Potential cubbies include: sources/evidence and provenance; questions and competing hypotheses; findings and decisions; tools and instruments; methods and quality controls; entities and cross-branch connections; neglected or underexplored areas; experiments and capability development; and deferred, superseded, or retired work.

A compartment card should contain only what helps someone decide whether and how to open it: **purpose, route, open-when trigger, status, last meaningful inspection, and return cue**. Add fields such as owner, source version/hash, coverage due, or review stratum only where they support a real operation. Deep instructions remain in linked modules and are loaded only when needed.

## How the tick and tock fit

The Credenza gives the attention/continuity rhythm a navigable place to point. Keep the meanings distinct:

- **Tick:** the small, repeatable place marker. It makes the active position and return route visible, typically as `PIN | NOW | NEXT | RETURN`. It is a breadcrumb, not a work-completion claim.
- **Tock:** a completed bounded operation that has produced a durable, recorded result. Advance an operation counter only after the result and its next cursor are recorded; a chat turn or tool call alone is not a tock.
- **Heartbeat:** a project-specific signal of meaningful human-directed activity or contact. It can inform when a dormant workflow may need attention, but it is not proof that substantive work occurred.
- **Attention cycle:** an optional rotation across different parts of the Credenza. It may advance on completed operations, turns, calendar triggers, or another explicit rule. These mechanisms are not interchangeable, and different workflows may need different rhythms or no ticker at all.

The active drawer should not monopolize attention.

**Renderer-safe grouped signature markers:** Where a message-level TICK/TOCK signature uses grouped scope markers, write them in square brackets (for example, `[bh]` and `[mbx]`), not literal curly braces. In renderer-backed assistant messages, `{...}` may be interpreted as an expression and disappear from the displayed text. Preserve the full signature exactly, including every grouped marker and its order. This is a display-compatibility convention only; it does not change token meanings or workflow semantics. The pin keeps the immediate task recoverable; the wider Credenza keeps other branches, tools, methods, and source collections visible enough to be rediscovered.

## The neglect sampler

A future sampler may help detect systematic inattention by combining two selection modes:

1. **Coverage rotation:** surface compartments, source strata, questions, or capabilities whose meaningful-inspection date or coverage state indicates that they are due.
2. **Stratified random sampling:** select from a defined inventory across branches, source types, dates, geographies, entities, methods, or other useful strata, including an explicit stratum for inventory that is incomplete or poorly understood.

A sample is an invitation to inspect, not a finding. Inspect the actual source or artifact, record exactly what was examined and how far, preserve provenance and version/hash, distinguish unexamined from unreadable or absent, and route the result to a bounded operation only when warranted. A null result or no-op may be useful when recorded accurately. Random selection does not establish importance, and a sampler cannot prove completeness.

For auditability, retain the sampling frame/version, selection method and seed where practical, item selected, selection reason, inspection extent, result, action taken, and next review cue. Keep the process inspectable, interruptible, and manually overridable. Evaluate useful discoveries and durable state changes against missed work, duplication, false triggers, source-cursor errors, drift, and overhead. Use coverage debt as a prompt for attention, not as a measure of evidentiary importance.

## The caretaker and adoption path

A lightweight caretaker periodically inspects the Credenza itself: broken or stale pointers, drawers with unclear purposes, important new tools or modules not yet routed, neglected branches, duplication, missing return cues, sampling-frame bias, and maintenance overhead. It may recommend changes; it should not silently change priorities, conclusions, ownership, or schedules.

A sensible build sequence is: (1) create a small registry and human-readable router; (2) connect existing project maps, queues, checkpoints, evidence catalogs, and capability guides; (3) test the cards and return cues during ordinary work; (4) build a sampler only when the inventory is sufficiently structured; and (5) automate only after a bounded pilot demonstrates useful state change. No new automation or schedule is authorized by this design note.

This is a model to experiment with, compare, and revise. Preserve workflow-specific variation. Promote components that improve orientation, rediscovery, coverage, and recovery after interruption; simplify or retire those that add ceremony without useful results.
