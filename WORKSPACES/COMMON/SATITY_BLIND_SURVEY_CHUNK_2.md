## Additional blind observations

A. Professional physics can legitimately argue that a coarse or scalar observable loses physically relevant structure while a richer representation retains it. This is explicit in the CP/magic paper and in the Schrödinger-vs-Heisenberg divisibility source from Chunk 1.

B. Professional physics routinely uses geometry that sounds highly speculative in popular language — wormholes, islands, holography, de Sitter horizons, emergent CFT spectra, quantum-corrected geodesics — without those concepts being fringe markers. What distinguishes the work is mathematical typing, relation to established frameworks, calculable bounds, and explicit scope.

C. Multiple representations/derivation routes are treated as a strength only where their physical equivalence or relationship is controlled. The q-desic paper derives the same construction through Lagrangian and Hamiltonian methods; the divisibility paper shows that even equivalent physical pictures can expose inequivalent secondary structures.

D. Professional theory frequently seeks a better map of already-accepted physics rather than rejecting accepted physics. `q-desics` modify the geometric description of motion while explicitly using GR as the classical anchor; wormhole/teleportation work uses established semiclassical/QFT/GR machinery rather than declaring those theories false.

## Important negative calibration

The following are **not** legitimate reasons to classify a programme as fringe based on this corpus:

- emergent structure;
- nontrivial topology/geometry;
- wormholes or horizons;
- holography/islands;
- alternate geometric representations;
- quantum-gravity corrections;
- hidden information in orientation/frame structure;
- lattice/discrete computational representations;
- challenging a standard explanatory assumption;
- trying to recover known physics from a deeper structure.

Those all occur inside normal professional physics. The discriminators must instead be evidentiary discipline, correct recovery of established limits, source/literature contact, technical consistency, independent checking, and empirical/operational constraint.
