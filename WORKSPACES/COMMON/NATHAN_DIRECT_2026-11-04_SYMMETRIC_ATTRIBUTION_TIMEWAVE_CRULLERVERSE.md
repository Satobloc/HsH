## 1. Symmetric assignment as a general principle

Nathan's clarification generalizes the default of even assignment beyond force or a two-term equation. It applies to equations, rotations, symmetry operations, chosen viewing conventions, shapes, properties, actions, and basic proportionality assumptions.

When a feature arises through interaction among two or more structures, and there is no known or suspected reason to regard one participant as preferentially instrumental or as the privileged carrier of structural information, assign the feature symmetrically by default. For two equivalent participants this means an even split; for a larger symmetry-equivalent set, use an equal assignment across that set.

This is a provisional assignment and a choice of representation, not an empirical claim that nature must divide the underlying physical contribution equally. Reassignment toward one participant or a nonuniform distribution is permitted when justified by evidence, theory, topology, or computational convenience. A computational convenience must not be reported as a physical asymmetry; an assumed equivalence must not be reported as a demonstrated equivalence.

The default is context-sensitive:
- If there is no reason to privilege a participant or direction, use symmetric attribution.
- If a distribution is unknown and no structural asymmetry is indicated, equidistribution is the Occam default.
- If the geometry, topology, dynamics, or evidence implies a preferred distribution, follow that structure rather than imposing equality.
- If a different representation is computationally easier, use it transparently without converting it into an empirical statement.

Nathan identifies this as a restatement and generalization of Forest Normalization. His additional conceptual clarification is that a viewing convention along a maximally symmetrical axis is an example of the same methodological idea. The authoritative original source and exact mathematical definition of Forest Normalization remain to be recovered; do not silently identify this clarification with the separate Forest Lab tangent-normalization candidate or its stipulated two-sided factor.

## 2. Reciprocal force attribution

Nathan extends the same interaction-centered reasoning to structures described as exerting and experiencing force: absent a justified asymmetry, exertion and experience should be treated as reciprocal aspects of one interaction, with equal-and-opposite attribution in the applicable formulation.

This is a proposed generalization to abstract structural constructions, not a completed theorem for all field, extended-body, or nonlocal interactions. Formalization must distinguish Newton's first-law inertia statement from the third-law reciprocal-force relation, and must account for momentum carried by fields or other mediating structures where relevant.

## 3. Contextual symmetry, chirality, and Crullerverse

Nathan's current tentative Crullerverse question is not that all cosmologies should produce equal matter/antimatter or equal handedness. The expected distribution depends on the generating geometry.

- Infinite-universe or nonrotating spherical-universe models may motivate an initial expectation of equidistribution.
- A twisted-torus Crullerverse may instead generatively favor a dominant, potentially monochiral orientation.
- Under the SAT hypothesis that matter is defined by the helicity of worldtubes, the universe's dominant sinestral chirality and apparent matter excess may be related consequences of the topology and the way worldtubes/ER bridges are produced and distributed.
- In that case, the existence of any minority-handed matter or reversal of bulk rotational symmetry is the feature requiring explanation, not an assumed equal split.
- Candidate higher-order mechanisms include worldtube length, repeated windings and crossings, accumulated twist, or a closure/snap-back process that takes up slack. These are exploratory possibilities, not established mechanisms.

The terms “isochiral,” “monochiral,” and “sub-euchirality” are retained as Nathan's exploratory vocabulary; their mathematical definitions and relation to measurable chirality observables remain open. The Crullerverse should eventually make definite generation/distribution predictions, but none are supplied by this record.
