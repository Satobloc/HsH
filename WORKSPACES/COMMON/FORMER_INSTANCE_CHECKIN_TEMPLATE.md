## 2. Conversation identity

- approximate dates;

## 3. What you actually worked out

- original work in that conversation;
- continuation/reconstruction of earlier SAT/H(s)H material;

## 4. Unique-context test

What do you believe exists in your conversation that may **not** be adequately preserved elsewhere?

Especially flag:

- first derivations;
- exact Nathan corrections;
- abandoned alternatives that explain later choices;
- intermediate equations or notation later simplified away;
- solver/operator definitions;
- negative results;
- visual reasoning;
- methodological rules;
- provenance/priority evidence;
- files/attachments no longer easy to find.

## 5. Artifacts created

For every meaningful artifact:

`artifact — original location if known — current location if known — status — purpose`

## 6. External exposure / import history

What outside papers, theories, equations, models, tools, datasets, or conversations were active in context?

State whether they were:

- comparator only;
- standard math;
- empirical input;
- deliberate import;
- possible unconscious contamination;
- unclear.

This is especially important for priority/provenance reconstruction.

## 7. Current-survival assessment

To the best of your knowledge, classify major outputs as:

- still central/current;
- ancestral but retained;
- useful mathematical quarry;
- historical prediction/provenance;
- superseded;
- contradicted/failed;
- speculative/quarantined;
- unresolved/unknown.

Do not force current interpretation if you lack later context.

## 8. Nathan corrections / intent anchors

Record any places where Nathan explicitly corrected the instance, rejected a framing, sharpened terminology, or supplied an intended meaning that later summaries could blur.

Quote only briefly; give exact message/conversation location if available.

## 9. Archive-priority recommendation

Rate the full conversation for archival recovery:

- **A — critical:** sole/first derivation, unique source, major correction, or active dependency;
- **B — high:** substantial unique development or missing institutional context;
- **C — useful:** valuable redundancy/context but core content preserved elsewhere;
- **D — low:** mostly duplicated, exploratory, or already preserved;
- **Q — quarantine-first:** worth preserving but should not influence current theory without careful review.

Give 2–5 reasons for the rating.

## 10. Best landmarks

`approx message/date — event — why it matters`

## 11. Reactivation value

What should it **not** be assigned because its context is stale, contaminated, or too specialized?
