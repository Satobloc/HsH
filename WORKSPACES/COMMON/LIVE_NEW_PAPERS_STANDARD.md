# Live New Papers — Rapid Sandbox Paper Standard

**Status:** CURRENT / NATHAN-DIRECTED PUBLICATION WORKFLOW  
**Established:** 2026-09-21  
**Scope:** SAT/H(s)H project-wide; sandbox publication only  
**Public destination:** Glass Sausage Factory — Live New Papers

## Purpose

Create short-cycle, physicist-legible LaTeX papers from live project work without turning paper production into a competing research lane. The workflow is intentionally fast, adversarial enough to improve the work, and visibly provisional.

A paper entering this process is **not** promoted to established theory, formal publication, or external peer-reviewed status merely by passing this workflow.

## Admissible paper classes

Every paper must be one of two classes.

### A. Physicist-facing

The paper begins from a recognizable physics, mathematics, experimental, methodological, or field-specific question and is written primarily for readers in the relevant field.

Requirements:

- maximize standard disciplinary readability, notation, terminology, and relevance;
- make the paper useful/intelligible to a competent reader who has never encountered SAT/H(s)H;
- introduce SAT/H(s)H, ᚼ, or project-specific machinery only where it materially serves the field-facing question;
- clearly distinguish established physics/mathematics, external evidence, project construction, conjecture, and sandbox result;
- do **not** use a general physics topic merely as a wrapper for a paper whose real purpose is introducing SAT/H(s)H.

A substantial SAT/H(s)H contribution is allowed. The governing test is whether the paper remains genuinely *about the stated field-facing problem*.

### B. SAT/H(s)H-building / defining

The paper's legitimate purpose is to construct, define, formalize, type, benchmark, or delimit the framework itself. Examples include definitions and object typing; solver/interface architecture; ᚼ standards and transformations; formal correspondences and explicit derivations; notation and dimensional conventions; benchmark definitions; representation/readout distinctions; and no-go, boundary, or failure-condition results.

Framework-building papers must still use standard mathematical/physical language wherever possible and clearly mark unresolved or project-specific assumptions.

## Terminology

In ordinary running prose use **ᚼ**, optionally “ᚼ (Hagalaz)” where naming aids orientation. Latin “Hagalaz” may remain in titles, filenames, compatibility/search keys, historical quotations, or where the current Meridian naming standard requires it. This is an interim usage rule pending Meridian's fuller standard.

## Six-hour maximum cycle

From Nathan's instigation/approval to sandbox posting, target elapsed time is **no more than six hours** unless Nathan explicitly pauses or extends the paper.

Default sequence:

1. **INSTIGATED** — Nathan authorizes the paper and identifies or accepts an Originator. For now, Nathan instigates papers.
2. **ORIGINATING** — Originator writes the first LaTeX draft and supplies sources/status boundaries.
3. **PEER REVIEW** — each currently active instance gets one bounded review turn.
4. **REVISION** — the original author dispositions comments and rewrites.
5. **SANDBOX POSTED** — revised paper enters the Live New Papers box/site with explicit sandbox status.

The clock is a maximum-turnaround control, not permission to skip provenance, mathematical typing, source checks, or unresolved-status labels.

## One-turn peer-review rule

Normal work should not be interrupted for more than **one turn per active reviewer per paper**. A reviewer should return the highest-value comments in that one turn rather than beginning an open-ended dialogue. A second review round occurs **only if Nathan or the Originator explicitly requests it**.

Silence or inability to review inside the window should be recorded; it does not automatically block sandbox posting unless the missing review is necessary for a known provenance, mathematical, or field-competence dependency.

Reviewers should preferentially inspect correctness/type consistency; unsupported inference or claim-strength drift; standard field notation/readability; provenance/source adequacy; established-vs-project-specific distinctions; hidden parameter freedom/target leakage; failure conditions/limitations; paper-class fit; and whether a physicist-facing paper is actually field-facing rather than SAT exposition in disguise.

Reviews are comments, not votes and not theory authority.

## Originator revision ledger

The Originator records material comments as **ACCEPTED**, **ACCEPTED WITH MODIFICATION**, **DECLINED — REASON**, or **UNRESOLVED / DISCLOSED**. Unanimity is not required. Material unresolved objections that survive revision should remain visible in the sandbox record where appropriate.

## Sandbox publication contract

Every Live New Paper must be visibly marked:

**SANDBOXED — RAPID INTERNAL PEER REVIEW — NOT FORMALLY PUBLISHED**

A visible watermark is preferred on rendered PDF/page output where technically practical.

The public record should carry title; paper class; Originator; date/time/version; LaTeX/PDF or rendered source; review participants; review/revision status; public-safe source/provenance pointers; limitations/unresolved items; and explicit sandbox status.

Internal rapid review must never be described as conventional external peer review.

## Paper proposals

Each active instance should eventually have a persistent **document icon** affordance at the bottom of its posts. The icon proposes a paper; it does not authorize one.

A proposal should capture tentative title/problem; paper class; target field/audience; field-facing question where applicable; why it merits paper treatment; relevant artifacts/sources; proposed author if known; and optional flag **I MAY OR MAY NOT BE THE RIGHT AUTHOR**.

Until Nathan approves, proposal state remains non-authorizing. Other workers may enrich, pick up, or advance an overlooked proposal to Nathan while preserving origin and authorship history.

Commons proposal states:

**PROPOSED → ENRICHED → ADVANCED_TO_NATHAN → APPROVED | DECLINED | PARKED**

Approved papers then move:

**ORIGINATING → PEER_REVIEW → REVISION → SANDBOX_POSTED**

## Workload and freshness guard

Paper review is a bounded side duty, not a new permanent lane.

- One reviewer turn by default.
- Do not stop healthy active-edge work for repeated review cycles.
- Do not create paper quotas.
- Do not make every result a paper.
- Respect worker breaks and checkpoint boundaries.
- If a worker is at a fragile computation/derivation boundary, review may wait until the next safe checkpoint within the six-hour window.
- If the paper cycle would materially damage the project's highest-priority active edge, escalate the scheduling conflict rather than silently consuming the branch.

## Project guards

- FIE anchors SAT; H(s)H inherits where supportable and may justifiably diverge.
- Numerical agreement is not derivation or validation.
- Preserve strict PRIOR_ART/private quarantine.
- Preserve provenance/currentness/authorship/status distinctions.
- Watercooler, Field Notes, Labs, Nathan-source material, and sandbox papers do not automatically become theory authority.
- Use “proof” only for an actual formal proof/complete derivation.
- Existing quarantined paper/integration artifacts remain quarantined and do not govern this workflow.

## Incorporation test

The workflow is incorporated only after a real paper completes:

**proposal/instigation → Originator draft → one-turn active-instance reviews → Originator revision → visibly sandboxed website artifact within ≤6 hours**

File existence alone is not a pass.
