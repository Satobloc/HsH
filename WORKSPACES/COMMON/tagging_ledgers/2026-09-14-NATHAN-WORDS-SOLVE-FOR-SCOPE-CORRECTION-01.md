## Scope correction

Earlier review ledgers used family-level shorthand including:

- `BLINDED-EQUATION-TEST`
- `PROVENANCE-STRIPPED-BY-DESIGN`
- `NO-SEMANTIC-INFERENCE`
- `WINNOW:NOT-THEORY-LINEAGE-SOURCE`

Those strings remain part of the historical review record, but future readers should interpret them narrowly:

- They record Nathan's stated purpose for the **reviewed Solve-for test conversations / reviewed test material**.
- They do **not** establish that every document sharing a title pattern, algebraic form, variable name, or neighboring archive location has the same purpose.
- They do **not** authorize ignoring a conversation if other material or a later explicit source bridge gives it additional provenance value.
- `NO-SEMANTIC-INFERENCE` means: do not reverse-engineer the deliberately withheld variable semantics **from the blinded test record itself**. It does not mean "never investigate this material again under any circumstances."
- `WINNOW:NOT-THEORY-LINEAGE-SOURCE` should be read as a review-lane disposition for the blinded test *qua blinded test*, not a permanent declaration that no relationship to theory could ever be established by independent evidence.

The preferred minimal document-purpose wording is therefore along the lines of:

> This document contains blind equation vetting.

Use stronger or broader wording only when the stronger/broader fact has itself been established.

## Context-recognition / training lesson

The larger training lesson is not "recognize Solve-for filenames." It is to ask, before classifying unusual or familiar-looking material:

- What am I looking at?
- What is the source genre and immediate purpose?
- What does raw provenance actually establish?
- What does the surrounding corpus make plausible without making it certain?
- Am I treating resemblance to SAT as identity with SAT?
- Am I treating unfamiliarity as an invitation to force the material into SAT?
- Would a direct source, Fundamental Intuitions / current orientation material, or a better-trained SAT/physics reading change the classification?

Context, scientific fluency, corpus familiarity, and active self-questioning are required complements to tags. Tags guide retrieval; they do not replace judgment.
