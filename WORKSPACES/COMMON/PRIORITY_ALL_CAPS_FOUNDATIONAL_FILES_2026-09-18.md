# HIGH-VALUE SOURCE HEURISTIC — ALL-CAPS TXT/PDF TITLES IN ORIGINAL SAT ARCHIVE

**Date:** 2026-09-18  
**Authority:** Nathan-direct archival interpretation rule  
**Status:** ACTIVE source-prioritization heuristic

## Core rule

In the old/original SAT repository, **simple ALL-CAPS `.txt` or `.pdf` filenames often indicate that Nathan regarded the document as high-priority, foundational, directive, or especially important for the then-current theory build**.

Examples explicitly identified by Nathan include:

- `GEL.txt`
- `SOME THOUGHTS.txt`
- `PROPER DIMENSIONALITY.txt`

This is a **strong archival heuristic**, not a rigid metadata law.

## Default interpretation

When an old SAT file has a simple ALL-CAPS title and is **not obviously a raw conversation/export/transcript**, raise its retrieval priority substantially.

Such a document is often one of the following:

1. a direct statement of Nathan's thinking on a specific problem;
2. a foundational or corrective doctrine document;
3. a compact synthesis intended to guide the current build at that historical moment;
4. a manually elevated note containing concepts Nathan considered especially important;
5. a Nathan-authored statement followed by appended conversation, assistant analysis, calculations, or supporting material.

Do not assume the entire file is single-author merely because the title is editorially important.

## Authorship segmentation

ALL-CAPS priority does **not** override normal provenance discipline.

For each such document, determine whether it contains:

- Nathan-only prose;
- Nathan statement followed by assistant conversation;
- pasted/generated calculations;
- mixed-source material;
- later annotations or revisions;
- a raw conversation that merely happens to have an all-caps title.

Where possible, segment the document internally and preserve authorship boundaries.

A common high-value pattern to expect is:

> **Nathan position / intuition / correction first → conversation, elaboration, or computational work afterward.**

The opening Nathan-authored section may therefore deserve distinct extraction even when the whole file is mixed.

## Priority use

Use this heuristic especially for:

- foundational-doctrine reconstruction;
- historical/current glossary reconciliation;
- scratch/from-scratch comparison;
- literal-first interpretation;
- methodology/epistemic-control recovery;
- GR↔QM reconstruction;
- dimensionality, timesheet, worldline/worldtube, mass, gravity, topology, constants, and scaling questions;
- identifying historical corrections that later summaries may have missed;
- determining what Nathan himself considered central at a particular stage.

When broad archive scans produce many possible hits, simple ALL-CAPS TXT/PDF documents should normally be promoted toward the front of the manual-reading queue.

## Important cautions

Do **not** infer automatically that an ALL-CAPS file is:

- current today;
- mathematically correct;
- official/core SAT;
- later than nearby material;
- Nathan-only throughout;
- a superseding document;
- more authoritative than explicit later Nathan corrections.

The filename is evidence of **historical emphasis / likely importance**, not proof of current status or validity.

Likewise, do not down-rank a major document merely because it lacks ALL-CAPS naming. This is a positive heuristic, not a completeness rule.

## Suggested tag

Where useful, add a source-level retrieval tag such as:

`ARCHIVE-EDITORIAL-SIGNAL:ALL-CAPS-HIGH-PRIORITY`

This tag should indicate that the file deserves elevated inspection because of Nathan's historical naming practice, not that its claims are verified or canonical.

## Preferred workflow

For each candidate ALL-CAPS TXT/PDF file:

1. record path, filename, approximate date/location in archive;
2. determine whether it is obviously conversation/raw export or an authored document;
3. inspect the opening section carefully for Nathan-direct statements;
4. segment authorship where mixed;
5. extract central propositions/corrections;
6. identify later confirmations, revisions, contradictions, or supersessions;
7. cross-link to current theory reconstruction;
8. route mathematical claims into independent audit rather than accepting self-report.

## Historical interpretation

Nathan's current archival guidance is that this naming pattern was often his way of saying, in effect:

> **this matters; this is a document to keep in view for the build.**

Use that signal when reconstructing historical SAT priorities and when deciding which old files deserve close reading first.
