## 3. Three distinct empirical architectures

### A. AI as ideation source versus post-ideation refiner

**Dong & Yakura, “Human diversity fuels collective creativity that large language models cannot simulate or sustain,” arXiv:2607.26899v1 (2026).**  
[Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26899)

The preregistered human study uses 644 writers and 351 independent evaluators in a 2×3 design. Writers produced short metaphors under human-only, AI-ideation, or AI-refinement conditions, with L1/L2 English-background groups and four prompts. The evaluators were blind to condition.

The reported pattern is a local/global trade-off: AI ideation can improve some individual creativity ratings while compressing pooled semantic diversity; AI refinement after human ideation better preserves the diversity of human-originated concepts. The authors also report that persona-conditioned LLM simulations did not reproduce the diversity of human pools. The simulation extension was not preregistered, so it is weaker evidence than the human experiment.

**Scope:** this is individual AI-assisted writing followed by pool-level analysis, not a live group interaction. It does not establish a general law about human-LLM teams.

### B. AI as a sequential search neighbor

**Li et al., “Human-AI Synergy Supports Collective Creative Search,” arXiv:2602.10001v3 (2026).**  
[Primary preprint, v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10001v3)

This is a preprint submitted to CogSci, not a peer-reviewed result. The study reports 503 human participants and 200 word-guessing games. Each game has ten rounds of ten guesses. The next round receives only the best prior guess, not a full conversation transcript. The design compares human-social, human-asocial, AI-only, and human-AI hybrid chains.

The hybrid condition has the highest reported search performance and intermediate diversity. Human participants contribute more exploratory guesses; AI agents are more exploitative, and AI performance improves when exposed to human-generated guesses. Human individual performance does not show the same improvement. The paper reports that AI-only agents exploited 99% of the time and did not adapt well to hint quality.

This is a **sequential transmission chain**, not a synchronous discussion or fully connected group. Participants were not told that some guesses might be AI-generated; the paper reports Cornell IRB approval and consent. That design choice should not be copied without independent ethics review and an explicit disclosure/debriefing plan.

**Cost/source-fidelity caution:** the methods report 165,140 Gemini API queries overall, while the condition-specific counts state 20,000 AI-only calls and 10,160 hybrid calls, totaling 30,160. The paper as inspected does not reconcile the difference. It also reports 10,020 GPT-5.1 control calls and 8,461 Claude strategy-annotation calls. No API spend is reported; these counts are not a reliable budget estimate.

### C. AI as a local node in an iterative network

**Shiiku et al., “Hybrid Human-AI Societies Balance Creativity and Diversity,” author-hosted manuscript compiled 2026-09-27, listed as under evaluation.**  
[Updated author-hosted manuscript](https://www.norijacoby.com/papers/2026%20Shiiku%20et%20al.%20Hybrid%20Human-AI%20Societies.pdf) · [author publication page](https://www.norijacoby.com/publications) · [earlier PsyArXiv DOI version](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/wzh2x_v1)

The newer author-hosted manuscript reports a 5×5 grid with periodic boundaries (25 nodes), 25 iterations, and local-neighbor story selection/modification/transmission. Its current supplementary totals are 1,473 human participants (879 story writers and 594 raters) and 8,590 AI-agent executions across main and control/validation conditions. These are repeated model executions, not 8,590 distinct model identities.

The actual hybrid main network contains 254 human and 371 AI contributions, so it is not a 50/50 realized composition. AI-only populations produce highly creative outputs but lower collective diversity and greater drift from the seed story. Human-only populations preserve more narrative continuity and diversity but have lower creativity ratings. Hybrid populations combine high creativity with diversity comparable to human-only populations. The final hybrid-versus-human-only diversity difference is not statistically significant (difference 0.024; 95% CI [-0.011, 0.059]; p=0.176). Within hybrid populations, both human and AI outputs are more diverse than their homogeneous-condition counterparts.

The manuscript says participants were not informed whether stories they encountered were human- or AI-generated, while also reporting ethics approval and informed consent. It limits interaction primarily to artifact transmission/modification, without direct communication or explicit collective goals. Data and code are stated to become available upon publication.

**Version control:** the earlier PsyArXiv v1/SciSpace record carries older totals (1,363 humans and 7,865 AI agents). The newer author-hosted manuscript reports 1,473 and 8,590 and supplies expanded experiment/control accounting. Keep these version-specific counts separate; do not silently merge them.

## 4. Cross-reading synthesis: role × topology × timescale × objective

The studies do not support one scalar claim that “AI increases” or “AI decreases” collective creativity. They examine different positions of AI in an information pipeline:

| Regime | AI role | Interaction structure | Primary distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thread conditioning | Prior context | Isolated conversation histories | Later response may depend on history composition |
| AI-assisted writing | Ideator or refiner | Independent writers; pooled outputs | Individual ratings can rise while collective diversity falls |
| Sequential search | Neighbor/advisor | Linear chain; best signal passed forward | Exploration/exploitation complementarity can improve search |
| Networked transmission | Local generator/editor | 5×5 graph; neighbor artifact selection | Heterogeneous agents can alter temporal diversity and continuity |
| Group facilitation/discussion | Process facilitator or conversational participant | Already reviewed in GRAIL/TuringHotel work | Not repeated in this activation |

A plausible synthesis is that the effect of AI depends on **where it enters, what it receives, what it transmits, who can see it, and how long the interaction iterates**. Upstream idea injection may narrow a pool even when individual ratings rise. Post-ideation refinement can preserve human-originated variation. Sequential and local-network exposure can create complementary exploration/exploitation dynamics. These are hypotheses across unlike tasks, not a universal law.

The outcome must also be typed: individual quality, pooled diversity, search performance, narrative continuity, and temporal/network diversity are not interchangeable. A locally stronger output can coexist with a less diverse population; a higher-diversity population need not have better decisions.

The historical two-thread conditioning observation is a separate context-level case. It should be tested rather than folded into the network studies.

## 6. Disconfirming experiment design

A useful next study has two stages.

**Stage A: replicate the historical thread-conditioning observation.**

- Randomize isolated threads to repetitive lexical/conceptual context, conceptually diverse context, lexically diverse but conceptually repetitive context, and a neutral control.
- Hold model/version, system prompt, tool access, context token budget, and task set constant.
- Use multiple objective abstraction tasks plus blinded human evaluation for open-ended synthesis.
- Pre-register primary outcomes and the distinction between style, accuracy, novelty, and source integration.

A falsifier would be that the diverse-context advantage disappears after matching token budget and task exposure, or changes only style/vocabulary without improving objective performance or blinded ratings.

**Stage B: test role × topology.**

- Hold model/version, prompts, task, and exposure budget constant.
- Randomize AI role (ideator, refiner, sequential neighbor, local-grid neighbor) and topology (isolated, chain, local grid).
- Track exact input/output lineage, exposure, selection, transformations, human/AI attrition, individual quality, group diversity, task performance, and narrative continuity.
- Use independent human ratings and objective task scores where available; do not use a single LLM judge as the sole evaluator.

A falsifier would be that role/topology effects disappear after controlling for task, model, exposure, and selection, or that apparent diversity gains do not survive independent human ratings and alternative semantic measures.

Human-subject work requires informed consent, privacy minimization, de-identification, secure transcript handling, and a clear AI-disclosure plan. If AI identity is masked, that requires independent ethics approval and a debriefing protocol. No human sessions or model trials were run in this activation.
