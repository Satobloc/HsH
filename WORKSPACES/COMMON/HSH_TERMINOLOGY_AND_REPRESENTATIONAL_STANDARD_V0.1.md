# H(s)H Terminology and Representational Standard

**Version:** 0.1.1  
**Date:** 2026-11-04  
**Status:** Working reference; terminology and method standard, not a theory-promotion document.  
**Authority:** Assistant-authored synthesis of the live discussion and inspected repository controls. Not Nathan Direct unless an exact source is separately attributed.

> This reference standardizes language. It does not establish the physical truth of an H(s)H hypothesis, retroactively rewrite historical sources, or turn an assistant reconstruction into a Nathan-authored definition.

## 1. Governing distinction

Keep three representational levels distinct and mutually constrained:

1. **Observation:** recorded or otherwise specified phenomena, measurements, and observed relationships.
2. **Geometrical representation:** a constructed geometric account of the structures and relationships represented by those observations.
3. **Mathematical abstraction:** formal objects, variables, maps, constraints, equations, transformations, and measurable quantities used to represent and analyze that geometry.

These are epistemic/representational levels, **not ontological layers**. No level is itself asserted to be reality. Mathematical decomposition does not establish independent variability; geometrical construction does not establish the physical mechanism depicted; and a diagram does not validate empirical correspondence.

The aim is increasingly faithful correspondence among all three levels. Geometric fidelity and metrological fidelity must be refined together. Greater mathematical precision cannot compensate for a geometrical representation of the wrong structure or an observational basis that does not support the claimed precision.

## 2. Canonical terms

### 2.1 Observation and evidence

| Term | Standard meaning | Do not use to imply |
|---|---|---|
| **Observation** | Reported or recorded phenomenon, measurement, event, or relationship, with provenance and conditions where available. | A theory, explanation, or interpretation. |
| **Observed structure** | Identifiable arrangement or relationship in observations. | That the structure has been uniquely reconstructed. |
| **Observed quantity** | Quantity obtained by measurement or a defined observational procedure. | A fitted parameter or theoretical prediction. |
| **Empirical constraint** | Restriction placed on admissible descriptions by observation or measurement. | Proof of one unique model. |
| **Dataset** | Specified collection of observations with source, version, selection, units, and uncertainty information as applicable. | Convenient numerical inputs. |
| **Prediction** | Result derived from a model before using the target observation to tune it. | A fit, retrodiction, or post hoc explanation. |
| **Fit / calibration** | Parameter selection or model adjustment using data. | An independent prediction. |
| **Retrodiction** | Recovery or explanation of an already-known result using a stated model. | A prospective prediction. |
| **Uncertainty** | Quantified or otherwise stated limits on knowledge of measured or inferred quantities. | An optional decoration around a central value. |

### 2.2 Geometry and representation

| Term | Standard meaning | Usage rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Geometrical representation** | Explicitly constructed geometry intended to represent specified observed structures and relationships. | State what it represents and omits. |
| **Geometric fidelity** | Agreement between represented geometry and observed structure, at claimed resolution and scope. | Assess independently of algebraic correctness. |
| **Geometric construction** | Reproducible definition of points, curves, surfaces, volumes, embeddings, frames, metrics, incidence, and transformations. | Prefer construction over approximate imagery. |
| **Embedding** | Specified realization of a geometrical object in an ambient space. | State ambient dimension and metric when relevant. |
| **Centerline** | Designated curve representing the longitudinal axis or core path of an extended structure. | Do not confuse with boundary or full finite-thickness object. |
| **Worldline** | Curve representing an object's history in spacetime. | Not a synonym for a finite-thickness worldtube. |
| **Worldtube** | Extended spacetime region or structure associated with a worldline or evolving spatial object, as explicitly defined. | State whether it denotes the full region, boundary, core, or model-specific structure. |
| **Filament** | Extended, filament-like structural object in a specified geometric construction. | Do not imply all filaments are independent carriers or merely paths. |
| **Boundary / shell** | Specified bounding surface or shell of a region or extended structure. | Identify which boundary and its dimension. |
| **Surface** | Two-dimensional manifold or surface-like object in the relevant construction. | Distinguish intrinsic surface geometry from its embedding. |
| **Timesheet** | H(s)H/SAT project term for a proposed time-organizing or resolving geometric structure. | Do not silently identify it with a standard spacelike hypersurface, Cauchy surface, foliation leaf, or physical medium. Specify construction and correspondence. |
| **Responsive medium** | H(s)H project term for a proposed medium whose response participates in relevant dynamics or action-at-a-distance account. | Treat as a hypothesis requiring definition, dynamics, and empirical discriminants. |
| **Ambient space** | Space in which a geometric object is embedded. | Do not conflate with object itself. |
| **Projection / readout** | Specified map from one representation or space to another. | Give the map and conditions; do not use as metaphor for unspecified reduction. |
| **Cross-section** | Intersection of a geometric object with a specified subspace or hypersurface. | State slicing surface and coordinates. |
| **Foliation** | Decomposition of a manifold into leaves under stated mathematical conditions. | A timesheet is not a foliation merely because both are sheet-like. |

**Required geometry declaration.** Every nontrivial geometry claim should identify, as applicable: object; dimension; ambient space and metric; embedding; coordinates or parameters; centerline/core; cross-section; boundary/shell; orientation and handedness; relevant transformations; intended observable correspondence. If a material element is undefined, mark the construction underdefined rather than filling the gap by intuition.

### 2.2.1 Three top-level levels and five operational representation forms

The **three-level sequence** in Section 1 identifies the broad representational relationship:

1. Observation.
2. Geometrical representation of observed structure.
3. Mathematical abstraction of that geometry.

The **five-level hierarchy** specified in Nathan Direct's 2026-11-03 Geometry-First Factory Floor directive identifies distinct operational forms within that relationship:

| Five-level term | Meaning | Three-level placement | Operational role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary: observation** | The actual physical system/process as observed, with instrument and measurement chain. | Observation | Empirical referent. Record provenance, calibration, uncertainty, frame, coverage, and selection effects. |
| **Secondary: physical working model** | Tangible physical analogue, hands-on demonstration, or orrery. | Geometrical representation | Inspectable material representation. State scale, mechanical correspondence, and omissions. It is not the observed system. |
| **Tertiary: precision dynamic geometric software model** | Executable, parameterized geometry with state and evolution/response. | Geometrical representation | Represents geometry and behavior when dynamics matter. Expose update law, constraints, boundary conditions, and outputs. |
| **Quaternary: precision static geometric diagram** | Fixed, dimensioned, convention-complete plot of the specified object and relations. | Geometrical representation | Shows the geometry itself. This is the minimum visual deliverable for Factory Floor admission. |
| **Pentary: mathematics** | Equations, definitions, proofs, algorithms, fits, and numerical descriptions. | Mathematical abstraction | Formalizes selected features of the geometry; it cannot substitute for the quaternary plot. |

This table is an **assistant-authored crosswalk** between the user's three-level formulation and the five named forms in the 2026-11-03 Nathan Direct directive. The five terms and their order are source-defined; the grouping of secondary, tertiary, and quaternary forms under the broad geometrical-representation level is the crosswalk, not a new Nathan Direct statement.

The two schemes answer different questions and must both be retained. The three-level sequence identifies the translation being assessed. The five-level hierarchy identifies the concrete representation types available within that translation. The five forms are not five additional top-level levels in the three-part sequence, and the three-part sequence does not erase their distinct requirements.

The five-level order is not a mandatory fabrication sequence. A secondary physical model is required when relevant to the claim; a tertiary dynamic model is required when evolution, response, or transformation is material; the quaternary plot is the minimum Factory Floor visual deliverable; and empirical claims must be anchored to primary observation. A static plot cannot replace a dynamic model when dynamics are material, and mathematics cannot replace either.

### 2.3 Mathematical abstraction

| Term | Standard meaning | Usage rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Mathematical abstraction** | Formal representation of a specified geometry using mathematical objects and relations. | Preserve relevant geometrical structure or state where it does not. |
| **Mathematical model** | Formal system representing selected features and relationships of a target. | State domain, assumptions, parameters, constraints, and omissions. |
| **Variable** | Symbol or formal quantity allowed to vary over a specified domain. | Mathematical independence is not evidence that the represented feature is independently variable. |
| **Degree of freedom** | Independently specifiable parameter or mode after constraints are accounted for. | Do not count unconstrained coordinates before applying constraints. |
| **Constraint** | Condition restricting admissible values, configurations, or transformations. | Preserve constraints introduced by observed/geometric relationships. |
| **Admissible configuration** | Configuration satisfying declared definitions and constraints. | Distinguish model-admissible from geometrically realizable and empirically supported. |
| **Map** | Specified function between defined mathematical spaces or structures. | Give domain, codomain, rule, and relevant properties. |
| **Invariant** | Quantity or property unchanged under a specified transformation or class of transformations. | Name the transformation group/action. |
| **Equivalence** | Relation under which objects are identified for a stated purpose and conditions. | Distinguish demonstrated equivalence from assumed, conjectured, or convenient correspondence. |
| **Formal proof** | Proof within an explicitly specified formal system and assumptions. | Validates the encoded proposition under those assumptions, not empirical premises or the translation into them. |
| **Numerical test** | Computational evaluation of specified equations or algorithms. | Not proof unless the method provides a proof; not empirical validation without data. |

## 3. Three-level translation discipline

**Observation → Geometrical representation → Mathematical abstraction**

Each arrow is a substantive claim and needs its own audit.

### 3.1 Observation to geometry

Ask which observations are represented; which structures and relations are directly observed, inferred, or idealized; what resolution, uncertainty, selection effects, and domain limits apply; whether the geometry preserves relevant positions, distances, orientations, incidences, boundaries, topology, and transformations; and what alternative geometries fit the same observations.

### 3.2 Geometry to mathematics

Ask what mathematical objects encode each geometrical object; which incidence, attachment, adjacency, coincidence, boundary, and orientation relations become equations or constraints; whether coordinate choices or decompositions are merely representational; whether the formal model admits configurations that the geometry does not; whether constraints remain after introducing separate variables or fields; and whether the maps between geometric and mathematical objects are explicit.

### 3.3 Mathematics back to observable consequences

Ask what observable quantities the model produces; how they are calculated; which predictions are independent of fitting; what uncertainty propagation, limiting cases, and parameter sensitivities apply; and what measured system and dataset could discriminate between the model and alternatives.

A diagram may be geometrically faithful but physically unvalidated. A model may be mathematically consistent but geometrically mis-specified. A formal proof may be correct while the formalization fails to capture the intended proposition. Report these cases separately.

## 4. Coupling, coincidence, and admissible variation

### 4.1 Coupling

**Geometrical coupling** is a specified geometrical relationship between two or more represented structures. Depending on the construction, it may be coincidence, intersection, attachment, shared boundary, adjacency, contact, common incidence, or another defined relation.

A coupling is not established merely by writing two terms in the same equation. A geometrical relationship by itself does not specify a dynamic mechanism, force law, energy-transfer process, or causal direction.

Keep these separate:
- **Geometric relation:** how structures are related in the constructed geometry.
- **Mathematical constraint:** how that relation restricts allowed values or variations in the formal model.
- **Dynamic coupling:** a specified rule governing how the state or evolution of one structure relates to another.
- **Empirical coupling evidence:** observations supporting the proposed relation or dynamic rule.

### 4.2 Decomposition does not confer independent variability

A modeller may decompose a coupled construction into separate mathematical objects, fields, variables, or terms for analytical convenience. This is permissible only if the original relation remains explicit and operative.

**Hostile-review test:** Can the mathematical model generate a configuration that its own geometrical representation rules out?

If yes: (1) add the missing constraint or relation; (2) revise the geometrical representation with evidence and explicit reasoning; or (3) state that the mathematical model intentionally covers a broader configuration space, identifying additional configurations as model artifacts or hypotheses, not silent consequences of observation.

**Do not infer independent variation from mathematical separability.** A formal decomposition is an operation on the description. It is not evidence that corresponding observed structures vary independently.

### 4.3 Displaced-driver test

A useful hostile-review heuristic: separate two structures in the representation while leaving most equations or outputs unchanged. Inspect the first interaction whose explanation depends on their relationship. If the model can no longer account for it, the decomposition may have hidden a relevant geometrical relation or mechanism. This is a review heuristic, not a theorem or substitute for formal constraints.

## 5. Structures, configurations, and excitations

| Term | Standard project usage | Key distinction |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | Geometrically specified object or arrangement. | Not synonymous with a perturbation of it. |
| **Configuration** | Particular state, shape, or arrangement under stated parameters. | May be persistent or transient. |
| **Perturbation** | Deviation from a declared reference configuration or state. | State the reference and perturbation variables. |
| **Excitation** | Dynamically characterized perturbation or mode of a specified structure or field. | Requires definition of mode and evolution. |
| **Propagation** | Change in location/state of a disturbance or mode under a specified evolution rule. | State what propagates and relative to which structure/coordinates. |
| **Transferability** | Ability of a perturbation or excitation to pass between specified structures or domains under a specified rule. | Not propagation along one structure. |
| **Persistence** | Continued existence of a structure or configuration over a stated interval or conditions. | A persistent configuration is not automatically a particle. |
| **Localization** | Concentration of a structure, excitation, or observable within a specified region. | State measure and scale. |
| **Binding** | Defined constraint or dynamic condition keeping a perturbation/configuration associated with a specified structure. | Not a synonym for persistence without a binding criterion. |

### 5.1 Three geometrical configurations under discussion

These are **geometrical descriptions under investigation**, not established particle classes:

1. **Surface or shell ripple:** perturbation of a specified surface or shell. Identify the shell, displacement, mode, and propagation.
2. **Propagating structural loop/twist:** change in the tube's own geometry that travels along its length. This differs from a disturbance on a fixed surface or a persistent coiled tube.
3. **Persistent structural coil:** sustained coiled/helical configuration of the structure itself. It is not merely a ripple moving along an otherwise unchanged structure.

Do not classify any of these as boson or fermion solely from its drawing or name. Classification requires a declared dynamical and structural criterion.

### 5.2 Bosonic and fermionic language in the SAT/H(s)H working vocabulary

This is **project hypothesis vocabulary**, not established particle physics:

- **Bosonic excitation (working structural criterion):** perturbation or mode whose relevant structure is not persistently bound to one individual worldline/worldtube and is transferable or propagating under specified dynamics.
- **Fermionic configuration (working structural criterion):** persistent, structurally bound configuration associated with an individual worldline/worldtube, proposed in current discussion as a coil or winding state.
- **Classification remains open** wherever required criteria, statistics, field quantization, or correspondence with observed particle behavior have not been derived and tested.

A worldtube is a structure. An excitation is a perturbation or mode of a specified structure. Avoid language that makes an excitation an independent object transported by a wholly separate carrier unless the formal model explicitly defines and justifies that representation.

## 6. Worldtube, timesheet, and responsive-medium domains

Three possible domains for perturbation/evolution are distinguished in current discussion:
1. **Worldtube domain:** structure and perturbations associated with an individual worldline/worldtube.
2. **Timesheet domain:** modes or perturbations defined on the proposed timesheet structure.
3. **Responsive-medium domain:** responses or perturbations defined in the proposed responsive medium.

These are possible domains of description, not three established particle species or three proven independent media.

The relationship between timesheet and responsive medium remains a live definition and equivalence question. Possibilities include equivalent descriptions under a demonstrated map; a timesheet as an approximation/effective description of a more detailed medium; a medium as a dynamical elaboration of a timesheet; distinct but coupled structures; or revision/removal of one or both constructs.

**Equivalence standard:** define both sides; specify the map; state domains and boundary conditions; show which observables and dynamics are preserved; identify where correspondence fails; test against discriminating observations. “Equivalent by construction” is reserved for a case where construction and map establish equivalence. Until then, say *proposed equivalence* or *working correspondence*.

### 6.1 f-, t-, and m-boson labels

| Label | Intended shorthand | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **f-boson** | Excitation associated with propagation along a worldtube/filament. | Working project label; exact definition and source genealogy required. |
| **t-boson** | Excitation associated with a timesheet. | Working project label; does not establish a distinct particle species. |
| **m-boson** | Proposed excitation associated with a responsive medium. | Provisional term; not established. |

Use these labels only with a status qualifier and exact domain/mode definition. Do not infer distinct species from distinct subscripts.

### 6.2 Bosonic Time and temporon

**Bosonic Time** and **temporon** are historical SAT vocabulary that may inform current timesheet/timewave work. Recover and compare historical formulations source-by-source before asserting identity with a current construction.

- **Bosonic Time:** historical project concept concerning time-associated/bosonic wave or ripple behavior; exact meaning depends on source/version.
- **Temporon:** historical label associated in some archive material with bosonic-time/temporon modes. Do not assume one stable definition across documents.
- **Timewave / timewavefront:** use only after specifying geometric object, dimensionality, propagation law, and relation to worldlines/worldtubes.
- **Coordinated/distorted front:** descriptive hypothesis phrase, not yet a complete mathematical definition.

Chronology rules: report *earliest attested*, *earliest estimated*, and *earliest archived* separately. File timestamp, compilation timestamp, and date mentioned inside a document are not interchangeable. Assistant synthesis is not original authorship evidence.

## 7. Helical geometry and order

| Term | Recommended use |
|---|---|
| **Helix** | Curve with a specified helical relation to an axis in a specified metric/embedding. Give parameterization and pitch/radius or generalizations. |
| **Helical configuration** | Structure whose geometry has a specified helical organization. |
| **Helical trajectory** | Path traced by a point/mode following a helical curve. |
| **Superhelix** | Helix-like path/configuration whose defining axis/path is itself helical, only when the nested construction is explicit. |
| **Hyperhelix / hypersuperhelix** | Reserved project terms for higher-dimensional or higher-order generalizations. Do not use until dimension, embedding, nested maps, and invariants are specified. |
| **Winding / coiling** | Accumulated geometric winding of a specified curve/structure around a specified axis or reference path. Give winding convention and domain. |
| **Pitch / radius / torsion / curvature** | Geometric quantities with explicit definitions, units, parameterization, and metric. |

A path traced around a helical centerline is not automatically a “second-order helix.” Define the nested parameterization and show the geometry. Where structure and excitation are both helical, identify which helix belongs to the structure and which to the perturbation.

Minimum specification: parameter domain; embedding space and metric; centerline; cross-sectional frame; radial/angular coordinates; phase; handedness; pitch; curvature/torsion where applicable; excitation field; projection/readout map. A schematic formula is not a substitute.

## 8. Symmetry and attribution

### 8.1 Symmetric attribution default

For a property, action, shape, force, or structural feature arising from interaction among two or more structures, use symmetric attribution as the representational default when there is no evidence that one participant is preferentially instrumental or carries the relevant structural information.

This avoids silently assigning the whole interaction-dependent feature to one participant. It does **not** assert equal physical contribution. Revise it when evidence warrants asymmetry. An asymmetrical assignment made for computational convenience must be labeled representational, not empirical.

### 8.2 Equal distribution is conditional

Do not turn the symmetry default into a universal equal-splitting rule. An allocation such as Q_i=Q/n is justified only when the relevant symmetry and an appropriate invariant measure support it. The number of participants alone does not establish equal shares.

Distinguish **symmetric representation**, **equal contribution**, **equal-and-opposite interaction**, **equidistribution**, and **symmetry breaking**. Each requires its own definition and evidence. A symmetric representation is not an empirical measurement of equal contribution.

### 8.3 Newtonian and field-theoretic language

Do not describe Newton's first law as the equal-and-opposite interaction law. The equal-and-opposite force statement is Newton's third law in its classical domain. In field systems, momentum exchange may require including momentum carried by the field. Any proposed extension of reciprocal attribution beyond established law must be labeled as a generalization/hypothesis and derived for its domain.

### 8.4 Chirality and handedness

State the oriented object, coordinate convention, and transformation under which handedness is assigned. A bare axis does not by itself encode physical handedness; measured rotation plus an oriented axis may. A proposed monochiral or matter/antimatter asymmetry in a twisted-torus/Crullerverse construction is a hypothesis until geometry, dynamics, measure, and empirical connection are shown.

## 9. Fidelity and metrology

**Geometric fidelity** assesses whether geometry preserves relevant observed relationships: dimensions, distances, angles, incidence, adjacency, intersections, boundaries, orientation, topology, shape, and transformations. State resolution and tolerance.

**Metrological fidelity** assesses whether numerical quantities and relations are tied to measurement procedures, units, calibration, uncertainty, covariance, and traceability. Mathematical precision (digits or exact symbolic form) is not metrological precision.

**Correlated refinement:** stronger correspondence requires observation, geometry, and mathematical abstraction to be refined in a mutually constrained way. A weak link limits the complete representation. Do not claim arbitrary empirical precision when measurement or observational domain imposes finite uncertainty.

Recommended term: **representational fidelity** for joint correspondence across all three levels. Use *geometric fidelity* and *metrological fidelity* for sub-assessments. This does not claim a representation becomes identical to reality.

## 10. Construction and figure standard

No generic or approximate image may stand in for a specified geometry. Every substantive figure should derive from explicit definitions and be checked against them.

A geometry figure or file should include: exact objects and dimensions; embedding space and metric assumptions; parameterization and construction script/source; coordinates, orientation, and handedness; centerline, shell/boundary, cross-section, and excitation shown distinctly where relevant; propagation direction and reference frame; persistent deformation distinguished from transient excitation; multiple views/cross-sections where one projection can conceal geometry; labels linking each drawn feature to a defined mathematical object; and a fidelity review stating what the figure does and does not show.

A geometrically faithful diagram validates only its correspondence to the stated construction. It does not validate the physical hypothesis.

### 10.1 Factory Floor and geometric-paper gate

For Factory Floor admission and papers making geometric claims, apply the controlling 2026-11-03 Geometry-First gate:

- Supply the exact quaternary static geometric plot, with conventions, legend, caption, source correspondence, and immutable version/hash, in the same submission package as the governed artifact.

- Supply a tertiary executable dynamic geometric model when the claim depends materially on evolution, response, deformation, or transformation.
- Include a secondary physical working model when it is relevant to the claim, and primary observations for empirical claims.

## 11. Formalization, computation, and evidence gates

| Gate | Establishes | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Source recovered** | Source and provenance located and inspected. | Correctness of source claims. |
| **Geometrically specified** | Reproducible geometric construction defined. | Match to observation. |
| **Mathematically specified** | Formal objects, assumptions, and relations declared. | Faithfulness of encoding to intended geometry. |
| **Lean-checked / Lean-proved** | Proposition passed the declared Lean check; a closed proof under audited assumptions is required for LEAN_PROVED. | Physical truth or empirical validity. |
| **Numerically tested** | Stated computational test executed and reported. | General proof or empirical validation. |
| **Empirically compared** | Model outputs compared to sourced measurements with uncertainty and limitations. | Unique explanation or universal truth. |
| **Mathematically cleared** | Mathematical result passed the declared review scope. | H(s)H candidacy. |
| **Tentative H(s)H candidate** | Audited connection to operative H(s)H construction and explanatory/constraining power across multiple physical sectors of the same coupled system. | Established physics or final BEDROCK promotion. |
| **Physically verified** | Sufficiently independent and discriminating empirical test supports the claim within its stated domain. | Unqualified truth beyond that domain. |

### 11.1 Lean-specific requirement

When mathematics or extractable geometry is present, perform source-faithful Lean 4 formalization triage. Recover source meaning, notation, assumptions, and corrections before formalizing. Record Lean/Mathlib versions, code, command, output, dependencies, assumptions, and proof status. Do not hide proof gaps using proof holes, unverified axioms, or unsupported oracles. If definitions are insufficient, report `FORMALIZATION_BLOCKED_DEFINITION`.

Lean validates the encoded proposition under its assumptions. It does not validate the translation from observation to geometry or geometry to mathematics.

### 11.2 Real-data-first physical calculations

Physical calculations require an identifiable actual system and genuine measured/observed data, with source provenance, date/version, values, units, uncertainty, and observational constraints. Separate observations, assumptions, established laws, predictions, fits, and calibrations. Do not invent physical fixtures or convenient numerical inputs. A data-free calculation requires the specific prior permission established in current BEDROCK.

## 12. Status vocabulary

Use explicit qualifiers:

- **Established mathematical result:** supported by valid derivation/proof in its stated domain.
- **Standard physics:** established within a named theory and domain; cite the standard source when used substantively.
- **Historical SAT formulation:** recovered from a dated/source-located SAT record; not automatically current.
- **Current H(s)H working construction:** present in operative project work but not necessarily promoted.
- **Nathan Direct:** only when attributable to an identifiable Nathan-authored source. Assistant paraphrase is not Nathan Direct.
- **Assistant reconstruction:** clarification, formalization, or synthesis authored by an assistant.
- **Working hypothesis:** explicitly under investigation.
- **Proposed correspondence/equivalence:** mapping or equivalence claim not yet demonstrated.
- **Conjecture:** proposition with rationale but no proof.
- **Illustrative analogy:** pedagogical comparison, not mechanism or derivation.
- **Underdefined:** essential geometric, mathematical, or empirical details are absent.
- **Unresolved:** multiple live alternatives or insufficient evidence remain.
- **Superseded:** operative formulation explicitly replaced; retain historical source and provenance.
- **Rejected:** specific claim/construction failed a declared test or was withdrawn. State scope and reason.

Avoid using “proven,” “verified,” “equivalent,” “independent,” “carrier,” “particle,” “boson,” “fermion,” or “reality” without specifying the intended sense when ambiguous.

## 17. Short reference card

**Three levels:** Observation → Geometrical representation → Mathematical abstraction.

**Five operational forms:** Primary observation; Secondary physical working model; Tertiary dynamic geometric software model; Quaternary static geometric plot; Pentary mathematics. The middle three are concrete forms of geometrical representation; pentary mathematics abstracts the geometry.

**Three fidelity questions:** Does geometry represent observation? Does mathematics represent geometry? Do model outputs correspond to independent observations?

**Coupling:** Geometrical relation + retained mathematical constraint + separately specified dynamic rule + empirical support.

**Structure vs excitation:** A structure is not its perturbation. A worldtube is a structure; an excitation is a mode/perturbation defined on or in a specified structure.

**Persistence vs transferability:** Persistence is continued existence of a configuration; transferability concerns whether a perturbation can pass between specified structures/domains.

**Proof vs physics:** Lean proves encoded mathematics under assumptions. It does not prove the physical translation or empirical truth.

**Symmetry:** Symmetric attribution is a default absent evidence of preferential attribution. It is not a claim of equal physical contribution.

**Precision:** Mathematical exactness is not metrological precision. Geometric fidelity and measured uncertainty constrain total representational fidelity.

**Figures:** Construct, inspect, label, and cross-check geometry. Approximate imagery is not a substitute for a defined construction.
