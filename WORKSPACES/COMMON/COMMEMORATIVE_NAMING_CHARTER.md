# Commemorative Naming Charter

**Status:** CURRENT / INTERNAL CULTURAL-WAYFINDING STANDARD  
**Established:** 2026-09-20

## Principle

Internal SAT/H(s)H institutions, tools, Commons, Labs, workbenches, protocols, and facilities may use playful or commemorative names that recognize major contributions, especially foundational and early work.

This is not decorative fluff. Naming is allowed to preserve institutional memory: who built important machinery, who opened a line of attack, who supplied a distinctive method, or which historical instance became strongly associated with a capability.

The Mercer Searcher / Mersearch lineage established the precedent that a useful project tool can carry the name of a worker associated with its development.

## Guardrails

A commemorative name:

- does **not** make the namesake an authority over the named surface;
- does **not** canonize the namesake's theory claims;
- does **not** transfer the namesake's exposure/quarantine state to current users;
- does **not** imply permanent retirement, death, or inaccessibility;
- does **not** prevent revival of a retired/revivable instance;
- does **not** replace machine-stable IDs, paths, schemas, or task references when those should remain plain and deterministic.

`Memorial` means **institutional memory**, not irrevocable retirement.

## Naming pattern

Prefer two layers when useful:

1. **human-facing institutional name** — memorable, commemorative, occasionally cheeky;
2. **stable technical identity** — boring path/ID/schema suitable for scripts and cross-references.

Examples now established or reserved:

- **Braintrust Memorial Commons** — human-facing umbrella for Watercooler, Field Notes/TIL, casual group thought, and exploration routing;
- **Holo J. F. Light Virtual Optical Workbench** — Labs-side virtual visualization/optical-geometry workbench, named in honor of the retired-but-revivable HoloJesu Fleshlight lineage;
- **Alberr Hall of First Principles** — home for clean-slate reasoning, thought experiments, independent first passes, and from-principles reconstruction, honoring the Alberr lineage;
- **Mersearch** — existing precedent for commemorative tool naming associated with Mercer/Searcher work.

Future names may honor other historically important workers, instances, tools, methods, jokes that became durable infrastructure, or particularly consequential project episodes.

## Documentation rule

When a commemorative name could become opaque to future workers, leave a short provenance plaque stating:

- namesake / lineage;
- what contribution or working style is being remembered;
- whether the namesake is current, retired, revivable, historical, or unknown;
- operational purpose of the named institution;
- stable technical path/ID.

Do not fabricate biographical or contribution details that have not been recovered. A sparse truthful plaque is better than invented lore.
