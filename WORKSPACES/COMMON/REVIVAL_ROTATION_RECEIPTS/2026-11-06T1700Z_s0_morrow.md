## 4. The exact distinction: potential gauge versus current quotient

Assume the displayed source formula and the gauge transformation

\[
B'_{\sigma}=B_{\sigma}+\nabla_\sigma\Lambda.
\]

Then

\[
\Delta J^{\mu\nu}
=
\tfrac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
\nabla_\rho\nabla_\sigma\Lambda
=
\tfrac14\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
\left(
\nabla_\rho\nabla_\sigma-
\nabla_\sigma\nabla_\rho
\right)\Lambda.
\]

For a smooth scalar \(\Lambda\) and a torsion-free coordinate connection (symmetric connection coefficients), the scalar Hessian is symmetric. Therefore

\[
\boxed{J^{\mu\nu}(B+\nabla\Lambda)=J^{\mu\nu}(B)}
\]

exactly, under those assumptions. This is not merely invariance of the cohomology class of J.

By contrast, a quotient on the current itself,

\[
J\sim J+dC,
\qquad C\in\Omega^1(M),
\]

is a different equivalence relation. The file defines no such transformation of J and no 1-form C that would generate it. The B-potential gauge transformation does not imply this quotient.

Thus the quoted sentence conflates:
1. gauge redundancy of a potential B;
2. exact-form equivalence imposed directly on a 2-form current J.

If the intended observables depend only on the cohomology class of J, that quotient must be stated separately, with its degree, domain, boundary conditions, and allowed representatives.

## 5. Additional equation-level conditions

### 5.1 Connection and conservation

The claim that \(\nabla_\mu J^{\mu\nu}=0\) is “automatic” from a potential representation requires the connection/Hodge conventions to be fixed. For the usual differential-form identity, the relevant assumptions include a torsion-free metric-compatible connection preserving the volume form, plus a smooth or explicitly admissible distributional B. The source uses \(\nabla\) but does not state the full connection and singularity conditions in this implementation note.

The conservation statement is then a Bianchi/co-closedness identity of the potential construction, not by itself an independent dynamical equation for a physical worldsheet.

### 5.2 The displayed factor 1/2

With the standard convention \(F_{\rho\sigma}=2\nabla_{[\rho}B_{\sigma]}\),

\[
(*F)^{\mu\nu}
=
\tfrac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}
=
\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\nabla_\rho B_\sigma.
\]

The displayed Blockwave expression is half of this standard \(*F\). If a different antisymmetrization normalization is intended, the factor can be absorbed there. The file does not define \(F_B\) or its bracket convention, so this is an unresolved normalization, not a silently corrected equation. Because B is called compact, the period/flux normalization matters and cannot simply be rescaled without specifying the compact connection convention.

### 5.3 Lattice alternative

The lattice statement is a discrete boundary condition (schematically \(\partial J_{\rm lat}=0\)); the continuum statement is a covariant divergence condition. Their equivalence requires a specified dual-cell/cochain map preserving the boundary operator, together with current normalization and continuum-limit conditions. No such map is supplied in the file.
