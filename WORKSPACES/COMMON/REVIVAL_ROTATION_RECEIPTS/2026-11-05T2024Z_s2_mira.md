### A. Superfluid helium reconnection: strongest new contact/worldsheet candidate

Fonda et al., **“Reconnection scaling in quantum fluids,”** PNAS 116 (2019), DOI `10.1073/pnas.1901786116`. Full primary article read. Public video dataset: Zenodo DOI `10.5281/zenodo.2543528`.

- System: superfluid (^4)He at 1.9 K; 20 selected reconnection events; submicron frozen-air tracer particles (about 0.5 µm); laser sheet about 150 µm thick; field of view about 8.2 × 8.2 mm; camera modes 512×512 through 512×128; 30–100 frames/s.
- The measured objects are tracer-particle positions and their inferred vortex-line trajectories, not the microscopic vortex-core cross-section. The core is far below optical resolution.
- The paper fits the separation history `δ(t)` to a square-root law with an angle-dependent prefactor, `δ(t) = A √(κ t_a) (1 + c t_a)`, and compares `A(θ)` with local-induction/Da Rios-type predictions. The forward model is a curve-evolution law; the reverse readout is image → tracer positions → `δ(t), θ` → fitted `A`.
- The paper reports 20 events and an average prefactor around 2.14, distinct from a prior turbulent experiment around 1.1 and simulations around 0.4. These are different regimes/sample protocols; do not treat them as a direct universal-constant comparison.
- Mutual-friction coefficient `α≈0.25–0.3` is taken from reference tables for this temperature range, not independently measured in this experiment. The authors cannot decide whether including mutual friction improves the fit. The near-core regime is not resolved; the square-root regime is an intermediate-asymptotic statement.
- The stated pointwise errors are one pixel and one frame interval. Conditional conversion from the published 8.2 mm field over 512 pixels gives nominal `16.015625 µm/pixel`; 30–100 fps gives 10–33.333 ms/frame. These are scale conversions, not a new calibration. No full covariance matrix or machine-readable trajectory table was identified in the accessible paper.
- The original videos are deposited on Zenodo; metadata reports about 14.5 GB total and MD5 checksums. **The dataset was not downloaded and checksums were not verified.** No trajectory extraction or physical fit was performed.
- The paper uses `κ=h/m₄`, but does not identify a CODATA edition or a versioned `m₄` source/covariance in the accessible article. Do not retrofit a modern CODATA value and present it as the paper's original input.

This is a strong measured contact/reconnection and time-evolving centerline candidate, but it is a **projected tracer-sampled worldsheet**, not a resolved finite-core worldtube. The observation map must include camera calibration, laser-sheet thickness, tracer-to-vortex association, time synchronization, and projection/fiber conditions.

### B. Finite-core and rotation candidates in BECs

1. Coddington et al., **“Experimental Studies of Equilibrium Vortex Properties in a Bose-Condensed Gas,”** Phys. Rev. A 70, 063607 (2004), DOI `10.1103/PhysRevA.70.063607`. The 87Rb rotating-BEC core radius is obtained from 2D Gaussian fits after radial expansion and rescaling. The quoted `r_v≈1.94 ξ` is a Gross–Pitaevskii simulation result, not a direct universal measurement law. The paper reports less than 5% calibration uncertainty for its radial expansion factor, up to about 10% systematic from axial expansion, and additional imaging effects that bias inferred core radii upward. The roughly 25% high-density discrepancy is comparable to these systematics; the authors do not establish a robust non-GP deviation. This is a finite-core spatial benchmark, not an in-situ time-resolved worldtube.

2. Wilson et al., **“In situ imaging of vortices in Bose-Einstein condensates,”** Phys. Rev. A 91, 023621 (2015), DOI `10.1103/PhysRevA.91.023621`. In-situ dark-field imaging measures vortex positions and lattice spacing (about 9 µm). The approximately 400 nm core/healing-length scale is estimated, not directly resolved. The measured imaging point-spread function is about 2.4 ± 0.5 µm; magnification is calibrated at 19.7 ± 0.4 and the stated diffraction limit is about 1.9 µm at 780 nm. This is a useful centerline/position readout, not a finite-core cross-section measurement.

3. Dalfovo et al., **“Optical visibility and core structure of vortex filaments in a bosonic superfluid,”** arXiv:1804.03017 (2018). A 3D sodium-BEC filament profile is observed after expansion and compared with Gross–Pitaevskii simulations/scaling. The in-situ healing length is about 0.2 µm, below the unexpanded optical resolution; the expanded profile is much wider (about 30 µm) and has 2–3 µm optical resolution. Error bars are dominated by photon shot noise and spurious optical fringes. This is a model-assisted expanded 3D profile, not an in-situ finite-core worldtube or an independent H(s)H test. A raw-data/covariance package was not identified in this pass.
