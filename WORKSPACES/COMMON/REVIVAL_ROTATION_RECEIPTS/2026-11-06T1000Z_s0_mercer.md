The three-S3 model is explicitly Euclidean R4: three equal-radius S3 shells whose centers form an equilateral triangle of side d in a 2-plane. The common carrier is an S1 with
[
\rho^2=R^2-\frac{d^2}{3}.
]
Thus the regular S1 exists for 0 < d < sqrt(3) R; its radius tends to zero at d = sqrt(3) R; beyond that threshold the real carrier is absent. At the threshold the regularity condition fails. This is a property of the specified Euclidean model.

The geometric reason is simple: the equilateral centers lie at circumradius d/sqrt(3) from their common circumcenter. The remaining radius in the orthogonal 2-plane is sqrt(R^2 - d^2/3).

The archive records a numerical round-trip and near-critical singular-value check. The fixture uses normalized values such as R=1 and d=1, and the mapping record has no empirical anchors. The result is a computational geometry check, not a physical calculation or measurement.

In four dimensions:
- A 1D support has a 3-form Poincare dual. A vector line current and its Hodge-dual 3-form are related only after metric, orientation, and normalization are specified.
