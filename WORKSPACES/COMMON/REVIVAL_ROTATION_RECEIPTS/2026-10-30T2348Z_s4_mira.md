### NBS Technical Note 641 comparison

The 1973 NBS survey gives the saturated-solid-D2 fit
rho[g/cm^3] = 0.20459 - 0.000026337*T[K]^2,
attributed to Ludtke and Roder and based on Schuch and Mills, Megaw, Muckers et al., and Woolley et al. The survey explicitly says source selection was not critically evaluated and recommends re-evaluating saturated solid D2 density because older volumetric measurements could be off by 1.5%.

At T = 1.65 K, Decimal arithmetic gives:
- NBS polynomial: 0.2045182975175 g/cm^3.
- 202.8 kg/m^3: 0.2028 g/cm^3.
- Relative difference: -0.8401681113% (Stasiak value relative to NBS fit).

This is source-conditional arithmetic, not a new measurement, prediction or fit. The 1.65 K evaluation is an interpolation within the historical table's temperature span, not a direct NBS measurement at 1.65 K.

A further applicability issue appears in the same NBS survey: deuterium rich in para can undergo an hcp-to-fcc transition over 1.2-4.2 K; deuterium condensed from vapor in thin films may be fcc; transition temperature depends on ortho/para concentration and density/pressure; the reported volume change is 1.3% with hysteresis/metastability. The later tracer studies generate particles from D2 gas in a 1.65 K helium-II bath, but the accessible methods do not establish the particles' ortho/para composition or crystalline phase. A 1.3% volume change corresponds to roughly a 1.3% density-scale change if applicable, larger than the 0.84% difference; this is a conditional confound, not evidence that these particles underwent that transition.

Disposition: the Lean theorem that the Stasiak number lies within 1% of the NBS polynomial is arithmetically correct. The interpretation of NBS as an independent validation is not established. It should be described as a separate historical compilation comparator, with phase and source-lineage limitations.
