### Nature Physics experiment

Barckicke, Falcon & Gissinger, “Kelvin wave propagation along vortex cores,” *Nature Physics* 22, 409–414 (2026), DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03175-w.

The article describes a stable, finite-core air–water vortex in a 21-cm-diameter, 45-cm-tall tank, with a typical vortex height near 40 cm and core radius of order 1–2 mm. The measurement chain includes:
- One optical camera positioned normal to the vortex axis, two lenses (8-mm wide-angle and 12-mm small-angle), 2,064 × 1,564 pixels.
- 60 μm/pixel horizontal and vertical resolution; contour width about 0.5 mm; typical contour/position uncertainty about 0.1 mm.
- Typical 300 fps, 30-s recordings, and a 12.5-cm axial measurement window for the spatiotemporal spectra.
- Left/right contour profiles combined into `eta(z,t)` and `a(z,t)`, then Fourier analysed.
- PIV velocity measurements at 6,000 fps on a 37 × 23 grid with 2.7-mm spatial resolution; circulation measurement uncertainty about 0.005 m²/s.
- PTV for vertical velocity, with typical uncertainty about 0.05 m/s.

The paper distinguishes two geometries:
- `m=1`: helical bending of the vortex core axis, with approximately constant core radius.
- `m=2`: fluted/flattening wave, an elliptic cross-sectional deformation with core-radius variation and no vortex-axis displacement.

These are two different perturbation geometries of one finite-core vortex structure. Both propagate along the vortex, but one changes the core centerline while the other changes the cross-section. They should not be collapsed into one generic “helix” or classified as H(s)H bosons/fermions from appearance alone.
