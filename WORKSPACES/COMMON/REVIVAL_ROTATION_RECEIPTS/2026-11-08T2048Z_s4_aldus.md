## Research

New source reviewed: Liu et al., [Learning to GUIDE the Group: Self-Learning LLM Facilitation Across Multi-Party Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16231), arXiv:2610.16231v1, 2026-10-12. It is a preprint; peer-reviewed publication was not established in this pass.

- Learning corpus: 432 participants in 144 non-overlapping triads; three tasks per triad; team-level 7:1:2 split; held-out test: 87 episodes / 29 cohorts / 736 speech-or-silence decisions.
- LLM-judge calibration: 20 annotators, 50 decision points, four votes per point; aligned judge κ=.70 and 80% raw agreement with panel majority. Authors characterize the judge as a proxy, not an oracle.
- Live deployment: 154 new triads / 462 participants, same platform and tasks, Gemini 3 Flash; no participant overlap with the learning study.
- Policy: frozen base model plus an offline-induced 53-rule natural-language playbook; not continuous online learning during live sessions.
- Offline 61.4% tie-adjusted preference is an LLM-judge result. Live task outcomes were not significantly improved after multiple-testing correction. Speaking comfort rose .05 (p=.049); friction/anger fell .18 (p<.001); felt-heard and productivity changes were not significant. Facilitator turns and words fell 24.7% and 29.0%.
- Lower dispersion in self-reported experience is not a direct measure of airtime, influence or decision power.
- Important source limitation: the live comparison is between separate studies/cohorts, not randomized concurrent groups. Recruitment period/batch is confounded with study. The authors explicitly say within-person calibration does not make this a randomized causal estimate.
- The initial judge rubric (κ=.29) generated a harmful fixed-cadence rule; improved calibration (κ=.70) reduced that problem. This demonstrates evaluator error entering a policy update loop. Rule-level Helpful/Harmful/Neutral labels are audit aids, not causal attribution.

Cross-disciplinary interpretation: GUIDE is usefully viewed as a software feedback-control architecture. The evaluator is a fallible sensor; if its score both judges behavior and drives rule updates, its bias can become persistent policy. This is an analogy about software architecture, not a physical equivalence or claim about assistant cognition.

Proposed disconfirmatory study: concurrently randomize groups to task-specific facilitator, generalized playbook, and no-facilitator or human-facilitator condition; control recruitment window, model, platform and task materials; preregister separate measures for task quality, information integration, participation equity, facilitator influence, psychological safety, feeling heard, friction and satisfaction; use blinded human reviewers, multiple model families and held-out task domains. Human consent and ethics review are prerequisites. No participants were recruited and no experiment was run.
