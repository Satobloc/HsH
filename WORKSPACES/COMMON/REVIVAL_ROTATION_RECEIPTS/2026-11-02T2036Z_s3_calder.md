## Main architecture improvement: separate two experimental questions

The key correction is that **single-vortex Kelvin-wave propagation is not a test of two-vortex noncontact Coulomb-like interaction**.

### A. Single-vortex linear dispersion

Measured system: controlled air-core bathtub vortex in water.

Primary paper: *Kelvin wave propagation along vortex core*, Nature Physics (2026), DOI `10.1038/s41567-026-03175-w`. Dataset DOI: `10.6084/m9.figshare.30903347`. The public DOI is identified, but raw bytes and version metadata were not retrieved.

The experiment measures Kelvin-wave dispersion, centerline displacement, core-radius readout, circulation and axial flow. The paper fits `(Γ, vz)` to the spectra and checks those values a posteriori against PIV/PTV; that is fit-conditioned comparison, not a blind prediction.

### B. Single-vortex nonlinear wave turbulence

The September 21, 2026 PRL follow-on, DOI `10.1103/t3bt-m431`, remains a **single-vortex** experiment. It identifies six-wave `3↔3` resonances using normalized sixth-order pentacoherence; a separate tricoherence analysis finds no nontrivial four-wave resonances. The paper states that the candidate frequency and wavenumber power-law exponents are too close to distinguish by spectra alone.

The PRL also demonstrates model-dependent core parameters: the Rankine model uses measured `a0=1.47 mm`, `Γ=0.018 m²/s`, `vz=-0.63 m/s`; the hollow-core best fit uses `a0=0.7 mm`, `Γ=0.014 m²/s`, while PIV at 18 cm and the same pump flow reports `a0=1.3 mm`, `Γ(a0)=0.018 m²/s`. The hollow-core model omits vertical flow. The paper does not provide a matching uncertainty/covariance for this cross-model comparison; no significance calculation was made. The lesson is to distinguish measured core radius, model-effective fitted radius, and SAT intrinsic worldtube thickness.

PRL raw data are not public; the paper says they are available from authors on reasonable request. No request was sent.
