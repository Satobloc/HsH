### Laboratory comparator: LAPD magnetic flux ropes

Gekelman et al., *Chaos in Magnetic Flux Ropes*, Plasma Physics and Controlled Fusion 56 (2014) 064002, DOI [10.1088/0741-3335/56/6/064002](https://doi.org/10.1088/0741-3335/56/6/064002), reports 3D magnetic-field/flow volumes: 16 planes × 4,282 positions = 68,512 points and 10,240 time steps at 0.64 μs for the helium case; 14 planes × 1,681 positions = 23,534 points and 12,288 steps for the argon case. The 3-axis magnetic probes are 3 mm, network-analyzer tested flat to 5 MHz, with a reported measurement accuracy greater than 100 μG and 16-bit digitization.

Critical limitation: the 3D maps are reconstructed from conditionally averaged data, using 15 shots per location; some poorly correlated shots are excluded. The authors explicitly note that event-specific topology can disappear in the average and that 10^4–10^5 detectors would be needed to capture every occurrence. The total electric field was not measured; only an inductive component was calculated, with a potentially important electrostatic-gradient contribution omitted.

BaPSF's [data-management policy](https://plasma.physics.ucla.edu/data-management.html) says run data are HDF5, per-channel probe/calibration/circuit/gain metadata are the user's responsibility, the PI has exclusive use, and community reuse requires PI permission; archived copies are available on request to the original user group. No public raw-data accession was identified. The 2009 cited B-dot calibration paper (DOI [10.1063/1.3246785](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3246785)) describes a related 2.5 mm probe, whereas the 2014 experiment reports 3 mm probes; exact per-probe constants for the 2014 campaign remain unverified.

**Disposition:** valuable laboratory geometry comparator, but data access and event-level reconstruction are blocked. A magnetic plasma flux rope is not a material climbing rope; no WWRD mechanical equivalence is asserted.
