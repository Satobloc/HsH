## 4. Research result: role routes versus dynamic group histories

The new source is Yamamoto, Tanikawa, and Kuzuoka, “A scoping review of trust in firsthand and secondhand communication involving humans and artificial intelligence,” Discover Artificial Intelligence 6, article 1445, published 2026-10-12, DOI 10.1007/s44163-026-02469-5.

The full open-access article was read, including methods, findings, discussion, and limitations. It screened 3,810 records and coded 124 published studies; it organized 21 factors into four overlapping dimensions (Decision Maker, Messenger, Message Property, Environment), six minimal route motifs (two dyads and four triads with a human decision maker), and four qualitative secondhand-route patterns (distorted trust calibration, trust transfer, extremity amplification, accountability diffusion).

The review explicitly states that its dimensions overlap and are not independent variables, and that its minimal motifs do not represent whole networks or automatically compose into larger network/trust processes. The search cutoff was 2025-02-01/02; only English-language published versions were included, preprints excluded, Google Scholar result counts capped, and the first author led screening/coding without independent recoding or reported intercoder reliability. The prior h916 Mira-labeled Review-MIND report #1956 already covered this study under source-lineage/selective-revision questions; this pass uses it only as a crosswalk to the new route-topology review.

The full arXiv PDF was inspected. It reports 127 humans and 65 LLM agents, eight conditions, 24-person sessions, 40 rounds, random pairwise interactions, and one tangram stimulus. The relationship between agent proportion and semantic convergence is non-monotonic; at 75% agent participation the reported consensus-strength measure is 0.725 versus 0.695 for the pure-human baseline. ## 5. New methodological proposal

Proposed a role-typed temporal claim-interaction multigraph. It separates:

- actor identity and human/AI/model-version class;
- event-specific role (source, intermediary, messenger, recipient, decision maker, peer);
- claim-instance and source-root lineage;
- input/output claim IDs for each transformation;
- transformation type (verbatim pass, summary, translation, ranking, interpretation, correction, rewording);
- recipient/exposure set;
- timestamp, round, context/prompt version, feedback;
- decision, confidence, endorsement, and responsibility attribution.

A coarse projection from detailed event history to a minimal route motif is lossy if it discards cycles, parallel paths, repeated exposure, feedback, source roots, or claim changes. Composing motifs into a group history requires explicit interface maps: output claim to next input claim, preserved source roots and transformation lineage, compatible event order/context, and explicit exposure/feedback paths. No braid-theory or physical-topology equivalence is asserted.
