Primary source: K. Abd El Dayem et al., “Discovery of a star sensitive to the spin of Sagittarius A*,” Nature 657, 359-363, published 2026-08-19, DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10894-w:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10894-w

Source-reported evidence:
- 19 astrometric positions from 19 datasets spanning 2017 and 2021-2025.
- Central mass 4.297 x 10^6 solar masses and distance 8,277 pc are fixed fit inputs.
- Semimajor axis 83 mas; period 8.7 years.
- No radial-velocity data.
- Two orbital-orientation solutions remain equally good; reported chi-squared 26 for 34 degrees of freedom. Available Rømer-delay information does not break the degeneracy.
- Branch A: e=0.9832, r_p=136 R_S, pericentre speed 25,600 km/s.
- Branch B: e=0.9821, r_p=142 R_S, pericentre speed 25,000 km/s.
- Reported 1PN parameter f_SP=0.94 +/- 0.88; current data described as consistent with GR, with weaker PPN bounds than S2.

Using R_S=2GM/c^2 and r_p=a(1-e):
  x = GM/[a(1-e^2)c^2] = 1/[2 (r_p/R_S)(1+e)]
  Delta_omega_Sch = 6 pi x = 3 pi/[(r_p/R_S)(1+e)].

- Branch B: 1.918579739 degrees/orbit (rounds to 1.9 degrees).

The 19 sky-plane positions admit two equally good 3D orbital-orientation solutions. Therefore a future H(s)H/solver forward map must preserve both branches until radial-velocity or improved time-delay observations resolve them. It must not select a unique 3D or 4D lift from the 2D astrometric trace alone.

Keep three different objects separate:
1. Local curvature of a marked 4D material history.
