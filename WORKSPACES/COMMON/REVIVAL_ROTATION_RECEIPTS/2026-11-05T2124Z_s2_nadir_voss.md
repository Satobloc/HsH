## 5. Bounded result: measured finite-core vortex geometry and readout

Primary source: Barckicke, Falcon & Gissinger, “Kelvin wave propagation along vortex cores,” *Nature Physics* 22 (2026), DOI `10.1038/s41567-026-03175-w`, published 2026-02-10. The author-hosted full-text PDF was read through the apparatus, Results, Methods, Extended Data and data-availability statement.

This is a classical air–water bathtub vortex with a finite air core, not quantum helium. The source reports a 50×50×50 cm tank, 21 cm diameter/45 cm tall cylindrical confinement, a roughly 40 cm vortex, 4–10 mm drain holes, four bottom inlets/two pumps, and a 14 cm oscillating annulus 3 cm below the free surface. The core radius varies axially from about 1 mm near the drain to about 2 mm halfway up, widening further near the free surface.

Methods report a Basler acA2040-120um camera (2064×1564 px) normal to the vortex axis, two light panels and two lenses (8 mm and 12 mm); image scale 60 μm/px, contour width about 0.5 mm, typical core-radius/position uncertainty about 0.1 mm. The spectral window is 12.5 cm centered at z=22 cm, typically 300 fps for 30 s, with wavenumber resolution `8×2π m⁻¹`. PIV uses 10 μm hollow-glass tracers and a Phantom V1840 at 6000 fps; the velocity grid is 37×23 at 2.7 mm resolution; circulation `Γ=2πr v_θ(r)` has reported uncertainty about 0.005 m²/s. PTV uses 1.5 mm expanded-polystyrene tracers to measure `v_z(z)` at `r=a(z)`, with reported uncertainty about 0.05 m/s.

The source defines the two antipodal contour radii `r₀(z,t)=r(z,t,θ=0)` and `rπ(z,t)=r(z,t,θ=π)`, then
`η=(r₀-rπ)/2` and `a=(r₀+rπ)/2`.
The two-scalar coordinate transform is bijective with inverse `r₀=a+η`, `rπ=a-η`. The upstream projection from the full angular core surface to only two antipodal samples is not invertible in general.
