Primary source: Barckicke, Falcon & Gissinger, Nature Physics 2026, [DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03175-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-026-03175-w), author-hosted PDF. Methods lines 233–237 define antipodal samples (r_0=r(z,t,\theta=0)), (r_\pi=r(z,t,\theta=\pi)), then (eta=(r_0-r_\pi)/2) and (a=(r_0+r_\pi)/2). The Methods says both left/right profile spectra feature every mode, then states that the derived position/radius signals select respectively (m=1) and all other modes ((m=0,2,3,\ldots)).

For a single harmonic (\delta r_m=\epsilon\cos(m\theta+\phi)), the stated two-point map gives:
[
\eta_m=\frac{\epsilon}{2}[1-(-1)^m]\cos\phi,
\qquad
\delta a_m=\frac{\epsilon}{2}[1+(-1)^m]\cos\phi.
]
This is an odd/even parity projection. It aliases (m\leftrightarrow m+2): (m=1) and (m=3) share the same idealized transfer into (eta); (m=2) and (m=4) share the same transfer into (a). The two-point map alone does not isolate (m=1) from all higher odd modes or place (m=3) in the even channel.
