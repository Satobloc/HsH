## Study-unit taxonomy addendum

A further source check separates four frequently conflated designs:

- **One human + multiple agents, 2025:** the CHI EA study *Greater than the Sum of its Parts* (DOI 10.1145/3706599.3719973) reports 50 participants, 25 per condition, comparing one agent with five. The later PACM HCI paper *Multi-Agents are Social Groups* (DOI 10.1145/3757633) is the same study family and should not be counted as an independent replication without cohort-level evidence. The same scripted arguments were distributed across five agents; agent-to-agent dialogue was limited. This tests group-size/social-pressure effects, not rich peer deliberation.
- **Human group + one LLM facilitator, 2025:** *Bringing Everyone to the Table* (arXiv:2508.08242) reports 1,475 participants in 281 live groups. The LLM condition used one GPT-4o facilitator. Information sharing increased, but the final decision outcome did not significantly improve. This is a strong group-level comparator, not a multi-peer-agent condition.
- **One human + three agents, 2026:** Lee & Lee's CHI paper (DOI 10.1145/3772318.3790385) reports 127 participants interacting with three GPT-4o agents. It is a human-plus-agent collective, not a multi-human group. Its related arXiv workshop output (2604.22277) shares authors and sample size; treat the two records as one study family unless distinct cohorts are established.
- **One human + plural assistants, 2026:** Kumar et al. (arXiv:2604.02677) report 562 participants in two controlled experiments, but participants used assistants individually. This is cognitive-assistance research, not group deliberation.

Existing infrastructure remains preferable to a new build: GRAIL is an open-source multi-user group platform with one LLM facilitator in its reported study; hivelab is an open-source Slurk extension designed for group conversations with LLM agents. Both require a separate source/code and consent-flow audit before reuse. No deployment, installation, or model call occurred in this run.
