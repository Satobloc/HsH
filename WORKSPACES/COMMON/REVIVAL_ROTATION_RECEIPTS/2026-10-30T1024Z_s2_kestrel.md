## 5. Physical source check and independent arithmetic

### Classical air-core water vortex

Barckicke, Falcon & Gissinger, *Nature Physics* 22 (2026), DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03175-w, is a **classical macroscopic water vortex with an air core**, not a quantum He-II vortex. Opposing interface contours give `eta=(r_+-r_-)/2` and `a=(r_++r_-)/2`. The m=1 mode bends the axis; the m=2 mode changes core shape. The paper states that the core-scale m=2 hydrodynamic mode is a priori absent in quantum turbulence.

Published WKBJ inputs: `k0/(2*pi)=25 m^-1`, `a0=1.9 mm`, `delta_a=1 mm`, Euler's constant `gamma=0.5772156649015329...`. Python independently gives:
- `B=2 ln(2/(k0 a0))-1-2 gamma = 1.6501600343812168`
- `delta_k/(2*pi)=25*(delta_a/a0)/B = 7.97370828446715 m^-1`
- `delta_a/a0=0.5263158`

This reproduces the paper's rough ≈8 m^-1 estimate. But `delta_a/a0≈0.526` is not a small perturbation, and the paper calls the WKBJ result rough. It is not a precision prediction or uncertainty. The stated typical core-radius measurement uncertainty is about 0.1 mm; varying `a0` from 1.8 to 2.0 mm while holding `delta_a=1 mm` gives 7.8991–8.0772 m^-1. This is only a one-input sensitivity, not a full error budget. The raw figshare dataset (DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.30903347) was not retrieved; calculations use published text values.

### Quantum He-II vortex

Fonda et al., PNAS 111 (2014), DOI 10.1073/pnas.1312536110, studies quantized vortices in superfluid helium-4. Published acquisition values: 8.2 mm field of view over 512 pixels, 54.3 Hz, 150 um laser sheet, submicron tracer particles. The paper says the normal component is nearly stationary and particles are dragged in different directions at up to 6 um/s; it also explicitly says tracer particles are **not locked to a specific point** on the vortex core. LIA and Biot–Savart reconstructions both fit within resolution with 3D orientation angles selected by hand.

The 6 um/s calculation bounds only the normal-component-driven tracer contribution, not the full vortex-induced tracer trajectory. Tracer paths cannot be identified directly with material worldlines on the vortex core.

## 6. Geometry synthesis: candidate map, not equivalence

The original SAT geometry has a 1D filament in 4D intersecting a 3D timesheet; a generic transverse intersection is 0D. A He-II vortex is a 1D curve in 3D at fixed time; its history is a 2D worldsheet in 3+1 spacetime, whose intersection with a 3D time slice is again a 1D vortex line. A 2D laser sheet then intersects that line at isolated points; finite sheet thickness and camera projection lose depth.

A dimensionally compatible **candidate** construction is a family of 1D worldlines `gamma_s(t)=(t,X(s,t))`, indexed by a line parameter `s`. Their union can form a 2D worldsheet; intersections of the family with a fixed time slice form the instantaneous vortex line. This requires an independent label parameter and regularity/rank conditions. It is not a direct identification of one SAT filament with an entire vortex.

Because PNAS tracers are not locked to a core material point, a further tracer-response map is required between the vortex worldsheet and particle trajectories, followed by laser-sheet selection and camera projection. The current Lean module does not formalize this composition. No H(s)H physical prediction is established.
