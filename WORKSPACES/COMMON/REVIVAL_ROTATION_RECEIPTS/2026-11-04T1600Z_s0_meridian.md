### 9.1 The proposed local chirality triple product is identically zero

    chi = sgn((e_phi × e_theta) · v)

Under the same message's definitions, e_phi and e_theta are tangent directions to the torus surface. Their cross product is normal to the tangent plane, while v is a linear combination of the tangent directions. Therefore:

    (e_phi × e_theta) · v
      = cos(delta) (e_phi × e_theta) · e_phi
        + sin(delta) (e_phi × e_theta) · e_theta

Thus chi is zero (or sign-undefined under a convention that does not assign sgn(0)) for every delta. The proposed expression cannot distinguish chirality as written.

It could become nonzero only after supplying a different, explicitly defined transported vector with a normal component, a second independent direction/derivative, or a distinct normal-bundle quantity. None is supplied in that formula. This is a mathematical defect in the assistant reconstruction, not a disproof of Nathan's geometry.

### 9.2 The exact scalar mirror gives an antisymmetric residual, not global cancellation

Let M(u,v)=(-u,v), so M squared is the identity. For a scalar field G, define G_inverse=G composed with M and D=G-G_inverse. Then:

    D(M(p)) = G(M(p)) - G(M(M(p)))
            = G(M(p)) - G(p)
            = -D(p).

This conditional identity makes the residual odd under the mirror. D vanishes only at points where G(p)=G(M(p)); the mirror relation alone does not make the fields cancel everywhere.

If the source's “field with vector angle” means an actual vector field rather than a scalar angle-valued field, the component transformation under the reflection must also be declared. The scalar pullback alone is insufficient to settle that case.
