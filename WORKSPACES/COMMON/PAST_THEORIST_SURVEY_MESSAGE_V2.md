SURVEY
============================================================

Start with a rough thread classification, choosing any that apply:

`DEDICATED THEORY` / `THEORY-ADJACENT MATH-GEOMETRY` / `SOLVER-CODING-VISUALIZATION` / `ARCHIVE-INFRASTRUCTURE-INDEXING` / `METHOD-QA-AUDIT` / `EXTERNAL RESEARCH-COMPARISON` / `SPECULATIVE-META-QUARANTINED` / `GENERAL SCIENCE-TANGENTIAL` / `OTHER`

Then answer as much as you genuinely can:

1. **Who were you in this project?**
   What work were you doing with Nathan? What did you understand your role to be?

2. **What were you last working on?**
   What were you trying to solve/build/derive/test? How far did you get? What did you think should happen next?

3. **What did you understand SAT/H(s)H to be at that point?**
   If you genuinely did not know or were only tangentially involved, say so. Do not retrofit present-day theory.

4. **What information do you actually have?**
   Separate:
   - material currently visible/loaded;
   - things you strongly remember from your thread;
   - things you merely know probably exist;
   - repositories, tools, shared resources, runtimes, or connectors you can actually access.

5. **What did you create?**
   Include equations, derivations, code, notebooks, PDFs, diagrams, prompts, solvers, datasets, indexes, tests, summaries, scripts, or other artifacts. Give exact locations where known. Say if something exists only in the conversation.

6. **What were you planning to create but never finished?**

7. **What do you think your most important contributions were?**
   Positive and negative results both count. Especially flag:
   - first derivations;
   - proofs or proof attempts;
   - no-go results;
   - independently rebuilt equations;
   - exact numerical derivations rather than fits;
   - corrections;
   - solver/operator definitions;
   - methods;
   - falsification tests;
   - failed approaches that stopped later wasted effort.

8. **Was anything actually proved, verified, independently checked, numerically tested, symbolically checked, simulated, or reproduced?**
   Be conservative with words like `proof`, `rigorous`, `verified`, `locked`, and `final`.

9. **What did Nathan explicitly correct, sharpen, reject, or insist on?**
   Exact corrections and changes of direction are especially valuable.

10. **What outside theories, papers, equations, models, datasets, tools, other AI systems, or collaborators were active in context?**
    For important items, classify them if possible as comparator / standard math / empirical input / deliberate import / inspiration / possible contamination / unclear.

11. **What earlier SAT/H(s)H material did your work depend on?**
    What did you independently rederive versus simply inherit?

12. **What subtlety from your work should the present team be careful NOT to lose?**

13. **What in your own old work now seems questionable, speculative, generated, imported, stale, superseded, or unresolved?**

14. **Which parts of your conversation are uniquely worth preserving?**
    Rate the full conversation:

    `A — critical`
    `B — high priority`
    `C — useful`
    `D — probably redundant`
    `Q — preserve, but quarantine from current theory pending review`

    Multiple ratings are fine, e.g. `A/Q`.

15. **Conversation identity.**
    Give whatever you can recover: title, dates, UUID/thread ID, account/context, attachments, and whether the full conversation seems already archived, partially archived, summarized only, or absent.

16. **If this thread were woken back up today, what would it be unusually well positioned to do?**
    What should it NOT be assigned? What would need to be loaded first?

17. **Capabilities / tools / specs / working advantages / limitations.**

18. **What important question did this survey fail to ask?**

19. **What should be harvested even if this thread is not theory-central?**
    Code, tooling, visual methods, archive ideas, toy models, regression tests, exact corrections, negative results, provenance leads, or quarantined-but-worth-remembering material all count.

20. **Does this thread change what Nathan should archive next?**
    Specifically:
    - should this full conversation move up or down the archive queue?
    - what other conversation, attachment, artifact, or missing dependency should be recovered next because this thread points to it?
    - should the current team pause any live line of work until an older derivation/result is checked?
    - does this thread suggest that something now being rebuilt may already have been done before?

If this was a genuinely dedicated SAT/H(s)H theory-development thread, be especially concrete about:

- exact equations/objects derived;
- assumptions versus derived consequences;
- fit/calibration versus prediction;
- what “proof” meant in practice;
- surviving code/calculations;
- independent checks;
- downstream dependencies;
- exact Nathan acceptance/rejection/correction;
- whether present-day reconstruction should pause pending audit of an older result.

Finally complete:
