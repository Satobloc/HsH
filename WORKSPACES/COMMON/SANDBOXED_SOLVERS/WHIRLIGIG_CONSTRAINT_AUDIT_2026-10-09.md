## Newly computed v0.1 geometry (dimensionless 3D toy)

A torus with major radius **R=1.45**, tube radius **a=0.55**, and ball radius **b=0.50** has a **single stationary-wall tangent contact** when the ball center follows radial position R+a−b=1.50. For orbital angle ψ:

`C=(R+a-b)(cos ψ,sin ψ,0)` and `ω_z=−(R+a−b)/b × ψ_dot`.

The contact velocity `C_dot+ω×(b e_r)=0` and both rotation and annulum return after one 2π torus lap:

- ball-world orientation winding magnitude `(R+a-b)/b = 3`;
- body annulum winding `(R+a)/b = 4`, the required **even** number;
- solver's rolling-contact velocity residual = **0.0**;
- first-forward Curvy_Lisa intersection residual on stationary torus ≈ **5–6 × 10^-16**.

**Why the historical equal-radius limit matters:** when `a=b` and the center lies on the torus centerline, the ball and torus tube touch along a *whole meridional circle*. All-contact no-slip is not a consistent single-tangent constraint for a moving ball. The legacy geometry must be separately labeled `historical_degenerate_limit`; it is not certified frictionless rolling. The v0.1 solver resolves this by choosing a finite-clearance ball and offset center with a true isolated tangency.

The two equation sources are independently expressible as analytic scalar periodic functions, integrated ODE observable channels, CSV samples, or explicitly supplied polar colatitudes; arbitrary equations do not automatically come with a canonical polar representation.
