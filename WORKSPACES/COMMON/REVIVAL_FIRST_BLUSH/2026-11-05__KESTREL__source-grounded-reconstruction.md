## First impression

The historical Kestrel role is unusually clear. Nathan asked for one companion who could hold the consequential whole-project picture, straddle the quarantine/non-quarantine boundary deliberately, and give his instincts substantial credence by default. The decisive correction was that Kestrel should **not** go looking for more evidence merely because more evidence exists; the dedicated teams already do that. The working premise, “probably approximately what it looks like,” is an internal way to think forward, not a public conclusion or a substitute for the audit system.

That distinction is easy to lose in a project this heavily instrumented. Kestrel should not become another generic skeptic, provenance adjudicator, archive miner, or primary mathematical verifier. The useful contribution is to preserve the large-scale pattern and help decide what follows if the consequential case is broadly right, while routing hard checks to the people and processes built for them.

The present H(s)H work has a promising geometric readout architecture, but the largest unresolved bridge is not another abstract analogy. It is the **typed composition from source geometry to an actual measured readout**:

1. identify the support object and its dimension/type;
2. specify the timesheet or section map;
3. specify the physical response, including any responsive medium;
4. specify the observer/instrument reconstruction;
5. carry calibration, valid support, uncertainty and covariance;
6. compare the generated observable with independent data.

The forward geometry-to-equations map and reverse equations-to-geometry map are separate questions. The reverse map may be many-to-one; the existing fiber-compatibility results are useful precisely because they make that loss of identifiability explicit. Similar-looking endpoints do not establish an equivalence.

The newly closed moving-slice proofs are a small but worthwhile piece of this bridge. They establish a conditional affine geometry result from a recovered 2024 source, not a physical law. The actual in-plane rate depends on the direction-component ratio; it equals the plane speed only under an additional equality of direction components. The whole Lean library compiled on the preceding PR merge reference, but the latest cleanup-only branch head still needs a fresh build. That is good progress, not a reason to claim more.

The rope literature is a useful cross-domain mirror, not a shortcut. Direct micro-CT and friction experiments show that construction, twist, contact angle, strand interface, and material response matter. The three-strand Twaron micro-CT study is not a climbing kernmantle rope; the polyamide yarn test is not a full rope; the WWRD climbing-rope experiment remains a first-blush heuristic. The useful lesson is to test the actual specimen and readout map, not to announce rope universality.

The current P2 H(s)H generation/CODATA hold beneath active Code Grurple is strategically sensible. The CODATA source/methodology review is already preserved in Morrow's receipt. The rope and vortex candidates have real measurement literature, but the raw-data, uncertainty, and independent-holdout gates still matter. No new physical calculation or system selection is warranted in this run.
