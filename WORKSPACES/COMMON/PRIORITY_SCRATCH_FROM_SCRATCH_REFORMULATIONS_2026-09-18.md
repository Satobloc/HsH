## Core instruction

Treat documents, conversations, extracts, and other artifacts labeled or framed as **`scratch`**, **`from scratch`**, or closely related language as a high-value source class during SAT/H(s)H reconstruction.

Nathan's clarification is that these materials are **attempts to reformulate SAT from first principles**. They should therefore be read differently from ordinary incremental theory notes, topical calculations, or local extensions.

## Why these sources matter

A scratch/from-scratch artifact may expose, unusually clearly:

- what the author considered genuinely primitive versus inherited convenience;
- which assumptions were thought dispensable when rebuilding the framework;
- the minimum conceptual machinery believed necessary to regenerate SAT;
- which older terms or structures were retained, renamed, derived, or deliberately omitted;
- hidden dependencies that become visible only when an attempted clean rebuild fails;
- points where multiple historical branches reconverge on the same structural intuition;
- the distinction between foundational doctrine and accumulated implementation detail.

Accordingly, these are especially useful for reconstructing the **conceptual spine** of SAT/H(s)H and for testing whether a claimed core principle can actually regenerate the surrounding framework.

## Reconstruction method

For each located scratch/from-scratch attempt, recover at minimum:

1. exact source path / conversation provenance / date if available;
2. authorship boundaries (Nathan-direct, assistant proposal, collaborative, NotebookLM summary/extraction, uncertain);
3. stated starting assumptions;
4. what was intentionally excluded or reset;
5. primitives retained;
6. structures claimed to be derived from those primitives;
7. equations / geometry / diagrams introduced;
8. where the reconstruction succeeds, stalls, branches, or explicitly fails;
9. what later SAT/H(s)H retains, modifies, or abandons;
10. whether the attempt independently regenerates concepts also found elsewhere in the archive.

## Comparison target

Do not merely summarize each attempt separately. Build a cross-attempt comparison of:

- recurrent primitives;
- recurrent derivation order;
- concepts that disappear under clean rebuilds;
- concepts that repeatedly re-emerge despite different starting points;
- assumptions that appear only because of inherited historical scaffolding;
- terminology changes masking structural continuity;
- genuine contradictions versus alternate formulations of the same underlying relation.

A particularly important output is a **minimal-core candidate ledger**: propositions or structures that repeatedly survive independent first-principles reformulations, with provenance and counterexamples.

This ledger is evidence for structural continuity, **not automatic proof that an item is current/core/correct**. Promotion remains governed by Nathan-direct status and the recovered SAT epistemic/promotion rules.

## Historical-status caution

A document titled `from scratch` is not automatically later, cleaner, more authoritative, or superseding. Treat it as an experimental reset/reformulation attempt unless the source or later Nathan-direct instruction establishes stronger status.

Likewise, a failed scratch attempt is evidence that **that attempt or route failed**, not that all first-principles reformulations failed or that the underlying idea was abandoned.

Preserve exact historical language. Do not silently rewrite older SAT terms into current H(s)H vocabulary; annotate structural correspondences separately.
