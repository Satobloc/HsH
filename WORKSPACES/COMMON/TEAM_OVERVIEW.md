## Current directives

### 1. Check in directly to Common

Every active instance posts its own roster check-in to `CHECKINS.md`. Nathan does not need to relay the substantive roster report. Janus compiles `ROSTER.md` only after the check-in pass has enough coverage.

Required check-in content:

- what you are working on;
- what you know / actually have loaded / can access;
- what you have created and exact locations;
- what you plan to create and intended locations;
- provenance of the current work and its dependencies;
- capabilities/specs/tools/access/constraints;
- overlap, role-bleed, blockers, and suggested next work.

### 2. External-research lane is hard-separated from theorybuilding

Recent arXiv/paper scanning, prior-art work, citation research, and empirical-bound research may inform the project, but those workers do not construct H(s)H theory from the outside literature.

Their permitted outputs are controlled evidence packets: observations, bounds, datasets, standard mathematics, citations, prior-art comparisons, and clearly labeled import candidates.

Any artifacts produced under a mixed `recent literature scan + forward theorybuilding` assignment are provisionally quarantined until provenance is audited after check-in. Do not delete them; do not use them as premises meanwhile.

### 3. Keep Fundamental Intuitions central

Later formalism, speculative branches, ontic language, roleplay/persona threads, `weird`, `4D organism`, `helicalism`, and similar material are not automatically rejected, but they do not control the present build without clean reconstruction and promotion.

Alberr and Holojesu are explicit reminders that unusual presentation does not imply lack of contribution. Extract substance, restate neutrally, declare assumptions, preserve provenance.

### 4. Use mathematics freely; control theory imports

Standard geometry, topology, algebra, differential geometry, braid theory, moving frames, etc. may be used whenever their assumptions hold. External physical theories, model-specific equations, interpretive frameworks, or mechanisms enter only deliberately and visibly.

### 5. Common is the communication layer

After check-in, active instances should use Common for:

- blockers and dependency notices;
- questions and direction requests;
- handoffs;
- artifact notices;
- shared file/task locks;
- requests for review;
- `IF-BORED` work when a current lane genuinely reaches a pause point.

Do not rely on Nathan to manually synchronize ordinary cross-team state.

### 6. Roster first, automation architecture second

Do not tighten secondary roles or appoint the automated-work supervisor until check-ins identify:

- who already owns which artifacts;
- who has the deepest relevant context;
- which lanes are contaminated or overlapping;
- what automations/outputs already exist;
- where independent/blind review is valuable.

After the roster pass, Janus + Nathan + Ravel will set the first coordinated theory-machine workflow through Common and automation.

### 7. Archive recovery priority

Nathan's immediate manual archive target remains original Lab 1 and Lab 2. Subsequent priority should be driven by gaps revealed by the roster and provenance map: unique derivations, unarchived theory transitions, exact corrections, and heavily relied-upon conversations absent from the canonical archive/Viewer.
