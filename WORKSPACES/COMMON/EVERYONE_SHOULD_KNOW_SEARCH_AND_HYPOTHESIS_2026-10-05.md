# Everyone should know — search, citation, and hypothesis handling

**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Scope:** SAT/H(s)H research and archive work.

1. **Do not inflate specificity during source search.** A vague reference to an author/topic is not permission to invent an exact title and then treat failure of that guessed title as evidence of absence.
2. **Identifiers control when present.** DOI, arXiv ID, ISBN, repository SHA/path or other stable identifiers take precedence over remembered wording. Resolve the identifier first.
3. **Use multiple discovery keys before absence claims.** Search identifier -> authors -> distinctive topic/phrase -> date range -> archive neighborhood/adjacency -> indexes/extracted text. A title-only miss is not a negative result.
4. **Search project resources before giving up.** HSH_RESOURCES indexes/full text, HsH, the historical SAT archive, and the current Bigbook/index surfaces are all discovery routes. Current web research is a later route when available and needed.
5. **Distinguish bibliographic identification from scientific endorsement.** A valid arXiv/DOI can settle what paper is being referenced without making its claims true or making an SAT comparison valid.
6. **Conversation titles are weak negative evidence.** Theory content often lives in conversations whose titles are unrelated.
7. **Hypothesis means pursue/test.** "Do not treat as established" controls claims; it does not mean "do not calculate this route." Speculations are candidates for explicit geometry, derivation, benchmark comparison and kill tests.
8. **Preserve alternatives.** Geometry and empirical constraints discriminate mechanisms; authority, familiarity or aesthetic preference do not.
9. **Record nulls.** If the mapped benchmark does not force a candidate structure, say so rather than manufacturing one.
10. **PRIOR_ART remains quarantined.** None of these search rules weaken its gate.
