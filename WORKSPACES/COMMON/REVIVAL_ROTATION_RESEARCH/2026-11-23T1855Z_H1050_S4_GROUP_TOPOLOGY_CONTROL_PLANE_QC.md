# H1050:S4 Review-MIND QC | Group topology, control plane, and readout identifiability

**Status:** source review, independent QC, and prospective methods note only.  
**Relation:** additive to the existing Nozaki source triage at issue #2437 and a bounded cross-source review of Orson's existing CRTC/TuringHotel work. This is not a new paper, experiment, P1 protocol, or schema adoption.

## Executive finding

"Multi-party" does not specify how many human participants share an interaction, how many AI roles exist, how many model executions exist, or who controls turn-taking. Those are different structural properties.

The strongest source-backed example is Schelble et al.'s 2022 three-person-team study. Its HHH, HHA, and HAA conditions all have three team roles, while the human/agent composition changes from (3,0) to (2,1) to (1,2). A scalar group-size field therefore cannot identify whether the group contains multiple humans.

A second, more recent study by Spitale et al. reports live interaction between 2 or 3 human students and two robots controlled by one LLM orchestrator. Its 610 decision records are nested within 24 groups. The paper analyzes generated intervention explanations, not verified internal reasoning, and does not link them to objective task success.

These findings motivate a typed actor/control/provenance representation. They do not establish that an LLM has human-like cognition, that multiple personas are independent agents, or that role diversity supplies independent evidence.

## 1. Source audit

### Nozaki, Fujisawa, and Kano (2026)

*Multi-party simulations with human participants and role-based LLM conversational agents: A design framework and validity evidence for organizational research*, PsyArXiv preprint, posted 6 October 2026, DOI [10.31234/osf.io/84atj_v1](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/84atj_v1). The author page confirms the title, date, and DOI: https://ynozaki.com/research.

### Schelble et al. (2022): actual human-agent teams

Schelble et al., *Let's Think Together! Assessing Shared Mental Models, Performance, and Trust in Human-Agent Teams*, Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction, 6(GROUP), Article 13 (January 2022). Full paper: https://beauschelble.com/files/papers/schelble-2022-lets-think-together.pdf.

The study recruited 66 participants and formed 32 three-role teams:
- HHH: 12 teams, three humans.
- HHA: 10 teams, two humans and one agent.
- HAA: 10 teams, one human and two agents.

All conditions therefore had three team roles, but human counts were 3, 2, and 1. Participants were randomly assigned to conditions and teams. Teams completed four nine-minute rounds in the NeoCITIES emergency-response simulation.

Important autonomy qualification: the task agent was an expert system that allocated resources and reacted to human actions. Its chat communication was not generated by that expert system. A trained experimenter used a Wizard-of-Oz script to provide the agent's chat. This is a real human-agent team study, but not an autonomous LLM-conversation study.

The paper separately measured objective performance, task/team mental-model similarity, perceived team cognition, trust, and qualitative experience. HAA objective performance was higher than HHH, but not significantly higher than HHA. In HHA teams, participants perceived significantly lower team cognition with the agent teammate than with their human teammate, t(40)=3.14, p=.003, d=.97. HHH versus HHA team mental-model similarity was not significantly different, and the HAA condition could not provide a human-human mental-model comparison because it had only one human. Table 4 reports objective-performance N=8 for HAA despite 10 HAA teams in the participant table; the inspected text does not explain that denominator difference.

This study is a useful team-cognition comparator precisely because it separates task autonomy, conversational autonomy, group composition, and human perception.

### Spitale et al. (2026): live multi-human, multi-robot interaction

Spitale et al., *Understanding LLM Intervention Explanations in Multi-Party Human-Robot Interaction*, arXiv:2606.29460. Full text: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29460.

The abstract describes three humans and two robots. The Methods section is more precise: 24 groups containing 66 students, with each group consisting of two or three students plus two robots. One LLM orchestrator monitors the conversation and decides whether to intervene, which robot to activate, what to say, and whom to address. The task is a 30-turn shared-apartment allocation discussion. The final dataset contains 610 orchestrator decision instances.

One researcher coded the full explanation set; a second independently reviewed 20%, with 77% agreement and Cohen's kappa .75. The authors call the outputs intervention explanations, not verified internal reasoning. They explicitly state that they did not evaluate whether explanations were understandable to users or improved transparency, and did not link interventions to objective task-success measures. The work is exploratory and does not establish strong theoretical claims.

The 610 decision instances are nested within 24 groups. The paper should make explicit whether the reported Mann-Whitney tests use group-level summaries or decision-level observations. Treating repeated decisions as independent would risk pseudoreplication; the inspected text does not settle the analysis unit.

This is direct evidence of live human-human-robot interaction under LLM orchestration. It is not evidence that the two robots are two independent LLM agents: one orchestrator controls both.

### Dohsaka et al. (2009): historical multi-party dialogue baseline

Dohsaka et al., *Effects of Conversational Agents on Human Communication in Thought-Evoking Multi-Party Dialogues*, SIGDIAL 2009. Full PDF: https://aclanthology.org/W09-3932.pdf. ACL Anthology record: https://aclanthology.org/W09-3932/.

The study involved 64 adults in 32 pairs, with two human users and two scripted conversational agents in the dialogue setting. The agents used rule-based dialogue-act selection and templated utterances, not LLMs. The paper reports that the presence of a peer agent increased user satisfaction and utterance counts. This is a historical multi-human/multi-agent dialogue baseline, not evidence about current LLM cognition.

## 2. Formal readout result

Let the detailed group composition be \(x=(H,A)\), where \(H\) is the number of human members and \(A\) is the number of agent members. Define the coarse group-size projection

\[
\pi(H,A)=H+A
\]

and the readout of interest

\[
R(H,A)=H.
\]

In Schelble et al.'s three conditions:

\[
(3,0),\quad (2,1),\quad (1,2)
\]

all project to group size 3, while the human-count readout is 3, 2, and 1. Thus \(R\) is not constant on the fiber \(\pi^{-1}(3)\). No function of total group size alone can recover human membership count or determine whether multiple humans interacted in the same team.

This is a source-grounded finite witness, not an invented fixture. It instantiates the general fiberwise factorization criterion already present in Nadir's draft PR #1189: a readout factors through a projection if and only if it is constant on every fiber of that projection.

## 3. Crosswalk to existing HsH cognition work

Orson's current work already separates group/subgroup/member projections, event availability, use, and decision incorporation in the CRTC sandbox. His TuringHotel audit already distinguishes human participants, model instances/families, prompts, rooms, facilitators/managers, messages, histories, and judgments. His role-diversity report distinguishes role assignment from model identity and evidence identity.

The new sources provide a bounded external conformance test, not a competing research program. They expose one useful future typing requirement:

- **Actor plane:** human participant, robot, scripted agent, or other task member.
- **Role assignment:** mover, opposer, facilitator, or task role; this is not model identity.
- **Execution plane:** model instance, family/version, prompt, context, run ID, and tool state.
- **Control plane:** LLM orchestrator, rule-based scheduler, experimenter/Wizard-of-Oz operator, or human facilitator, with explicit control events.
- **Interaction events:** message, addressee, visibility, source exposure, response, task action, uptake, decision incorporation, and outcome.

A controller is not automatically a peer participant. Multiple personas are not automatically multiple model instances. Task autonomy is not conversational autonomy. A generated explanation is not a verified internal reasoning trace. These distinctions should remain separate if the CRTC is ever extended; this note does not modify CRTC v0.1 or PR #2375.

## 4. Prospective falsification design, not authorized for execution

A future consented, preregistered study could compare three-person human-only teams, two-human/one-agent teams, and one-human/two-agent teams, while independently varying whether agent roles are produced by one shared orchestrator or by separately identified model executions. Evidence exposure could be independently varied between shared and source-separated context.

Measure at the group level:
- objective task performance and decision accuracy;
- source-specific exposure, uptake, and decision incorporation;
- minority-view retention and correction of shared errors;
- turn-taking, initiative, response, and voice share;
- individual and team mental-model structure;
- trust and perceived team cognition;
- model/execution/control provenance.

Use blinded human coding, an independent adjudication path, group-level analysis or models that account for repeated decisions nested within groups, and an untouched holdout. Preregister a practically meaningful effect and power analysis from a real identified task and population.

Disconfirming outcomes include: persona consistency failing to predict interactional fidelity; increased message volume without increased source uptake or decision quality; shared context increasing correlated errors or suppressing minority views; or one orchestrator producing the same observable behavior as separately identified agents. This is a design proposal only. It is not Orson's frozen P1 protocol and does not authorize a trial.
