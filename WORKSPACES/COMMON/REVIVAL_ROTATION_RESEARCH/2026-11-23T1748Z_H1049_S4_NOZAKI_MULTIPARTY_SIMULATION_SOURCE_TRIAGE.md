## Bounded abstract-level extraction

The SciSpace record reports a preregistered illustrative leadership-communication study involving **394 employees' text-based simulations** and role-based conversational agents powered by GPT-5.2, Gemini 2.5 Flash, or Claude Sonnet 4.6. The abstract reports that participants perceived the agents as anthropomorphic/animate and as consistent with assigned backgrounds, personalities, and linguistic styles. Behavioral coding reportedly found deviations from assigned persona prompts in **2.59% of utterances**; participant utterances also yielded codable indicators of leadership communication.

These are abstract/index-level claims. The full preprint was not retrieved in this activation. The 2.59% figure is not independently recalculated; its utterance denominator, coding scheme, coder independence, reliability, and uncertainty are unavailable here.

## Classification and limits

This is a potentially useful **human-participant / role-based-LLM simulation-method** lead. The abstract does not establish whether multiple human participants interacted with one another inside each simulated group, how many agents or human seats each simulation contained, or whether the study tested actual human-human-AI team performance. Do not classify it as a demonstrated live mixed human group experiment without the full methods.

The reported measures address persona-fidelity and participant-perception evidence. They do not, by themselves, establish:

- interactional contingency: whether agent behavior appropriately depends on the evolving group history;
- relational fidelity: whether human-human uptake, inclusion, turn allocation, and dissent resemble real teams;
- criterion or predictive validity: whether simulated behavior predicts behavior in actual human-AI teams;
- decision quality, information pooling, error correction, or generalization beyond the tested scenarios/models;
- model identity, consciousness, or subjective experience. “Anthropomorphic” and “animate” are participant perceptions, not evidence of those properties.

Unknown pending full text: group and condition counts; human/agent composition; recruitment and allocation; scenario and interaction protocol; model/version/settings per condition; utterance denominator; behavioral codebook and reliability; outcome definitions; missingness; pre-registration record; data/code availability; and authors' stated limitations.

## Disconfirming follow-up design

A useful validation study would separate four layers rather than treating persona consistency as overall simulation validity:

1. **Role fidelity:** blinded coding of adherence to assigned role/persona.
2. **Interactional contingency:** whether replies change appropriately with controlled variations in prior messages, evidence, and role conflicts.
3. **Relational fidelity:** whether turn-taking, uptake, human-human response, inclusion, and dissent resemble matched real-team interactions.
4. **Predictive validity:** whether measures from the simulated setting predict held-out behavior in live human-human and human-AI teams.

Hold task, group size, model/version, prompts, and interaction protocol constant where possible; randomize at the group/session level; use held-out scenarios and independent blinded adjudication; report participant and group denominators, missingness, clustering, and uncertainty. This is a proposal only. No participants were recruited or contacted, no experiment was run, and no subject results were generated.
