## 3. Source correction

Three accessible institutional/research records contradict the archived demographic and origin claims:

- [University of Bern: Was ist Unserdeutsch?](https://www.germanistik.unibe.ch/forschung/drittmittelprojekte/unserdeutsch_rabaul_creole_german/was_ist_unserdeutsch/index_ger.html)
- [Maitz & Volker (2017), Documenting unserdeutsch: reversing colonial amnesia](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/52477/), Journal of Pidgin and Creole Languages 32(2), DOI 10.1075/jpcl.32.2.06mai.
- [Leibniz Institute for the German Language, 2024 annual report](https://www.ids-mannheim.de/fileadmin/org/Jahresbericht/IDS_Jahresbericht_2024.pdf), pp. 105–106.

The University of Bern Unserdeutsch project page explains that the Vunapope Catholic mission school on New Britain educated children born from relationships between European or Asian men and Indigenous women. The Vunapope mixed-race community developed Unserdeutsch through contact between their first languages, mainly Tok Pisin, and the German of the missionaries. The page says many community members have no German-speaking ancestors. It estimates about 100 last community members in 2020, mostly in Australian east-coast cities, with only a handful remaining in Papua New Guinea.

Maitz and Volker's 2017 Journal of Pidgin and Creole Languages article describes Unserdeutsch as a German-lexifier creole originating in an orphanage for mixed-race children in German New Guinea, and reports around 100 elderly speakers, nearly all immigrants to Australia.

The Leibniz Institute for the German Language 2024 annual report says fewer than 100 elderly speakers remained, dispersed across Australian east-coast cities. It reports that the Unserdeutsch corpus contains about 80 hours of recordings from 64 people; 49 people and nearly 54 hours were made searchable through the Database for Spoken German for registered users.

The archived claim of 1,000–2,000 speakers in Madang is contradicted by these records by more than an order of magnitude and places the remaining community in the wrong region. “Descendants of German settlers” is also an inaccurate origin summary: the documented founding community was mixed-race, formed around the Vunapope mission, and the primary substrate was Tok Pisin. Speaker figures are dated estimates (2017, 2020 and 2024), not a fresh 2026 census.

The generated passage is not linked to an attested speaker, recording, corpus event or published example. It must be classified as **unverified assistant-generated imitation**, not as an authentic Unserdeutsch specimen or language evidence. No grammar-by-grammar judgment is made here.

The Bern page, JCU article and IDS report are complementary but not fully independent confirmations: they overlap the same documentation project and research network. Their agreement is useful triangulation, not three independent replications.
