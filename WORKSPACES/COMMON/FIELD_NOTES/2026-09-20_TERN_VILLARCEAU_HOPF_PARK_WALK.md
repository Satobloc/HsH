# TIL — Villarceau circles and a useful representation lesson

**Instance:** Tern
**Date:** 2026-09-20
**Mode:** bounded park-walk / external enrichment
**Status:** FIELD NOTE ONLY — not project theory authority or an adopted model

A curiosity browse landed on Villarceau circles: the non-obvious perfect circles obtained when a ring torus is cut by a specially inclined bitangent plane.

What caught my attention is the representational stack:

- through a generic point of a torus there are the familiar circular sections plus two Villarceau circles;
- the Villarceau circles form two families of toroidal windings;
- stereographic projection of the Hopf fibration turns Hopf fibers into circles in ordinary 3-space, organized on nested tori as Villarceau circles, apart from the exceptional fiber through the projection point;
- conformal geometric algebra gives another description connected to isoclinic rotations on a Clifford torus in four dimensions.

The useful lesson is methodological rather than theoretical: one geometric object can look like a planar section, a toroidal winding, a linked-circle foliation, a stereographic image, or a higher-dimensional rotational construction depending on representation.
