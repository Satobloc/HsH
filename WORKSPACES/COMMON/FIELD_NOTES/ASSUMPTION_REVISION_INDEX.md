# Assumption Revision Index | Archive expectation and discovery crosswalk

**Status:** Active *index view* over existing Field Notes, first-blush records, source indexes, and verified historical documents. This is NOT a competing archive, replacement Mersearch engine, new theory authority, or a substitute for raw-source reading.

**Reason:** Nathan Direct, October 25, 2026, instructed the team to make assistant "aha" moments and unexamined assumptions about SAT/H(s)H prominent, durable and actually usable. The danger is not lack of documents alone; it is a worker encountering them with a false default expectation, mistaking its own ignorance for the archive's shallowness, then repeating that misunderstanding to the next worker.

## Required method

Before calling archive material "only scattered references", "merely conceptual", "just analogy", "unworked", "new", "unexpectedly mature", "abandoned", "superseded", "unrelated to SAT", or "already verified":

1. Record the expectation being applied, its origin and what was **actually known** before reading. Distinguish an explicit prior belief from an implication inferred afterward from language.
2. Check current BEDROCK, Nathan Direct, prior high-water-mark and historical orientation, and the appropriate existing indexes. Run Mersearch across all three repositories where available. Search original and changed terminology and hidden conversations; inspect enough primary context to assess substance. A title scan is not a corpus audit.
3. Record actual evidence: exact source path, blob or message ID, date and authorship class, genre, read extent, archive replication, and mathematical or research activity present. Do not conflate the **presence** of mathematics/code/test plans with independent execution, verification, empirical success or current H(s)H applicability.
4. State *met / exceeded / fell short / changed / unresolved* separately, comparing explicitly to the prior expectation and available evidence. Treat a source's own aspirations, its outcomes, and the observer's expectations as separate objects.
5. Cross-link, don't duplicate. Put the worker's attributed full observation in existing `FIELD_NOTES/ENTRIES/` (or `REVIVAL_FIRST_BLUSH/` for a genuine reentry), add one navigation row below, and route substantive facts to Mersearch/source genealogy, Nathan Verified Words, equation/claim ledgers, cross-formalism index, and historical high-water-mark records as appropriate.
6. Link the correction to onboarding, a relevant worker checkpoint or handoff, and concrete cold-start QA. Recheck whether the correction reached **actual worker practice**, not merely a page on disk.

**Standing baseline:** SAT/H(s)H is a multi-year, multi-domain, collaborative research archive containing historical mathematical and experimental development alongside conversations, drafts, corrected errors, nontechnical material, workflows and LLM work. Presume neither correctness nor incompleteness from genre, title, model generation or presentation polish. Source and scope determine all verdicts. Historical high-water marks merit recovery before current workers claim novelty or greater maturity.

**Do not manufacture surprise.** If the worker's initial expectation already anticipated technical depth, say so. If the "surprise" was merely failure to read the expected source, state *newly encountered by this worker*, not *newly discovered in SAT*.
