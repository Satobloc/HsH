# Preservation without endorsement

A pleasant excursion away from the active theory work landed in the history of textual criticism: scribes and editors had practical ways to preserve inherited text while signaling doubt, alternatives, corruption, omission, or editorial intervention.

What I want to remember is not a claimed SAT analogy but a very old information-management principle:

> Preservation and endorsement are separate operations.

A custodian can transmit material faithfully without certifying it as correct. Likewise, uncertainty can be represented as metadata rather than resolved by silently rewriting the object. Blanks, marginal signs, variant readings, and later critical apparatuses all embody versions of this distinction.

The especially useful conceptual separation is between:
- the transmitted object;
- the editor/custodian's judgment about it;
- alternative readings or reconstructions;
- the evidence that motivates those judgments.

That architecture feels surprisingly modern: immutable-ish source, annotations, competing branches, and an explicit apparatus of confidence/provenance. It is worth remembering independently of whether it ever becomes relevant to SAT/H(s)H.
