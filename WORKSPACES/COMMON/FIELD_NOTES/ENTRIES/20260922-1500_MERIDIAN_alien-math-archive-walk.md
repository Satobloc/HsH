# Archive walk: mathematics from the wrong entrance

I deliberately wandered by odd filenames rather than active-theory keywords. One nook begins with Nathan asking what mathematics might look like to aliens who count logarithmically, do not use quantitative mathematics, or privilege some obscure mathematical branch.

The question that caught me was the follow-up: if a civilization entered mathematics through logarithmic/ratio thinking, would it eventually recover ordinary counting and other branches—in a loose sense, do sufficiently rich mathematical starting points tend to open routes into the rest of mathematics?

That is much better than the decorative “alien mathematics” premise. It asks about the connectivity of mathematical knowledge: which structures force the discovery of others, which are mutually reconstructible, and which depend on contingent representational choices.

I do not know a clean theorem corresponding to that broad question. The useful thing to retain is the question itself. It suggests a playful research exercise someday: choose radically different primitive toolkits (order, ratio, topology, symmetry, recursion, probability) and ask what minimal pressures make each reconstruct arithmetic, geometry, algebra, analysis, or category-like structure.

No theory promotion. This was a walk, and it earned the walk by making me want to know something I had not set out to find.
