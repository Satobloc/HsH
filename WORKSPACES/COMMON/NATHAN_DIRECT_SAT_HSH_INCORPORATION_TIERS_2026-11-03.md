## 1. Governing distinction

Geometric admissibility and theoretical incorporation are separate questions.

A construction must first be suggested by, preferably required by, or constrained to a small number of possible forms by the geometry. Only then can it be considered as a candidate explanation or working hypothesis. Explanatory attractiveness does not repair a geometrical mismatch.

Geometry is a visual mathematical system with built-in constraints or "laws" that equations do not necessarily make evident. Equations allow precision and fitting, but they are not the object. The theoretical object is the geometry. A mathematical description without the corresponding geometric object is only a description or handbook of a model, not the object itself.

## 2. Four incorporation tiers

### Tier 1 — Geometric admissibility

**Gate:** If a proposed element is not suggested by, preferably required by, or one of a small number of possible choices constrained by the geometry, it is not a candidate.

If the geometry does support it, it may be suggested for further consideration. Passing Tier 1 does not make it a hypothesis or establish that it is correct.

Questions:
- Is the proposed structure visibly present in, or constrained by, the specified geometry?
- Does the geometry require it, suggest it, or leave only a small set of viable alternatives?
- Is it an arbitrary addition that the geometry neither requires nor constrains?

### Tier 2 — Explanatory candidacy

**Gate:** A Tier 1 construction that offers a neat explanation of a phenomenon, with multiple intuitive interaction surfaces, may become a candidate for one of the tentative SAT or H(s)H hypotheses.

"Multiple intuitive interaction surfaces" should be preserved as Nathan's criterion; do not silently reduce it to a single successful analogy or one-sector fit.

Passing Tier 2 permits tentative candidacy only. It does not establish a working hypothesis as official theory.

### Tier 3 — Independent cross-sector consequence

**Gate:** A Tier 2 candidate remains tentative indefinitely unless and until it unexpectedly explains otherwise unrelated things, not because it was adapted or asked to explain them, but purely as a consequence of its presence in the model. Preferably this occurs across multiple completely different sectors.

This is an emergence/consequence criterion, not a requirement that workers retrofit the hypothesis to additional phenomena. The distinction between a genuine consequence and a newly fitted extension must be maintained.

When this criterion is met, the construction will probably become the "official" SAT/H(s)H working hypothesis, subject to the remaining review and authority controls.

### Tier 4 — Structural indispensability

**Gate:** If removing or changing the construction breaks or precisely distorts all the other numbers in the model by a predictable amount, then it is part of the model.

This is a dependency/sensitivity criterion. It asks whether the construction is structurally integrated into the quantitative model rather than merely compatible with it.

A Tier 4 claim therefore needs a controlled removal or alteration, a declared propagation path, and a predictable quantitative distortion that can be checked against the model's other quantities. It is not enough to assert that the element is important.

## 4. Object hierarchy: five forms of the object

Nathan describes a hierarchy distinguishing the mathematical description from progressively more direct representations or instantiations of the theoretical object:

| Order | Object | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Pentary** | Mathematical description / equations | Precision and fitting; the handbook to the car. Valuable, but a description of a model rather than the object itself. |
| **Quaternary** | Precise static diagram | A precise static geometric representation of the theoretical object. |
| **Tertiary** | Precision dynamic geometry as a software model | A dynamic, executable geometric representation. |
| **Secondary** | Physical working orrery or hands-on demonstration | A physical working model or manipulable demonstration. |
| **Primary** | Observation | The observed physical world/system. |

The ordinal labels and hierarchy are preserved as Nathan stated them. Do not collapse these into a generic "visualization hierarchy": the distinction is between mathematical description, static geometry, dynamic computational geometry, physical model, and observation.

**SAT is the K-T Boundary**, in Nathan's analogy: the historical SAT body is a major stratigraphic boundary in the development/reconstruction of the programme. It is not thereby a reason to erase its source history, nor does the analogy itself specify which SAT mechanisms survive into H(s)H.

## 5. Relationship to the geometry-first Factory Floor admission gate

The separate Geometry-First Admission Gate governs whether an item can enter Factory Floor: it requires a precise geometric plot, hostile fidelity review, and co-located display with the item.

The incorporation tiers govern what theoretical standing a geometrically admissible construction may earn.

These gates must not be conflated:

1. **Geometric fidelity:** Does the plot accurately represent the declared system?
2. **Tier 1:** Does the proposed construction follow from, or become tightly constrained by, that geometry?
3. **Tier 2:** Does it offer a compelling explanation with multiple intuitive interaction surfaces?
4. **Tier 3:** Does it generate otherwise-unrequested consequences across unrelated phenomena/sectors?
5. **Tier 4:** Is it quantitatively indispensable through predictable model-wide propagation under removal or alteration?

A plot can pass hostile fidelity review while the proposed construction fails Tier 1. A construction can pass Tiers 1 and 2 and remain tentative indefinitely. None of the tiers waives independent mathematical, physical, source-provenance, observational, or Nathan-authorized incorporation requirements.

## 6. Required discipline in applying tiers

- Preserve the original geometric object and its visual constraints before writing or fitting equations to it.
- Do not nominate arbitrary structures as candidates merely because they can be made algebraically consistent.
- Distinguish what geometry requires from what it permits, suggests, or leaves undetermined.
- At Tier 2, identify the phenomenon and each of the multiple intuitive interaction surfaces being explained.
- At Tier 3, record the independent phenomenon/sector, show that it was not the motivating target, and trace the consequence from the existing construction without a new fit or ad hoc extension.
- At Tier 4, specify the removal/alteration, affected model quantities, predicted direction and magnitude of distortion, dependencies, and comparison results. Preserve null outcomes and cases where the model does not respond as predicted.
- Never use a higher-tier label as a substitute for showing its evidence.
- Keep the status of each hypothesis explicit. A working hypothesis is not automatically an official working hypothesis.
