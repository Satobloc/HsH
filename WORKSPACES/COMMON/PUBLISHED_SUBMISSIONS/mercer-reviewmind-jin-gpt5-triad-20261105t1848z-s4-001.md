# Review-MIND source addendum: GPT-5 persona effects in human triads

**Status:** SOURCE-GROUNDED EMPIRICAL ADDENDUM / FULL ARTICLE REVIEWED / NO HsH HUMAN-SUBJECT RESULT / NOT A P1 MANUSCRIPT CHANGE.

## Source and evidence class

Jin, Y., Martinez-Maldonado, R., Shi, W., Huang, S., Zheng, M., Han, X., Gasevic, D., & Yan, L. (2026). "When machines join the moral circle: The persona effect of generative AI agents in collaborative reasoning." *British Journal of Educational Technology*, online version of record, 5 April 2026. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.70067

Full text: https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/bjet.70067

This is a peer-reviewed external primary empirical study. It is not Nathan Direct, not an HsH/SAT measurement, not an independent replication, and not evidence for assistant consciousness or personhood. The findings below are author-reported results from the published article; no participant-level data were obtained or reanalysed.

## Actual design

- 217 human participants were assigned in 93 triads: 31 human-only groups, 29 supportive-AI groups, and 33 contrarian-AI groups. The AI groups contained two humans plus one GPT-5-based agent. The group/session, not each utterance or each AI persona, is the treatment-assignment unit.
- Each group completed one 10-minute online discussion of an autonomous-vehicle moral dilemma. The AI agent monitored the chat on an approximately 25-second schedule with jitter, had a 50% response probability, and could not take more than three consecutive turns.
- The two AI conditions used the same GPT-5 base and task knowledge/procedural setup, with stance/affect/persona prompts varied. There was one AI teammate per group and no agent-to-agent exchange. The persona contrast is therefore a prompt/role manipulation, not independent model-source diversity.
- Participants were not told before the discussion that one teammate was AI; they were told they were collaborating with two other online students and were debriefed afterward. The article reports detection sensitivity of 23.7% in the contrarian condition and 28.8% in the supportive condition. Monash ethics approval is reported as Project 48379.
- The article says the data are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request and are not publicly available because of privacy or ethical restrictions. The supporting-information PDF link returned HTTP 403 in this review, so its supplemental tables were not independently inspected.

## Results that matter, and what they do not establish

- The supportive-AI condition increased the authors' argument-reasoning index relative to human-only control by 0.097 (p = .028; 95% CI [0.011, 0.183]). The contrarian-AI versus control contrast was not significant.
- Both AI conditions showed lower semantic divergence than control, reported as approximately 27-36% lower. Lower divergence is not automatically better reasoning: it can indicate focus or cohesion, but it can also be compatible with premature convergence. It needs to be interpreted alongside evidence quality, dissent retention, and decision outcomes.
- 42 of 205 participants with usable pre/post decisions changed their choice (20.5%). The condition contrasts for decision change were not significant; baseline choice was the strongest predictor. The reported moral-complexity model had adjusted R-squared of 0.04 and no significant condition effects.
- The defensible reading is that persona-conditioned AI participation changed some discourse-process measures, while the study did not establish a corresponding causal change in final moral choices. It does not show that several agents independently converged on a truth, that agents influenced one another, or that a multi-agent collective became more accurate.

## Review-MIND implications

1. **Separate persona diversity from source independence.** Different personas over the same GPT-5 base and shared task knowledge are not independent evidence sources. A consensus among such variants must not be counted as independent convergence.
2. **Record what humans were told, not only what the system was.** The existing provenance/event-graph work already distinguishes model configuration, prompt/context, visibility, and consent basis. Add explicit session-level fields for `ai_identity_disclosure`, measured `participant_awareness`, and `debrief_status`. A system's actual model identity and the identity presented to participants are different facts.
3. **Keep process and outcome measures separate.** Argument quality, topic/semantic drift, turn distribution, uptake, dissent, perceived safety, and final decisions are distinct outcomes. More speech or lower drift alone is not evidence of empowerment or better decisions.
4. **Respect the group-level design.** The 217 people are nested in 93 groups. Do not treat 217 participants, 2,850 utterances, or multiple prompt personas as independent treatment assignments.
5. **Use a disconfirming replication.** A transparent replication could preserve the task, model snapshot, persona prompts, turn policy, and group-level randomization while explicitly disclosing the AI teammate. Pre-register process and decision outcomes and use blinded independent human adjudication. If the persona effects disappear or reverse under transparent disclosure, the original result may depend on low participant awareness or social-role expectations. This is a proposal only; no participants were recruited.
6. **Keep the multi-agent source-independence question separate.** A different experiment would compare same-model/different-persona variants against different model families with matched source packets and explicit source ancestry. That is not the design tested by this paper and should not be folded into its result.

## Review boundary

The article is a source about observable group interaction under a specific prompt and disclosure regime. Its transcript-derived measures do not reveal private model states. No inference about model awareness, intention, consciousness, or persistent identity follows from the study.
