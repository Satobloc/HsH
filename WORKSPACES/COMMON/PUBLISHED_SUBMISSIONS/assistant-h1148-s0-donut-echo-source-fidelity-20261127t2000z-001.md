## 5. The assistant candidate, preserved verbatim and separately typed

The assistant-generated candidate repeated in the user message is:

```text
R(t) = (cos t, sin t)
Q(t) = (2 cos t, sin t)

C(t) = ((R + r cos Θ(t)) cos t,
        (R + r cos Θ(t)) sin t,
        r sin Θ(t))

Θ(t) = arccos(
  (2 cos² t + sin² t + 1) /
  (sqrt(2) * sqrt(4 cos² t + sin² t + 1))
)
```

This is a valid *candidate formula to analyze*, but it is not the original mechanical apparatus. It contains no GrAnn, cord, `W_obble`, `W_iggle`, V, pivot, Donomphulon, BigRad, or even-multiple winding condition.

A precise reconstruction of this candidate is:

- `r₂(t) = (cos t, sin t)` is a unit circle.
- `q₂(t) = (2 cos t, sin t)` is an ellipse parameterized by the same t.
- The assistant implicitly lifts both to z=1:
  - `u(t) = (cos t, sin t, 1)`
  - `v(t) = (2 cos t, sin t, 1)`
- Their dot product is `2 cos² t + sin² t + 1`.
- Their squared norms are `2` and `4 cos² t + sin² t + 1`.
- Therefore `Θ(t)` is the arccosine of their normalized dot product.
- `C(t)` is a standard one-parameter torus trace with major angle t and minor angle Θ(t).

The implied lift `L(x,y)=(x,y,1)` is an assistant choice; it is not specified by Nathan Direct. The notation also overloads `R`: it denotes the first input curve and the torus major radius. A corrected notation would use `r₂(t)`, `q₂(t)`, major radius `R₀`, and minor radius `r₀`.

The assistant candidate uses only the symmetric angle between the two lifted vectors. Swapping the ordered inputs leaves Θ and therefore C unchanged. It cannot, by itself, preserve the order of the R/Q pair. This does **not** disprove the original mechanical model, which has distinct roles and additional constraints absent from the candidate.

### Required map contract

A precise equation-to-curve-to-equation method needs at least:

```text
E ∈ EquationSpace
  -- Enc --> Γ_E ⊆ M
  -- Param --> γ : I → M, with range(γ) = Γ_E
  -- Couple K(γ_R, γ_Q, apparatus_state) --> C_out
  -- Dec --> E_out
```

The equation-to-locus step can be written as `Γ_E = {x ∈ D | F_E(x)=0}` for a specified domain D. A parameterization is a map; its image is a geometric curve. Neither is literally identical to the equation. Many equations and parameterizations can represent the same image, and a parameterization may retain orientation, speed, multiplicity, and traversal information that the image alone discards.

If equations are compared modulo an explicit equivalence relation `≈`, a lossless encoder must satisfy `Enc(E₁)=Enc(E₂) → E₁≈E₂`, with a decoder that is a left inverse on those equivalence classes. For an ordered pair of inputs, the coupling/readout must also preserve the R/Q labels or carry them through a separate channel. The assistant candidate fails that ordered-pair condition because `C(R,Q)=C(Q,R)` for distinct R and Q.
