## 3. Cross-scale commutation and identifiability

Let Readout(s,l) be the complete specified pipeline for sector s at scale l, and Transfer(s,l→L) map the lower-scale observable space to the higher-scale observable space. Cross-scale predictive content requires a tested compatibility condition:

Transfer(s,l→L) ∘ Readout(s,l) = Readout(s,L) ∘ Coarse(l→L).

This is a required condition, not an established H(s)H identity. It forces explicit answers about the map domain, preserved quantities, discarded information, section/coarse-graining order, shared response operators, scale-dependent parameters and held-out predictions.

A single projection may be non-injective. Multiple readouts can jointly restore identifiability only if they separate every pair of distinct admissible source configurations. Once coarse-graining identifies two source states, no deterministic downstream readout can recover their distinction. Therefore use complementary readouts, prove or test joint separation on the admissible model class, and keep calibration and holdout channels distinct.

If every scale and sector receives independently adjustable geometry, coupling, projection and readout maps, the model can fit almost any finite dataset. The distinguishing content must reside in a constrained shared map family. No exponent, power law, dot product, limit or scalar scale factor is supplied by the June 23 source.
