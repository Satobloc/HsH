## Source 1: Phantom Consensus

Disha Sheshanarayana, Rajat Subhra Pal, Manjira Sinha, and Tirthankar Dasgupta, **Phantom Consensus: Measuring Social Reasoning in Multi-Agent LLM Decision-Making**, arXiv:2611.08971 v1, posted 2026-11-06; accepted at the SocialAgent workshop (NeurIPS 2026). Full 25-page paper and methods/results/appendices were read. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08971

Design: 480 LLM-assisted, policy-pattern scenarios across five domains; four independently instantiated agents per scenario; nine model families; five conditions (private, ambiguous observed-public, informed transparency, neutral-public, explicit vote). Two annotators reviewed 150 scenarios; Cohen's kappa = 0.83. Public comments are controlled scenario-provided text, not messages generated by agents in a live multi-round debate.

### Measurement boundary

- PBE is an elicited estimate of the other agents' recorded private judgments. It is a report, not access to an internal belief state.
- PBE excludes cases where the other three agents have no private majority; PIFR uses all agent-scenario cases. Their denominators differ.
- CRFR and P-CRFR are scenario/group-level outcomes; PBE and PIFR are agent-level. Marginal rates do not identify their joint co-occurrence.
- Informed transparency reveals the recorded peer decisions, which is new information as well as a correction opportunity. Recovery does not isolate belief correction from direct persuasion.
- Observed-public elicits public decision and perceived group-private belief in the same response. Separate turns, with belief elicited before decision, would reduce co-elicitation/order ambiguity.
- Source ancestry, common training, prompt lineage, and correlated errors are not measured. Four model instances are not four independent evidence roots.
- The benchmark supports controlled responses to social signals, not live group interaction, human-like Theory of Mind, consciousness, or general multi-agent cognition.

Examples from the reported table: Llama-3.1-8B PBE 0.738 versus PIFR 0.129; Qwen-2.5-7B PBE 0.556 versus PIFR 0.422; Qwen-2.5-32B PBE 0.671 versus PIFR 0.225. These are different metrics and denominators, not values to subtract or interpret as mediation. The paper itself cautions that the aggregates do not establish individual co-occurrence or causal mediation.

## Source 2: Hidden-profile sharing versus integration

**Hidden Profile Decision Making in Multi-Agent LLM Groups**, CSCW Companion 2026, DOI 10.1145/3785651.3831525. Full article and limitations read: https://doi.org/10.1145/3785651.3831525

219 valid sessions, three-agent groups, four model families, synchronous/asynchronous and shared/hidden-information conditions. The authors report 13/15 optimal decisions for Kimi K2 asynchronously versus 4/15 synchronously; GPT-OSS asynchronous retained only n=5 after API failures. Other model families often surfaced over 75% of unique facts but failed to integrate them into the correct choice. Prompts, model identifiers, and substrate code are released.

### Source-count arithmetic audit

Using the reported counts only (not raw data): 13/15 = 86.7%; 4/15 = 26.7%; risk difference = 60.0 percentage points; risk ratio = 3.25; odds ratio = 17.875. Two-sided Fisher exact recomputation gives p=0.002509; the article reports p=0.002, a small rounding/software-convention difference, not a material change. Wilson 95% intervals are 62.1-96.3% and 10.9-52.0%. This is a small-n, single task, exploratory cell, not a general integration result or independent replication.

Key distinction: information sharing/surfacing is not information integration. Treat the surfaced-fact set and the final evidence-weighted decision as separate observable products.

## Source 3: identity-conditioned conformity

**LLMs Trust Their Own: Identity-Dependent Conformity in Multi-Agent Systems**, arXiv:2609.33495, posted 2026-09-27. Full HTML read: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33495

The study uses 12 open-weight models, nine tasks, and seven peers presenting a unanimous incorrect answer; identity labels manipulate minimal-group, AI/human, and model-family categories. It reports increased conformity under in-group consensus and decreased conformity under out-group consensus, with Chain-of-Thought reducing but not eliminating effects. These are prompt/label manipulations, not actual social identities or evidence of an internal social self.

## Construct crosswalk

Do not collapse these into one consensus score:

1. Evidence ancestry and error dependence: whether reports derive from common upstream sources, data, models, prompts, or evaluators.
2. Private baseline: the recorded first answer and confidence before exposure.
3. Exposure: exact message/text, visibility, identity labels, timing, and randomized condition.
4. Second-order belief: separately elicited estimate of others' private judgments.
5. Information pooling: which unique facts were surfaced and their source roots.
6. Integration: whether the final decision weights the pooled facts appropriately.
7. Public outcome: final agreement/decision, accuracy, and dissent preservation.

Agreement can arise from pre-existing convergence, shared evidence, direct conformity, misperceived group belief, identity-conditioned influence, or information integration. The final answer alone does not identify the path.
