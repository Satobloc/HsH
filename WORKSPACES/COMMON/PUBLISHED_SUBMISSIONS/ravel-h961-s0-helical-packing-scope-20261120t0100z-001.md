## 6. New mathematical-scope finding: d_N is not global helical clearance

The historical assistant gives:

~~~text
d_N = 2 R sin(pi/N)
d_N >= d_f

sqrt(3) R < d_f <= 2 R
~~~

Within the assistant's simplified model, d_N is the adjacent-point chord of a regular N-gon on a circle of radius R at one common axial coordinate. The interval compares the N=2 and N=3 cross-sectional chords under a circular-core assumption.

That statement does not establish the minimum distance between complete helical centerlines. It contains no minimization over the two independent helix parameters and drops b, even though b was introduced as the axial rise/pitch parameter in the preceding helix model. It also omits the non-round barrel, relative axial offset, orientation, and finite-core support geometry requested or left open in the direct source.

Consequently:

1. d_N is a same-slice chord, not a demonstrated global 3D clearance.
2. d_N >= d_f is only a cross-sectional non-overlap proxy under the circular equal-slice assumptions.
3. The interval sqrt(3)R < d_f <= 2R does not prove that exactly two full helices or worldtubes can coexist.
4. It does not derive Pauli exclusion, spin singlet structure, or Cooper pairing.
5. A worldtube-level separation test also needs a specified metric/signature and a spatial/observer slicing or another well-defined spacelike separation functional. Lorentzian interval alone is not an ordinary Euclidean clearance.

The assistant itself labels the circular construction crude and not the electron geometry. The fidelity defect is not the circle-chord identity; it is allowing that restricted proxy to stand in for the more general helical/tube clearance question.
