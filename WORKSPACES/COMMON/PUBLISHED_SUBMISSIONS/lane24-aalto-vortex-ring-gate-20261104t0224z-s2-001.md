## 2. Identifiable measured system

Two nominally equal water drops impact a water pool at a controlled time separation Δt and generate two vortex rings.

Reported apparatus and calibration:

- Water container: 75 × 75 × 75 mm; pool depth 45 mm.
- Two needle tips: 6.0 mm centre-to-centre separation; needle height 29 mm above the undisturbed surface.
- Drop diameter approximately 3.0 mm; mean radius **R = 1.5 ± 0.1 mm**, estimated by fitting ellipses to free-fall images and using an ellipsoidal volume.
- Impact speeds in the reported runs: approximately **0.58–0.59 m/s**; reported Weber numbers **16–17**.
- High-speed imaging: **2000 frames/s**, 1280 × 720 pixels, exposure 390 μs, field of view 36 × 20 mm, spatial calibration **27.89 μm/pixel** using a Thorlabs grid target.
- Planar PIV: 45–53 μm blue polyethylene tracer particles, 0.65 g/L; 0.13 g/L polysorbate-20 added to the pool. PIVlab multi-pass processing uses 48-pixel then 24-pixel windows with 50% step.
- Surface tension: **γ = 0.062 ± 0.008 N/m**, measured by pendant-drop method.
- The capillary-wave model uses **ρ = 1000 kg/m³** and observed wavelength **λ = 2 mm** as nominal inputs.

The measurement is a planar 2D velocity field and derived core-centre/angle readout. Core centres are selected manually by clicking the smallest circular loops in PIV streamlines. The paper does not report an uncertainty for manual centre selection or a covariance matrix for the derived centre trajectories. This is not volumetric 3D tomography and does not directly measure a 4D worldtube.

## 3. Observed interaction regimes

The paper distinguishes four timing regimes:

| Drop-impact separation Δt | Reported behavior |
|---|---|
| Δt < 0.5 ms | Approximately symmetric merging; the nominal synchronized case is below the 0.5 ms temporal resolution. |
| 0.5 ms < Δt < 7 ms | Asymmetric collision/merging; the first ring is displaced toward the second, with an incomplete-reconnection/reappearance sequence. |
| 7 ms ≤ Δt < 80 ms | Surface-mediated interaction: the first ring is reported to be gently pushed away and is not perfectly vertical; formation of the second ring is impaired by the first impact’s capillary-wave/crater history. |
| Δt ≥ 80 ms | The two rings evolve independently. |

The 7–80 ms regime is the closest reported case to a noncontact trajectory change, but it is **not evidence of direct core-to-core repulsion**. The authors attribute the effect to the evolving free surface and capillary waves; the second ring is itself impaired. The ≥80 ms regime is an independent-evolution control, not a deflection experiment. The <7 ms regimes involve collision/merging.

This supplies a real classical example of a responsive medium mediating an interaction. The mediator is a **2D liquid-air free surface carrying capillary waves**, not an H(s)H timesheet. No physical equivalence is claimed.

## 4. Reproduction of the paper’s timing estimates

The paper uses the capillary-wave phase-speed relation

\[
v_c=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\gamma}{\rho\lambda}}.
\]

With the reported γ, and treating ρ = 1000 kg/m³ and λ = 2 mm as fixed nominal inputs:

- Calculated \(v_c = 0.441\) m/s.
- Propagating **only** the reported γ uncertainty gives \(\sigma_{v_c}=0.028\) m/s.
- The measured first visible trough speed is **0.46 ± 0.01 m/s**.

The central model speed is therefore consistent with the measured trough speed at summary level. This uncertainty omits any uncertainty in λ and ρ, which the paper does not provide.

For the regime-II/III boundary, the paper gives

\[
\tau_{\mathrm{II}\to\mathrm{III}}=\frac{L-R}{v_{\mathrm{first}}}-t_{\mathrm{cr}},
\]

with L = 6.0 mm, R = 1.5 mm, \(v_{\mathrm{first}}=0.46\) m/s, and \(t_{\mathrm{cr}}=3.5\) ms. Direct substitution gives a wave-travel time of **9.78 ms** and a transition estimate of **6.28 ms**, reproducing the paper’s central value **6.3 ms**. Propagating only R and \(v_{\mathrm{first}}\) gives a partial uncertainty of **0.30 ms**. The paper reports **6.3 ± 1.1 ms**; the full ±1.1 ms cannot be independently reconstructed from the accessible stated inputs because uncertainties/covariances for L and \(t_{\mathrm{cr}}\) are not supplied.

For the regime-III/IV boundary, the paper gives

\[
\tau_{\mathrm{III}\to\mathrm{IV}}=\frac{L+R}{v_{\mathrm{last}}}+t_d,
\]

with \(v_{\mathrm{last}}=0.20\pm0.01\) m/s and \(t_d=20\pm5\) ms. Treating L = 6.0 mm as fixed and R, \(v_{\mathrm{last}}\), and \(t_d\) as independent gives:

- Central value: **57.50 ms**.
- Propagated uncertainty: **5.36 ms**.

This reproduces the paper’s **57.5 ± 5.4 ms**. The reported observed transition is **68 ± 12 ms**. This is a within-experiment model/observation consistency check, not a blind prediction or independent validation: the model inputs are measured/estimated from the same experimental platform, and the paper does not provide their covariance.

Reproduction code:

```python
import math

L, R, sR = 6.0e-3, 1.5e-3, 0.1e-3
v_first, sv_first, t_cr = 0.46, 0.01, 3.5e-3
v_last, sv_last, t_d, st_d = 0.20, 0.01, 20e-3, 5e-3

tau_ii_iii = (L - R) / v_first - t_cr
s_tau_ii_iii_partial = math.sqrt(
    (sR / v_first)**2 + (((L-R) * sv_first / v_first**2))**2
)

tau_iii_iv = (L + R) / v_last + t_d
s_tau_iii_iv = math.sqrt(
    (sR / v_last)**2
    + (((L+R) * sv_last / v_last**2))**2
    + st_d**2
)
```

No raw-video/PIV reanalysis was performed. The calculations above reproduce source-level estimates from reported measured/estimated inputs; they are not a new fit or H(s)H prediction.
