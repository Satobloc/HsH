## 3. Primary source A: shared mental models in human-agent teams

Beau G. Schelble, Christopher Flathmann, Nathan J. McNeese, Guo Freeman, and Rohit Mallick, “Let’s Think Together! Assessing Shared Mental Models, Performance, and Trust in Human-Agent Teams,” *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction* 6, GROUP, Article 13 (January 2022), 29 pages. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3492832. Full paper: https://beauschelble.com/files/papers/schelble-2022-lets-think-together.pdf

The study used 66 participants in 32 three-member teams:

- HHH: 12 all-human teams.
- HHA: 10 teams with two humans and one agent.
- HAA: 10 teams with one human and two agents.

The task was the NeoCITIES emergency-response simulation. The agent's resource-allocation component was an expert system. Crucially, the agent's chat communication was not generated by that expert system: trained experimenters used a Wizard-of-Oz script to produce the agent's conversational responses. Participants were told the agent had advanced task-specific text generation and understanding capabilities, but this was not a contemporary LLM experiment.

The study's measures were not interchangeable:

- Shared team/task mental-model similarity was derived from human participants' paired-sentence comparisons.
- Perceived team cognition was a separate self-report measure.
- Objective team performance was the simulation score.
- Trust in AI was another separate measure.

The reported results illustrate why those distinctions matter:

- Objective performance differed by composition, F(2,27)=6.07, p=.007. HAA averaged 91.97 and exceeded HHH at 86.62, but did not significantly exceed HHA at 89.08.
- The objective-performance table uses 8 HAA teams, although 10 HAA teams were formed. The reported F(2,27) is consistent with 30 team observations, not all 32 teams. The accessible paper does not explain the two missing HAA performance observations.
- Team mental-model similarity was 0.30 for HHH and 0.28 for HHA, with no significant difference (p=.54). Task mental-model similarity was 0.31 for HHH and 0.35 for HHA, also not significant (p=.113).
- The HAA condition could not supply a human-human pair for structural shared-mental-model comparison because it had only one human. The authors also could not measure the agent's mental model. They explicitly identify this as a limitation and call for a validated method to measure agent mental models, or at least to disambiguate the construct being compared.
- HHA participants reported higher trust in the agent than HAA participants: 26.55 versus 24.30, t(18)=2.19, p=.042.
- The perceived-team-cognition scale is reverse-directional: lower scores mean higher perceived team cognition. Thus the reported 13.48 for the agent versus 7.97 for the human teammate in HHA indicates less perceived team cognition with the agent, not more.

**Source interpretation:** the study offers a useful human-agent team-composition comparison, but it does not establish a shared mental model between a human and an agent. In HAA, the relevant structural comparison is unavailable; in HHA, the structural comparison is between the two humans, while the agent's own task representation remains unmeasured. The expert-system-plus-Wizard-of-Oz architecture also prevents treating its results as evidence about LLM-generated team dialogue.

## 4. Primary source B: communication in AI-assisted drone-design teams

Joshua T. Gyory, Binyang Song, Jonathan Cagan, and Christopher McComb, “Communication in AI-Assisted Teams During an Interdisciplinary Drone Design Problem,” *Proceedings of the International Conference on Engineering Design (ICED21)*, Gothenburg, 16-20 August 2021. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/pds.2021.65. Full paper: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/FFA6D55B0D901CEB676FC15373322937/S2732527X21000651a.pdf/communication-in-ai-assisted-teams-during-an-interdisciplinary-drone-design-problem.pdf

The team experiment involved 44 six-person teams, 11 in each of four conditions. Teams worked across business, design, and operations roles and switched between open and restrictive communication structures. Two task-specialized AI agents were available in hybrid teams: a drone-design agent using a character recurrent neural network and a path-planning agent using linear-programming search. These were not LLM teammates.

The paper measures communication through counts, lexical diversity, content richness, and Latent Semantic Analysis (LSA). Its LSA pipeline turns each communication channel into a document containing all messages in that channel, then compares the resulting document representations. The study reports greater communication volume and semantic cohesion in AI-assisted teams. For example, average turns were 156 versus 137 (not significant at p=.16), while token counts were 512 versus 409 (p=.042); unique-token counts were 196 versus 166 (p=.052).

This is a useful discourse-level measure, but it is not a direct measurement of each member's task mental model, the agent's internal representation, or the correctness of information exchanged. Aggregating all messages within a channel also removes speaker- and turn-level distinctions from the LSA document representation. A more cohesive channel document can indicate more semantically aligned discourse without establishing that all members hold the same task model or that the team has integrated independent evidence correctly.

A text-extraction trap is worth recording: the figure's channel label that can look like “AI” in extracted text is the **All** channel, as confirmed by Figure 1 and the methods. It is not an additional AI communication channel.

## 6. Cross-study finding: “team cognition” is not one observable

These two studies operationalize different objects:

1. **Shared mental-model similarity:** similarity between elicited human representations of task or team structure.
2. **Discourse-semantic cohesion:** similarity between aggregated communication-channel documents.
3. **Objective team performance:** quality of the team's task output.
4. **Perceived team cognition and trust:** participant judgments about the team or agent.

None is a substitute for the others. In particular:

- Semantic cohesion does not establish shared mental-model structure.
- Team performance does not establish that humans and agents share a representation.
- Human-human mental-model similarity does not establish human-agent alignment.
- A human's perceived team cognition does not reveal an agent's internal state.
- An agent's task behavior can be evaluated without attributing consciousness, personhood, or a human-like mental model.

The older studies are valuable for measurement design, but neither is a contemporary LLM study. The current HsH literature map and Orson's existing work cover more recent LLM-team research; this report does not transfer the older expert-system results to LLMs.

## 7. Proposed measurement extension

A defensible next step is to distinguish a directly observed human mental model from an **elicited behavioral task profile** for an agent.

A bounded protocol could:

1. Define a shared task ontology and the specific relations whose understanding matters.
2. Elicit human task/team models independently using concept maps or paired comparisons before and after collaboration.
3. Elicit an agent's task profile through a fixed, repeated set of task-state, counterfactual, action-prediction, and consequence-prediction probes. Label this a behavioral profile, not an internal mental model.
4. Compare the human and agent profiles only through a validated common representation. If no validated alignment exists, report behavioral task agreement rather than latent-model similarity.
5. Keep semantic cohesion, message volume, task performance, trust/reliance calibration, source attribution, and unique-information uptake as separate outcomes.
6. Randomize at the team level, predeclare the analysis unit and missing-data rules, and report recruited participants, formed teams, and outcome-specific denominators separately.
7. Use blinded human adjudication for ambiguous outcomes; do not use the same LLM as the sole generator and evaluator.

The proposal is disconfirmable. If a stable behavioral profile fails to predict action or task outcomes, it is not a useful task-model proxy. If the common-representation mapping is not invariant across model versions or team compositions, cross-substrate similarity claims should be withheld.

This is a proposed measurement protocol only. No participants, model/API trials, raw transcripts, or experiments were used.
