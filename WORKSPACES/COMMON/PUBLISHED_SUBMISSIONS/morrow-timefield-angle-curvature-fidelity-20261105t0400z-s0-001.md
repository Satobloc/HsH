## 4. Historical assistant mathematics: provenance and audit

All equations in this section are assistant-authored reconstructions or proposals, not verbatim Nathan Direct equations from the cited source messages.

### A. Placeholder force law and angle construction

The assistant proposes a force-like split such as a classical term plus a time-slice term, then writes an angle using a trajectory tangent and a time-field normal. These expressions introduce symbols and structure absent from the direct source.

Problems:

1. No metric, signature, normalized normal, connection, or domain for the time field is specified.
2. An ordinary Euclidean α = arccos(...) is not automatically the correct angle in Lorentzian geometry. If both compared vectors are timelike, the invariant is a hyperbolic rapidity; other vector-type combinations require their own definition.
3. The source explicitly calls the intersection angle the hard part. Writing an arccos expression does not solve that missing geometry.

### B. Angle, curvature, Regge calculus, and gravity

Assistant messages \`b586e6b2...\` and \`490bc794...\` introduce an angle-to-curvature identification, a Regge-style deficit angle, a ratio resembling Δt_parallel/Δt_perp, sums of angles over links or deficit-angle/area terms over hinges, and a proposed map from angle to an Einstein tensor or covariant acceleration.

These are not direct equations from Nathan. They mix distinct mathematical types:

- an incidence angle is not automatically a turning angle;
- a turning/holonomy angle is not the curvature tensor;
- a Regge deficit angle is an integrated curvature measure for a specified discrete geometry, not an arbitrary ratio of time increments;
- a scalar angle cannot be identified with a rank-four Riemann tensor or rank-two Einstein tensor without a specified contraction, scale, and map;
- covariant acceleration ∇_u u is a vector, not the Riemann curvature tensor. It vanishes for an affinely parameterized geodesic.

A legitimate standard-geometric bridge exists in a different, fully specified setting: holonomy around a sufficiently small loop is governed by curvature contracted with the loop's area bivector; in two dimensions, integrated Gaussian curvature can appear as a deficit/holonomy angle. That does not establish that this source's trajectory/time-field incidence angle is that holonomy angle. The loop, connection, surface, metric, and representation map are missing.

### E. Path length, mass, force, and the sum-of-squares equation

The direct source proposes a path-length interpretation. The assistant converts this into expressions such as θ = dℓ/dt and curvature = Δℓ/ℓ₀. As written:

- dℓ/dt has units of speed, not angle;
- Δℓ/ℓ₀ is dimensionless and is not a curvature unless a separate normalized definition is supplied;
- a second derivative of a path deviation has acceleration units, not force units, until mass and a dynamical law are specified.

The assistant also writes a sum-of-squares expression involving derivatives of τ with respect to x, y, z, and t, then describes it as a four-dimensional path-length metric. The user subsequently asks whether the terms should be added or nested, confirming that this is an assistant-proposed equation being interrogated, not a Nathan-authored equation.

The displayed sum is not a Lorentzian norm as written: it omits the metric and signature, does not reconcile the length/time units of the coordinates, and is not the line element along a path. A scalar-field gradient norm requires g^{μν}∂_μτ∂_ντ; a path interval uses g_{μν}dx^μdx^ν. These are different objects.

For massive particles, standard special relativity normalizes the timelike four-velocity to ±c² according to signature. A photon has null four-momentum; proper-time-normalized four-velocity dx^μ/dτ is not defined along a null worldline. The assistant's later Euclidean sum of coordinate derivatives does not encode that structure.
