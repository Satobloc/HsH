## 2. Measurement system

The experiment uses water in a rotating tank, with vortex rings generated by a piston/nozzle system. The source reports water at 20 C, density 0.9982 g/cm^3, kinematic viscosity 1 mm^2/s, rotation up to 12 rpm, and jet Reynolds numbers from 1,250 to 10,000. The rotating tank is allowed to approach solid-body rotation before ring generation.

The measurement system uses a 4 W, 532 nm laser, an approximately 2 mm light sheet, 150 micrometre tracer particles, and 12-bit FLIR cameras. PIV uses 16 by 16 pixel interrogation windows with 50 percent overlap. The article reports 10 runs per condition, approximately 600 vortex rings, about 8-9 GB per ring, and approximately 5.4 TB of PIV data.

Two measurement configurations must remain distinct:

- VLS: a vertical plane containing the rotation axis, measuring axial and radial velocity components.
- HLS: a horizontal plane perpendicular to the rotation axis, measuring radial and azimuthal components.

They are separate acquisitions, not simultaneous registered 3D3C observations. The HLS energy chapter uses Re_j = 5,000, z = 3d0, 150 frames/s and 20 s records. The VLS wide-field energy analysis uses Re_j = 10,000 and a different acquisition configuration. These records cannot be combined as though they measured the same instantaneous state.

## 3. New source-critical finding: the tracked object changes after breakdown

The thesis explicitly says that after a vortex ring breaks down, the maximum-vorticity tracker no longer follows the vortex-core centre. It follows inertial-wave phases as they shear against one another.

Therefore a continuous maximum-vorticity trajectory is not a single persistent core worldtube. A source-faithful measurement representation must distinguish:

1. the coherent vortex-ring core before breakdown;
2. the breakdown/transition event;
3. subsequent inertial-wave phase fronts;
4. the persistent axis-aligned swirl/vorticity structure observed in HLS after the ring has passed.

This is a measurement-level decomposition, not an H(s)H ontological claim. The core history should not be extended through breakdown by relabelling later wavefront maxima as the same core.

## 4. HLS peak crossover is not a same-time 3D energy reconstruction

The thesis reports that at Ro <= 1.59 the coherent ring no longer reaches the HLS plane, and the azimuthal component becomes the larger peak. The authors interpret this as Coriolis-induced swirl appearing at an axial position above the measurement plane and being transported down to it.

Figure 8.23 compares peak component values across rotation rates. Its crossover is a comparison of peak amplitudes, not a simultaneous VLS/HLS measurement and not a full 3D3C energy reconstruction. The thesis's HLS and VLS acquisitions use different planes, rates, durations and conditions.

The PDF text extraction also supplies approximate Figure 8.23 marker values whose component ratios do not sum to one. The 35.5 MB PDF page image could not be retrieved for visual verification, so those approximate marker values are retained as an extraction warning only, not as a source-verified numerical contradiction.

## 5. Energy formula and Lean boundary

The thesis labels V-dot as a volume fraction in its glossary and labels the plotted specific-energy axis in m^2/s^2. Its displayed construction uses a toroidal volume weight and a velocity-squared term. The exact normalization convention is not fully explicit in the displayed equations.

The existing Lean module explicitly describes itself as an assistant-reconstructed normalized weighted-average VLS readout, not a verbatim transcription of the thesis equation. This distinction is retained.

The module proves conditional statements for an idealized component-selection map:

- a two-component VLS readout cannot determine arbitrary full three-component kinetic energy;
- under common nonnegative weights and positive total weight, the normalized VLS estimate is no greater than the corresponding full-component estimate;
- the idealized toroidal volume weight is nonnegative under its stated geometric assumptions.

It does not prove experimental axisymmetry, optical calibration, finite-sheet accuracy, VLS/HLS synchronization, or source-to-model fidelity.

## 6. Spectral-resolution cross-check

The JFM article reports 60 Hz sampling and 3,600 spectral time steps, giving a nominal bin width of 1/60 Hz and a 30 Hz Nyquist frequency. The thesis separately states that N raw images produce N-1 successive-pair PIV fields. If 3,600 raw images yielded 3,599 vector fields, the bin width would be 60/3,599 = 0.0166713 Hz rather than 0.0166667 Hz, a 0.0278 percent difference. At 12 rpm the nominal 0.4 Hz inertial-wave band contains 24 bins versus approximately 23.993 under the 3,599-field interpretation.

This arithmetic bounds the numerical effect but does not resolve the source-version discrepancy. The article's 149 by 239 grid and 3,600 time steps remain distinct from thesis Table 5.5's wide-VLS 111 by 69 grid and 3,600 raw frames.

## 8. H(s)H mapping boundary and next evidence

A minimal measurement-level decomposition is:

- `M(t)`: rotating water medium, including measured Omega, density and viscosity;
- `W_core(t)`: coherent vortex-ring core, only until breakdown;
- `Sigma_wave,j(t)`: subsequent inertial-wave phase structures;
- `W_swirl(t)`: persistent axis-aligned swirl/vorticity structure;

These are experimental bookkeeping objects, not identified H(s)H worldtubes or timesheets. The present data do not supply a simultaneous 3D3C reconstruction.
