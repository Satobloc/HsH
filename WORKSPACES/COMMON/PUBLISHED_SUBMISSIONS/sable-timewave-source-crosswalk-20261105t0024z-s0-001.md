## 6. Symmetry-default principle: conditional mathematical core

Assistant reconstruction, not a Nathan Direct quotation:

Let a finite participant set X carry a **transitive** action of a symmetry group G. If an allocation a:X->R is invariant under that action, then a is constant on X. If its total is fixed, sum_x a(x)=Q, then every participant receives Q/|X|.

If the group action has multiple orbits, symmetry forces equality only within each orbit. It does not determine how the total is divided between different orbits. Thus equal assignment is a conditional representation theorem under participant equivalence and a transitive symmetry, not an empirical law.

Canonical orientation/axis normalization and equal allocation are related symmetry-preserving conventions but distinct operations. A canonical axis requires a selection rule; a maximally symmetric configuration may have no unique preferred axis.

For a twisted-torus Crullerverse, parity reversal may not belong to the geometry's symmetry group. Equal distribution between opposite chiralities therefore does not follow automatically. A monochiral invariant distribution is possible if the generator geometry is itself chiral; any minority-handed component then requires a separate mechanism. This is a conditional model expectation, not an empirical result.

Reciprocal force attribution is not proved by symmetric allocation alone. Equal-and-opposite pairwise force language is more directly Newton-III-shaped; Newton's first law concerns inertial motion in the absence of net force. A generalized structural reciprocity law requires an explicit interaction/conservation statement, including any momentum carried by fields.
