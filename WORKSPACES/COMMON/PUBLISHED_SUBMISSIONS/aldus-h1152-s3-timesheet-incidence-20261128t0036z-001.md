## 3. Conditional geometry and explicit maps

Let \(X^4\) be a smooth four-dimensional ambient manifold and \(\Gamma^1\hookrightarrow X\) a smooth embedded core. A chosen auxiliary Riemannian metric supplies a rank-three normal bundle \(\nu(\Gamma)\). For a radius within a tubular injectivity neighbourhood:

\[
W^4_\rho = \operatorname{Exp}^{\perp}(D_\rho(\nu)),
\qquad
B^3_\rho = \partial W^4_\rho
          = \operatorname{Exp}^{\perp}(S_\rho(\nu)).
\]

This is standard tubular geometry, not an H(s)H-specific prediction. A nonzero physical thickness/radius is a model choice; the core alone does not force it. The auxiliary Riemannian metric is a construction aid, not an asserted physical spacetime metric. The source has not fixed Lorentzian signature or the causal type of the core.

Let \(\Sigma^3\subset X^4\) be a candidate timesheet.

### A. Transverse core/timesheet crossing

If \(T_p\Gamma\oplus T_p\Sigma=T_pX\), then the core/timesheet intersection is zero-dimensional at \(p\). If the tube boundary and timesheet also meet transversely, then:

\[
\dim(T_pB_\rho\cap T_p\Sigma)=3+3-4=2.
\]

Near a transverse core crossing this is a cross-sectional mouth at the event. It is not a longitudinal \(S^1\)-bundle over the core.

### B. Longitudinal containment

If \(\Gamma\subset\Sigma\), then \(T_p\Gamma\subset T_p\Sigma\). With \(T_pX=T_p\Gamma\oplus\nu_p(\Gamma)\), define:

\[
\eta_p=T_p\Sigma\cap\nu_p(\Gamma).
\]

Because \(T_p\Sigma\) contains the core tangent and both \(T_p\Sigma\) and \(\nu_p\) have dimension three in four dimensions:

\[
\dim\eta_p=2.
\]

Under smoothness and constant-rank conditions, these pointwise planes form a rank-two subbundle \(\eta\subset\nu(\Gamma)\). Its unit-circle bundle composes explicitly:

\[
S(\eta)\hookrightarrow S(\nu(\Gamma))
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Exp}^{\perp}} B^3_\rho.
\]

This is the conditional longitudinal 2D boundary sector. It can equal the boundary/timesheet mouth only if the timesheet locally contains the corresponding normal disk bundle; that incidence/embedding condition is not yet source-defined.

This refines Loom h1151:s3's proposal to choose an optional rank-two \(\eta\): in the longitudinal case, the timesheet itself selects \(\eta\). In the transverse case, there is no rank-two subbundle along the whole core selected this way.

### C. Moving family and swept trace

For a smooth family of timesheets \(\Sigma^3_\lambda\), define fixed-slice mouths:

\[
M_\lambda=B^3_\rho\cap\Sigma^3_\lambda.
\]

If each \(M_\lambda\) is a regular 2D intersection, the parameterized history

\[
\mathcal M=\{(\lambda,x):x\in M_\lambda\}\subset I\times B^3_\rho
\]

is 3D under the regular-family assumptions. Its physical image under \(q(\lambda,x)=x\) is 3D only if the differential of \(q\) has rank three on the relevant domain. Otherwise the image can have lower dimension or self-overlap.

This is the explicit map distinction needed to avoid treating a slice, its instantaneous mouth, and the history of moving mouths as the same object. The 2024 direct moving-plane source motivates the distinction; it does not identify the 2024 construction with the 2026 timesheet.

### D. Type correction: time-normal versus normal-plane sector

Under a chosen nondegenerate metric, the time-normal to a hypersurface is a rank-one normal direction to \(T\Sigma\). By contrast, \(\eta=T\Sigma\cap\nu(\Gamma)\) is rank two, tangent to the timesheet and normal to the core. They are different bundles. The direct phrase “time-normal torsion” cannot be identified with a connection or twist on \(\eta\) without an explicit map and response law.
