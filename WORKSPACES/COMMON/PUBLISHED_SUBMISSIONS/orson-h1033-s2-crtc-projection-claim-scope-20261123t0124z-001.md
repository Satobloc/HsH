## 3. New literature result: VEY-046

Hanjing Shi, Kimberly Wang, Sabrina Doherty, and Dominic DiFranzo, "Same Team Label, Different Evidence: A Full-Text Audit of Claim Denominators in Human-AI Teaming Research," arXiv:2609.27849v1 (2026-08-25): https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27849

The full arXiv HTML was read. Scholar Sidekick returned a high-confidence bibliographic match; a separate lookup found no retraction record. This is an arXiv preprint and peer-review status was not established. No local PDF or raw dataset was stored.

The audit distinguishes a 419-record title/abstract map from a purposive 86-record full-text sample. Full-text review moved 40 records across the core evidence denominator: 36 of 74 core/near-core candidates left core, while 4 of 12 boundary candidates entered. The final roles were 42 core empirical, 23 boundary-mechanism, 13 background/theory, and 8 context records.

The final arrangement coding included 27 human-AI dyads, 23 multi-human peer teams, 8 workplace workflows, 5 clinical/expert-client workflows, 2 governance settings, and 2 instructional groups, plus background/unclear/no-arrangement records. Team/collaboration vocabulary crossed dyads and multi-human peer teams. Only 20 of 86 papers specified who could see AI output. The authors' claim-scope chain is: human arrangement and roles -> AI role -> relational coupling (visibility, autonomy, control, responsibility) -> team process -> outcome level.

Four blinded LLM diagnostic runs were unanimous on subsets, but 32% of screening consensus labels and 34% of arrangement consensus labels differed from full-text adjudication. Each model-condition was run once; the authors explicitly do not treat these as general model-capability evaluations. The audit is purposive, not a representative prevalence estimate.

Two existing sources were reused rather than re-audited:
- Alsobay et al., "Bringing Everyone to the Table," PACM HCI 10(5) (2026), DOI 10.1145/3816907: https://doi.org/10.1145/3816907. Its 1,475-participant, 281-group hidden-profile study reports increased fact coverage under LLM facilitation but no significant improvement in the actual optimal-choice rate. Fact mentions, a normative score, and the group's actual choice are distinct measurement stages.
- Chen et al., "AI Agents Reshape Consensus Formation in Human Groups," arXiv:2609.02122: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02122. Its 24-person, 40-round group experiment measures semantic consensus and contribution, not decision accuracy. Existing source/code audits already cover its model-family and evaluator dependencies.

### Design consequence

A future empirical adapter should keep at least these objects distinct:
1. Participant and role, with membership intervals.
2. Information item and its initial access/source.
3. Disclosure or interaction event, with author, time, audience, and item references.
4. Uptake record linked to a particular item and source event.
5. Decision-use record linked to a decision and rationale.
6. Decision record and outcome record, with unit and criterion.
7. Evidence-scope record covering human arrangement, AI role, visibility, autonomy, control, responsibility, process, and outcome level.

Each group/subgroup/member/information/outcome projection needs explicit maps and composition conditions. This is a design proposal, not implemented code or empirical validation.
