The dimension count is a decisive type check. In a 4D ambient space, under a transverse-intersection assumption:

- a full-dimensional 4D region intersected with a 3D hypersurface can yield a 3D region;
- a 3D hypersurface intersected with a 3D hypersurface generically yields a 2D set;
- a 2D worldsheet intersected with a 3D hypersurface generically yields a 1D set;
- a 1D worldline intersected with a 3D hypersurface generically yields isolated 0D points.

Therefore the direct claim of a 3D intersectional trace requires either a full-dimensional finite-core 4D region or a specified non-transverse/projection operation. The assistant's one-dimensional curve parameterization alone cannot produce a generic 3D trace. ### Symmetric critique

- The assistant's carrier construction was a legitimate differential-geometric model of a different object. The error was treating it as the user's physical ontology after the user specified one core and no carrier.
- The assistant's one-core tangent formula is a real improvement, but it overstates completion. It supplies a curve, not the finite-core 4D tube or its slice map.
- The user's early flexibility/tension language is genuinely ambiguous. Later source material narrows the intended Newtonian constraint to inertia/worldtube bending, but the transition should remain visible.
- "Worldtube curvature equals inertia" is well-defined only after choosing which curvature: coordinate bending κ_w, proper worldline curvature κ_M, or curvature data of the extended tube κ_W. These are not interchangeable by naming.
- The 3D-trace claim is not yet typed tightly enough to determine whether the 4D filament is a full-dimensional region, a hypersurface with non-transverse contact, or something projected by the Klein Fabric.
