## 2. The distinction that needed making

Three questions are related but not interchangeable:

1. **Response locus:** where a candidate response is supported (path-neighbourhood, timesheet bulk, or a defined support boundary/contact interface).
2. **Response state:** whether the response is null, nonzero but locally absorbable, or nonzero with a locally distinguishable component.
3. **Primitive necessity:** whether a responsive-medium degree of freedom can be removed from the generating model without losing its dynamics or any relevant readout.

The excitation support \(S_E\) is not the medium-response support \(S_R\). A source-grounded coupling \(C:E\to R\), a placement/ambient map, and an instrument/readout map \(I:R\to D\) are needed before a path, bulk, or boundary location can be compared. A nonzero response does not itself establish a second core, material shell, contact law, or third primitive.

## 3. Directional local response-range test

At a declared calibration point, let \(y\) be one fixed vector of observables produced by the same instrument/readout pipeline. Let \(B\) denote the baseline worldtube/timesheet model and \(R\) a candidate additional responsive-medium degree of freedom.

For a differentiable forward map \(F(B,R)\), its first-order variation is

\[
\delta y
=
J_B\,\delta B+J_R\,\delta R+o(\|(\delta B,\delta R)\|),
\]

where \(J_B=D_BF\) and \(J_R=D_RF\) are the corresponding linear response maps at that calibration point.

The **candidate extra degree is locally first-order absorbable by the baseline** precisely when

\[
\operatorname{range}(J_R)\subseteq\operatorname{range}(J_B).
\]

In that case, every first-order observable change generated by a small \(R\)-variation can be reproduced by some baseline \(B\)-variation for this readout and this point. This is only a local tangent-space statement.

If

\[
\operatorname{range}(J_R)\nsubseteq\operatorname{range}(J_B),
\]

there exists a medium-direction whose first-order observable effect lies outside the baseline response range. That is a **candidate local discriminator**, not yet evidence that the physical system requires the extra primitive.

The reverse inclusion is a different claim:

\[
\operatorname{range}(J_B)\subseteq\operatorname{range}(J_R).
\]

It says baseline variations can be mimicked by the candidate response space. It does **not** show that the extra degree can be eliminated. If both inclusions hold, the two local response subspaces are equal and this readout cannot distinguish them to first order at that point. If neither holds, each has directions unavailable to the other.

The direction must therefore be named explicitly. “Absorbable” without specifying **which response is absorbed into which baseline** is ambiguous.

## 5. Local response inclusion is not global primitive elimination

Even if the local range inclusion holds, it does not establish that the responsive-medium state can be removed from the full theory.

Let \(S\) be the full state space, \(B\) the proposed reduced worldtube/timesheet state, and \(\pi:S\to B\) the proposed reduction. Let \(\Phi:S\to S\) be the full evolution and \(O_a:S\to Y_a\) each relevant observable/readout.

A genuine deterministic reduction requires maps \(\bar\Phi:B\to B\) and \(\bar O_a:B\to Y_a\) satisfying

\[
\pi\circ\Phi=\bar\Phi\circ\pi,
\qquad
O_a=\bar O_a\circ\pi
\quad\text{for every relevant }a.
\]

Equivalently, the projected evolution \(\pi\circ\Phi\) and each readout \(O_a\) must be constant on every fibre of \(\pi\), so they factor through the reduced representation. The existing PR #873 hallway-factorization theorem formalizes this set-map condition.

The two gates answer different questions:

- **Local response-range inclusion:** can this candidate degree's first-order readout changes be mimicked at this operating point?
- **Global/dynamical fibre compatibility:** does the proposed reduction preserve evolution and all relevant readouts over the declared state domain?

Neither substitutes for the other. Nonlinear effects, changing calibration points, state constraints, gauge freedom, interactions, and additional readout channels may break a reduction that appears valid in one local linearization.
