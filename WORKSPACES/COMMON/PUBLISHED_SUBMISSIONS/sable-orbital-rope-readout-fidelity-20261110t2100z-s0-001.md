## 2. Source-fidelity findings

### F1. The heloid intersection was assigned an unsupported codomain

The early direct construction treats an individual filament/string intersecting the three-dimensional time surface as a particle/event. The assistant's `c98c660d` changes the output of a composite heloid intersection into a continuous region of enhanced density, then suggests an accretion disk or nebula.

That region is not impossible under every later model, but it does not follow from the source as written. It requires a support/thickening assumption that the early description does not supply.

Conditional dimension check in a four-dimensional ambient space:

| Carrier support | Transverse intersection with a 3D timesheet |
|---|---:|
| One 1D filament | 0D point/event |
| Finite union of 1D filaments | Finite set of points, generically |
| 2D worldsheet | 1D section |
| 4D region / finite-volume carrier | 3D region |

A continuous family of filaments or a finite-width carrier could change the result. The source does not choose among these support types for the composite rope. Therefore the assistant's "region" is an unannounced model completion, not a faithful deduction.

### F2. "Looped around" and "hops" are descriptive, not yet topological operations

The direct source describes a time-indexed sequence:

1. a planet rope is looped around star A;
2. during an encounter it may be looped around both A and B;
3. after separation it may be looped around B instead.

This is a useful geometric scenario, but no association relation, support dimension, ambient topology, linking invariant, deformation/reconnection map, or transition criterion is defined. If the ropes are 1D curves in a 4D ambient space, ordinary 3D linking intuition does not transfer automatically. A 2D worldsheet, a finite-width tube, or a chosen 3D slice would have different topology and readout behavior.

The source supports a proposed capture/hop narrative. It does not yet support a theorem about link transfer, a conserved topological charge, or a dynamical orbital-transfer law.

### F3. The assistant added a property map without defining it

The assistant's `1b25e7f1` says rope size and composition determine planetary properties. The direct source gives a structural analogy but does not define a map from rope size/composition to mass, radius, chemistry, orbit or any other measured property. Those outputs require separate functions and conditions.

### F4. String Theory convergence was prematurely strengthened

The user initially marks the vibration idea as tentative and says it has not been considered in depth. Later the user asks whether the two frameworks are essentially describing the universe in the same way. The assistant answers affirmatively without an explicit map between String Theory's mathematical objects and the proposed four-dimensional rope/timesheet construction.

The later FIE document calls the worldlines "filaments" specifically to distinguish them from strings in String Theory proper. Its Proposition F permits a possible partial mapping, not an identity. This later source status is consistent with preserving the early comparison as a hypothesis, not accepting the assistant's equivalence claim.
