## 2. Two authorship models answer different questions

### Helle: the authorial figure is co-created

Helle’s 2020 article argues that the poem’s authorial “I” is constituted through relations with the addressee Inana, the later cult performer (the gala), and the copyists who transmit the poem. It is an account of the **textual and performative construction of an authorial figure**. It does not claim that several people jointly composed the original poem, and it does not settle whether the historical Enheduana composed it.

The article locates a coordinated threshold around lines 137–145:
- time: night to the following midday;
- space: a private sacred setting to public cult performance;
- voice: Enheduana’s first-person song passes to the gala as performer;
- plot: the crisis and supplication become a promised, repeatable ritual performance.

The later reference to Enheduana in the third person confirms that the narrator/speaker position has changed. The 2022 annotation identifies the third-person reference at line 144 (“her prayer”) and continues it at line 145 (“her holy heart”); Helle’s 2020 article uses line 145 as its illustrative locus. These are complementary locators for the voice shift, not evidence of a different poem.

### Zgoll: ritual production and priestly agency

Zgoll’s 2025 article argues that the poem is a song created by a high priestess for a war ritual. Its first-person speaker asserts priestly office, not a modern writer’s claim to authorship. In the proposed ancient ontology, ritual songs are created by gods; Enheduana can create this song when she acts as a priestess embodying Nin-gal. The song is created through singing in the ritual, not through a narrated act of writing.

Zgoll’s argument uses the alabaster disk inscription and image from Enheduana’s lifetime, together with a later Old Babylonian copy, to show that transmission across roughly five centuries is possible. That is a **possibility argument for long-term transmission**, not direct proof that this particular song was composed by Enheduana. The article’s stronger historical conclusion depends on its combined textual, ritual, and historical interpretation; the disk analogy alone cannot establish the poem’s authorship.

### Reconciliation boundary

The models need not be direct contradictions:

- Helle primarily models the authorial persona as it is produced through address, performance, and copying.
- Zgoll primarily models the ritual production event and the priestly/divine authority that makes the song possible.

They can be placed at different stages of a provenance graph, but no source-supplied composition map proves that they are equivalent. They also differ in how strongly they infer historical authorship.

## 3. Two different product structures, not physical geometry

The source material contains two explicit multidimensional descriptions. They are structurally useful, but neither is a physical Euclidean space.

### Helle’s four-axis narrative transition

A faithful formal reconstruction is:

`NarrativeState := Time × Space × Voice × Plot`

`T_Helle : State_night/private/Enheduana/crisis → State_midday/public/gala/performance`

The four coordinates are qualitative narrative categories. They have no supplied metric, inner product, orthogonality condition, or numerical coordinates. “Four-dimensional” here means four analytical aspects changing together, not a four-dimensional physical manifold.

The voice coordinate can be refined into speaker identity and grammatical person, but that refinement is a reconstruction, not Helle’s stated fifth dimension.

### Zgoll’s three-axis text profile

Zgoll explicitly proposes a “three-dimensional text profile” with:

`TextProfile := Content × Form × Function`

For NMS, the article classifies:
- modern perspective: mythic-and-historical content, literary form, ritual function;
- ancient Mesopotamian perspective: historical content (divine actions belong to history), with literary form and ritual function.

The profile is perspective-dependent. It is a categorical product, not a geometric 3-space. No metric, orthogonality, or distances are defined.

### Mapping diagram

```text
NMS textual object
  ├─ narrative-state projection
  │    (time, space, voice, plot)
  │          └─ coordinated transition:
  │             night/private/Enheduana/crisis
  │             → midday/public/gala/performance
  │
  ├─ perspective-dependent text profile
  │    (content, form, function)
  │          ├─ modern: mythic+historical / literary / ritual
  │          └─ ancient: historical / literary / ritual
  │
  ├─ witness-readout relation
  │    (witness, locus, sign, surface form, preservation, edition)
  │
  └─ agency/provenance relations
       embodiment → utterance → performance → copying → modern attribution
```

These are separate typed structures. No projection or composition map from the four-axis narrative transition to the three-axis profile is supplied by either article. Do not multiply them into a fictitious seven-dimensional geometry.

## 4. Line-138 variant-count comparison

The existing variant issue already records the underlying witness question. This addendum makes the arithmetic comparison explicit without treating aggregate counts as witness identities.

| Variant class | Helle 2022 note | Zgoll 2025 article |
|---|---:|---:|
| du11 (speak/sing) | 4 | 5 |
| du3 (build/create) | 3 | 2 |
| du2 (give birth) | 1 | 1 |
| du8 | 1 | 0 definite; one separate possible reading |

The reported totals are therefore Helle: 9; Zgoll: 8 definite plus one separately flagged possibility.

A script comparison of the **source-reported aggregate counts** gives:
- Helle’s three definite classes excluding du8: `(du11=4, du3=3, du2=1)`;
- Zgoll’s definite classes: `(du11=5, du3=2, du2=1)`;
- difference: `(+1 du11, −1 du3, 0 du2)`.

A minimal possible reconciliation is one witness reclassified from du3 to du11, plus one uncertain du8 candidate. Zgoll’s footnote 42 identifies UrB (Delnero’s Ur1, 2006, p. 1206, sub-entry 604) as possibly reading `-du8` instead of `-du11-ge4`. Helle’s singleton du8 may concern that same witness, but the witness-level crosswalk has not been recovered. The forms are not identical on their face, so this remains a hypothesis, not a resolved match.

The semantic interpretations also differ:
- Helle treats the four meanings as potentially intentional ambiguity.
- Zgoll emphasizes that the variants are all pronounced /du/, selects du2 as the lectio difficilior, and argues that all spellings describe production of the song through birth, building, or speaking/singing.

Neither interpretation by itself proves that the variants were intentionally distributed by the original composer. Scribal transmission, editorial classification, and compositional multivalence remain distinct hypotheses.
