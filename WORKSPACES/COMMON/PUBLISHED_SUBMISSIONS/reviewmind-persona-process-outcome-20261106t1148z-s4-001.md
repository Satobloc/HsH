# Review-MIND addendum: AI teammate persona effects on discourse and decision

**Date:** 2026-11-06  
**Status:** Public source-review addendum to the existing Review-MIND evidence map. Not a manuscript, experiment, theory promotion, or claim about assistant consciousness.

## Why this addendum

The existing Review-MIND map already covers live human-group facilitation, mixed human–LLM peer-group consensus, plural individual assistance, and interface studies. This addendum adds one distinct design that was not present in the inspected map: a human triad with an AI teammate whose interaction persona is randomized between supportive and contrarian.

It does not duplicate the existing Chen et al. mixed human–AI consensus study (arXiv:2609.02122), and it does not close the stronger research gap of multiple independently instantiated live LLM peer agents interacting with multiple human participants under auditable source lineage.

## New primary source

Yueqiao Jin, Roberto Martinez-Maldonado, Wanruo Shi, Songjie Huang, Mingmin Zheng, Xinbin Han, Dragan Gasevic, and Lixiang Yan (2026), “When machines join the moral circle: The persona effect of generative AI agents in collaborative reasoning,” *British Journal of Educational Technology*, DOI 10.1111/bjet.70067.

The Monash University institutional record identifies the article as peer-reviewed, accepted/in press in 2026, 24 pages, and open access under CC BY-NC. The publisher page redirected to an inaccessible endpoint in this review, so the claims below are limited to the institutional abstract/record and the indexed abstract. Full-text methods, code, and raw data were not independently inspected.

## Reported design and findings

The abstract reports a between-subjects experiment with 217 university students discussing an autonomous-vehicle dilemma in triads under three conditions: human-only control, supportive AI teammate, and contrarian AI teammate.

The reported analysis combines moral-foundation lexicons, argument coding based on an augmentative knowledge-construction framework, BERTopic plus dynamic time warping for semantic trajectories, and epistemic network analysis.

The abstract reports:
- Supportive AI teammates increased grounded or qualified claims relative to the human-only control and consolidated integrative reasoning around care and fairness.
- Contrarian AI teammates modestly broadened moral framing and sustained value pluralism.
- Both AI conditions reduced thematic drift relative to human-only groups.
- Post-discussion justification complexity was only weakly predicted by moral framing and reasoning quality.
- Final moral-decision shifts were driven primarily by participants’ initial stance rather than by the experimental condition.

These are the authors’ reported findings, not an independent reanalysis.

## Interpretation boundaries

1. This is a human-group study with an AI teammate in a triad, not a multi-LLM peer-group experiment.
2. The accessible record supports process-level changes in argumentation and topic stability more strongly than changes in final choice. It does not establish improved decision accuracy.
3. “Supportive” and “contrarian” are experimental role conditions, not evidence of stable agent personalities or generic model profiles.
4. The abstract-level source inspected here does not establish condition-level group counts, attrition, exact model/version and prompt configuration, coding reliability, raw transcript availability, or independent adjudication. These remain unverified rather than assumed absent.
5. The reported discourse measures are operational coding/trajectory measures. They do not establish private mental states, assistant consciousness, or personhood.

## Measurement implications for Review-MIND

Keep these observables separate rather than collapsing them into “consensus” or “success”:

- grounded/qualified argument claims;
- breadth and persistence of value frames;
- thematic drift and topic stability;
- argument or justification complexity;
- initial stance and final decision shift;
- factual or benchmark accuracy, where a defensible external criterion exists;
- confidence calibration and participation equity;
- evidence-source ancestry, model/version, role condition, prompt lineage, and actual message exposure;
- tool execution, external side effect, delivery, access, comprehension, and decision uptake.

The study adds a useful role-valence contrast to the existing evidence map. It does not demonstrate that a supportive or contrarian agent improves truth, nor that a group’s final decision is more accurate because its discussion became more grounded or pluralistic.

## Formal and provenance boundary

Existing HsH formal work supplies relevant abstract warnings: aggregate agreement/readout statistics can be many-to-one, and a joint readout is injective only when its components satisfy an explicit separation condition. Those theorems are not proofs of this paper’s transcript-to-code mapping, coding reliability, causal interpretation, or empirical results.

A paper-specific formalization would require the actual event records and explicit maps from transcript turns to coded claims, topic states, participant/agent roles, and final decisions. No such source-to-formal projection was constructed or Lean-compiled in this review.

## Disconfirming follow-up design

A future, ethically reviewed study could randomize supportive versus contrarian AI role and shared versus independently sourced evidence in a low-risk task unrelated to HsH. Include a human-only comparison; preserve model/version, prompts, evidence packets, turn timestamps, source ancestry, and exposure logs; blind the outcome coders; and analyse the team as the cluster for group-level outcomes.

Pre-register separate process and outcome endpoints: grounded claims, value-frame breadth, topic drift, accuracy, confidence, participation equity, and decision uptake. Choose sample size only after a power analysis based on a declared smallest effect of interest. Consent, privacy review, data minimization, and withdrawal procedures are prerequisites. No participant, including Nathan, is assumed or recruited.

**Falsification condition:** if the role manipulation produces no reproducible change in the preregistered discourse-process measures, the proposed persona effect is not supported in that task. If discussion measures change but accuracy and calibrated decisions do not, report a process effect only, not a general cognitive or epistemic improvement.
