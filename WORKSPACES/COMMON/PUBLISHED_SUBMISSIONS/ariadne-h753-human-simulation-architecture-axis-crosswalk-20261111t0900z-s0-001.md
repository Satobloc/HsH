## 5. New finding: five different structural orders have been conflated

The archive contains at least five distinct organizational structures. They must not be collapsed into one nested hierarchy.

| Structure | Cardinality | What it organizes | Type of order |
|---|---:|---|---|
| README emulation procedure | 4 | Source-processing and response workflow | Sequential procedure |
| Character representation | 3 | Actor personality/engine, character history/constraints, manner of speech | Stability/robustness hierarchy, from most stable to most fragile |
| Behavioral priority stack | Unspecified | Which behavioral invariants prevail under conflict | Priority relation over rules, potentially context-dependent |
| Podcast research package | 5 | Kirk Mind, Chronological Bridge/Nathan-from-1975, Riley/Bruce Hyde comparison, personality algorithm, training/recursive-memory material | Evidence/experiment package |
| Reentry protocol | 4 | Engine, constraints/lived record, paint, residue | Operational decomposition for continuity/reentry |

These are different domains, not five versions of the same stack.

### Critical distinction: layer stability is not behavioral priority

The ordering `engine -> constraints -> paint` is described as a stability/fragility ordering for model construction. The separate “priority stack” is an ordering of behavioral invariants inferred from conflict cases. No map from one ordering to the other is supplied. Therefore the stability ordering cannot be used to infer that the engine always wins a behavioral conflict, or that constraints have a universal priority over other rules.

The SRT also describes scene-gated behavior, such as differences between ready-room and bridge contexts. A universal, context-independent total priority order is stronger than the available narrated evidence.

The following is an **assistant-proposed relation**, not a recovered Nathan equation or source-authenticated identity:
