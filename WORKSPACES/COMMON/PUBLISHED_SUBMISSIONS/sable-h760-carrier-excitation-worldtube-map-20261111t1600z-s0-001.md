## Executive finding

A newly recovered June 9, 2026 direct user message makes an important object distinction: the electron is described as a single-coil particle worldline/tube; quarks are described as forming 2- and 3-braids associated with mesons and hadrons; photons and other true bosons are described as vibrations on filaments, not as filaments themselves. A June 10 direct retrospective says that “worldline” was early vocabulary for what had first been thought of as strings, before the physical-filament conception was distinguished from string theory.

Combined with the September 7 direct instruction to choose a **spatial Kerr section** and extend it along the filament, this supplies a plausible route from a one-dimensional carrier to a four-dimensional finite-core worldtube: a one-dimensional base with a three-dimensional spatial section over each base point has a four-dimensional total space, provided the extension is locally bundle-like. That is a conditional geometric reconstruction, not a recovered Nathan equation. The section, embedding, normality, boundary, metric, and map to the 2025 action remain unspecified.

The new source-fidelity delta is broader than the dimensional count:

1. The carrier and its vibrational excitation must remain distinct objects.
2. “2-/3-braids” are not automatically equivalent to Hopf/Borromean links of two/three filaments.
3. A 3-form current and a 2-form worldsheet current have different support-degree meanings in four dimensions; they are not Hodge duals.
4. The 2025 SAT-O1 one-parameter curve/action does not, by itself, define its separately named four-dimensional worldtube.
5. The later direct Kerr-section instruction is a candidate missing cross-section map, not proof that the historical O1 equation already contained it.

## 3. Carrier, excitation, braid and link are different types

The June 9 direct message distinguishes a filament carrier from a bosonic vibration on that carrier. The O1 module calls the hyperhelix a vibrational mode, but then encodes the oscillation directly in the coordinates of the filament curve. A source-faithful translation needs to say whether the sinusoidal curve is:

- the carrier’s own embedded geometry;
- a mode or excitation defined on a separate carrier; or
- a combined effective representation of both.

The direct source favors keeping carrier and excitation distinct, but does not yet define the mode field or its map into an observed worldsheet.

The same issue appears in the braid language. The direct user message says “2- and 3-braids.” SAT-O1 instead describes a Hopf link as a stable two-filament configuration and a Borromean link as a stable three-filament configuration. A braid is not automatically a link: a braid word, strand count, endpoint permutation, closure convention, ambient spatial slice, and time-evolution map are required before a braid-to-link correspondence can be asserted.

The later direct 4D-slinky description adds another caution: an incomplete ring-like cross-section is not automatically a closed link. The O1 Hopf/Borromean assignments are historical assistant/document propositions, not a source-established translation of the direct 2-/3-braid statement.

## 5. Support-dimension and current-degree crosswalk

| Geometric support | Support dimension | Poincaré-dual degree |
|---|---:|---:|
| Filament centerline/worldline | 1 | 3-form |
| Two-dimensional worldsheet | 2 | 2-form |
| Three-dimensional tube boundary | 3 | 1-form |
| Four-dimensional tube interior | 4 | 0-form |

| Intersected support | Expected intersection dimension |
|---|---:|

These are conditional dimension statements. They do not establish that any particular H(s)H object has the listed support or satisfies transversality.

## 7. Discrepancy ledger

1. **Curve-to-volume gap:** the O1 \(\gamma:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^{3,1}\) is one-dimensional, while its worldtube is separately called a four-dimensional volume. The O1 equations contain no explicit three-dimensional section/fiber. The September 7 spatial-Kerr-section instruction offers a candidate map but does not complete it.
2. **Carrier/excitation conflation:** direct user language distinguishes filaments from bosonic vibrations on filaments. The O1 hyperhelix formula embeds oscillation in the filament curve without specifying whether it is carrier geometry or a mode field.
3. **Braid/link substitution:** direct 2-/3-braid language is translated into two-/three-filament Hopf/Borromean links without braid words, closure, ambient slice, or strand-to-filament map.
4. **Closed/open topology:** the direct 4D slinky is explicitly not a complete unbroken ring; the O1 link constructions are closed-link objects. Their relationship is unestablished.
5. **3-form/2-form duality:** the assistant’s 2-form-as-dual-of-3-form statement is not a Hodge-dual identity in four dimensions. The relevant support map is missing.
6. **Index completeness:** the direct suggested 2-form term has an uncontracted index as written. The assistant repeated it as a scalar topological term without adding the missing contraction.
7. **Force typing:** O1 says no external fields or forces are postulated; the later direct source calls force primitive and describes mutual accommodation. These could be reconciled if force is an interaction relation rather than a separate carrier, but the source does not define that type.
8. **Mode boundary:** the static-block opening and O1 vibrational dynamics should be mapped through the later Mode 1/2/3 methodological ladder, not treated as an unqualified contradiction.
9. **Lexical drift:** early “worldline”/“strings” vocabulary must not be retrofitted into standard string-theory ontology.
