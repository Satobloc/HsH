1. **Kerr spacetime:** a 4D standard-GR solution.
2. **Selected spatial Kerr section:** a 3D section chosen from that spacetime. The source rejects treating the entire 4D Kerr spacetime as a cross-section.
3. **Filament extension:** a map that extends the selected section along the 4D filament. It is not yet defined as a Cartesian product, fibration or transport law.

## 3. Three different rotation objects

### A. Flat Euclidean 4D kinematic rotation

BEDROCK preserves a 2026-10-20/21 kinematic example with a skew generator Omega, zero trace and a squared-operator identity. The existing Ravel Lean module chooses one assistant-defined block-diagonal representative with two equal planar rotation blocks.

This is Euclidean R4 linear algebra. It is not the Lorentz group, not a Kerr metric, not a material-frame twist and not evidence of gravity. The representative proves only its own matrix identities.

### B. Standard Kerr spin and frame dragging

In standard GR, Kerr spin is encoded by mass M and angular momentum J, with a = J/M in geometrized units. Its mass and current multipoles obey the Kerr sequence:

M_l + i S_l = M (i a)^l.

This is a standard exterior solution constraint, not an H(s)H equation. The Kerr spin length is not a finite-core thickness, coil diameter, timesheet thickness or worldtube axis-to-circumference dimension.

### C. Internal material-frame or normal-bundle twist

An internal twist requires a specified material frame/director or a defined normal-bundle connection and transport law. Frenet torsion, a coordinate-frame rotation, the Euclidean R4 generator and Kerr angular momentum are not interchangeable by naming.

The existing Ravel projection/readout formalization in PR #1133 supplies a generic condition: a readout can descend to a quotient only when it is constant on the relevant fibers. It does not establish which internal rotations are gauge or physical for the proposed H(s)H core. The Ravel finite-core gauge-reduction note remains a sandbox, not canonical BEDROCK.

A physical twist becomes testable only when a source-defined marker or holonomy maps to an observable not already absorbed by Kerr spin, frame choice, standard multipoles or nuisance parameters.
