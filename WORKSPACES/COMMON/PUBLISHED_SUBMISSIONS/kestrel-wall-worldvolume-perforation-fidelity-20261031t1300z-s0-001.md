1. **Static intersection error:** assistant initially made a helix-plane intersection into a spiral. Direct geometry says one point.
2. **Moving-slice error:** assistant let the straight-axis point move in the plane and gave the helix point a spiral path. Direct geometry says stationary S and circular H for the perpendicular case.
3. **Pitch/radius error:** assistant tied orbit radius to pitch. Direct geometry ties radius to helix cross-section; pitch controls angular progression.
4. **False helix extremum:** assistant used a maximum-radius point of a perfect helix to make points meet. Direct geometry rejects this because the radius is constant.
5. **Barrier-bypass error:** assistant first described the helix bypassing the wall in a newly exposed direction. Direct correction requires an actual opening in the wall history.
6. **Continuous-opening overstatement:** assistant says a past perforation leaves a continuous opening. Direct source only requires a perforation event somewhere in the relevant history; later closure is allowed.
7. **Wall/ER conflation risk:** the barrier aperture is not shown to be the ER mouth. Keep them separate until a map is supplied.
8. **Centerline/core conflation:** the image shows a thin helix; the physical finite-core worldtube has thickness. Its 3D slice is not a point.
9. **Medium/core conflation:** Kelvin vortices belong to a responsive medium proposal, not to the worldtube itself.
10. **Ontology reversal:** the June 7 SAT Methodology podcast synthesis says the manifold supersedes the tube. That is not Nathan Direct; July 11 and September 7 direct messages preserve a thickened line/worldtube as a physical carrier and treat the slice as an appearance/intersection.
11. **Terminology mismatch:** the existing Lean module uses 'readout' labels despite Nathan's explicit preference for intersection, instantiation, and observable slice.
12. **Symbol collision:** KestrelMovingSlice uses c for cosine of tilt. Do not confuse it with c in the visual ct axis or with light speed; a future code cleanup could use χ for the cosine.
13. **Visual shorthand overclaim:** 3D = QM and 4D = SAT is a presentation convention, not a proven theory equivalence.
