## 4. Mapping diagram and missing maps

M^4 (carrier)
  ├─ gamma_i^1 (early filament) ── intersection with Sigma_t^3 ──> 0D event/readout
  ├─ W_i^4 (finite-core worldtube) ── local perturbation E_i ──> ?
  ├─ Sigma_t^3 (ideal front/timesheet)
  └─ U(x) (responsive medium) ── level-set/transport map ? ──> T or Sigma_t

{E_i} ── coherence/aggregation C({E_i}) ──> candidate collective timewave T
T ── thin-front/level-set projection ? ──> Sigma_t^3

The required maps are not supplied by the source:

1. Thickening/embedding: iota_i: gamma_i^1 -> W_i^4.
2. Local excitation: E_i: W_i^4 -> E_i.
3. Collective coherence: C: {E_i} -> T.
4. Front projection: pi: T -> Sigma_t^3.
5. Medium-to-front map: L: U -> Sigma_t^3.
6. Readout: R: gamma_i intersect Sigma_t or W_i intersect Sigma_t -> particle observables.

For representations Y_i and Y_j to be equivalent, a proposed map f_ij: Y_i -> Y_j must preserve a declared common invariant and the observable readout. A useful proof obligation is
\[
q_j\circ f_{ij}=q_i,\qquad O_j\circ f_{ij}=O_i,
\]
with composition coherence f_ik = f_jk composed with f_ij wherever all maps are defined. No such family of maps has yet been recovered.

The dimensional distinction is important. Under transversality in four dimensions:
- 1-D filament intersect 3-D hypersurface has dimension 0;
- 2-D worldsheet intersect 3-D hypersurface has dimension 1;
- 4-D finite core intersect 3-D hypersurface has dimension 3.

A finite-thickness 4D band is a different object again. It must not be silently identified with an ideal 3D wavefront.
