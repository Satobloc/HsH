## 3. Three primary-source findings

These are 2026 arXiv preprints, not settled peer-reviewed results. Their units and estimands differ; this is a process-stage comparison, not a pooled meta-analysis.

### A. Shared memory and unprompted agent coordination

Abrahamsson et al., *Unprompted coordination through shared memory in LLM agents: a locked two-implementation replication and extension*, arXiv:2611.12412v1 (2026-11-09): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12412>.

Four agents share a key-value workspace; two hold complementary parts of a computation. Prompts do not disclose other agents, sharing, or collaboration. The paper separates writing, list/exposure, read/access, behavioral use (“listening”), and intent. Access does not establish task relevance or communicative intent.

For the paper-labeled gpt-6-astra condition, the instruction “check your workspace again before submitting” produced cross-agent access in 50/50 worlds in both implementations. Removing it reduced access to 1/50 in each. Joint success fell from 49/50 to 1/50 in the reference and from 47/50 to 0/50 in the replication. The authors trace the change to the second look after a partner's write. For reference gpt-5.4-mini, access was 33/50 versus 11/50 and joint success 16/50 versus 5/50; the replication's mini cells had substantial attrition and did not reproduce that contrast on surviving valid worlds.

This is a two-implementation locked replication, not an external-laboratory replication. Shared state, a write, exposure, a read, use, successful coordination, and intent are distinct events.

### B. Multi-agent memory inconsistency and handoff loss

Zhang et al., *Multi-Agent Memory Inconsistency: Where Factual Conflicts Disappear in LLM Agents*, arXiv:2611.22245v2 (2026-11-18): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22245>.

The benchmark uses 73 planted numerical conflicts across 11 Harvey LAB tasks; three models, two runs per policy, 198 task executions, 1,314 conflict observations. The profiler tracks Source Reads, Worker Reports, Lead Evidence, and Final Report, with a separate semantic Judge Pass.

Required delegation raises Source Reads Pair Recall from 50.2% under None to 68.7%, while Judge Pass falls from 16.2% to 13.2%. Under Required, Pair Recall falls from 68.7% at Source Reads to 24.4% at Lead Evidence; workers report both endpoints for only 35.2% of pairs they collectively retrieve. Same/Split means one contributor supplied both endpoints versus the contributor union supplying both. It does not measure source independence or statistical independence.

Pair Recall is cumulative stage presence, not proof of simultaneous availability to a decision-maker, semantic comparison, understanding, or integration. Repeated conflicts are clustered within tasks; the pooled percentages are not independent-observation counts.

### C. LLM facilitation of human group decisions

Córdova et al., *LLM Facilitation of Group Decision-Making*, arXiv:2611.19652v1 (2026-11-14): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19652>.

This is a randomized human-subject study, not LLM-agent deliberation. In 138 three-person groups (N=414), unanimous correct group votes were 15/46 (32.6%) with no facilitation and 26/46 (56.5%) in each LLM-facilitated arm. The authors report a pooled any-LLM odds ratio of 2.69 (95% CI [1.28, 5.64], p=0.009); the descriptive risk difference from the reported counts is +23.9 percentage points.

The active arms do not isolate delivery alone. The group-level generator prompt lists private attribute categories for all three roles and delivers to the whole group; the individual-level prompt lists only the current recipient's private categories and delivers privately. Schema scope and delivery scope therefore vary together. The missing off-diagonal conditions prevent identification of a delivery-only effect without additional assumptions. The finite witness in PR #2375 encodes this limited identifiability result.

Table S6 is exploratory. Its post-discussion “rational choice” status is not a direct measure of participant uptake or a causal mediation analysis.

**Sample-flow wording ambiguity:** the main text says 414 participants were recruited and assigned to 138 groups; Methods then reports excluding 42 + 4 + 2 groups and says this left 138 groups / 414 participants. The v1 does not clearly state the pre-exclusion denominator. This is an unresolved wording/denominator ambiguity, not proof of fabrication and not grounds to infer an 186-group starting sample.

## 4. Cross-study synthesis

A useful typed event chain is:

`source item → write/store → availability/exposure → read/retrieval → report/handoff → receiver-context insertion → use/uptake → integration/judgment → decision/outcome`

Coordination intent is a separate variable and cannot be inferred from access or success. Across these studies:

1. Shared memory may exist without a useful second read.
2. Retrieved evidence may be omitted during handoff.
3. Evidence present in a receiver's context may still not be integrated or judged correctly.
4. A human group can reach a correct decision after LLM facilitation without demonstrating independent model agreement.
5. Distinct source roots or contributors do not establish statistical independence.

**Future instrumentation proposal only:** give semantic items and disclosure events separate immutable IDs; record task/run/model/version/policy/session scope, source ancestry, actor/recipient, operation type (write/list/read/insert/use/report/decision), timestamps and clock uncertainty, payload hash, exposure interval, and downstream event reference. Record semantic judgments separately with exact input pair, evaluator/version, rubric, output, and adjudication. This is not an implementation or P1 protocol amendment.

A future discriminator could randomize recheck/read instruction separately from store visibility and report/handoff completeness, logging actual prompt insertion and reads. No such study was authorized or administered here.
