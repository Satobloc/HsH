## Executive finding

The recurring translation failure is a collapse of four different things:

1. the one-dimensional carrier or core;
2. the finite-dimensional support around it;
3. one intersection with a timesheet;
4. the history or family of those intersections.

A second failure then attaches later physical labels to earlier geometry without preserving the date and status of the identification.

The source record is more coherent when these are kept separate. The early sketch is described by Nathan as an intersection geometry; the 2024 construction carefully distinguishes a static section from the trajectory of that section as a plane moves; the July 2026 Minkowski construction deliberately steps back to a worldline and timesheet without a tube; the October/November 2026 H(s)H description adds a four-dimensional tube around a one-dimensional core, a three-dimensional boundary, and a possible mouth/interface with the timesheet. The maps connecting those descriptions remain partly undefined.

The new contribution is a source-preserving translation contract and a chronology of recurring assistant over-completions. This is not another general worldtube hierarchy audit and does not replace the existing Kestrel h892/h903 reports.

## 9. Source-preserving translation contract

For subsequent work, keep six epistemic labels separate:

- DIRECT_GEOMETRY: the user's described object, dimension, relation, or transformation;
- DIRECT_INTERPRETATION: the user's later physical or conceptual identification;
- HISTORICAL_SOURCE: an older equation/document whose authorship and date are separately recorded;
- ASSISTANT_RECONSTRUCTION: a mathematically plausible completion not stated by the user;
- STANDARD_CONDITIONAL: a mathematical consequence that follows only under declared assumptions;
- MEASURED: an independently sourced observation with provenance, units, uncertainty, and prediction/fit status.

For every proposed hierarchy or nesting:
1. type the source and target objects;
2. state intrinsic and ambient dimensions;
3. declare the coordinate/metric and parameter;
4. type the interaction/force relation without assuming a third independent carrier;
5. define the projection, thickening, slice, or response map;
6. give the conditions under which maps compose;
7. state which observables are constant on projection fibers;
8. preserve source equations verbatim and place corrections separately;
9. use intersection/instantiation/observable-slice language rather than defaulting to "readout".

No new canonical symbol is registered in this report. All labels used for the geometry diagram are local namespace labels.
