A particle is their intersection; energy exchange and back-reaction are central. GR, QM, and the Standard Model are to be imported essentially intact and conceptually translated. Extension B proposes a common distortion mechanism and “the same equations” for fermions, bosons, gravity, and time.

That is a common-geometric-programme hypothesis, not yet a defined mathematical isomorphism between GR and QM. The later “Relativistic–Quantum Isomorphism” label, single constrained S³ curve, Lagrangian, and spectral bridge occur in generated NotebookLM/LLM output. The reconstruction collapses the source’s two interacting objects into one curve and calls the result an exact identity without maps, inverses, or structure-preservation conditions.

## Formalization boundary

A common parent with projections \(W\xrightarrow{\pi_{\rm GR}}G\) and \(W\xrightarrow{\pi_{\rm QM}}Q\) is a correspondence, not automatically an isomorphism. A map \(\Phi:G\to Q\) satisfying \(\pi_{\rm QM}=\Phi\circ\pi_{\rm GR}\) exists on the image of \(\pi_{\rm GR}\) exactly when \(\pi_{\rm QM}\) is constant on each fiber of \(\pi_{\rm GR}\):

\[
\pi_{\rm GR}(x)=\pi_{\rm GR}(y)\Rightarrow
\pi_{\rm QM}(x)=\pi_{\rm QM}(y).
\]

An isomorphism additionally needs an inverse, identity compositions, and structure preservation. [Kestrel PR #873](https://github.com/Satobloc/HsH/pull/873) contains relevant abstract factorization infrastructure, not a physical GR–QM map; its overall current build is not green. No new Lean theorem was written or compiled because the source-defined domains, codomains, operators, projections, and inverse maps are missing. This is a source-definition block, not a failed proof.
