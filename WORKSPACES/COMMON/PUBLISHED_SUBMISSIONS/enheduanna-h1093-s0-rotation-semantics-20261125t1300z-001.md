## 2. Rotation is overloaded

The source corpus uses “rotation” for different objects and operations:

1. **Kerr rotation:** stationary axisymmetric geometry and angular momentum. \(a=J/(Mc)\) is not fixed by mass alone; for fixed \(M,J\), the stationary Kerr parameter is fixed. This does not establish a time-varying diameter.
2. **Minkowski thread rotation:** a direct, tentative proposal whose object, material parameter, metric, and timesheet relation remain underspecified.
3. **Block-universe filament/topological motion:** the October 9 approximation treats the filament as stationary while particle motion is associated with its topology.
4. **Nambu–Goto material-string rotation:** a time-dependent string embedding with a two-dimensional worldsheet and specified tension action.
5. **SO(4) point-orbit rotation:** an assistant-generated one-parameter orbit in positive-definite four-space, not a filament or finite-core worldtube.
6. **Cruller internal rotation/worldtube birth:** a direct proposal whose relation to material motion and topology is not defined.

The October 9 stationary-filament statement and October 20 rotating-thread/Cruller language are not automatically contradictory. They may use rotation at different levels, but no source-defined map distinguishes material motion, geometric orientation, topological traversal, and changing timesheet intersection.

## 3. Existing mathematical controls

### Point-worldline control

The assistant’s \(3+1\) Minkowski model

\[
X^\mu(t)=(ct,R(t)\cos\phi(t),R(t)\sin\phi(t),0),
\qquad
v^2=\dot R^2+R^2\dot\phi^2
\]

shows that angular motion can coexist with constant \(R\). It is a point-worldline decomposition, not a thread, worldsheet, or finite-core support.

## 4. Diameter and object typing

At least four quantities must remain distinct:

- \(D_{\mathrm{Kerr}}\): a coordinate parameter/diameter associated with a Kerr representation.
- \(D_{\mathrm{int}}\): intrinsic diameter of a filament or finite-core support.
- \(D_{\Sigma}\): diameter of a timesheet intersection.
- \(D_{\mathrm{proj}}\): coordinate-projected width of an observed trace.

The Nambu–Goto controls use their own slice-dependent spatial diameter and projected width. The SO(4) theorem concerns the norm of a point. Neither supplies the direct H(s)H diameter functional. The min/max conjecture cannot be evaluated until the object, metric, slice, and diameter functional are specified. “Mass-independent” does not establish “independent of fixed \(M,J\).”
