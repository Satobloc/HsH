## 2. Evidence class: advice-taking, not live agent interaction

The article reports two separate human-participant studies, with (N=60) and (N=50). They use a medical information-cascade / advice-taking task: participants receive private evidence and public advice attributed to human or AI sources.

The source-identity result is conditional on the accuracy design. In the equal-accuracy study, human advice receives greater weight than AI advice. When advisor accuracy varies in the second study, the human-versus-AI source effect disappears. The authors interpret this pattern in terms of informational and normative influence; source identity is not a direct measurement of private motive, and perceived credibility or validity remains an alternative explanation.

This is a real human-subject decision/advice study, but it is **not** a live, multi-turn human-LLM group interaction. The design does not supply reciprocal human-agent or agent-agent message exchange, nor does it measure the behavior of a live LLM teammate. The original evidence map's classification on that boundary remains correct.

## 3. Relevance to the existing MIND evidence map

Keep this article in the **human-AI advice-taking / source-identity** category. Do not pool its two study samples with the samples in live peer-group experiments, and do not treat it as a replication of multi-agent deliberation. It is a useful comparator because it separates source identity from advice accuracy, but it does not close the documented gap for studies with multiple human participants, multiple live LLM peers, reciprocal communication, traceable evidence packets, and independent outcome adjudication.
