## 2. The new decision layer: eliminate, absorb, retain, or remain unresolved

The decision is about the representation of a responsive sector, not whether the Kelvin-equivalent candidate may be ignored. | Outcome | Required condition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `ELIMINABLE_AT_DECLARED_SCALE` | All relevant readouts and evolution close on the worldtube/timesheet state; the response has no independent initial/boundary data after source-defined constraints are applied. | The response is derivable from the two-state representation at this scale and for the declared observables. |
| `ABSORBABLE_INTO_TIMESHEET` | An explicit encoding folds the responsive-sector degrees into an extended timesheet state, and both evolution and readout close after encoding. No separate medium initial data or unconstrained retuning is needed. | Internal response state is retained, but not as an independent third primitive at this representation/resolution. |
| `INDEPENDENT_THIRD_STATE_CANDIDATE` | No admissible timesheet encoding closes both evolution and readout; the responsive sector has independent initial/boundary data and dynamics, plus a separately measurable signature that survives nuisance control and predicts a genuine holdout. | A third state-bearing sector is a candidate. This is not yet an established material-medium ontology or a promoted theory constituent. |
| `UNRESOLVED / NOT_SEPARATELY_IDENTIFIABLE` | Source-defined maps, measured contrasts, or data are insufficient to decide. | Do not infer either absence or independence. Carry the candidate and state exactly what is missing. |

Two non-substitution rules matter:

1. A response term, current-like connection, fitted coefficient, or improved in-sample fit does not by itself establish an independent primitive.
2. Failure to observe a medium-specific effect with an acquisition that cannot vary or resolve it does not establish that the medium is absent or eliminable.

A response may be observable yet absorbable into an extended timesheet state. Conversely, a response may be invisible at one resolution but still be needed for closure at a finer resolution. Every conclusion is therefore scale- and observable-scoped.

## 3. Explicit map contract

The following are proposed interface names and domains, not source-defined physical equations:

- **Full state:** worldtube state + timesheet state + responsive-sector state.
- **Encoding map:** full state → reduced state, where the reduced state is either worldtube + timesheet or worldtube + extended-timesheet state.
- **Full evolution:** full state at one declared slice/time label → full state at a later label.
- **Reduced evolution:** reduced state at the first label → reduced state at the later label.
- **Full observation:** full state → calibrated instrument record.
- **Reduced observation:** reduced state → the same instrument-record space.

Absorption requires both of the following diagrams to commute on the declared admissible state set:

1. **Dynamical closure:** encode the full state after full evolution, or evolve the encoded state with the reduced evolution; the resulting reduced state must be the same.
2. **Observation factorization:** observing the full state must equal observing its encoded reduced state.

For elimination, the encoding is the projection that drops the responsive-sector state. For absorption, the encoding folds that state into an explicitly defined extended timesheet state. For an independent third-state candidate, no source-admissible encoding into the timesheet closes both maps, and the remaining state must have independently constrained initial/boundary data and an identifiable observable consequence.

This is stronger than showing that one instantaneous observable factors through a projection. It tests whether the reduced state is sufficient for future evolution as well as present readout. Apply the test separately at each declared scale/coarse-graining level; a coarse-scale closure does not prove fine-scale closure.

The direct source does not yet define the required state spaces, full/reduced evolution maps, encoding map, or instrument composition. The diagrams are therefore a specification of what must be recovered or constructed, not a claim that the maps already exist.
