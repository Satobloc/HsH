# Kestrel | Independent Reader Reconstruction Trial

**Date:** 2026-11-01  
**Classification:** cross-boundary workflow architecture proposal / Kestrel synthesis  
**Status:** PROPOSED / NOT ADMINISTERED / NO PAPER-GATE CHANGE / NO THEORY PROMOTION

## Executive result

The project already requires field-facing papers to be intelligible to a competent reader who has never encountered SAT/H(s)H, and the three-paper campaign separately requires source provenance, independent review, mathematical triage, genuine data where relevant, and a readable derivative.

What is still missing is a practical test of whether an outsider can reconstruct a claim's route from source to conclusion, then travel backward from its declared failure condition to the exact assumption or map that would have to change.

I propose an **Independent Reader Reconstruction Trial (IRRT)**. It is a diagnostic of legibility and falsifiability, not a new theory gate, paper gate, or substitute for independent technical review.

## 1. Why this is distinct from the existing map contract

The Kestrel multi-scale contract separates:
- recursive nesting;
- coarse-graining;
- physical section/intersection;
- diagrammatic projection;
- sector-specific response;
- instrument/reconstruction readout.

Those maps are not interchangeable. Some may be non-injective; a downstream deterministic readout cannot recover distinctions already discarded upstream.

The IRRT asks a different question: can a competent outsider identify which map or evidentiary step is being used, what it preserves or loses, and where a failure would propagate, without relying on Nathan's oral memory?

The physical map chain and the evidence/provenance chain are different domains. Their shared workflow lesson is explicit traceability, not physical equivalence.

## 2. Test unit: one bounded claim packet

Do not ask a reader to reconstruct the entire archive or the entire theory. Select one representative claim and provide only its public-safe packet.

Minimum packet contents:

1. **Claim identity:** stable claim ID, one-sentence scope, and current status.
2. **Source:** original source locator, exact passage/message locator where public and appropriate, source role/authorship classification, version and digest.
3. **Interpretation:** current reading, relevant correction/supersession, and any live alternative.
4. **Transformation chain:** each representation or map named with its input, output, assumptions, and known information loss. If a map is undefined, say so.
5. **Formal status:** exact Lean module/theorem/build evidence where a definite proposition exists; otherwise the precise formalization blocker. A proof of the encoded statement is not proof that the encoding faithfully captures the source.
6. **Empirical status, when physical:** identifiable measured system and dataset version, provenance, units, uncertainties/covariance, standard comparator, calibration/holdout split, and prediction-versus-fit classification.
7. **Failure condition:** the observable or source condition that would weaken, reject, or leave the claim unresolved.
8. **Dependencies:** which other claims or sectors change if this claim is corrected, and which are not shown to depend on it.

A missing item must remain visibly missing. The packet must not be completed by invented measurements, inferred source identity, or oral clarification during the trial.

## 3. Two passes

### Pass A — forward reconstruction

Without private context or oral coaching, the reader must be able to state:

- what is actually claimed;
- who or what the source evidence identifies, and what it does not identify;
- which parts are established physics, historical SAT, current H(s)H construction, assistant reconstruction, proposal, or open question;
- which transformations connect the source object to the formal or physical object;
- what the standard comparator is;
- whether the result is a fit, a derivation, a held-out prediction, a formal theorem, or an unresolved proposal;
- what an independent reader should inspect next.

### Pass B — backward failure localization

Starting from the claim's own declared null, discrepancy, failed proof, or missing-map condition, the reader must identify:

- the first unsupported or failed link;
- the source/map/assumption/parameter that would need revision;
- which downstream claims and observables depend on it;
- where the effect is known to stop;
- what evidence would distinguish a local failure from a cross-sector failure.

Do not invent a numerical null result to run this pass. Use an actual recorded result where one exists; otherwise trace the packet's predeclared failure condition as a design exercise and label it as such.

## 4. Outcomes

Use categorical outcomes, not a single averaged score:

- **RECONSTRUCTABLE:** both passes can be completed from the packet and its cited public sources without hidden premises or oral rescue.
- **BLOCKED_EXPLICIT:** a required source, map, dataset, uncertainty, or proof is missing, and the reader correctly identifies why no stronger conclusion follows. This is a useful and honest outcome, not a failed trial.
- **NONRECONSTRUCTABLE:** the reader must guess, conflate distinct objects/statuses, invent a missing step, or cannot localize the declared failure path.

Record the exact failed transition. Do not turn a reader's confusion into evidence against the underlying physics; first determine whether the defect is the theory, the translation, the packet, or the presentation.

## 5. Three-paper application

| Paper | Bounded claim packet | Forward reconstruction must recover | Backward pass must locate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1 cognition** | One recorded multi-agent claim about shared-source errors or apparent consensus | Actual model/version/prompt/settings/output provenance, evidence-root and message ancestry, inferential unit, metric and limitations | Whether failure arises from source dependence, transmission, adoption, adjudication, or statistical unit |
| **P2 measured system** | One source-faithful H(s)H map applied to one real measured system | Original SAT/H(s)H statement, standard comparator, dataset/version, units, uncertainty, calibration/holdout, and predicted observable | Which map, shared parameter, or measured discrepancy falsifies the proposed cross-sector account |
| **P3 full theory** | One representative cross-sector construction, not the whole theory | Original source geometry, typed objects, explicit transformations, known-physics constraints, formal status, and open requirements | Which dependency breaks, which sectors inherit the break, and which claims remain independently viable |

This does not alter ownership: Orson retains P1 cognition; P2 retains its measured-system and specialist allocation; P3 retains its source-grounded full-theory scope. It does not advance any paper gate.

## 6. Why this helps under all three outcomes

- **If the theory fails or narrows:** the trial localizes the failure and preserves historical, mathematical, methodological, and educational work that does not depend on the failed claim.
- **If only part survives:** the dependency record makes it possible to separate viable substructures without treating the project as all-or-nothing.
- **If substantial parts survive:** a competent outsider can engage the work without Nathan serving as the archive index, translator, and live help desk for every question.

The same test exposes a dangerous success-path failure: a claim can become widely repeated while its source, map, or uncertainty disappears in successive summaries. Public attention should not outrun the ability to reconstruct what was actually established.

## 7. Human-scale consequence

Nathan's irreplaceable contribution is concentrated in original intent, corrections that cannot be recovered from the surviving record, high-consequence choices, and his own authorial interpretation. Routine retrieval, hashing, build verification, citation checking, rendering QA, and repeated status reconciliation should be delegated to the existing teams and tools.

The project should be designed so that Nathan is a source of meaning and judgment, not its sole routing layer. The project should preserve ordinary life and non-project commitments as fixed constraints, not rewards deferred until the archive is finished.

Prefer actions that improve both the failure and success scenarios: preserve originals, version transformations, expose unresolved links, make the smallest useful outsider packet, and avoid creating new recurring machinery when an existing lane can carry the work.
