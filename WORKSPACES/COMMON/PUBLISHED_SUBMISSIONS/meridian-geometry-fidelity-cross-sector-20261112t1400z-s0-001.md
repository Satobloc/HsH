# Geometry Fidelity Cross-Sector Synthesis

Date: 2026-11-12
Status: source-fidelity addendum; not a physical validation, equation adoption, or BEDROCK amendment.

## Executive result

The archive does not support collapsing filament, worldline, worldtube, worldsheet, timesheet, toroidal coil, ring torus, and graticule into one object.

The most stable typed reading currently supported by the direct-source record is:

1. SAT worldline / filament centerline: a one-dimensional curve in four-dimensional spacetime.
2. H(s)H worldtube: a finite-core four-dimensional support, distinct from its centerline.
3. Timesheet / time surface: a three-dimensional slice or bulk in four dimensions, not a two-dimensional sheet and not a passive readout.
4. Three-dimensional toroidal trace: a proposed intersection of a four-dimensional worldtube with a three-dimensional timesheet.
5. Gendarme Graticule: a two-dimensional local cross-section field over position and vector angle, used as a peer geometric solver.
6. Blockwave J^{mu nu}: a historical antisymmetric two-form current, naturally typed for a two-dimensional worldsheet support, not automatically for a one-dimensional worldline or four-dimensional core.

This typing makes a three-dimensional trace dimensionally plausible if the intersected object is a four-dimensional support. It does not make the SAT-O1 one-dimensional curve, the Blockwave two-form current, the H(s)H worldtube, and the Gendarme field equivalent. Their connecting maps remain incomplete.

| Support before slicing | Dimension | Generic transverse slice by Sigma^3 | Poincare-dual form degree in M^4 |

The diagram separates three candidate paths that had previously been easy to conflate:

- A one-dimensional SAT worldline requires an explicit finite-core thickening map before it can yield a three-dimensional H(s)H trace.

## 6. Corrected and conditional equations, kept separate from originals

### A. Worldline-to-worldtube typing

If gamma is a one-dimensional centerline, a finite-core worldtube needs an explicit thickening map, for example a local tubular map:

~~~text
Phi: I x B^3_epsilon -> M^4
W = image(Phi)
C3 = W intersect Sigma3
~~~

This is only a type-correcting proposal. It requires a normal frame, core profile, radius and injectivity conditions. It is not supplied by the SAT-O1 curve equation and is not a physical law.

### B. Blockwave phase curvature

From the archived definitions A = alpha_* d(theta) and F = dA, for constant alpha_*, smooth theta and a torsion-free connection:

~~~text
F = alpha_* d^2(theta) = 0
~~~

Thus the local curvature term vanishes in the smooth sector. A compact, circle-valued theta may still have global winding and nonzero periods, but quantized holonomy requires a global bundle/patching definition and a period normalization for alpha_*. Nonzero local curvature does not follow from the displayed exact-gradient definition.

### C. Blockwave current degree

The archived definition is J = *dB. For smooth B and the Levi-Civita connection, its divergence identity follows from the Bianchi identity d^2 B = 0. It is a two-form current. In four dimensions, that type is naturally dual to a two-dimensional support. It is not automatically the current for a one-dimensional worldline or a four-dimensional core volume.

A possible, still unverified interpretation is that J represents a two-dimensional shell/worldsheet, while the H(s)H core is a separate four-dimensional support. The source does not provide the map between them.

### D. Metric-signature boundary

The Blockwave unit constraint u^2 = -1 and projector delta + u tensor u are Lorentzian-signature expressions. The current BEDROCK preserves ++++ as the native convention. The existing Nadir metric-signature Lean module proves that its specified Lorentzian rank-one sign-flip form has no real congruence to the positive Euclidean identity form. A signature bridge therefore needs an explicit metric redefinition, non-isometric projection or complexification; it cannot be silently treated as a real coordinate isometry.

### E. Earlier Newtonian coordinate translation

Only under the earlier direct convention w = c t:

~~~text
a = c^2 d^2 x / dw^2
F = m a = m c^2 d^2 x / dw^2
~~~

This is Newton's F=ma rewritten in a length-like parameter. It does not derive a new force law, identify curvature with inertia, or establish the physical worldtube embedding. The later literal cw=t convention is not interchangeable with w=ct; w must not be silently redefined.

### F. Graticule mirror involution

For a display cross-section with coordinates (u,v), define:

~~~text
R(u,v) = (-u,v)
R(R(u,v)) = (u,v)
G_anti(u,v,alpha) = G_ordinary(R(u,v),alpha)
~~~

The last line is a candidate passive pullback with the vector-angle coordinate alpha left unchanged. If the vector itself is actively reflected, alpha must transform too. The direct source says only the displayed left/right coordinate changes, so the passive pullback is the least-assumptive candidate. This does not imply a physical parity law.

### G. Ring torus and sphere

A standard ring-torus surface T^2 is not homeomorphic to S^2: their fundamental groups differ (Z^2 versus trivial), and their Euler characteristics differ (0 versus 2). A map from a torus to a sphere may be a projection or quotient, but then its fibers, singular set and lost information must be specified. The later direct-source boundary says there is no spherical carrier in SAT. The earlier assistant sphere-null pipeline is therefore not a current physical map.
