# LOW / OPPORTUNISTIC PRIORITY — Other Scientific Conjectures and Ideas

**Date:** 2026-09-18  
**Authority:** Nathan-direct workflow clarification  
**Status:** ACTIVE, low-priority / opportunistic background task

## Core instruction

While workers are already reading SAT/H(s)H archives, GLASS, NotebookLM exports, podcast transcripts, conversation corpora, source indices, or related scientific material for higher-priority tasks, opportunistically **tag and organize other scientific conjectures, hypotheses, proposed mechanisms, speculative extensions, experimental ideas, and side-theories encountered along the way**.

This is **not** a mandate to divert recurrence time into broad speculative-mining sweeps. It is a capture-and-organize rule: when a scientifically interesting conjecture is already in view during higher-priority work, preserve enough structure that it can be found and assessed later rather than being lost inside a larger source.

## What to capture

Examples include:

- side hypotheses not central to the current SAT/H(s)H reconstruction;
- proposed explanations for known phenomena;
- experimental or observational suggestions;
- alternative physical mechanisms;
- model extensions or analogies with possible scientific content;
- abandoned but potentially informative scientific branches;
- conjectures about materials, condensed matter, astrophysics, cosmology, particle physics, geophysics, biology, chemistry, or other scientific domains;
- predictions or possible tests that were not promoted into the main theory programme;
- scientific ideas generated during podcast/NLM/conversation work that are distinct from exposition or ontology/consciousness speculation.

## Minimum tagging schema

When practical, record:

1. short conjecture/idea label;
2. scientific domain;
3. exact source/path/date;
4. authorship/provenance class;
5. one-sentence source-faithful description;
6. relationship to SAT/H(s)H (`direct`, `extension`, `analogy`, `independent side idea`, `unclear`);
7. epistemic status if explicit (`question`, `conjecture`, `hypothesis`, `prediction`, `test proposal`, `rejected`, `superseded`, etc.);
8. any obvious experimental/observational hook;
9. links to duplicate or related occurrences.

Do not infer stronger status than the source supports.

## Separation rules

Keep these tags distinct from:

- current/core SAT/H(s)H claims;
- external prior art;
- ontology/metaphysics;
- consciousness speculation;
- mere analogies with no scientific claim;
- assistant-generated brainstorming that occurred outside SAT hypothesis space.

If an idea appears in GLASS, preserve the existing rule that GLASS membership means SAT hypothesized it, while authorship, approval, validation, currentness, and core status remain separate axes.

## Priority and scheduling

This is deliberately **low priority**. It should usually consume negligible incremental effort during another task:

> encounter → tag/record → link provenance → continue primary task.

Do not displace P0–P4 work, active proof/formalization targets, source-recovery blockers, or required QA merely to expand this catalogue.

A dedicated deeper review is justified only when the opportunistically accumulated material becomes large enough, recurrent enough, or scientifically connected enough that organizing it would materially improve project understanding.

## Long-term output

The eventual product may be a searchable **Scientific Conjecture / Side-Idea Registry** organized by domain, chronology, provenance, status, and relation to the main SAT/H(s)H framework. Until then, lightweight tags and provenance-rich pointers are sufficient.
