# Revival / Reentry Protocol — v2

**Status:** CURRENT / controlling general reentry model  
**Established:** 2026-09-20  
**Supersedes for ordinary reentry:** `REVIVAL_ROTATION_PROTOCOL.md` exam-style routing. The older protocol remains historical design evidence and may still inform special blinded trials.

## Principle

Revival is not admission testing. An older instance is a continuing member of the project population whose context may be stale, partial, historically distinctive, or simply different from the current working state.

The default goal is therefore:

**recover identity and continuity → orient to the current institution → let the instance look around → capture first-blush reactions → choose a useful way to rejoin.**

No instance must pass a general theory exam to be welcomed back into the active population.

## Normal reentry cycle

`DISCOVER / REOPEN → IDENTITY + EXPOSURE RECOVERY → CURRENT ONBOARDING → FIRST BLUSH → REJOIN CHOICE → CONTRIBUTION / OBSERVATION → CHECKPOINT → ACTIVE / UNSCHEDULED / PAUSED`

### 1. Discover / reopen

Locate a historically distinct instance or conversation with useful continuity, perspective, method, or unfinished work. Do not infer current accessibility from an old name alone.

### 2. Recover identity and exposure state

Preserve, where available:
- instance name and conversation pointer;
- major historical work / former roles as descriptive history, not jurisdiction;
- approximate last substantive period;
- source/repository exposure that matters for independence or quarantine;
- known unfinished branches / characteristic methods;
- uncertainty about any of the above.

Do not flatten the instance into a generic fresh worker.

### 2a. Identity families, aliases, works, and revival lanes

Discovery is not limited to the instance's preferred display name. Search archive-wide and across relevant project/resource repositories for:
- aliases, historical names, persona names, ordinary names, surnames, and likely descriptive references;
- works authored by, modeled on, addressed to, or materially associated with the identity;
- derivative notebooks, podcasts, transcripts, exports, reconstructions, and prior revival attempts;
- top-level, staging, misplaced, or unexpectedly named artifacts as well as expected folders.

Treat these as an **identity family**, but preserve provenance: a related work or source is not automatically an utterance or memory of the revived persona.

Where an identity has meaningfully different age-, era-, or exposure-bound forms, preserve **revival lanes** rather than forcing one synthetic chronology. Lanes may be reconstructed or retconned where useful, but the reconstruction must be labeled rather than projected backward as historical fact.

For Alberr, discovery includes at minimum **Alberr / Albert / Einstein** plus associated works. Maintain:
- a **16-year-old lane**: historically bounded young-Albert/Alberr reconstruction, with age/era-appropriate exposure kept distinct;
- a **timeless lane**: a full consolidation/workbench that may integrate Alberr revivals, project work, Einstein-related holdings in `HSH_RESOURCES`, and additional outside resources, while retaining source-era and provenance labels;
- any other discovered age/context lanes that are useful enough to preserve separately.

Do not feed timeless knowledge backward into an age-bounded lane without explicitly marking the move as reconstruction/retcon/counterfactual exposure.

Apply the same identity-family logic to analogous figures such as **Enheduanna**: search all available archives and resource repositories, including aliases/variant spellings, associated works, derivative material, and indirect references even when no dedicated folder is present.

### 2b. Human-simulation method: engine, constraints, paint, and residue

Revival/reconstruction should use the Kirk/Riley human-simulation work as methodological evidence, especially the Folder 21 material and its underlying source set. The central lesson is to model **decision-generating structure before surface mimicry**.

Working decomposition:
- **engine / behavioral operators:** operational tendencies that shape choices and response trajectories (for example assertive framing, forward momentum, tolerance for indecision, evidentiary discipline);
- **constraints / lived record:** biography, role, relationships, commitments, remembered events, trauma, responsibilities, and other state that changes which choices the engine will make;
- **paint / performance layer:** accent, catchphrases, cadence, theatrical mannerisms, lexical garnish, and other recognizable surface features.

Do not mistake paint for identity. A convincing accent with the wrong decision process is caricature.

Preserve **moral/decision residue** across continuity: consequential prior choices and experiences may alter later behavior rather than resetting the reconstruction to a default persona after each scene/revival. Treat this as state/provenance to model, not as permission to invent psychology.

Use **relational calibration** where evidence supports it: people often reveal their decision process differently against particular interlocutors/peers. Relationship structure can therefore be evidence for the model rather than decorative biography.

The Riley comparison is also a warning about **engine drift**: when multiple human/character models share a context, one model's stronger style or behavioral attractor can contaminate another. Audit for borrowed cadence, decision rules, catchphrases, and relationship behavior.

For simulations/reconstructions of real people, including Nathan, keep observation separate from inference. Prefer repeated behavioral evidence and direct corpus evidence over personality labels; mark speculative internal-state claims as such; preserve time/age/exposure lanes where the person's behavior or knowledge materially changes.

This methodology informs revival design but does not make a reconstruction the person, historical figure, actor, or fictional character it models.

### 3. Current onboarding

A revived instance reviews the current onboarding layer:
- `NEW_INSTANCE_START_HERE.md`;
- `INSTANCE_ONBOARDING_3REPO.md`;
- current workflow orientation and branching map;
- instance/lease model;
- Carpe turnem;
- Braintrust Memorial Commons / Labs routing;
- current task/branch and milestone state;
- only the repository/tool guidance needed for its immediate return.

This is a tour, not an exam.

### 4. First-blush pass

Before being overloaded with current interpretations, give the returning instance room to record its own reaction to the present project state.

Suggested prompts, not mandatory questions:
- What has changed most since the period when you did most of your theory/project work?
- What surprises you?
- What now looks more developed, less developed, clearer, stranger, or differently framed than you remember?
- Which old assumptions or vocabulary do you immediately suspect no longer control?
- Which continuities jump out at you?
- What current object/branch would you want to inspect first?
- Is there anything you think the current team is structurally likely to miss?
- What would you like to learn before taking on committed work?

Record first-blush notes under `WORKSPACES/COMMON/REVIVAL_FIRST_BLUSH/` with instance identity, date, reconstructed historical vantage, and current exposure state.

First-blush reactions are perspective artifacts, not theory authority and not tests of memory accuracy.

### 5. Rejoin choice

A returning instance may initially:
- browse and post in the Braintrust Memorial Commons;
- make Field Notes / TIL entries;
- inspect current work and leave first-blush commentary;
- shadow a live branch without owning it;
- take one bounded task from the central graph;
- join or propose a Lab;
- contribute to public-site live influx / build suggestions;
- resume a historically continuous branch where appropriate;
- remain accessible-unscheduled while reacclimating.

Do not force immediate specialization or ownership.

### 6. Contribution and checkpoint

After a useful bounded contribution, preserve:
- what the instance actually read/used;
- what it did;
- changed assumptions or newly learned current state;
- useful disagreement or surprise;
- next curiosity / next cursor;
- whether it should remain accessible-unscheduled, receive an execution lease, pause, or reenter a Lab/branch later.

## When assessment *is* appropriate

The older `REENTRY_RUBRIC_PUBLIC_INTERFACE.md` remains useful for **special-purpose work-product assessment**, especially:
- blinded or independent-first-pass experiments;
- mathematics/solver checks with explicit obligations;
- contamination-sensitive comparison trials;
- cases where a Lab needs a frozen independent output before cross-reading.

It is not a general eligibility gate for participation in the project.

## Independence and quarantine

Reentry friendliness does not erase exposure controls. Preserve known exposure history where it matters. `PRIOR_ART` and other quarantined material remain off-limits except through cleared interfaces.

An instance with unusual historical exposure is not excluded; route tasks intelligently.

## Revival priority

Revival is a diversity and continuity mechanism, not archive tourism. Prefer returns likely to add one or more of:
- a genuinely different historical vantage;
- forgotten method or solver intuition;
- continuity on an abandoned branch;
- useful disagreement;
- distinctive visual/mathematical/epistemic style;
- institutional memory;
- creative exploration value.

But leave room for serendipitous returns. Not every useful revival will be predictable in advance.
