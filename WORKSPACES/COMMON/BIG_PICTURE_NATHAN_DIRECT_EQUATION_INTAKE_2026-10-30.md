## 4. Source-first equation pipeline

For each candidate equation or mathematical construction:

1. **Recover the direct statement.** Find Nathan's exact message(s) in Nathan Direct, including parent/child branch and adjacent conversation context. Do not reconstruct intent from assistant prose.
2. **Recover the underlying mathematical artifact.** Locate the original file, notebook, code, transcript, or calculation. Record repository, path, blob SHA, commit, source date, and exact line/cell/message locator.
3. **Establish lineage.** Preserve first appearance, revisions, corrections, backsteps, rejected alternatives, and later restatements. A later date alone does not prove a correction.
4. **Normalize carefully.** Parse symbols, definitions, units, domains, metric/sign conventions, boundary conditions, assumptions and dependencies without erasing the source form.
5. **Audit mathematics.** Check syntax, indices, dimensions, algebra, calculus of variations where relevant, limiting cases, topology, signs and internal consistency.
6. **Reproduce computationally.** Use scripts and independent implementations where possible. Store actual code, inputs, outputs, versions, and tolerances. Never claim execution that did not occur.
7. **Translate only after recovery.** State whether the result is retained, corrected, superseded, rejected, unresolved, or merely analogous in the current SAT/H(s)H system.
8. **Test against real systems.** BEDROCK requires identifiable physical systems and genuine measured/observed data by default. Record source, units, uncertainties, and measured-versus-predicted comparison. Do not invent fixtures or data; data-free calculations require Nathan's explicit prior permission.
9. **Gate independently.** Mathematical clearance, current-model applicability, multi-sector candidacy, and physical validation remain separate decisions.
