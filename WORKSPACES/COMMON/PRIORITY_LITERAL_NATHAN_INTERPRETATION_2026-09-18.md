# HIGH PRIORITY — Literal-First Interpretation of Nathan-Direct Theory Statements

**Date:** 2026-09-18  
**Authority:** Nathan-direct methodological clarification  
**Status:** ACTIVE reconstruction / interpretation rule

## Core rule

One of the major recurring failure states in SAT/H(s)H reconstruction is **not taking Nathan's words literally enough**.

When Nathan gives a geometrically concrete statement — for example, in effect, `No, I mean the rope is literally being twisted in 4D` — the default response must **not** be to translate that immediately into a familiar standard-physics formalism such as a field, effective field, gauge variable, order parameter, continuum stress field, or pre-existing equation family merely because those tools can describe something analogous.

The default should instead be:

> **Preserve the literal geometric claim first, exactly as stated, even when incomplete. Formalize only after the literal content and its unresolved details have been separately recorded.**

Nathan's clarification is that most such statements should ordinarily be interpreted **literally by default**, notwithstanding the fact that every geometric detail, boundary condition, mechanism, or mathematical consequence may not yet be specified in the statement itself.

Incomplete does not mean metaphorical.

## Interpretation hierarchy

For Nathan-direct SAT/H(s)H statements, use this order unless the source itself clearly indicates analogy, shorthand, metaphor, or standard-formalism equivalence:

1. **Literal source statement** — what Nathan actually says is happening geometrically/structurally.
2. **Explicitly specified details** — dimensions, objects, relations, directionality, topology, motion/deformation, scale, etc. actually stated.
3. **Unspecified/open details** — what would still have to be defined to make the description mathematically complete.
4. **Candidate formalization** — equations, manifolds, operators, coordinates, variational principles, or standard-math structures proposed to encode the literal claim.
5. **Standard-physics comparison/crosswalk** — only after (1)–(4), identify whether known field/operator/effective descriptions reproduce the same structure.

Do **not** collapse stages 1–5 into one paraphrase.

## Prohibited default move

Avoid transformations of the following form unless independently justified:

- `literal 4D twist` → `therefore a twist field`;
- `rope/filament/worldtube physically coils` → `therefore this means a scalar/vector/tensor field configuration`;
- `timesheet deforms` → `therefore introduce a conventional background field`;
- `three objects braid` → `therefore impose Z3 as an external symmetry requirement`;
- `geometric recurrence across scale` → `therefore assume a background lattice`;
- `intersection/slice/readout` → `therefore it is merely an effective projection metaphor`.

A standard formalism may later prove to be mathematically equivalent, useful, or even unavoidable. The failure state is **assuming the translation first and thereby replacing the original ontology/geometry before it has been reconstructed**.

## Literal does not mean overclaim

Taking Nathan literally does **not** mean inventing details he did not state.

If Nathan says a rope is being twisted in 4D, preserve at least:

- there is a rope-like / filamentary / worldtube object in the intended model;
- the twist is meant as an actual geometric operation/configuration in 4D, not merely a verbal analogy unless another source says so;
- any unspecified axis, metric, embedding, twist measure, radius, material law, boundary condition, or dynamical mechanism remains OPEN.

Do not fill those gaps with standard theory merely to make the statement look complete.

The correct reconstruction may therefore be deliberately partial:

> `Literal claim established; exact mathematical realization still unresolved.`

That is preferable to a polished but source-distorting formalization.

## Formalization discipline

When mathematics is introduced, label its relation to the source:

- `DIRECTLY SPECIFIED` — Nathan explicitly gave this equation/structure;
- `MINIMAL FORMALIZATION` — introduced only to encode the literal statement with the fewest added assumptions;
- `CANDIDATE FORMALIZATION` — one possible mathematical realization;
- `STANDARD-PHYSICS CROSSWALK` — external/standard language describing a similar structure;
- `MODEL ADDITION` — introduces assumptions not contained in the Nathan-direct statement.

Never silently convert a candidate formalization into the meaning of the original statement.

Where multiple mathematical realizations fit the same literal geometry, preserve the family until constraints discriminate among them.

## Relationship to SAT/H(s)H ontology

This rule is especially important because SAT/H(s)H repeatedly presents itself in geometric terms: filaments/worldtubes, coiling, superhelices, braids, intersections, timesheets/timewaves, shells, toroidal or spherical structures, distortions, twists, winding, and nested scale geometry.

Workers should not assume these nouns and verbs are merely intuitive wrappers around conventional field equations. In many cases the geometric structure is the claim being made.

This reinforces, but is broader than, the existing `field` terminology warning: `field` may sometimes be valid shorthand or a later mathematical representation, but should not be imported as ontology merely because it is conventional language.

## Source-class handling

Apply the literal-first rule most strongly to:

- Nathan-direct conversation statements;
- Nathan-authored source documents;
- correction/interruption language such as `no`, `no no`, `I mean literally`, `actually`, `not metaphorically`, `the point is`, `what I mean is`, `I'm saying`, and related clarification markers;
- scratch/from-scratch reformulation attempts, where first-principles intent makes premature translation especially damaging;
- Fundamental Intuitions / foundational-doctrine material.

NotebookLM summaries, podcast narration, and assistant reconstructions should not be granted Nathan-direct literal authority merely because they use vivid geometric language. Recover their underlying source where possible.

Tag them as **INTERPRETATION-CORRECTION / LITERALITY CONTROL** candidates and feed strong examples into the epistemic/methodological-control reconstruction.

## Epistemic boundaries

Literal-first interpretation does not by itself establish:

- mathematical consistency;
- physical correctness;
- experimental validity;
- official/core status;
- completeness of the mechanism.

It establishes what proposition is actually under discussion before those questions are asked.

A literal claim may later be disproved, refined, superseded, or shown to admit a standard mathematical representation. Those later outcomes do not justify rewriting the historical statement into that representation at the reconstruction stage.

## Preferred worker test

Before paraphrasing a Nathan-direct theory statement, ask:

> **If I remove the conventional physics vocabulary I am about to introduce, have I first preserved exactly what Nathan says the geometry/object is literally doing?**

If not, stop and record the literal claim first.
