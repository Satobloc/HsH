## Methodological assessment

- **Image-grounding failure:** the assistant misidentifies a phone anamorphic lens plus polarizer as a dichroic cube/beam splitter. Nathan has to correct it. This is a concrete example of why object identity and optical function must be confirmed before proposing an experiment.
- **Polarizer versus beam splitter:** a polarizer selects/transmits polarization components; a beam splitter divides an incident beam into separate paths. A taped polarizer on an anamorphic phone attachment is not functionally equivalent to a polarizing beam splitter without a specific optical arrangement and measured transfer behaviour.
- **Anamorphic optics:** an anamorphic attachment changes imaging geometry/aspect and may introduce distortion, flare, and aberration. It does not establish a SAT phase retarder.
- **Angle-dependent vase colour:** the reported colour shift is a real observation from Nathan's description, but Rayleigh scattering is only one possible explanation. Depending on glass composition and structure, refraction, dispersion, thin-film effects, scattering, fluorescence, coatings, and illumination spectrum may contribute. It should be characterized before any theoretical interpretation.
- **UV lamp and shade:** a blacklight inside a translucent shade is a source-plus-diffuser arrangement. Its spectrum, irradiance, glass transmission, fluorescence, and angular distribution need measurement. It is not inherently a field emitter or tension vessel.
- **RAW capture:** Nathan says RAW is always enabled. This is a useful existing workflow condition, but RAW does not imply phase measurement or “SAT-detection precision.” Exposure, ISO, aperture, lens, focus, white balance metadata, sensor response, and processing still require control.
- **Inventory-first instruction:** Nathan explicitly says more objects should be shown before planning begins. The assistant should respect this sequencing and avoid prematurely assembling a rig from partial, uncertain identifications.
