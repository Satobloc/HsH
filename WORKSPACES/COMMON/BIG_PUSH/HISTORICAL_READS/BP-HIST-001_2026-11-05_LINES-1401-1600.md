## Methodological assessment

- **DSLR phase measurement:** a DSLR records intensity through a color-filter array and camera processing. Phase can be inferred only if the optical setup converts phase differences into measurable intensity/polarization changes, with calibration. Calling the camera a phase sensor is inaccurate as stated.
- **RAW/RGB analysis:** useful for retaining and comparing channel-specific sensor responses, but requires fixed exposure, white balance, lens, aperture, ISO, focus, illumination spectrum, and dark-frame/flat-field controls. RGB changes may reflect spectral transmission, sensor response, demosaicing, or processing rather than phase.
- **Unknown Agfa slide film:** characterize transmission spectrum, density, haze, and polarization effects independently. A consistently green developed result may arise from aged/failed chemistry, processing incompatibility, storage, or exposure conditions. It does not establish a special spectral or SAT response.
- **Liquid light between glass:** emulsion thickness, scattering, absorption, adhesion, drying, chemical access, and optical contact all affect results. A sandwich that cannot be processed reproducibly may not be a useful measurement element. First characterize individual slides and coatings.
- **Black coatings:** use as ordinary low-reflectance masks only after measuring reflectance and checking wavelength dependence. No evidence in this range supports treating them as field boundary conditions.
- **Stacking many unknown components increases ambiguity.** Characterize each element separately before building a multi-element stack; otherwise the cause of any color, halo, or brightness change is underdetermined.
- **The next step should be inventory and geometry inspection, not an immediate SAT interpretation.** Photographs can establish layout and practical mounting options, but not field coupling.
