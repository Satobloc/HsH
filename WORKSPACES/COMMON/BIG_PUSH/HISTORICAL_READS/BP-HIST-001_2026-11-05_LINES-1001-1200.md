## Methodological assessment and corrections

1. **The analogy-to-detection leap is the central failure in this tranche.** A material analogue of a geometric effect is not evidence that the underlying spacetime field is being measured. Direct detection requires a derived coupling between the hypothesized field and a measured degree of freedom, plus a calibration chain.

2. **No crack-to-SAT-field coupling is established.** A chip or crack is a discontinuity in a material surface and may create ordinary scattering, diffraction, stress birefringence, reflection, and aberration. Calling it a θ₄ kink or τ defect does not make it one.

3. **A lens flip is a compound intervention.** It changes surface orientation, optical path, aberration, focal geometry, chip location, contact geometry, and possibly stress. Matching one scalar distance does not necessarily match the optical state. Better controls characterize the lens and compare measured transfer functions under each orientation.

4. **The assistant's “distance irrelevant to SAT” table entry is an unsupported assertion.** Whether distance matters to a proposed SAT observable depends on the actual field equation and measurement geometry. It cannot be declared irrelevant by stipulation.

5. **Fixed optics reduce some confounds but remove the rotation path.** If the proposed signature is holonomy from a closed path, a static fixed-lens arrangement may test a different effect. The hypothesis must specify whether it predicts a static local response, a path-dependent response, or both.

6. **Long exposures integrate exposure, not phase.** A long-duration pinhole solar track works because the moving Sun creates a changing intensity distribution recorded over time. It does not demonstrate that film accumulates geometric phase. A long exposure may be useful for slow intensity variations, but only with a defined observable and controls for source drift, ambient light, film reciprocity failure, temperature, and mechanical stability.

7. **A diameter-mismatched lens stack has rich ordinary-optics behaviour.** It may produce vignetting, internal reflections, aberrations, diffraction, Fresnel effects, field-dependent illumination, and contact/stress effects. These need to be measured or modelled before any residual is assigned to SAT.

8. **“Noise reduction” must be tied to the target observable.** Fixed geometry can improve repeatability, but systematic optical errors can be highly repeatable. Replication alone does not distinguish an ordinary systematic from a new effect.
