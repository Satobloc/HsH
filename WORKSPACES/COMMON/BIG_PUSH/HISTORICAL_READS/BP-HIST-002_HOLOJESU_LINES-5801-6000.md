2. **The mapping may still be a useful visual/conceptual analogy.** Preserve it as a candidate mnemonic or design hypothesis, but require each symbol's type, domain, codomain, action, and relation to standard differential geometry before formal use.
3. **The assistant's description of Δ as a “second-order jump” is not generally correct.** A Laplacian, finite difference, and discontinuity operator are distinct concepts. The intended Δ must be specified.
4. **The assistant's claim that all structures are “₄” is unsupported.** The user asks whether they are all 4; the assistant asserts a universal fourth-direction invariant without deriving it or checking the notation.
5. **The symbolic chain is explicitly musing/numerology.** Nathan labels it as such. Do not convert it into an axiom, equation, or formal derivation.
6. **The parameter-holding principle is methodologically valuable.** It corresponds to controlled variation, partial derivatives, ceteris paribus analysis, and experimental isolation of effects. Use it to test dependencies one at a time, while checking interactions afterward.
7. **Holding a variable fixed is not proof that its effects disappear physically.** It is a mathematical/experimental intervention that removes its variation from the comparison. Distinguish holding a parameter constant from setting it to zero or eliminating the underlying field.
8. **The assistant again reframes a general calculus insight as SAT itself.** Preserve the useful method without turning it into a theory-specific discovery absent derivation.
