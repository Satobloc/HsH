## Attribution and methodological assessment

- The detailed claims that bellows emulate ∇·uᵘ, long tubes accumulate ψ waves, cracked lenses are domain-wall lattices, and shallow depth of field amplifies click envelopes are historical assistant inventions in this passage. They are not established SAT mappings.
- The assistant presents a click-rate equation in malformed/transcription-corrupted form. It must not be treated as a valid equation or used in calculations without recovering the authoritative source and exact notation.
- The assistant's description of “non-reversible” behavior conflates several distinct concepts: ordinary mechanical hysteresis, path-dependent polarization/optical response, geometric/Berry phase, noncommuting transformations, and a genuinely irreversible residual. These require separate operational definitions.
- A path-dependent phase is not automatically irreversible, dissipative, or evidence of new physics. A geometric phase can depend on the path while remaining reversible under an appropriately reversed path.
- The transcript itself records Nathan's central objection: ordinary optics already produces strange images, so any SAT test needs a quantitative SM baseline and a distinctive residual prediction.
- Long exposures on film integrate optical intensity/exposure; they do not automatically integrate optical phase. Interference or another phase-to-intensity conversion mechanism is required, and film reciprocity, spectral response, processing, and stability must be calibrated.
- X-ray film's UV response is emulsion- and screen-dependent. It is not safe to assume broad or uniform UVA/UVB sensitivity from the label “X-ray film.” Spectral sensitivity must be measured or obtained from the exact stock documentation.
- UV/visible atmospheric comparisons are a potentially useful conventional-optics/atmospheric-photography project, but any differences under cloud, fog, foliage, or shade have ordinary scattering, absorption, geometry, and sensor/emulsion explanations to control before invoking SAT.
