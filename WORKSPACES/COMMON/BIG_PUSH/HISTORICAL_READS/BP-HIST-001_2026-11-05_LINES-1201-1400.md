## Methodological assessment

- The motorized apparatus is a useful upgrade from a finite wind-up mechanism, but indefinite operation does not guarantee constant angular velocity, low vibration, or phase stability. Motor speed, runout, vibration, and synchronization need measurement.
- Rotation and acoustic excitation are separable variables in principle. A useful control structure compares rotation on/off and sound on/off. Muting the hammers may change mechanical loading and vibration, so sound level and mechanical vibration should be measured independently.
- Sound-on/sound-off optical differences have ordinary explanations: acoustic pressure can modulate refractive index, while vibration can move optics, change path length, alter alignment, or shake the detector. These effects need quantitative baselines.
- Film exposure does not directly measure optical phase. A digital camera or photodetector can record intensity changes; a phase claim requires a defined, calibrated phase-sensitive arrangement.
- A beam and tube do not automatically form an interferometer. A phase measurement needs a reference path or another defined phase-to-observable conversion.
- Long exposure may average away periodic acoustic or motor signals. Time-synchronized acquisition of audio, rotation, and optical output is more suitable for this question.
- “Sound influences geometry” is not yet a quantitative prediction. A valid test needs an ordinary acousto-optic/mechanical model and an independently derived SAT correction with predicted sign, magnitude, scaling, and uncertainty.
