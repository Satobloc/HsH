## Methodological assessment and corrections

1. **Long-exposure film is not inherently a phase integrator.** Ordinary photographic film or paper accumulates an exposure response to incident light intensity over time. It does not directly preserve optical phase. Phase must first be converted into a measurable intensity or polarization observable through a defined arrangement, such as calibrated interference, polarimetry, or another phase-sensitive method.

2. **Rotation alone does not establish holonomy.** A rotating platform may change illumination geometry, mechanical alignment, lens orientation, polarization relative to the optical axis, or exposure distribution. A phase-holonomy claim requires a defined state transported around a specified closed path, a measurable phase observable, and a prediction for path reversal and repeated cycles.

3. **The music-box apparatus is mechanically and optically under-specified.** Relevant ordinary effects include platform runout, speed variation during spring unwinding, vibration, lens decentering/tilt, glass reflections, stray light, film movement, source flicker, changing source-to-film geometry, and temperature drift. The apparatus should first be characterized with no lens and with inert/known optical elements.

4. **A fixed 0.24-rad phase offset remains unsupported.** The inspected transcript ranges provide no derivation, parameter dependence, uncertainty, or operational measurement procedure for this number. It must not be carried forward as an established SAT prediction.

5. **Order asymmetry is not automatically non-standard.** A→B versus B→A can differ in ordinary optics when elements are anisotropic, non-isotropic, spatially varying, misaligned, polarization-sensitive, or otherwise non-commuting. A valid test needs measured component transfer functions and a Standard Model/Maxwell-optics baseline for each ordering.

6. **A static phase drift is not automatically a new field.** Thermal expansion, mechanical creep, source drift, air currents, stress relaxation, detector drift, and environmental changes are ordinary candidate causes. The test needs simultaneous controls and a measured drift model.

7. **UV and X-ray film claims need stock-specific evidence.** “X-ray film” is not a generic UV detector. Spectral sensitivity, screen use, emulsion construction, reciprocity, processing, and filter transmission must be established for the exact stock. A blacklight's spectrum and irradiance also need measurement; decorative LEDs and blacklights are not calibrated monochromatic sources by default.

8. **The proposed acoustic-optical coupling is a hypothesis, not a protocol yet.** Conventional acousto-optic effects are known and must be modelled as the null baseline. Any SAT claim needs a distinct coupling term, predicted scaling with acoustic frequency/amplitude and optical wavelength/polarization, and controls that separate ordinary refractive-index modulation, vibration, and mechanical motion.
