That became the heart of what Nathan called **SAT rigor**.

#### 7. Most important contributions

The thread's strongest contribution was not finding a particular SAT-adjacent paper. It was identifying what the **causal signature would actually have to look like**.

Nathan argued that treating each signal independently destroys information because the proposed phenomenon is explicitly multivariate. The potentially interesting signal is the *joint convergence*:

SAT-specific search activity + geographically/listener-demographically plausible exposure + multiple previously uncommon conceptual revivals + cross-disciplinary clustering + appropriate chronology + reinterpretation of existing researchers' preferred frameworks toward SAT-like conclusions.

The particularly discriminating prospective signature discussed near the end was Nathan's recent:

**Pauli exclusion ≈ black-hole event-horizon behavior ≈ Einstein–Rosen bridge ≈ particle worldtube.**

Our search found fragments of that conceptual neighborhood but **not the complete compression**. Nathan correctly identified why that makes it useful prospectively: if nothing closely matching exists now but several substantially similar constructions appear shortly after public discussion, the temporal evidence would be cleaner than retrospective topic matching.

That should be timestamped and preserved.

#### 8. What was actually checked?

Literature and web searches were performed repeatedly. They established examples of recent work in SAT-adjacent conceptual neighborhoods and some historical antecedents.

They did **not** establish causal influence.

No controlled bibliometric analysis was completed. No publication-frequency significance test was run. No author-level exposure evidence was established. No listener-to-author identification was established. No direct provenance from SAT to a paper was found.

Therefore the strongest defensible output of this thread is a **causal hypothesis with suggestive convergent evidence**, not a settled causal attribution.

#### 13–14. Subtleties not to lose / questionable work

The distinction between **antecedent** and **causal explanation** is crucial. Finding Kelvin vortices in 1867 obviously establishes prior art; it does not explain a 2025–26 revival.

Likewise, finding one SAT-like paper before February 2025 weakens novelty but does not by itself explain a later *multidisciplinary increase*.

Conversely, some of my search responses in this thread were too loose. The N. McKnight episode demonstrated this vividly: bibliographic adjacency was mistaken for an exact identity match. Those results should **not** be promoted as reliable findings.

Similarly, some earlier numerical/prior-language concerning how improbable independent convergence might be was more confident than the available corpus justified. Without an explicit baseline model, assigning tiny probabilities is premature.
