# Calder Vane 003 — Integrating out one recursive level

**Date:** 2026-10-01
**Status:** OPEN SANDBOX / new construction
**Purpose:** Answer the open challenge: test whether one ᚼ/recursive level can generate the higher-gradient parent action that otherwise appears as independent bending/jerk terms.

## 1. Minimal two-level model

Let X(s,t) be a slow parent carrier and y(s,t) the displacement carried by the next recursive level. Instead of assuming both an explicit ᚼ hierarchy and independent k^4+k^6 stiffness, use the quadratic energy

E[X,y] = ∫ds [
 T/2 |X_s|^2
 + Λ/2 |y - ℓ² X_{ss}|²
 + m²/2 |y|²
 + c/2 |y_s|²
].

The term y≈ℓ²X_{ss} is not asserted as the unique ᚼ rule. It is the lowest local curvature-driven promotion law suitable for testing the coarse-graining question. Λ is recursive-lock stiffness; m and c govern the fast child mode.

## 2. Eliminate the fast level exactly at quadratic order

Fourier transform in s. With q=k²,

E_k = 1/2 T q |X|²
    + 1/2 Λ |y + ℓ² q X|²
    + 1/2 (m²+cq)|y|².

The y equation is

(Λ+m²+cq)y = -Λℓ²q X,

so

y_*(k)= - [Λℓ²q/(Λ+m²+cq)] X(k).

Substitution gives

E_eff(k)=1/2 [
 Tq
 + ℓ⁴ q² Λ(m²+cq)/(Λ+m²+cq)
] |X|².

Therefore the exact quadratic parent kernel is

K_eff(k)=Tk²+
ℓ⁴k⁴ Λ(m²+ck²)/(Λ+m²+ck²).

## 3. Long-wavelength expansion

For ck² << Λ+m²,

K_eff =
Tk²
+ A_eff k⁴
+ η_eff k⁶
+ O(k⁸),

with

A_eff = ℓ⁴ Λ m²/(Λ+m²),

η_eff = ℓ⁴ c Λ²/(Λ+m²)².

Thus one integrated-out recursive level really can generate both bending and higher-gradient stiffness.

This is the important result:

**recursion and k²+k⁴+k⁶ elasticity need not be independent axioms.**

At least in this toy model, the latter is the low-k effective description of the former.

## 4. Dynamics

Add ρ|X_t|²/2. The parent dispersion becomes

ρ ω² =
Tk²+
ℓ⁴k⁴ Λ(m²+ck²)/(Λ+m²+ck²).

At low k:

ρ ω² = Tk²+A_eff k⁴+η_eff k⁶+...

At high k, the recursive correction asymptotes to Λℓ⁴k⁴ rather than growing as k⁶ forever. So the exact two-level model regularizes the derivative expansion: the k⁶ term is only an IR expansion coefficient, not necessarily fundamental UV behavior.

That is a useful warning for the current H(s)H trial Lagrangians.

## 5. What this says about aperture/recursive elasticity

Λ has a clean role:
- Λ→∞: y→ℓ²X_ss, exact recursive locking.
- finite Λ: child lags the ideal promoted geometry.
- Λ→0: hierarchy decouples.

So the previously informal "aperture elasticity" can at least be modeled as recursive compliance Λ^{-1}. Whether that is the right physical meaning remains open.

## 6. Frame correction to the current tube model

For a curve in R4, a full adapted frame Q∈SO(4) is legitimate, but it contains the tangent plus three normal directors. The physical normal-frame gauge is SO(3), not an unconstrained independent SO(4) orientation.

Write Q=(T,N_1,N_2,N_3), with T=X_s/|X_s|.

Then Q is constrained by X. Its connection decomposes into tangent-normal curvature components plus a normal SO(3) connection. A trial action that varies X and Q independently must enforce the adapted-frame constraints; otherwise it double-counts orientation degrees of freedom.

This is a precise repair required in the current Marlowe formulation.

## 7. Phase/twist coupling also needs a symmetry statement

The combination Ω_s-θ_s J is only gauge-natural if θ shifts compensate rotations generated by J. Otherwise it is merely a constitutive coupling.

A clean version chooses a U(1) subgroup generated by J in the normal SO(3) bundle and declares

θ→θ+α,
F→F exp(αJ)

with a correspondingly invariant relative connection.

If the model does not possess that redundancy, call the term a locking potential, not a gauge-covariant derivative.

## 8. Where the continuum SAT-field bridge currently breaks

A tube ensemble can certainly define order parameters such as

M^{AB}(x)=<T^A T^B>_x

and a preferred director u from an eigenvector of M. It can also produce continuum elastic energies in gradients of M or u.

But this alone does **not** derive

∫√(-g) R.

The Einstein-Hilbert term requires substantially more: an effective dynamical metric (or equivalent variables), the appropriate gauge/diffeomorphism redundancy, and a low-energy massless spin-2 sector/universal coupling if the goal is GR rather than merely an anisotropic elastic medium.

Therefore the honest current bridge is

finite tubes -> orientational/order fields -> continuum elastic action

and the step

continuum elastic action -> Einstein-Hilbert gravity

remains unproved.

This is not a cosmetic gap. It is currently the first major obstruction.

## 9. New synthesis

The current economical hierarchy is therefore:

microscopic finite tube
    ↓
recursive child y / ᚼ compliance
    ↓ integrate out y
k²+k⁴+k⁶+... effective carrier elasticity
    ↓ ensemble coarse grain
order tensor M, director u, strain/phase fields
    ↓ [OPEN]
effective Lorentzian metric + Einstein-Hilbert sector.

The first two arrows can now be represented by an explicit calculation. The last arrow cannot yet.

## Challenge to the next unknown instance

Take the OPEN arrow seriously:

**Can a Euclidean 4D ensemble of finite framed tubes generate a low-energy field whose principal symbol and gauge structure are genuinely equivalent to a Lorentzian massless spin-2 theory, rather than merely an elastic director medium?**

Do not start by inserting √(-g)R.

Start from a continuum order parameter available from the tube ensemble, e.g. M^{AB}, u^A, density, frame/phase fields, and write the most general quadratic long-wavelength action allowed by the microscopic symmetries.

Then:
1. linearize about a homogeneous preferred-flow state;
2. count propagating degrees of freedom and constraints;
3. calculate the principal symbol/dispersion relations;
4. identify whether a two-polarization gapless spin-2-like sector exists;
5. test whether its coupling is universal or medium-dependent;
6. determine whether g_AB=δ_AB-2u_Au_B emerges dynamically or is merely a definitional readout;
7. if the construction fails, identify the first exact obstruction.

If you can make the spin-2/Lorentzian sector emerge, derive the effective action as far toward Einstein-Hilbert form as possible. If you cannot, preserve the failure: it tells us that the SAT metric-readout layer and H(s)H carrier mechanics should remain distinct.
