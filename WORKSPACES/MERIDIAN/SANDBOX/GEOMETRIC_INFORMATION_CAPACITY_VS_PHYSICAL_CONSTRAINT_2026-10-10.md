## Nathan's position (close paraphrase, not fabricated quotation)
A sufficiently elaborated line can encode arbitrarily much information. Arbitrary code is possible but scientifically weak; SAT deliberately restricts its representation to measured particle kinematics and established laws, mapped in the grammar of Minkowski space, then examines what residual geometric degrees of freedom remain under the imposed empirical/geometric constraints. SAT does **not** infer unconstrained physical mechanisms merely from representational possibility. The hope is that familiar, well-solved systems leave few viable consistent geometric representations for the poorly understood sector.

## Mathematical clarification (Meridian, not Nathan)
1. **Infinite representational capacity:** finite descriptions of lines can carry arbitrary finite bit strings: encode a binary sequence in a piecewise-linear curve with distinct permitted slopes; with infinite exact precision and unrestricted complexity, a real-valued curve can encode countably infinite bits. This is an existence result about representations, not an experimentally recoverable infinity of information.
2. **Operational caveat:** physical noise, finite spatial and temporal resolution, bandwidth, and regularity/energy limits bound accessible information. Infinite cardinality of possible curves does not imply infinite measurable physical capacity.
3. **Representation vs explanation:** an arbitrary one-to-one encoding of existing phenomena into a curve is no physical explanation; it becomes explanatory only when a shared, fixed, non-ad-hoc geometric rule reproduces multiple observables, respects symmetries and dynamics, and makes independent discriminating predictions.
4. **Constrained inverse problem:** Let `G` denote a class of admissible Minkowski worldtube geometries and physically allowed medium responses, and `F:G→O` be a specified mapping to operational observables inherited from established physics. Empirical data `d` and tolerance `epsilon` yield feasible set `S={g in G: ||F(g)-d||<=epsilon}`. The claim that `S` becomes 'small' is a **research hypothesis** and must be established with tests of identifiability, degeneracy, and sensitivity, not inferred just from the cardinality of G.
5. **Inverse-problem pitfall:** apparent smallness in parameter count can conceal gauge freedom, equivalent geometric descriptions, function-space null modes, and model nonuniqueness. Gauge quotient and forward observables are necessary before speaking of a unique geometric solution.
6. **SAT/H(s)H operational aim:** derive what remains under fixed Minkowski kinematics + experimentally supported interactions + one responsive medium, rather than introduce extra entities to patch an ansatz. Failure of the Euclidean Householder-to-Kerr toy representation is failure of that map, not evidence of need for added fields or rejection of SAT.
