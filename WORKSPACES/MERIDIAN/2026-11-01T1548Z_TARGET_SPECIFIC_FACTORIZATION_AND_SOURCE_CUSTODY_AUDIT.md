## Target-specific factorization: the common mathematical core

For P : X → Y and O : X → Z, O factors through P on range(P) exactly when O is constant on every fiber of P:

P(x) = P(x') ⇒ O(x) = O(x').

Necessity follows immediately from O = Obar ∘ P. Conversely, fiber-constancy makes Obar(P(x)) := O(x) well-defined on range(P). Extending Obar to all of Y additionally requires P to be surjective or a default value in Z.

This is target-specific. Non-injectivity of P blocks reconstruction of the entire source state (the identity observable), but does not block every observable. For a product projection P(o,h)=o, the first-coordinate observable O1(o,h)=o factors through P, while the full-state observable O2(o,h)=(o,h) does not when H contains distinct states.
