## Material result
The ReDonut source is internally self-correcting in a way that materially sharpens the Whirligig representation benchmark.

Earlier in the source, assistant-generated text asserts that rolling creates a unique/isometric mapping, that curvature/torsion persistence permits reverse-engineering to source equations, that pairwise-prime/rational frequencies secure uniqueness/closure, and that the resulting composite is mathematically reversible and complete.

Later in the *same source*, the critique explicitly withdraws the automatic inference from those local/mechanical properties to global injectivity/recoverability. It states that:

- rolling/nonholonomic dynamics are not automatically information-preserving;
- complete controllability/reachability does not imply recoverability of path history from an endpoint;
- a smooth spherical curve is not a unique representation of an ODE because distinct equations can generate the same trajectory;
- equation→curve injectivity must therefore be designed explicitly or the equation domain restricted;
- the full control history (or a demonstrably sufficient statistic) must be retained if reversal is required;
- holonomy is not automatically the desired symbolic derivative merely because it integrates curvature.

This later section is mathematically the safer statement and directly blocks treating the earlier `curvature/torsion persist => source equation recoverable` claim as established. The fundamental theorem of space curves can determine a sufficiently regular curve from curvature/torsion up to rigid motion under its hypotheses; it does **not** establish an injective map from arbitrary differential equations to trajectories. Multiple dynamical laws can share a trajectory/solution, and endpoint reachability is weaker than reconstruction of the controls/history that produced it.

## Benchmark consequence
The historically appropriate representation-invariance test should now distinguish at least three levels:

1. **Curve-level information preservation:** does the geometric transform retain enough information to reconstruct the chosen input *curve/trace* (up to declared equivalence)?
2. **Encoding-level injectivity:** does the selected equation/object→curve encoding distinguish the intended input class, or can distinct source equations alias to the same curve?
3. **Derivational invariance:** across materially different admissible information-conserving encodings of the same source objects, does Whirligig preserve the same decoded relationship/invariant even if raw traces differ?

Passing (1) does not imply (2); passing either does not imply (3). This is the cleanest current interpretation of Nathan's instruction that one may choose an information-conserving curve rather than a unique canonical encoding.
