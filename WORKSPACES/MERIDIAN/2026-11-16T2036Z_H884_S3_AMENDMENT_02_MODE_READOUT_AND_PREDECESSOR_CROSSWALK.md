## Nature Physics supplementary mode/readout distinction

The Nature Physics article page (published 2026-02-10) describes its supplementary videos as follows:
- Supplementary Video 1: m=1 counterrotating bending waves, with a helical displacement of the vortex axis and no variation in core radius.
- Supplementary Video 2: m=2 double-helix flattening modes, with elliptic deformation of the core and no displacement of the vortex axis.

Primary source: [Nature Physics article and supplementary-information listing](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-026-03175-w), DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03175-w.

For the candidate matrix this means:
- eta(z,t), the centerline-displacement readout, is the primary observable for the reported m=1 bending mode.
- a(z,t), the core-radius/shape readout, is the primary observable for the reported m=2 flattening mode.
- The two observables remain derived from the same opposite-side contour samples in the stated readout construction. The mode distinction does not make them independent acquisitions.
- Neither mode by itself measures an H(s)H normal-bundle twist, a 4D worldtube thickness, or a contact-surface law.

This is a source-level mode/readout distinction, not an H(s)H equivalence.
