## Key result

The **first unresolved mathematical bottleneck is upstream of the Donut itself**: the map from `R,Q` to `i_r,i_q` lacks a recovered equivalence rule. Consequently, even a perfectly deterministic downstream Donut construction cannot yet establish representation invariance. If two admissible encodings of the same `R` or `Q` are not identified by an explicit gauge/equivalence relation, then `Curvy_Lisa` can change while the intended source objects remain the same.

This sharpens the intervention budget into three independent layers:

1. **encoding intervention** — choices used to turn `R,Q` into `i_r,i_q`;
2. **construction intervention** — choices inside `GrAnn → W_obble → W_iggle/V → Donut`;
3. **readout intervention** — parameterization/orientation/starting-point or other choices used to turn the Donut trace into `QwrtR`.

A successful representation-invariance benchmark must vary these separately. Collapsing them into one end-to-end comparison would not tell us where invariance succeeds or fails.

## Minimal benchmark specification

Before any holonomy completion is tested, construct a source pair `(R,Q)` with at least two demonstrably equivalent encodings for each source object. Hold the source pair fixed and run a factorial comparison:

- encoding A/A vs B/A vs A/B vs B/B;
- hold downstream construction rules fixed;
- compare `Curvy_Lisa` first as a parameterized curve and then under the weakest plausible curve equivalences (starting-point shift, orientation reversal, reparameterization, and torus symmetries only when source-licensed);
- only after the curve equivalence class is stable, compare the claimed `QwrtR` readout.

This benchmark can fail usefully at any layer. A failure is not automatically a failure of the Donut idea; it identifies which additional structure must be declared physical or quotiented as representational.
