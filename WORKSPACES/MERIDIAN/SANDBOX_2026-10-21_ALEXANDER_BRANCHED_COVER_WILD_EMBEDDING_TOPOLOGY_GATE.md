**Keep two distinct ideas separate**:
(1) For closed oriented PL n-manifold M, there exists finite-degree branched covering f:M→S^n. For closed orientable connected 3-manifolds Hilden–Montesinos strengthen to a simple degree-three covering of S³ branched over a knot/link, with monodromy from pi_1(S³\L) to S_3. A branching locus lives in the *target base*, and the domain is a manifold. This is NOT automatically an embedding into R³, and theorem existence alone gives no chosen physical branch set.
(2) Alexander horned sphere embeds S² *wildly* into R³. Its exterior has nontrivial and non-finitely-generated fundamental group. Intrinsic boundary topology S², embedding type and complement topology are independent. Infinitely nested linked horns accumulate on a Cantor set; not a finite PL embedded sphere. The horned sphere is NOT the general branched-cover theorem illustrated with spikes.

## New exact gate 1: torus as a branched double cover of S²
For an oriented degree-d branched covering of compact surfaces with simple branch points, Riemann–Hurwitz:
χ(Σ_g)=dχ(S²)−B, or B=2d−(2−2g).
For Σ_1=T² and d=2, B=4.
Thus T² is a two-sheeted cover of S² with **four simple branch points**; e.g. its elliptic-curve model y²=(x−e1)(x−e2)(x−e3), the fourth point at infinity. This is an exact bridge between sphere-based reading and toroidal sections, but NOT a statement that the complete Cruller spacetime equals that elliptic curve, and not a claim for four physical defects.
