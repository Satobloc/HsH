For independent relative moment noise `epsilon`, first-order determinant uncertainty was taken as `sigma_D = epsilon sqrt(2(m0 m2)^2+4m1^4)` and `eta_D=|D|/sigma_D`. Fixed `A=1.44`, `B=0.4225`, `x=1.21`, `epsilon=1e-12`; 3,000 randomized trials per gap over `Delta=1e-1...1e-5`.

**Result:** `eta_D` is useful as a rank/separability warning but is not an accuracy certificate. Median squared-frequency-pair error was `2.69e-3` already at `eta_D≈489` (`Delta=1e-4`), `2.62e-2` at `eta_D≈48.9`, and `2.60e-1` at `eta_D≈4.89`; 95% error at the latter reached `0.949`. Thus even a determinant tens/hundreds of its own estimated noise scale can coexist with poor decoded parameter accuracy because the inverse map compounds sensitivity through division by `D` and root separation.

**Architectural consequence:** adaptive rank should use separate (1) rank/separability and (2) requested-parameter-accuracy gates. Determinant significance alone must not authorize a two-mode decode.
