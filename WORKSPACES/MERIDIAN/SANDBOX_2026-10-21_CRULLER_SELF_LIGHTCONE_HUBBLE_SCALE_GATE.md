## Lightcone self-overlap and a geometric topology scale

For flat FLRW compact spatial quotient R3/Gamma and conformal radial distance chi(z)=integral_0^z dz'/H(z'), an observer's last-scattering sphere of comoving radius chi_* can intersect its image under a deck transformation gamma, separation D_gamma. When 0<D_gamma<2chi_* the two spheres intersect in matched circles of angular radius alpha satisfying
```
cos(alpha)=D_gamma/(2chi_*),    D_gamma=2 chi_* cos(alpha).
```
This provides a *conditional* topological compactification scale or lower bound; alpha and topology image identifications need data. It is the observer seeing the same physical region along more than one allowed light path, not another universe. A single light cone at different time slices naturally has nested spherical sections even in noncompact FLRW and DOES NOT by itself prove compactness or self-overlap. This distinction is essential.

Published null: Planck 2015 background geometry/topology search reports no significant matched-circle pair (temperature/polarization), and for its tested cubic torus a bound R_i>0.97chi_* at 99%-related limits (https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201525829). Petersen et al. 2022/2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02603, discusses model-dependent loopholes to circle searches. Thus at this stage the big circles are a proposed topology mechanism with model constraints, not empirical detection.

## Hubble tension supplies a second independent inverse-length scale, not yet curvature

Planck 2018 base-LambdaCDM H_early=67.4 +/-0.5 km/s/Mpc vs SH0ES distance-ladder H_local=73.04 +/-1.04 km/s/Mpc; these commonly quoted comparison values appear in current research e.g. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jheap.2026.100648 . Their fractional difference relative to H_early is 5.64/67.4=0.0836795 (8.37%). Hubble lengths R_H=c/H differ reciprocally, not directly in the same fraction. In Planck units H=1/R_H with physical conversion.

The Hubble **length** R_H=c/H0 is distinct from particle horizon chi_*(or chi_photon) and from global spatial curvature radius R_K=a/sqrt(|K|). The FLRW Friedmann equation is H²=(8pi/3)rho - K/a² + Lambda/3 (c=G=1), and intrinsic spatial scalar curvature (3)R=6K/a². Differences between inferred H0 values do NOT uniquely fix K, R_K, a global simultaneous-surface radius, or the intervening metric. The two H0 results depend on distinct inferential pipelines, so interpreting their discrepancy as directly measured metric curvature needs an independently specified mapping.
