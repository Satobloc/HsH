## 2. Most precise source-labeled user geometry

### 2024: alternatives posed, not selected

At lines 143-145 of SATy IfThen, the user asks about two candidate geometries:

1. One-dimensional loops embedded in the three-dimensional surface of the wavefront.
2. One-dimensional filaments tracing or wrapping into complex helical structures, with or without material-like interaction.

The message asks whether these ideas remain candidates and whether they should be pursued. It does not define a worldsheet, an action, a string tension, a discrete click parameter, a collective sheet construction, or a map from a filament to a loop.

The assistant responds by introducing three branches: a surface-loop embedding, a helical world-trace, and an emergent string sheet. It calls the helical branch the front-runner for SAT-X and assigns a numerical scorecard. The next source-labeled user turn at line 185 explicitly corrects the project status: “consider it noted and parked” and makes it a “big priority for SAT Y.” That is a direct scope decision. The assistant's prior SAT-X recommendation is not the accepted disposition.

The following user messages ask open questions about intersection geometry, energetics, and interaction between filaments. Those questions keep the geometry exploratory. They do not endorse the assistant's candidate equations or its branch ranking.

### 2025: a more specific composite geometry

The SAT-Y 4D transcript records a user correction at line 302: proton and neutron should each be tight bundles of three filaments, arranged side-by-side, with the electron coiled loosely around them. This is more specific than the 2024 alternatives, but it is still a spatial arrangement. It does not itself define a two-dimensional worldsheet, a four-dimensional worldtube thickening, or a string-theory action.

The assistant's earlier two-single-filament deuterium depiction was corrected. Later code represents a three-strand proton helix with 120-degree phase offsets. That is one possible radial arrangement, but it is not specified by the source-labeled “side-by-side” instruction, and no map reconciles the two geometries. The code also does not implement the complete proton-plus-neutron-plus-electron arrangement in one source-faithful construction.

Critique of the direct source: both the 2024 alternatives and the 2025 three-filament instruction leave important types open. “Helix” could mean a one-dimensional curve in four-dimensional spacetime, a one-dimensional spatial curve on a three-dimensional slice, or a two-dimensional worldsheet swept out over time. “With or without material-like interaction” is an open physical question, not a geometric definition. The 2025 three-filament arrangement still needs an embedding, time evolution, cross-section, and relationship to the current worldtube/timesheet primitives.

A spatial loop family requires an additional construction:

\[
L_t^1\subset\Sigma_t^3,\qquad
F:I\times S^1\longrightarrow M^4,
\]

where F is a sufficiently regular family of embeddings whose image is the two-dimensional worldsheet. The source does not supply F or the projection/slicing conditions.

Thus:
- A single 1D worldline intersecting a 3D timesheet generically gives a point, not a loop.
- A 2D worldsheet intersecting a 3D timesheet generically gives a 1D curve or loop.
- A 4D finite-core support intersecting a 3D timesheet generically gives a 3D section.
- A 1D loop already embedded in a 3D slice is an instantaneous string configuration. Its history is a worldsheet only after a time-family map is defined.
- A “helical world-trace” remains ambiguous until it is identified as γ¹, L¹_t, or W².
- An “emergent string sheet” made from many filaments requires an explicit collective map from the filament family to W².

Standard comparator: Brandon Carter, [Essentials of Classical Brane Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0012036), distinguishes the one-dimensional worldline of a point particle, the two-dimensional worldsheet of a string, and higher-dimensional brane worldvolumes. This is standard geometric terminology, not evidence for an SAT correspondence.
