## 4. New source-readiness result: Knister counter-rotating vortex experiment

The new candidate is a different physical system from the Barckicke bathtub-vortex candidate already profiled in issue #1484. The present source map was published through the existing issue publisher intake as issue #1766; it is candidate-only and does not select P2.

Primary sources:

- Daniel Knister, 2024 University of Michigan dissertation, *Experimental Study of Cavitation Inception During Vortex-Vortex Interaction and Investigation of Constrained Volumetric Velocimetry Methods*, Deep Blue handle 2027.42/193437.
- Knister, Ganesh and Ceccio, 2026, *Journal of Fluid Mechanics* 1032, A57, DOI 10.1017/jfm.2026.11120.
- Related follow-up, *Incipient cavitation resulting from the nonlinear interaction of a pair of counter-rotating vortices*, JFM 1034, A37, DOI 10.1017/jfm.2026.11441. This is the same experimental programme, not independent-laboratory replication.

The physical system is a pair of unequal-strength counter-rotating vortices generated by cambered hydrofoils in a recirculating cavitation channel. The weaker secondary develops a Crow-type instability, wraps around the primary, forms Ω-shaped loops, and breaks into vortex rings.

### Measurement hierarchy

- High-speed video: two Phantom v1212 cameras, 1280 × 800, 12-bit, 12,600 frames/s. Side and mirror-assisted top-down views show global path and cavitation, but the thesis says the fields of view do not quite overlap.
- Volumetric velocimetry: four Phantom v1212 cameras with a high-speed laser and Shake-the-Box PTV. Midstream/downstream acquisition is 10 kHz; upstream rate varies with flow speed. Typical spatial resolution is 0.42 mm upstream and 0.35 mm midstream/downstream.
- Three spatial measurement locations: 0.7c, 1.2c and 1.7c downstream, with nominal volumes 4×38×40 mm, 5×35×40 mm and 7×55×45 mm. Different flow conditions require separate volume placements. The downstream volume captures only a portion of the secondary loop at a given instant.

The source-defined reconstruction chain is camera images → calibrated particle observations → STB particle tracks → VIC# Eulerian velocity/vorticity/pressure fields → thresholded vortex regions → local cross-sections and moments → derived vortex properties.

Circulation is integrated over vorticity above 10% of the local peak, with a connected-region constraint. The vortex centre is a vorticity-weighted centroid. Core radius is a vorticity-weighted second spatial moment about that centre. Eccentricity comes from eigenvalues of a normalized second-moment matrix. Downstream properties are calculated on planes interpolated locally orthogonal to each vortex; λ2 isosurfaces are used for topology visualization. The core radius is therefore an algorithm-defined moment of a thresholded vorticity region, not a directly imaged sharp material boundary.

### Measured versus inferred

The thesis reports ±0.08% accuracy for the differential-pressure transducer used to infer freestream velocity, giving ±0.06% velocity uncertainty, and ±0.08% for the static-pressure transducer. These are instrument uncertainties, not an uncertainty budget for the reconstructed core pressure.

STB-VIC# returns a zero-average pressure field. A modified Pitot probe away from the vortices supplies a steady boundary-pressure anchor; a probe in the middle of the flow would perturb the interaction. The core pressure is inferred, not directly probed.

The thesis says there is no generally accepted a-posteriori uncertainty method for the volumetric PTV/VIC# pressure reconstruction. Published vortex-property table spreads are dominated by physical variability across temporally subsampled fields. At least 1,000 fields were used per case, with autocorrelation between successive subsampled fields reported between −0.1 and 0.1.

### Resolution limitation and source correction

The thesis reports approximately seven velocity vectors across the secondary core, against eight as a typical minimum for accurate vortex-pressure reconstruction. Particle ejection and the required volume thickness further limit core resolution. The authors infer that secondary-core vorticity and pressure drop are underestimated, then apply an additional correction proportional to the square of the upstream/downstream secondary-circulation ratio.

The reported correlation between reconstructed pressure drop and Γ²/r_c² (about −0.85 to −0.9) is not independent validation: both quantities derive from the same STB-VIC# velocity reconstruction. The circulation correction is model-dependent, not a direct pressure measurement.

### Historical correction

The 2024 dissertation explicitly corrects an earlier 2022 interpretation: the reported correlation between time-integrated axial strain and secondary-vortex pressure reduction was an error, attributed to a coding artifact in the time integration of axial strain. The dissertation says the earlier conclusion was not supported by the data. Preserve that 2022 claim as superseded; do not use it as evidence for an axial-strain pressure mechanism.

### Model applicability

Generalized Crow linear stability reasonably predicts initial/linear-stage growth. Its long-wavelength premise requires core radius much smaller than vortex separation; the dissertation says this condition is not met in the studied cases. Predicted wavelength becomes less accurate when core size is comparable to separation, most notably for the largest-core case. The linear model does not describe full nonlinear loop formation and ring breakup.

This is a useful finite-core benchmark, but not an elastic rope experiment and not evidence for WWRD/climbing-rope physical equivalence.
