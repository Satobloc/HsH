The APS abstract explicitly frames fragmentation as occurring **via unspecified dynamics** and evaluates time-sliced quasi-static black-hole configurations using kinematic and entropy considerations. The university release describes the horizon boundary as taking a trouser-like one-to-two shape through time.

### What the paper establishes, and what it does not

1. **Schwarzschild:** the Bekenstein–Hawking area/entropy relation disfavors or forbids a split into two smaller black holes under the paper's classical assumptions.
2. **Kerr:** entropy alone can leave a loophole, but kinematic constraints on the fragments' ability to separate close it for most configurations. A possible window remains near extremal rotation.
3. **Other regimes:** the paper discusses the Gregory–Laflamme instability in higher dimensions, possible superradiant instabilities with non-axisymmetric radiation trapping, and gravity models that modify the entropy/horizon-radius/mass relations.
4. **Occurrence:** entropic favorability is not sufficient. The initial kinematic configuration and the ability of the fragments to escape matter.
5. **Evidence status:** this is a theoretical admissibility analysis, not an observed black-hole split, a probability estimate, or a complete time-dependent solution for ordinary four-dimensional Kerr.

A secondary SciSpace extraction of indexed full text suggests that the paper also cites prior dynamical-instability work in higher-dimensional and quadratic-gravity settings (reference groups [10,14] and [41–43]). The full APS PDF was not available to this run, and those bibliography entries were not independently resolved. Keep this as a **provisional literature lead**, not a verified attribution. It does not change the paper's own stated limitation: its fragmentation dynamics are unspecified.

### Pair-of-pants versus Alexander horned sphere

The standard pair-of-pants is a compact, orientable genus-zero **2D surface** with three boundary circles and Euler characteristic -1. It is an elementary cobordism between one circle and two circles (or the reverse under time reversal).

The Alexander horned sphere is a **wild embedding of S^2 in S^3** (equivalently described in R^3) whose complementary region is not simply connected. It is a static embedding/complement-topology example, not by itself a time-evolution cobordism or a split/merge operation.

Therefore:
- the black-hole paper supports the relevance of a particular pants-like one-to-two horizon history;
- the paper does not establish the Alexander horned sphere as a general split/merge model;
- the horned sphere may be a comparator for complicated boundary embeddings and complement topology, but not a substitute for a cobordism;
- the 2D horned sphere does not directly match the proposed 3D boundary of a 4D H(s)H tube. A higher-dimensional analogue or a specified lower-dimensional cross-section map would be required.
