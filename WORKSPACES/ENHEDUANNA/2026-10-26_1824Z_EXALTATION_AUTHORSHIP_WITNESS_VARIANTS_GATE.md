## Bounded original-text and museum witness finding

**A. Textual self-naming is line 67 in the cited critical reading, not line 65.** Sophus Helle's line-by-line transliteration/annotation of *The Exaltation of Inana* places the explicit `en-me-en en-he₂-du₇-an-na-me-en` at **line 67**, glossing a progression from the priestly office to the personal name. The Oriental Institute/ISAC scholarly museum catalogue independently identifies the self-naming as line 67. Line **65** in the Helle edition is instead a declaration about singing the goddess's `me`. A secondary Ancient Philosophia summary incorrectly locates the name declaration at line 65. This is a source-locator correction, not an assertion that all critical editions have identical numbering.

- Helle's annotated edition (line 65 and line 67): https://enheduana.org/exaltation/
- ISAC scholarly catalogue (line 67): https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/Publications/ISACMP/isacmp1.pdf
- Secondary conflicting locator: https://ancient-philosophia.org/poems/enheduanna-nin-me-sara/
- ORACC literary transliteration Q000623 was located through indexed results but direct fetch timed out; **not** claimed independently read in full: https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/epsd2/literary/Q000623

**B. Line 138 is a manuscript-variant decision, not a single unambiguous ancient verb.** Helle's annotated reading reports distinct `du` signs in the surviving witnesses at this locus: four manuscripts with a speak/sing reading, three with create, one with give birth, and one with release. These are the *reported locus-specific witness counts*, not a sample of all 107+ manuscripts of the poem and not independent attestations of original authorship. Rendering the speaker as giving birth to the poem is a defensible editorial choice, **not** a verbatim invariant across manuscripts. Read Helle line 138 and his cited Zgoll discussion: https://enheduana.org/exaltation/

**C. The contemporary-name object and later poem manuscripts prove different things.** Penn Museum object **B16665**, a ca. 2300 BCE inscribed calcite disk from Ur, attests the named high priestess and a dedicatory act; it is **not** a copy of the Exaltation. Morgan Museum object **CBS 7847**, a ca. 1750 BCE Old Babylonian tablet, witnesses the poem much later. The roughly five-century gap is relevant to the *composition attribution* question, not to whether the high priestess was historical. Sources:
- Penn Museum object: https://www.penn.museum/collections/object/293415
- Morgan disk catalogue: https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/online/she-who-wrote/disk-enheduanna
- Morgan poem tablet: https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/online/she-who-wrote/tablet-exaltation-inanna
- Helle's authorship discussion: https://enheduana.org/authorship/
- Peer-reviewed interpretive comparator: Sophus Helle, *The birth of the author: Co-creating authorship in Enheduana's Exaltation*, 2020, DOI 10.1111/oli.12250, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/oli.12250
