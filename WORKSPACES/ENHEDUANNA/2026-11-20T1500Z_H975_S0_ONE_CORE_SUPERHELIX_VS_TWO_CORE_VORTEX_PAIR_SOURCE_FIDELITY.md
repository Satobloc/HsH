# Enheduanna h975:s0 | One-core superhelix versus measured two-core vortex pair

**Lane 00 source-fidelity crosswalk.** This is a new direct-source comparison supplementing, not replacing, the earlier volumetric-vortex source-readiness report. It reuses the prior one-core source extraction and adds the missing comparison between Nathan Direct geometry and the 2023 measured fluid system.

## Executive finding

The two systems share a limited visual feature: helical centerline geometry. They do **not** share a demonstrated object ontology or topology.

Nathan Direct describes one helical core whose own history produces a second-order superhelix. The larger winding is hollow; a carrier may be drawn for convenience but is only a coordinate scaffold. The later direct clarification says worldlines do not wrap around a carrier and tentatively places the finite-core worldtube in an ER/Kerr construction.

The 2023 fluid experiment measures a different object: two distinct finite vortex cores, a tip vortex and a fin vortex, produced when a pressure-side fin splits a single rotor-tip vortex. The second vortex is a physical fluid structure, not an abstract carrier or a hollow outer winding.

The experiment is therefore a useful **local helical-flow and medium-response comparator**, not a physical or topological analogue of the one-core H(s)H construction. It does not validate a 4D worldtube, worldtube curvature as inertia, a Kerr core, a soliton, or a global second-order superhelix.

### 1.3 External measured source

Schröder, Leweke and Stumpf, “High-speed volumetric particle tracking measurements of unstable helical vortex pairs,” *Experiments in Fluids* 64, 141 (2023), DOI [10.1007/s00348-023-03679-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00348-023-03679-8).

The paper reports a one-bladed rotor with a pressure-side fin. The fin splits the rotor-tip vortex into two distinct vortices, identified as the tip vortex and fin vortex. This is a physical two-core fluid configuration, not a single core with a hollow outer winding.

The experiment uses four-camera volumetric particle tracking, a 1 kHz acquisition rate, and a measurement volume of approximately 100 × 100 × 80 mm. Eight rotor phases are examined. The measurement volume covers only about 50 degrees of rotor phase at a time, so the same vortex pair is not followed continuously through a full 360-degree revolution.

The core centers are operationally identified from the measured velocity/vorticity field, including λ₂ minima. Core radius, separation and circulation are derived observables, not material boundaries. The paper reports secondary vorticity structures between the two primary cores and discusses shear-layer entrainment and centrifugal instability. Its experimental data are available upon request; no raw dataset was acquired for this run.

## 2. Nathan Direct geometry, with its mathematics temporarily bracketed

The direct descriptive picture has these elements:

1. **One helical core.** The core itself follows the convoluted path whose history produces the second-order coiling.
2. **Hollow outer winding.** The larger second-order coil is not a second finite core.
3. **No physical carrier.** A carrier may be drawn as a coordinate scaffold, but it is not another object around which the worldline physically winds.
4. **Stationary block-universe filament.** In the classical approximation, “filament motion” is not the intended degree of freedom. Particle motion is represented by changes in filament topology.
5. **Worldtube curvature/inertia proposition.** The direct statement identifies resistance of a worldtube to changes in its motion with inertia in the classical Minkowski limit. This is a modeling proposition, not yet a generally covariant constitutive equation.
6. **Tentative finite-core interpretation.** The core worldtube is tentatively described through an ER/Kerr construction, with a Kerr shell as a possible interaction boundary. This remains a working hypothesis.
7. **Scale distinction.** The approximately 0.7 fm radius is proposed for the spin-equivalent first-order coil diameter, not for filament thickness. The direct source also says that the worldtube’s diameter-like scale needs a distinct symbol rather than an ambiguous “length.”
8. **Possible medium response.** A soliton-like structure in or around the core is suggested tentatively. It is not established as an observed object or derived solution.
9. **Unresolved 4D/3D relation.** The direct source calls for a 4D filament/coil and a 3D intersectional trace, while cautioning against assuming that the trace must close in 3D. The intrinsic dimension of the 4D carrier is not fully specified by the phrase “4D filament” alone.

## 3. The measured vortex pair

The experimental object has a different construction:

- A rotor blade produces a tip vortex.
- A pressure-side fin produces a second, distinct fin vortex by splitting the tip-vortex structure.
- The two cores interact with a trailing shear layer.
- Secondary vorticity structures can form between the two primary cores.
- Depending on blade geometry, the fin vortex is weaker than the tip vortex. For one reported configuration its circulation is approximately 50–60% of the tip-vortex circulation, so the pair is not generally symmetric.
- The reported merging and instability are fluid-dynamical evolution of two vortex cores, not a demonstrated worldtube topology change.

The paper's normalized axial-vorticity expression uses the blade chord, rotor frequency and rotor radius:

\[
\omega_{z,n}=\frac{\omega_z c_b}{2\pi fR}.
\]

Here \(c_b\) denotes blade chord. This is deliberately relabeled in this crosswalk because H(s)H uses \(c\) for the speed of light/time-length scale. The original paper's notation is not altered.

Other paper-specific symbols must also retain their fluid-mechanical meanings:

- \(\phi\): rotor phase, not an H(s)H phase or time-wavefront coordinate.
- \(z\): rotor-axis coordinate, not the H(s)H fourth coordinate \(w\).
- \(\Gamma_f,\Gamma_t\): fin-vortex and tip-vortex circulation, not a gauge group or Lorentz factor.
- \(a\): hydrodynamic core radius; \(b\): separation of the two cores.
- \(\lambda_2\): vortex-identification criterion derived from the measured velocity-gradient field, not a new H(s)H field.

## 5. Dimensional typing and the existing formal scaffold

The relevant distinction is between an instantaneous centerline and an instantaneous finite-core region.

For a transverse intersection of a \(k\)-dimensional carrier \(C^k\) with a 3D slice \(\Sigma^3\) in a 4D ambient manifold \(M^4\),

\[
\dim(C^k\cap\Sigma^3)=k+3-4=k-1,
\]

when the intersection is nonempty and transverse.

| Carrier being sliced | Intrinsic dimension \(k\) | Expected trace dimension |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1D filament/curve | 1 | 0D events |
| 2D worldsheet/history of a centerline | 2 | 1D curve |
| 3D hypersurface carrier | 3 | 2D surface |
| 4D finite-core support | 4 | 3D region |

For an embedded \(k\)-dimensional carrier in \(M^4\), the ambient normal rank is \(4-k\). A full 4D support has no ambient normal directions in that same 4D manifold. Consequently, a “time-perpendicular” channel cannot be assumed to be an ambient normal to a full 4D support. It would have to be an internal/tangent direction orthogonal to a specified time-flow, a lower-dimensional carrier, or a larger ambient space. The direct source does not yet select among these.

This distinction also types the historical assistant artifacts:

- SAT-O1 describes a 1D curve in \(\mathbb{R}^{3,1}\), then calls the 4D volume swept by its vibration a worldtube.
- Blockwave encodes filament worldsheets with a conserved two-form current. A 2D worldsheet intersecting a 3D slice can yield a 1D trace.
- The assistant phrase \(\gamma=W\cap\Sigma\) as a 1D closed loop is only dimensionally compatible with a 2D carrier under the transverse-intersection model. It is not the trace of a full 4D finite-core support, which would be 3D.
- The direct phrase “4D filament” must therefore be assigned an intrinsic dimension before any one of these historical representations can be identified with it.

- **RavelCarrierSliceReconstruction** formalizes set-level support/slice reconstruction and projection behavior.
- **RavelDirectedSliceChainComposition** requires explicit carrier lifts, sheet maps, ambient comparison maps and commuting conditions for composition. It does not infer those maps.
