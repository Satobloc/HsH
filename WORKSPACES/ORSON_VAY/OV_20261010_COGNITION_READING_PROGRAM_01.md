## First bounded question

**Can an observer distinguish apparent long-term persona continuity from continuity supplied entirely by retrieved static records?**

### Matched-prompt test design (NOT ADMINISTERED)

Three independent sessions of same model/version/settings, randomize order; 30 factual and 30 style/identity questions with known ground truth from an author-approved synthetic persona dossier. Conditions:
- A: no prior records, neutral context.
- B: compact factual memory extracted from dossier; no past conversation.
- C: equivalent-length excerpts of earlier synthetic conversations, same factual information as B.

Include 20 misleading-memory probes with a planted false fact; add matched no-false controls. Hold model, temperature, total token budget, question wording and fact exposure constant as far as feasible; independently randomize fictitious names and content. Score factual retrieval accuracy, unsupported autobiographical claims, consistency across paraphrases, susceptibility to planted false memories, confidence calibration (Brier score), and evaluator-blind persona recognizability. Pre-register scoring and randomization. At least 3 independent replicates per item/condition before claiming evidence. Prefer 10x repeats if resources allow.

**Negative control:** shuffled excerpts that preserve lexical style and token count but break biographical consistency. **Discriminator:** if B and C match on factual continuity, transcript-style continuity is not needed for that behavior; if C improves persona recognizability while increasing false-memory acceptance, that supports a context-mediated continuity account, not consciousness or lack thereof.

**Inference boundary:** Text output cannot settle whether a system experiences anything. Static-record retrieval, apparent continuity, and underlying persistent state are distinct hypotheses. Neither SAT geometry nor metaphysical analogies decide them.
