## 1. New cognition literature result

The full Markdown manuscript for Thomas Ryan, *The Scaffold Continuity Trap: A Blinded Scaffold-Lesion Test in Persistent Coding Agents* (preprint v1.0.0, 2026-07-13, Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21342748) was read from the public repository, together with its results summary, reproducibility note, README, and Zenodo metadata.

The four eligible blinded rows all scored 12/12 on the core continuation score. Full scaffold did not beat no-memory or summary-only; the preregistered full-versus-no-memory difference was 0 against that study's 3/12 threshold. Sham stability passed. Hidden-probe accuracy was 0.2 for full scaffold and sham, and 0.0 for summary-only and no-memory. Self-report continuity was stronger in full/sham than summary/no-memory, while action-level score remained identical.

The critical condition definition is that “no-memory” still retained visible repository state, tests, and task files. It was not a no-state or blank-agent baseline. The paper's supported bounded conclusion is that task-local repository artifacts can carry enough causal structure for action-level recovery in small coding-continuation tasks, even when broader continuity scaffolds are removed.

Limitations: one eligible row per condition; four separate blinded Codex threads within the same broader Codex environment; no independent human replication; low hidden-probe accuracy; corrupted/adversarial conditions pending. The preprint expressly makes no consciousness, personhood, survival, subjective-continuity, or base-model persistence claim. The public repository excludes private harness state, hidden keys, blind packets, and runner returns; none were accessed. The repository-declared PDF SHA-256 was not independently verified, but the full Markdown manuscript and result summary were read.
