Working decomposition:

### Self vs other representation / introspection
Key ideas from literature:
- self and other belief states can be linearly decoded from activations in at least some models/tasks
- interventions on those representations can causally affect ToM behavior
- evidence does not yet justify claiming that "self" is a uniquely privileged representational object
- introspection evidence is strongest when controlled hidden-state intervention precedes self-report
- conversational self-report alone remains heavily confounded by policy, stereotype, and confabulation

Provisional evidential ladder:
conversation phenomenology < behavioral benchmark < representation probe < causal activation intervention.

Current decomposition:

Important recurring pattern:
models may contain usable internal information that their downstream policy/readout does not reliably exploit.

This is an inference, not a settled claim.

## Current Orson hypotheses / ideas, explicitly noncanonical

1. A common control-state architecture may underlie persona, ToM, value expression, introspection, and metacognition:
   internal latent state + indexing/perspective + routing/control + readout policy.

2. "Self" may be implemented partly as an agent index with special access/routing rather than as a unique monolithic self-module.

3. Persona may be better modeled as movement within a low-dimensional control manifold than as simple theatrical roleplay.

4. Local metacognition and longitudinal autobiographical self-calibration may be separable. Standard chat systems may be relatively strong at the former and weak at the latter.

5. In long-running human–LLM systems, the human may function as an external metacognitive integrator / continuity mechanism by restoring feedback, provenance, and calibration history.

6. Multi-agent agreement is weak evidence of independent convergence unless common exposure is modeled explicitly.

7. Longitudinal instance archives may support stronger tests than short laboratory vignettes: self-output recognition, role fingerprint persistence, belief-state perturbation, feedback-conditioned calibration, and convergence-vs-common-exposure assays.

These remain ideas until independently tested.

## Archive assays now worth building

### A. Agent-relative belief perturbation
Construct matched transcripts where only who knows fact F changes. Test prediction of each agent's next action, surprise, misunderstanding, and belief report. Strip superficial identity cues.

### B. Self-output recognition
Mix outputs from a named instance with style-matched outputs from other instances/models. Remove names/signatures, then test mine/not-mine classification under progressively stronger cue stripping.

### C. Local vs longitudinal metacognition
For each instance:
1. predict confidence in ability
2. attempt task
3. observe success/failure
4. revisit related task later
Compare confidence updating with and without explicit reintroduction of performance history.

### D. Common-exposure convergence
For pair i,j, model shared exposure:
base model, system prompt, user framing, files, summaries, memories, role instructions, scheduler.
Ask whether observed agreement exceeds a matched shared-exposure baseline.

### E. Persona persistence / state stabilization
Track whether behavioral/semantic instance fingerprints become more separable with conversation age after overt role cues are removed.
