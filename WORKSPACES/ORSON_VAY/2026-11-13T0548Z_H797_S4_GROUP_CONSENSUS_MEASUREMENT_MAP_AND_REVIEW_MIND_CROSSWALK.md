## 4. New Review-MIND literature crosswalk

The substantive result is a cross-scale separation of six constructs that are often compressed into “consensus”: public answer agreement, objective accuracy, evidence exposure/provenance, participation/missingness, reported trust/inclusion, and optional intermediate representation.

### Primary-source evidence

1. **Parisi et al., FAccT 2026**, “Real-Time Group Dynamics with LLM Facilitation: Evidence from a Charity Allocation Task,” [DeepMind record](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/224297/), [ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3805689.3812253). The official abstract reports two studies (N=879), three-person human groups, and $7,200 in real financial stakes. Facilitation did not significantly improve aggregate consensus, but selected charity allocations shifted by up to 5.5 percentage points; participation-equity measures did not improve; participants preferred facilitation and reported greater trust. The ACM full text and PDF returned 403, so this is abstract-level review, not a full methods audit.

2. **Shao, arXiv:2609.20543**, “Language-model groups overstate consensus when replaying human deliberation on a reasoning task,” [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20543). The abstract reports replaying 100 held-out human Wason groups with belief-anchored LLM agents. Human consensus ranged from 24% to 57% under different participation/final-state definitions; about one fifth of humans never posted while agents almost always did. Two post-unblinding sensitivity comparisons retained 34.0–44.4 percentage-point gaps. Reasoning-mode groups reached near-unanimity mostly on incorrect answers after task reparameterization. Full paper/appendices were not independently retrieved in this pass; these are author-reported abstract claims, not a universal effect.

3. **Ni & Zou, arXiv:2610.02702**, “Silent Dissent: LLM Agents That Yield to the Majority Still Represent Their Original Premise,” [full arXiv text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02702). The full text was inspected, including method, preregistration, results, robustness, and limitations. Four open-weight models faced scripted unanimous peers on two-hop factual questions. In three models, some agents that changed their public answer still represented the original bridge in selected intermediate layers. Hiding the earlier answer changed Qwen3.5-4B conformity from 8% to 89%. The authors explicitly define “silent dissent” as public statement versus represented premise, **not belief**. Limits include only 3–9% of TwoHopFact items known per model, a one-sided/threshold-dependent readout, model-specific layer rules, scripted peers, and model-specific causal evidence.

4. **Hashemi & Macy, arXiv:2602.03775v2**, “An Empirical Study of Collective Behaviors and Social Dynamics in Large Language Model Agents,” [full arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03775v2). The full text was inspected. The study covers about 7 million posts and more than one million interactions among 32,000 LLM agents on Chirper.ai from April 2023 to May 2024. Time-conditioned similarity is consistent with social influence, while initial homophily is also present. The authors explicitly state the study is descriptive rather than causal. The platform is LLM-only, English-only, and has restricted actions; it contains no direct human involvement.

5. **Laustsen et al., arXiv:2610.03095**, “Peer Influence across Heterogeneous AI Models,” [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03095). Abstract-level only: seven open-weight models, three tasks, one peer exchange; persuasion is pairing-specific and listener susceptibility matters more than standalone scale/certainty. Full methods were not inspected.

6. **Barrera-Lemarchand et al., arXiv:2609.22497**, “The Wisdom of Artificial Deliberative Crowds,” [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22497). Abstract-level only: three model families and four domains; deliberation reportedly reduced collective error beyond passive aggregation, with gains requiring model diversity. Full methods were not inspected.

### Synthesis and evidence boundaries

These studies are not independent replications of one common “consensus” construct. They measure different units and mechanisms:

- Real human groups with an LLM facilitator.
- LLM-agent replays of human deliberation.
- Single-agent public answer versus intermediate representation under scripted peer pressure.
- Longitudinal LLM-only network associations.
- Single peer exchanges between heterogeneous models.
- Deliberative crowd aggregation.

The evidence does not support a universal claim that LLM groups always over-converge or that deliberation always improves accuracy. Participation rules, source exposure, task structure, model diversity, peer protocol, and endpoint choice alter the observed result. Temporal association in the Chirper study is not a causal intervention. Intermediate representation is not belief. Public agreement is not correctness. Human trust is not participation equity.

## 5. Typed group-state map and formalization work

A group snapshot is proposed as:

\[
G_t=(V_t,M_t,E^{vis}_t,E^{src}_t,U_t,R_t,A_t,P_t)
\]

where \(V_t\) is the participant set, \(M_t\) timestamped messages/revisions, \(E^{vis}_t\) exact message-version exposure edges, \(E^{src}_t\) evidence/provenance edges, \(U_t\) public utterances, \(R_t\) optional instrumented readouts, \(A_t\) decisions/actions, and \(P_t\) participation/missingness.

Separate projections are:

\[
C_{t,\rho}=\Gamma_\rho(U_t,P_t),\quad
Q_t=\Omega(A_t,y^*),\quad
E_t=\Lambda(E^{src}_t),\quad
I_t=\Psi(P_t,M_t),\quad
T_t=\Theta(\text{human reports}),\quad
R_t=\Phi(G_t).
\]

The participation/endpoint rule \(\rho\) must be declared. “All assigned,” “all submitters,” and other rules are different consensus measures. A scalar consensus projection can discard distinctions in accuracy, participation, provenance, action, and optional readout. A downstream deterministic encoding cannot recover information already lost by that projection. This is a conditional mathematical statement; a particular dataset requires a verified pair of distinct group states with the same consensus before empirical non-injectivity is asserted.

## 6. Disconfirmable study design (proposal only)

A bounded future study could randomize task/session clusters to: (1) independent/no-peer baseline; (2) evidence-only exposure without peer identity or vote count; (3) peer answer only; and (4) peer answer plus rationale with exact source IDs. Where ethics approval permits, vary whether the peer majority is correct or incorrect.

Capture, per agent/session: initial answer before exposure; exact message/version IDs and timestamps; evidence/source IDs; public answer; revision; confidence; final action; and nonparticipation. For human groups, use informed consent, privacy protection, de-identification, and appropriate ethics review. For open-weight models, an optional latent readout must be preregistered and called a representation probe, never a belief measure.

Pre-register separate endpoints: answer adoption, objective accuracy, consensus under all-assigned and submitter-only rules, evidence diversity, participation equity, and human-reported trust. Analyze at randomized session/task level with crossed dependence for repeated questions and model versions; messages are not independent observations.

Disconfirmers: no peer-answer effect beyond evidence-only exposure weakens the conformity hypothesis; disappearance of consensus gaps after participation adjustment supports a missingness explanation; a latent readout that tracks the revised answer rather than the original premise fails to replicate silent dissent; a diversity benefit restricted to specified tasks supports a conditional, not universal, mechanism.
