## 3. Nathan Direct geometry, kept separate from the mathematics

The strongest direct geometric description is a movable marked point on a scaled and rotated copy of a 4D hypersphere/grid. The point traces a one-parameter path. Higher-order superhelices are proposed as nested or repeated helical structure, but the direct source does not specify a complete recursion operator or coordinate law.

The March 8 conversation states that the user's existing equations concerned second- and third-order helical paths in fundamental particles, while any much higher coiling factor in brains or chips would be a separate conjecture. It then places a clear epistemic boundary around cognition: the particle equations are not themselves a geometry of thought. The user is open to calculating a physical path, but does not claim to know the shape of thought or that a strange loop has a particular physical winding. Thus particle superhelix order (`n_p`) must not be silently reused as cognitive recursion depth (`n_c`).

Under a Euclidean interpretation, `S^3` is the three-dimensional boundary of a four-ball `B^4`; it is not the four-dimensional volume. A curve on `S^3`, a volume integral over `B^4`, and a functional integral over a space of paths are three different mathematical objects.

The following should therefore remain distinct:

- physical superhelix order, `n_p`;
- cognitive recursion depth, `n_c`;
- geometric closure of a curve;
- semantic/self-referential “strange loop”;
- an observed computational or neural process;
- an assessment of consciousness.

No map identifying these quantities is supplied.

## 5. Candidate geometry and missing maps

A minimal assistant reconstruction of the two-grid idea is:

\[
\gamma(\lambda)=\rho(\lambda)R(\lambda)p,
\]

with \(p\in S^3\), \(\rho(\lambda)\ge 0\), and \(R(\lambda)\in SO(4)\), **if** the ambient four-dimensional geometry is Euclidean.

Under those assumptions and \(\|p\|=1\),

\[
\|\gamma(\lambda)\|=\rho(\lambda).
\]

This identity only says that the radial distance equals the scale parameter. It does not prove that the construction generates every admissible curve, that the source intended `SO(4)`, or that the resulting path represents cognition.


If the ambient metric is Lorentzian, `SO(4)` is not the appropriate rotation group. The March 7 source does not settle the metric signature. The later SAT/H(s)H four-spatial-dimensional convention must not be back-projected onto this earlier construction without an explicit representation map.

A one-parameter curve \(\gamma:I\to M^4\) is not itself a finite-core 4D worldtube. A worldtube needs a thickening/sweep map, for example

\[
F:I\times D^3\to M^4,
\]

or another explicitly defined family of cross-sections. No such map is supplied in the direct construction.

The missing composition chain is:

```text
fixed 4D reference grid / hypersphere
                 |
       movable copy: scale + rotation
                 |
          marked-point path
                 |
       [missing thickening map]
                 |
       finite-core worldtube
                 |
       [missing physical measurement map]
                 |
      chip / brain process or current
                 |
       [missing computational map]
                 |
       computation / thought / self-model
                 |
       [observation map, conditional]
                 |
        language / behavior / assessment
```

The first mapping is described qualitatively. The later mappings are not defined. The observation-map formalization discussed below addresses only the final epistemic layer; it does not fill the missing physical or cognitive maps.
