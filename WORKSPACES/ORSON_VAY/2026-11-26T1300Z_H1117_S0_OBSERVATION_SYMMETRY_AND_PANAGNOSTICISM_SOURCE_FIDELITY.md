## 3. Nathan Direct propositions, independently extracted

The direct transcript material supports these propositions, with different strengths:

- **First-person access:** one's own present subjective experience is treated as directly accessible to oneself; another entity's experience is not directly available in the same way.
- **Mediated third-person evidence:** behavior and language are important observable evidence about other entities. Physical properties may correlate with behavior associated with consciousness or its loss, but are not themselves direct access to another's subjective experience.
- **Conditional evidential symmetry:** when two entities are observed with the same quality of evidence, assigning them different consciousness assessments requires some further differentiating reason. The user explicitly raises, then conditionally accepts, equal-likelihood language in this setting.
- **Meta-epistemic claim:** an observer can know that they do not know whether another entity is conscious. The user presents this as a reason for panagnosticism.
- **Candidate cognitive features:** complexity, world-model construction, interaction with those models, revision from subsequent input, feedback, self-reflection and self-modeling are proposed as possible features relevant to consciousness. The user leaves their sufficiency and precise thresholds open.
- **Candidate system boundary:** the user asks whether the interaction comprising the human, words and computer could itself be a relevant self-reflective system. This is a question, not a defined system or an assertion that the interaction is conscious.

These are conceptual and methodological propositions. The transcripts supply no metric on evidence quality, probability space, prior distribution, observation function, state-transition law, formal system boundary or necessary-and-sufficient consciousness criterion.

## 7. Corrected discrepancy ledger

1. **Probability attribution refined.** The earlier h832 report's D8 grouped probability parity with assistant strengthening. That was too coarse. A direct user transcript explicitly raises and conditionally accepts equal-likelihood language when two entities have observations of the same quality. This is a real direct-source contribution. However, it is not a specified Bayesian model and does not establish equal posterior probability without assumptions about priors, likelihoods and what counts as equal-quality evidence.

2. **Evidence equality versus evidence quality.** The formal map `O_a(w)=O_a(v)` is exact equality. The direct phrase “same quality of observations” is qualitative. A metric, equivalence relation, tolerance or feature-matching rule would be needed to connect them. No such structure is supplied.

3. **Meta-knowledge versus universal ignorance.** Knowing that one does not know a proposition is a higher-order epistemic claim. It does not, by itself, prove that every observer is ignorant of every distinct entity's consciousness in every possible world.

4. **No direct access versus no inference.** The direct source distinguishes direct first-person access from mediated third-person evidence. It does not simply erase behavior, language or physical correlates as evidence. The assistant's stronger formulations that external claims are wholly unjustifiable or that behavior is the sole criterion require separate attribution and argument.

5. **Opposite-twin premise.** The manuscript's universal conclusion requires, for every relevant observer, target and world, an accessible world with identical evidence and opposite consciousness value. Absence of a known bridge principle does not entail that premise. S5 does not generate it.

6. **First-person knowledge.** Positive introspection does not by itself establish `K_a C(a)`. First-person access must be separately axiomatized. The existing Lean module keeps this distinction.

7. **Strange-loop boundary.** The user asks whether the whole interaction might be a relevant system. The assistant's more affirmative description is not a demonstrated boundary or mechanism. A typed system boundary, state space, coupling and transition map are absent.

8. **Cognitive dynamics omitted.** The modal manuscript treats consciousness as a Boolean predicate over worlds. It does not model the direct candidate features of self-modeling, model revision, feedback, persistence or dynamic equilibrium; nor does it represent graded consciousness.

9. **S5 is model-relative.** The existing formalization obtains an S5-style relation by defining indistinguishability as equality of a declared evidence map. That is a property of the reconstruction, not a source-established fact about every real observer.

10. **Authorship and chronology remain unresolved.** The direct transcript witnesses lack raw conversation IDs/message UUIDs and exact composition dates; the manuscript lacks verified author/date metadata. None is silently attributed to Nathan or an assistant.
