## 3. New substantive improvement: a typed source-to-observable contract

The central gap is not another generic geometry theorem. Existing work has source-address/claim gates, projection-factorization gates and experimental admission requirements, but these remain separate. There is no single workflow record joining a source-bound claim to a specific physical acquisition, an explicit representation-map chain, a calibrated instrument operator and a locked held-out observable.

I added a public-safe contract that binds seven address layers to the same claim/target/scope:

1. **Claim:** claim ID, target ID and scope ID.
2. **Source:** repository/commit, immutable blob hash, locator, source role/author status, source class and extractor version.
3. **Acquisition:** identifiable system, independent run ID, instrument, calibration, raw-file hashes, units, uncertainty/covariance and replicate structure.
4. **Representation:** versioned maps with domain/codomain, operation class, fibers/kernel, inverse/section, invariants, composition order, regularity and error bound.
5. **Model:** standard comparator, H(s)H version, shared/nuisance/optional parameters and frozen calibration state.
6. **Readout:** observable ID, estimator, units, observation operator, covariance propagation and scoring rule.
7. **Decision:** predeclared calibration/holdout split, threshold or predictive score, execution record and independent readback.

### Map-class distinction

The contract distinguishes:

- invertible coordinate/readout change;
- embedding or restriction/local chart;
- quotient/coarse-graining;
- intersection or response;
- instrument/observation operator;
- reconstruction/inverse problem;
- approximate correspondence.

This prevents a common category error: an invertible coordinate change is not a coarse-graining; a camera reconstruction is not a direct observation of the complete state; an instrument operator is not a geometric projection; and an intersection is not automatically a contact law.

For sequential maps p1 through pn, the relevant composite is P = pn ∘ … ∘ p1. A deterministic readout can descend to the final representation only when it is constant on the fibers of that composite. A result for one stage does not automatically license a later many-to-one reduction. A noisy instrument requires a stochastic observation model and covariance; the deterministic fiber theorem does not supply those.

## 5. Falsification and optional-structure rule

The proposed comparison ladder is:

- H0: standard model plus its declared instrument/measurement operator.
- H1: minimally sufficient H(s)H representation, with no undeclared free parameters.
- H2+: separately declared optional extensions such as thickness, twist, contact law or medium response, each with its own map and parameter count.

All models must use the same acquisition data, calibration policy, nuisance treatment, observable definition and holdout. Fit only on calibration data; predict the held-out observable distribution before opening the holdout.

An added parameter is identifiable in a channel only if its effect on a preregistered held-out observable cannot be absorbed by shared or nuisance parameters. Otherwise record UNIDENTIFIABLE_IN_THIS_CHANNEL, not validated or absent. If removing a structure changes the source-defined object type, record NOT_ABLATABLE / REPRESENTATION_CHANGE rather than claiming a clean ablation.

Continuous readouts require a covariance-aware predictive score after the full covariance and effective independent dimension are established. Stochastic outcomes require calibrated predictive distributions and a preregistered proper scoring rule. Numerical thresholds and replicate counts remain unset until raw data, covariance and power analysis are available.

A calibration-only improvement is FIT_ONLY. Failure of a preregistered holdout criterion rejects that H(s)H instantiation for the tested regime. Indistinguishable H(s)H and standard predictions establish no distinct empirical claim in that channel. If both fail, inspect the shared measurement/instrument model before treating the result as support for either theory.
