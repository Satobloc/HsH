## 4. New external measured system: rotating ³He-B

Primary source:
- J. T. Mäkinen et al., “Rotating quantum wave turbulence,” Nature Physics 19, 898–903 (2023), DOI 10.1038/s41567-023-01966-z.
- Publisher correction dated 2023-04-17, DOI 10.1038/s41567-023-02057-9. The corrected Methods calibration gives the quartz-tuning-fork geometric factor scaling C ∝ p_F^4. This correction matters to the pressure-dependent temperature-to-mutual-friction calibration.
- Zenodo version record: DOI 10.5281/zenodo.7525698, v1; created 2023-02-14, published 2023-02-17, modified 2024-07-12; 16 files, 1.6 MB; Aalto portal lists 10.5281/zenodo.7525697 and links to the 7525698 version record. Preserve both identifiers; the exact concept/version relation was not verified through the Zenodo API.
- Aalto catalogue lists CC-BY-4.0. The Zenodo record describes the deposit as the minimal dataset required to reproduce the experimental findings.

### Apparatus and observables

- Rotating superfluid ³He-B in a quartz cylinder about 150 mm long and 5.85 mm in diameter.
- Two NMR pickup coils separated axially by 90 mm.
- NMR precession frequency is an orientation/texture observable; relaxation is used to infer vortex-line density. These are indirect readouts, not direct imaging of individual vortex cores.
- Quartz tuning-fork thermometry is calibrated against the Leggett frequency. The paper reports a geometric factor about 10 ± 1.5 kHz and intrinsic zero-temperature resonance width about 10–100 mHz. Corrected pressure dependence C ∝ p_F^4 is part of the calibration model.
- The paper describes a quantum boundary layer at the rough bottom boundary, where enhanced mutual friction and vortex pinning couple vortex lines to the driven normal component. This is an apparatus-specific mechanism, not a universal medium law.

### Public data coverage

The 16-file Zenodo record contains figure-organized CSVs, DatasetDescription.pdf, and Fig2c.tar.gz (a simulation archive). The record's MD5 manifest is preserved in the public submission. Selected CSV previews were inspected. DatasetDescription.pdf was present but its binary preview could not be decoded in this reader runtime. No dataset bytes were downloaded to the local runtime and no independent SHA-256 of the raw data files was computed; Zenodo MD5s are quoted as published.

The deposit is a minimal figure-data package, not a complete acquisition archive. The paper states additional data and analysis code are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request. No full joint covariance matrix, raw NMR time-domain archive, complete tuning-fork calibration log, or rotation-controller telemetry was verified in the public deposit.

### Epistemic separation

- **Measured:** NMR frequency/relaxation signals at two axial locations, tuning-fork resonance curves, applied rotation protocol, pressure, and temperature-control observations.
- **Calibrated:** thermometer response and pressure-dependent conversion from measured temperature to mutual-friction parameter α.
- **Derived:** vortex tilt/orientation and line density inferred from NMR; dissipation times and cascade extent inferred from relaxation.
- **Fitted:** boundary-layer pumping efficiency β and decay time τ. The paper fits the full exponential decay and reports that fitting only the tail increases scatter.
- **Simulated:** a Biot-Savart vortex-filament calculation with 19 vortices, 0.125 mm spatial resolution over 50 mm lengths. The paper says this resolution is insufficient to determine the Kelvin-wave spectrum reliably. The 127 reported reconnections occur in this simulation, not as a direct experimental count.

The paper estimates systematic uncertainty in converting temperature to α at up to 30% at higher temperatures. At the lowest temperatures, tuning-fork resonance-width uncertainty corresponds to ΔT below 0.01 T_c. The paper says uncertainty in τ⁻¹ in Fig. 4a is below symbol size and gives 1σ intervals for relevant Fig. 3c quantities. A full joint covariance matrix was not located.

The paper explicitly says the experiment cannot distinguish between the proposed Kelvin-wave cascade models. The supported result is qualitative: inferred cascade extent increases as α decreases. The core-size trend used to interpret pressure-dependent pinning is a prior-literature interpretation, not a direct core-radius measurement in this experiment.
