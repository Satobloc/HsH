# BEDROCK — SAT / H(s)H Theory Control Surface

**Status:** ACTIVE — central living theory-premise register  
**Authority:** Nathan Direct controls this register.  
**Scope:** SAT → H(s)H theory work.  
**Rule:** **Read this before substantive theory work. Return here when theory work changes what may safely be assumed.**

> This document is a control surface for theory work, not a claim that the research program is physically correct. It records what the project currently permits workers to treat as premises, what remains tentative, and what has been overridden, contested, or superseded.

## 0. Central routing rule

All substantive SAT/H(s)H theory work should use this document as its central premise-state reference.

The working flow is:

**Foundational sources / historical SAT → BEDROCK → sandbox theory work → tentative finding → testing / comparison / Nathan review → BEDROCK**

Do not reconstruct the project's bedrock from the newest paper, the newest conversation, a synthesis, memory, outside mathematics, or repeated AI agreement when this register or its cited primary sources answer the question.

This register does **not** replace primary sources. It points to them and records their current authority/status.

### Two central theory surfaces

- **[BEDROCK.md](BEDROCK.md)** answers: **What may theory work presently assume, and with what authority/status?**
- **[STATE_OF_THE_THEORY.md](STATE_OF_THE_THEORY.md)** answers: **Where is the theory now, what is live, what is being translated/built, and what remains unclosed?**

`STATE_OF_THE_THEORY.md` is subordinate to BEDROCK and newer Nathan Direct. It is the living map, not an independent source of authority.

## 1. Authority and status strata

### ND — Nathan Direct / Override

Explicit current direction from Nathan. This is the highest local authority for what the project presently means, assumes, calls something, or treats as live.

A newer Nathan Direct statement may promote, modify, demote, or override an older entry. **Do not delete the older state.** Mark the relationship so the historical transition remains visible.

### FB — Foundational Bedrock

Premises inherited from material explicitly designated foundational by Nathan, especially **The Fundamental Intuitions — Extended**. These may be reformulated or developed later, but workers should not silently discard or rewrite them.

### WB — Established Working Bedrock

Premises sufficiently established within the current project state that ordinary sandbox work may use them without reopening them every time. This status means *operatively usable*, not mathematically proven or physically validated.

### TF — Tentative Finding

Current result, construction, interpretation, mapping, or hypothesis important enough to carry forward but still defeasible. **Tentative findings must not silently become downstream bedrock merely through repetition.**

### OC — Open / Contested

Live ambiguity, unresolved alternatives, or a proposition currently under challenge. Downstream work must preserve the uncertainty.

### SS — Superseded / Historical

Previously important premise or formulation no longer controlling current theory work. Preserve it with provenance and point to what superseded it.

## 6. Promotion / demotion discipline

Allowed state movements include:

- `TF → WB` — a tentative finding becomes an ordinary working premise.
- `TF → OC` — evidence or interpretation becomes materially unsettled.
- `TF → SS` — abandoned or superseded.
- `WB → OC` — a working premise is reopened.
- `WB → SS` — no longer controlling.
- `ND` may modify any state at any time.

A status movement should name **why** it occurred and link the source/test/directive that caused it. Do not use generic language such as “verified.” State what was checked and how.

`FB` is an ancestry/authority designation and need not mean every historical formulation remains a current working proposition. A foundational idea can have later descendants, corrections, or scoped reinterpretations while its historical foundational role remains intact.

## 9. Maintenance rule

Keep this document **shorter than the theory it governs**. It is an indexed premise register, not a replacement synthesis.

Prefer one precise proposition plus source links over long explanatory prose. When an entry grows large, move the detailed derivation/history to its proper source document and leave the controlling proposition and pointers here.

The objective is that any competent fresh instance can answer, before beginning theory construction:

1. What may I presently treat as bedrock?
2. What is only tentative?
3. What is contested or superseded?
4. What did Nathan directly establish or override?
5. Where is the primary evidence/source?
6. What must I not silently assume?
